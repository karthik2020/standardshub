Conceptual Framework Linkage

Extending a control-transfer concept beyond ordinary revenue
Not every gain-generating transaction is a REVENUE transaction – selling a building, a piece of equipment, or an intangible asset to a party OTHER than a customer produces a GAIN or LOSS, not revenue, since it's not part of the entity's ordinary revenue-generating activities. ASC 610 exists to apply the SAME control-transfer logic ASC 606 uses for customer revenue to these OTHER, non-customer asset disposal transactions – extending a consistent measurement philosophy across the whole Codification rather than leaving nonfinancial asset sales to a separate, disconnected framework.
2

Scope – Sales of Nonfinancial Assets to Non-Customers

PP&E, real estate, and intangible asset disposals
ASC 610-20 applies to the derecognition of NONFINANCIAL assets (and IN-SUBSTANCE nonfinancial assets) – including PP&E, real estate, and intangible assets – sold or otherwise transferred to a party that is NOT a customer in the ordinary course of business. Sales to actual CUSTOMERS of nonfinancial assets that ARE part of an entity's ordinary output remain governed by ASC 606 directly.
3

Why ASC 610-20 Exists

Borrowing ASC 606's control model, applied to a different population of transactions
ASC 610-20 REPLACED much of the older, more piecemeal real estate sale guidance previously in ASC 360-20 – extending ASC 606's CONTROL TRANSFER concept broadly to nonfinancial asset sales generally, rather than maintaining a separate, older, real-estate-specific gain recognition framework. The entity applies ASC 606's core mechanics (assessing when control transfers, measuring the transaction price including any variable consideration or financing components) to determine the TIMING and AMOUNT of the gain or loss recognised – even though the counterparty isn't a "customer" in the ordinary revenue sense.
4

Sale-Leaseback Interaction With ASC 842

A control transfer test gates the sale-leaseback outcome
Where a nonfinancial asset sale is combined with a LEASEBACK, ASC 610-20's control-transfer principles work together with ASC 842 (Leases) to determine whether a genuine SALE has occurred at all – if control has NOT genuinely transferred (e.g. the seller retains substantive control through the leaseback terms), the transaction is instead accounted for as a FINANCING arrangement rather than a sale, with no gain recognised.
5

Partial Sales of Nonfinancial Assets

Recognising a proportionate gain
Where an entity sells only a PARTIAL interest in a nonfinancial asset (e.g. a partial interest in a real estate property, retaining an ongoing interest itself), the entity applies the control transfer model to determine the extent to which control of the sold interest has transferred, potentially recognising a PARTIAL gain on the interest sold while continuing to carry its retained interest at its existing basis.
6

Worked Example – Gain on Sale of a Building to a Non-Customer

Applying the control transfer model outside of ordinary revenue
Example: Selling a surplus office building
A manufacturing entity (whose ordinary business is producing goods, not selling real estate) sells a surplus office building with a carrying amount of $6,000,000, to an unrelated buyer for $8,500,000 cash, with control transferring immediately upon closing.
Applying ASC 610-20's cross-reference to ASC 606's control transfer principles, the sale is recognised at the CLOSING date (when control transfers): Debit Cash $8,500,000 / Credit Building $6,000,000 / Credit Gain on sale of nonfinancial asset (OTHER INCOME, not revenue) $2,500,000 – presented as a GAIN, distinct from the entity's ordinary product revenue, consistent with this Topic's "Other Income" characterisation.

ASC 610 vs IAS 16/IAS 38 – a Difference in Elaboration, Not Outcome

Comparison – ASC 610-20 vs IAS 16/IAS 38 derecognition guidance
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 610-20)IFRS (IAS 16 / IAS 38)
Dedicated Topic for nonfinancial asset sale gainsYes – explicitly cross-references the full ASC 606 modelNo separate standard – IAS 16/IAS 38 contain their OWN, simpler derecognition guidance directly
Underlying principleControl transfer, using ASC 606's full mechanics (variable consideration, financing components, etc.)Derecognise when control transfers; recognise the difference between net proceeds and carrying amount in profit or loss – a SIMPLER, more direct calculation
Typical outcomeOften the SAME numerical result as the simpler IFRS approachSame underlying economics, reached via a less elaborate mechanic
This is a good example of a difference in STRUCTURE and ELABORATION rather than substance – both frameworks reach for the same control-transfer concept and often the same numerical gain/loss, but ASC 610-20's explicit, detailed cross-reference to the FULL ASC 606 machinery (variable consideration, financing components, and so on) makes it a considerably more ELABORATE mechanism than IAS 16/IAS 38's comparatively simple, self-contained derecognition guidance.

Quick-reference glossary preview
In-substance nonfinancial asset
A group of assets or a subsidiary substantially comprised of nonfinancial assets, treated similarly to a direct nonfinancial asset sale under this Topic.
Other income
Gains from transactions outside an entity's ordinary revenue-generating activities, distinguished from revenue on the Income Statement.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Nonfinancial asset
An asset other than cash, an equity interest, or a contractual right to receive/deliver cash or another financial instrument – e.g. PP&E, real estate, and intangible assets.
Retained interest
A continuing ownership stake in an asset following a partial sale, carried at its existing basis while a proportionate gain is recognised on the sold interest.
