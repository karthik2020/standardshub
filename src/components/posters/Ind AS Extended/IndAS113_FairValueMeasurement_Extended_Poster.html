Conceptual Framework Linkage

Identical exit-price philosophy to IFRS 13
Ind AS 113 applies the IDENTICAL definition as IFRS 13 – fair value is the price that would be RECEIVED to sell an asset or PAID to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between MARKET PARTICIPANTS at the measurement date – a market-based, NOT entity-specific, exit price notion.
HOW IND AS 113 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Comparability – a SINGLE fair value framework applied consistently across all Ind AS standards requiring fair value measurement – identical to IFRS 13Faithful representation – the fair value hierarchy prioritises OBSERVABLE market data over unobservable estimates – identical to IFRS 13Interaction with India's valuer regulatory framework – Ind AS 113's fair value MEASUREMENT principles operate alongside the Companies Act/IBBI's Registered Valuers framework, which governs WHO is legally qualified to perform certain statutory valuations in India – a genuinely important practical, though not accounting-substantive, Indian consideration
Ind AS 113 introduced NO change to the fair value definition relative to IFRS 13 – India's distinctive feature lies in its professional valuer REGULATION, not the accounting framework itself.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 113 covers
Ind AS 113 applies WHEN another Ind AS requires or permits fair value measurement/disclosure – IDENTICAL "framework, not trigger" scope to IFRS 13, with the SAME scope exclusions (share-based payment, leases, NRV-based inventory measurement).
Key definitions you must know
Fair value
IDENTICAL definition to IFRS 13 – an exit price notion.
Registered Valuer
A professional registered with IBBI (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India) under the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017, required for certain statutory Indian valuations.

The Fair Value Definition – Exit Price

Identical to IFRS 13
The PRINCIPAL market (or most advantageous market, absent a principal market) determines the relevant price – IDENTICAL to IFRS 13, with NO Indian-specific modification to the underlying definition.

The Fair Value Hierarchy (with worked example)

Identical classification approach to IFRS 13
Example 1: Classifying an Indian company's fair value inputs
An Indian company holds NSE-listed equity shares (quoted daily), a corporate bond priced using observable yield curves from comparable rated Indian bonds, and an unlisted startup equity investment valued using a discounted cash flow model with company-specific growth assumptions.
NSE-listed equity: Level 1 (quoted price, active market)
Corporate bond: Level 2 (observable yield curve inputs)
Unlisted startup investment: Level 3 (unobservable company-specific assumptions)
An IDENTICAL classification methodology to how IFRS 13 would categorise the same instruments globally.

Highest and Best Use – Non-Financial Assets

Identical to IFRS 13
Fair value of a non-financial asset considers a market participant's ability to generate value through its HIGHEST AND BEST USE – IDENTICAL to IFRS 13, relevant for Ind AS 40 investment property and Ind AS 16 revaluations.

Registered Valuers – The Indian Regulatory Context (with worked example)

A genuinely important Indian professional regulation layer
The Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017 (under IBBI) require CERTAIN statutory valuations (e.g. for company law purposes such as mergers, share allotments) to be performed by a REGISTERED VALUER – a specific professional qualification. This governs WHO may perform certain Indian statutory valuations, operating ALONGSIDE Ind AS 113's own fair value MEASUREMENT methodology, similar in spirit to how SEBI/RBI/IRDAI regulate other specific sectors.
Example 2: Registered Valuer requirement for a share swap ratio
An Indian company undergoing a merger under Sections 230-232 of the Companies Act, 2013 requires a fair value-based share exchange ratio determination.
This valuation MUST be performed by a Registered Valuer under IBBI rules – a REGULATORY requirement governing WHO performs the valuation – while the underlying fair value MEASUREMENT PRINCIPLES applied (market participant perspective, valuation techniques, hierarchy) remain governed by Ind AS 113, IDENTICAL to IFRS 13's own framework.

Fair Value at Initial Recognition

Identical to IFRS 13
Transaction price is presumed to equal fair value at initial recognition unless evidence indicates otherwise (e.g. related party transactions, forced transactions) – IDENTICAL to IFRS 13.
