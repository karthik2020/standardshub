Conceptual Framework Linkage

The matching counterpart to revenue
Cost of sales represents the direct cost of goods or services an entity has sold – the natural counterpart matched against revenue to arrive at gross profit. ASC 705 governs a handful of SPECIFIC presentation and measurement questions within this broad caption, most notably how VENDOR REBATES received by a customer should be reflected (Section 4) – essentially the CUSTOMER-side mirror of ASC 606's own "consideration payable to a customer" guidance from the vendor's perspective.
2

Scope & Applicability

A narrow, specific-question Topic
ASC 705 applies to the presentation of cost of sales/services generally, with its most substantive content addressing (a) consideration a customer receives from its VENDOR (rebates, allowances), and (b) the classification policy choice for shipping and handling costs.
3

General Presentation

A standard income statement caption
Cost of sales (or cost of services) is presented as a distinct caption within the Income Statement, subtracted from revenue to arrive at GROSS PROFIT in a multi-step presentation – the same basic presentation convention used broadly across both US GAAP and IFRS practice.
4

Consideration Received from a Vendor (705-20)

Presumed to reduce cost, unless proven otherwise
Cash consideration (rebates, allowances) a CUSTOMER receives from its VENDOR is PRESUMED to be a reduction of the PRICE of the vendor's goods or services – and therefore reduces the CUSTOMER'S cost of sales (or inventory cost, if not yet sold) – UNLESS the consideration is a payment for a DISTINCT good or service the customer has separately provided TO the vendor (in which case it is recognised as the customer's own revenue instead). This is essentially the MIRROR IMAGE of ASC 606's "consideration payable to a customer" guidance viewed from the VENDOR's side – the same underlying transaction, analysed from both parties' perspectives using parallel logic.
5

Shipping and Handling Costs – a Policy Election

A genuine accounting policy choice
An entity may elect, as an accounting POLICY, to treat shipping and handling activities performed AFTER a customer obtains control of a good as a FULFILMENT activity (expensed as incurred, typically within cost of sales) RATHER than evaluating them as a separate performance obligation under ASC 606 – avoiding the need to allocate a portion of the transaction price to shipping specifically. This policy choice must be applied CONSISTENTLY, and disclosed.
6

Worked Example – a Vendor Rebate Reducing Cost of Sales

A volume rebate from a supplier
Example: An annual purchase volume rebate
A retailer purchases $4,000,000 of inventory from a supplier during the year. Under the purchase agreement, the supplier pays the retailer a $120,000 rebate once annual purchases exceed $3,500,000 – a rebate not tied to any distinct service the retailer provides the supplier.
The $120,000 rebate is presumed to be a REDUCTION of the PRICE the retailer paid for the inventory – reducing INVENTORY cost (if unsold) or COST OF SALES (for the portion already sold) by $120,000, rather than being recognised as separate income or revenue for the retailer.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where judgement matters most
The central judgement is determining whether vendor consideration is genuinely a PRICE REDUCTION (the default presumption) or actual payment for a DISTINCT good/service the customer has provided – requiring evidence that the customer transferred something separately identifiable and reasonably priced to the vendor, rather than simply receiving a rebate.

ASC 705 vs IFRS – a Mirror of ASC 606's Own Guidance

Comparison – ASC 705 vs IFRS equivalents
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 705)IFRS equivalent
Dedicated cost of sales TopicYes – a specific, narrow TopicNo standalone equivalent – vendor rebates are addressed through IAS 2's inventory cost principles and IFRS 15's mirror "consideration payable to a customer" guidance
Vendor rebate presumptionReduces cost of sales/inventory, unless proven to be payment for a distinct serviceSame substantive presumption, given the shared joint-project drafting heritage between ASC 606 and IFRS 15
Shipping/handling policy electionExplicit, codified electionSimilar flexibility exists in practice under IFRS 15, though without an equally explicit named policy election
Given ASC 606 and IFRS 15's shared origin, the vendor consideration guidance here is broadly converged in substance, even though US GAAP houses it in its own separate, dedicated Topic rather than folding it entirely into the revenue standard.

Quick-reference glossary preview
Consideration received from a vendor
Cash or credits a customer receives from its supplier, presumed to reduce the price paid for goods/services rather than constitute separate income.
Fulfilment activity
An activity (such as shipping) an entity performs to fulfil a promise to a customer without transferring a good or service to that customer, expensed as incurred rather than treated as its own performance obligation.
