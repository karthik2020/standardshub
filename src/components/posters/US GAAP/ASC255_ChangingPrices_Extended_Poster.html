ASC 255 — Changing Prices
US GAAP · Subtopic: 255-10 Overall · Largely dormant legacy Topic — still technically part of the Codification, but its substantive disclosure requirements were made voluntary decades ago and are essentially unused today
⚠ LARGELY DORMANT — Substantive requirements made voluntary in 1986 (SFAS 89); rarely applied in current practice
What ASC 255 originally addressed
ASC 255 traces back to SFAS 33 (1979), issued during a period of high US inflation, which REQUIRED large public companies to disclose SUPPLEMENTARY information showing the effects of CHANGING PRICES on their financial statements — specifically, CONSTANT PURCHASING POWER (general price-level) restatements and CURRENT COST (specific price-level) restatements of items such as inventory, PP&E, and cost of sales, alongside conventional historical-cost figures.
Why it faded — and why it's not formally "withdrawn"
As US inflation subsided through the mid-1980s and the significant cost of preparing these supplementary disclosures became hard to justify against their diminishing usefulness, the FASB issued SFAS 89 (1986), which made the specific numerical disclosures VOLUNTARY rather than mandatory. In practice, essentially NO preparers continued voluntarily providing this information once it was no longer required, and the disclosures fell out of use entirely. Unlike a withdrawn SIC or IFRIC, ASC 255 was never formally rescinded — it remains a placeholder Topic in the Codification, but with essentially no live, actively-applied content today.
Where this stands today, and the IFRS comparison
Current statusTechnically still part of the Codification (Topic 255), but its substantive disclosures are voluntary and virtually never provided in practice
Why it hasn't been fully removedThe Codification generally retains topics unless a specific ASU formally supersedes them; changing-price disclosure was never technically prohibited, only made optional, so the Topic persists as an inactive shell
IFRS comparisonIAS 29 (Financial Reporting in Hyperinflationary Economies) remains an ACTIVE, mandatory IFRS standard — but it applies only to entities whose FUNCTIONAL currency is hyperinflationary, a narrow and specific trigger, unlike ASC 255's broader (and now dormant) general price-change disclosure concept. There is no current, general-purpose IFRS equivalent to ASC 255's original supplementary constant-purchasing-power/current-cost disclosure concept for NON-hyperinflationary economies either — both frameworks have effectively moved away from general inflation-adjusted supplementary reporting for ordinary (non-hyperinflationary) economies.
