Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why insurance needed a genuinely NEW measurement model
Insurance contracts bundle a service (bearing insurance risk) with FUTURE, uncertain cash flows spanning many years. The Conceptual Framework's faithful representation objective demands measurement reflecting the TIME VALUE of money and RISK, updated as new information emerges — something the previous "IFRS 4" interim standard (which largely preserved diverse national GAAPs) never achieved. IFRS 17 introduces a genuinely NEW, current-value, risk-adjusted measurement model.
HOW IFRS 17 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Faithful representation — the liability is measured as the PRESENT VALUE of future cash flows, updated EVERY period for new information — not a static, day-one estimate Relevance — the Contractual Service Margin (CSM) defers PROFIT recognition until insurance SERVICES are actually provided, matching revenue to the period of coverage Comparability — a SINGLE global measurement model replaces the wide diversity IFRS 4 permitted, dramatically improving cross-jurisdiction, cross-insurer comparability
IFRS 17 replaced IFRS 4 — an INTERIM standard that largely permitted CONTINUATION of existing national insurance accounting practices, deliberately analogous to IFRS 6's approach for mineral resources, pending a comprehensive project.
The building blocks, at a glance
Insurance contract liability = Fulfilment cash flows (estimates of future cash flows + discounting + risk adjustment) + Contractual Service Margin (CSM)
ℹ️ The RISK ADJUSTMENT compensates the entity for bearing NON-FINANCIAL risk uncertainty — a DIRECT reflection of the Framework's faithful representation of genuine risk exposure.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRS 17 covers
IFRS 17 applies to insurance contracts issued (including reinsurance contracts issued), reinsurance contracts HELD, and investment contracts with DISCRETIONARY PARTICIPATION FEATURES issued by an entity that ALSO issues insurance contracts.
An "insurance contract" is one under which the issuer accepts SIGNIFICANT INSURANCE RISK from another party (the policyholder) by agreeing to compensate the policyholder if a specified UNCERTAIN future event adversely affects them.
Key definitions you must know
Contractual Service Margin (CSM)
A component of the insurance contract liability representing UNEARNED PROFIT the entity will recognise as it provides insurance coverage in the future.
Risk adjustment
The compensation an entity requires for bearing uncertainty about the AMOUNT and TIMING of cash flows arising from non-financial risk.
Coverage unit
A measure of the insurance contract services provided in a period, used to allocate the CSM to profit or loss.
3

Level of Aggregation

Portfolios, cohorts, and profitability groups
Contracts are aggregated into PORTFOLIOS (contracts subject to similar risks, managed together), then divided into GROUPS by issue year (ANNUAL COHORTS) and by PROFITABILITY at initial recognition: (1) contracts that are ONEROUS at initial recognition; (2) contracts with NO significant possibility of becoming onerous; and (3) remaining contracts.
This granular grouping PREVENTS profitable contracts from masking losses on onerous ones — a direct comparability/faithful representation safeguard, ensuring loss-making business is visible rather than netted away.

The General Measurement Model — Building Blocks (with worked example)

Three building blocks, updated every period
Estimates of future cash flows — probability-weighted, using all reasonable and supportable information available Discounting — adjusts for the time value of money, using rates consistent with observable market prices Risk adjustment — compensation for bearing non-financial risk uncertainty
Example 1: Building the fulfilment cash flows
An insurer estimates future claim/expense cash outflows of $9,500,000 (probability-weighted), and future premium inflows of $10,800,000. Discounting reduces the net figure by $200,000. A risk adjustment of $150,000 is added for non-financial risk uncertainty.
Net cash flows = 10,800,000 − 9,500,000 = $1,300,000 (net inflow)
After discounting = 1,300,000 − 200,000 = $1,100,000
Fulfilment cash flows (net of risk adjustment) = 1,100,000 − 150,000 = $950,000 (net asset/liability position before CSM)
5

The Contractual Service Margin (CSM) (with worked example)

Deferring profit until service is delivered
At INITIAL recognition, if the fulfilment cash flows show a NET INFLOW (expected profit), NO profit is recognised immediately — instead, a CSM liability equal to that net inflow is established, and released to profit or loss SYSTEMATICALLY over the coverage period, based on COVERAGE UNITS provided.
Example 2: Establishing and releasing the CSM
Continuing Example 1: fulfilment cash flows show a net expected inflow of $950,000 at initial recognition. The contract provides coverage over 5 years, with EQUAL coverage units expected each year.
CSM established at initial recognition = $950,000 (NO day-one profit recognised)
CSM released to profit each year = 950,000 / 5 = $190,000 per year, recognised as insurance services are actually PROVIDED — matching profit recognition to the delivery of coverage, not to the contract's inception.
6

Onerous Contracts — Loss Component (with worked example)

Losses recognised IMMEDIATELY — no CSM buffer
If fulfilment cash flows show a NET OUTFLOW (expected LOSS) at initial recognition, NO CSM is established — the ENTIRE expected loss is recognised IMMEDIATELY in profit or loss, since a CSM cannot be negative.
Example 3: An onerous contract group
A different group of contracts shows fulfilment cash flows of a NET OUTFLOW of $400,000 at initial recognition (expected claims exceed premiums, after discounting and risk adjustment).
This ENTIRE $400,000 loss is recognised IMMEDIATELY in profit or loss at initial recognition — NO CSM is created (since it cannot be negative), directly surfacing the loss-making nature of this contract group, rather than allowing it to be masked by profitable business elsewhere.

The Premium Allocation Approach (PAA)

A simplified alternative for short-duration contracts
For contracts with a coverage period of ONE YEAR OR LESS, or where the PAA produces a measurement NOT materially different from the general model, an entity MAY elect the simplified PAA — broadly similar to a traditional unearned premium reserve approach, without the full building-block complexity of the general model.
8

The Variable Fee Approach (VFA)

For contracts with direct participation features
For contracts where the entity's obligation is to pay the policyholder an amount equal to a SUBSTANTIAL SHARE of the fair value returns on underlying items (e.g. unit-linked or with-profits contracts), the VFA modifies the CSM to also absorb the entity's share of CHANGES in the fair value of underlying items — treating the entity's fee as more akin to an ASSET MANAGEMENT fee than a traditional insurance margin.
9

Presentation — Insurance Revenue

A genuinely new revenue concept, distinct from premiums received
"Insurance revenue" under IFRS 17 does NOT equal premiums received — it reflects the CONSIDERATION for the PERIOD's coverage and services, EXCLUDING any investment/deposit components. This represents a fundamentally different, and often SMALLER, revenue figure than the premium cash actually collected, aligning insurance revenue recognition conceptually with IFRS 15's control-transfer philosophy.

Disclosure Requirements

Required disclosures
Explanation of recognised amounts, including reconciliations of the CSM, risk adjustment, and liability for remaining coverage/incurred claims Significant judgements in applying IFRS 17, including methods for estimating fulfilment cash flows, discount rates, and risk adjustment Nature and extent of risks arising from insurance contracts (insurance risk, credit risk, liquidity risk, market risk)

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Contract boundary determination (which future cash flows to include) Level of aggregation and grouping of onerous vs profitable contracts Choice of coverage units used to release the CSM
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Probability-weighted future cash flow estimates (claims, expenses, premiums) Discount rates reflecting the characteristics of the insurance liability Risk adjustment for non-financial risk
⚠️ IFRS 17 is widely regarded as one of the MOST complex standards ever issued by the IASB, requiring extensive actuarial input and systems investment — its implementation was one of the longest in IFRS history.

IFRS vs US GAAP

Comparison — IFRS 17 vs US GAAP
FeatureIFRS (IFRS 17)US GAAP
Measurement modelCurrent, risk-adjusted, discounted fulfilment cash flows + CSMUS GAAP retains a MORE traditional model (ASC 944), with LESS emphasis on current discount rates for most long-duration contract types, though a 2018 update (LDTI) introduced some current-value elements
Profit deferral (CSM equivalent)Explicit CSM, released over the coverage periodNo direct CSM equivalent for most contract types under traditional US GAAP models
Revenue presentation"Insurance revenue" excludes investment componentsPremium revenue presentation differs, generally closer to premiums written/earned
IFRS 17 represents a SIGNIFICANTLY more comprehensive overhaul than the parallel US GAAP long-duration contract reforms — IFRS and US GAAP insurance accounting remain SUBSTANTIALLY different, unlike the close convergence seen in IFRS 15/9/13.
