1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

A distribution is a genuine exchange transaction, not a mere transfer
Before IFRIC 17, practice varied on whether a non-cash dividend (e.g. a subsidiary spun off to shareholders, or property distributed in kind) should be measured at the CARRYING AMOUNT of the asset given up, or at its FAIR VALUE. IFRIC 17 concludes it is, in substance, an exchange – the entity settles an obligation to its owners using an asset – and should be measured accordingly, with any difference between carrying amount and fair value recognised as a gain or loss, just as it would be on any other disposal.
Faithful representation of economic substance over form
Measuring the distribution at carrying amount would silently embed unrealised gains or losses into equity without ever passing through profit or loss – even though the entity is, economically, giving up an asset worth its fair value in settlement of an obligation. IFRIC 17's fair value approach ensures that economic event is properly reflected in performance.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRIC 17 covers
IFRIC 17 applies to non-reciprocal distributions of non-cash assets by an entity to its owners acting in their capacity as owners, and to distributions that give owners a CHOICE between a non-cash asset and a cash alternative. It does NOT apply to distributions of a non-cash asset that is ultimately controlled by the same party (or parties) both before and after the distribution – i.e. common control transactions are excluded.
TYPICAL FACT PATTERNS
Spin-off of a subsidiary to existing shareholders (dividend in specie)Distribution of property, plant, or investments directly to ownersDividends where owners can elect cash or shares/assets
Key definitions
Non-cash asset
Any asset other than cash distributed to owners, including businesses as defined in IFRS 3, ownership interests in another entity, or unincorporated business assets.
Dividend payable
The liability recognised when a distribution is appropriately authorised and is no longer at the discretion of the entity.
3

When the Dividend Liability Is Recognised

The recognition trigger
An entity recognises a liability to pay a dividend when the dividend is APPROPRIATELY AUTHORISED and is no longer at the discretion of the entity – the specific point at which this occurs is determined by the relevant jurisdiction's laws (for example, when the dividend is approved by shareholders at a general meeting, or approved by the board where board approval is sufficient under local law).
⚠️ Proposed but not-yet-approved dividends are NOT recognised as a liability – consistent with IAS 10's general principle that dividends declared after the reporting period are non-adjusting events.
4

Measuring the Dividend Payable

Fair value, not carrying amount
The dividend payable is measured at the FAIR VALUE of the assets to be distributed. Where the entity gives owners a choice between a non-cash asset and a cash alternative, the entity estimates the dividend payable considering BOTH the fair value of each alternative AND the associated probability of owners selecting each alternative.
5

Remeasurement at Each Reporting Date

Tracking fair value changes through equity, not profit or loss
At the end of each reporting period, and at the date of settlement, the entity REVIEWS AND ADJUSTS the carrying amount of the dividend payable to reflect changes in the fair value of the assets to be distributed, with any changes recognised DIRECTLY IN EQUITY as adjustments to the amount of the distribution – NOT in profit or loss, since the entity's own performance has not changed; only the value of what it has committed to give owners has moved.
6

Settlement – The Gain or Loss on Distribution

Where the gain or loss finally crystallises
When the entity settles the dividend payable by distributing the non-cash assets, it recognises the DIFFERENCE, if any, between the carrying amount of the assets distributed and the carrying amount of the dividend payable in PROFIT OR LOSS. This is the point the "exchange" logic from Section 1 fully plays out – the entity effectively disposes of an asset at fair value in settlement of its obligation, and any resulting gain or loss is recognised just as it would be on a sale.

Worked Example – Full Distribution Cycle

Spin-off of a subsidiary to shareholders
Example: Dividend in specie of a subsidiary's net assets
A parent's board approves a distribution of its wholly owned subsidiary to shareholders. At the date of authorisation, the subsidiary's net assets have a carrying amount of $18,000,000 and a fair value of $25,000,000. By the settlement date three months later, the subsidiary's fair value has risen to $26,500,000; its carrying amount is unchanged at $18,000,000.
At authorisation: Debit Retained earnings $25,000,000 / Credit Dividend payable $25,000,000 (measured at fair value).
At settlement, remeasure first: Debit Retained earnings $1,500,000 / Credit Dividend payable $1,500,000 (fair value increase recognised directly in equity) – dividend payable now $26,500,000.
On distribution: Debit Dividend payable $26,500,000 / Credit Subsidiary net assets $18,000,000 / Credit Gain on distribution (profit or loss) $8,500,000.
8

Choice-of-Alternative Dividends

Probability-weighted estimation
Where owners can choose between a non-cash asset and an equivalent cash amount, the entity does not simply record the higher (or lower) of the two – it estimates a PROBABILITY-WEIGHTED dividend payable, reassessed at each reporting date as more information about likely owner elections becomes available, converging on the actual amounts distributed once elections are finalised and settlement occurs.

IFRIC 17 vs US GAAP

Comparison – IFRIC 17 vs US GAAP (ASC 845-10)
FeatureIFRS (IFRIC 17)US GAAP (ASC 845-10)
Measurement basis for the distributionFair value, with gain/loss recognised in profit or loss on settlementGenerally FAIR VALUE for a nonreciprocal, pro rata distribution to owners of a business (spin-off) is a notable EXCEPTION – such distributions are typically recorded at CARRYING AMOUNT (no gain recognised), a key divergence from IFRIC 17
Non-business, non-pro-rata distributionsFair value under IFRIC 17Generally also fair value under US GAAP for non-pro-rata or non-spin-off distributions, narrowing the gap in those specific cases
Liability recognition triggerAppropriate authorisation, no longer at the entity's discretionBroadly similar declaration-based trigger, though precise mechanics follow US corporate law conventions
The SPIN-OFF carve-out under US GAAP (carrying amount, no gain) is the standout divergence – it means an identical pro rata spin-off transaction can produce a substantial recognised gain under IFRS but NO gain at all under US GAAP.

Quick-reference glossary
Dividend in specie
A dividend satisfied by the transfer of a non-cash asset (such as shares in a subsidiary, property, or investments) rather than cash.
Common control transaction
A transaction where the non-cash asset distributed is ultimately controlled by the same party both before and after the distribution – explicitly EXCLUDED from IFRIC 17's scope.
14

Quick-Reference Glossary

Non-reciprocal distribution
A transfer of assets to owners for no consideration in return, made in their capacity as owners – the defining feature distinguishing a dividend from an ordinary exchange transaction.
Owners acting in their capacity as owners
A qualifying condition confirming the recipient receives the distribution BECAUSE they are an owner (e.g. pro rata to shareholding) rather than in some other capacity, such as an employee or supplier.
