IFRIC 13 – Customer Loyalty Programmes (WITHDRAWN)
Extended reference poster · Withdrawn 2018 · Superseded by IFRS 15's five-step model · Retained for historical/comparative reference
🚫 WITHDRAWN INTERPRETATION – SUPERSEDED BY IFRS 15 – IFRIC 13 pioneered the treatment of loyalty award credits (e.g. airline miles, retail loyalty points) as a SEPARATE component of a sale, allocating consideration between the initial sale and the award credits based on RELATIVE FAIR VALUE – a multi-element deferred revenue approach that directly foreshadowed IFRS 15's own performance obligation model. Conceptual Framework Linkage (Historical)

A multi-element revenue pioneer, later generalised into IFRS 15
IFRIC 13 required entities granting CUSTOMER LOYALTY AWARD CREDITS (points, miles, vouchers) as part of a sale transaction to treat those credits as a SEPARATELY IDENTIFIABLE component of the sale – allocating part of the consideration received to the award credits (deferred until redemption/expiry) rather than recognising the ENTIRE sale price as revenue immediately.
WHY THIS BECAME THE CONCEPTUAL SEED FOR IFRS 15
Multi-element allocation logic – IFRIC 13's relative-fair-value allocation between the initial sale and the award credits is the DIRECT conceptual ancestor of IFRS 15's own multi-performance-obligation allocation modelDeferred revenue recognition – recognising award credit revenue only upon REDEMPTION (or expiry) anticipated IFRS 15's own "satisfaction of performance obligation" trigger
IFRIC 13 is widely regarded as one of the MOST influential interpretations in shaping IFRS 15's eventual five-step model – its withdrawal reflects GENERALISATION into a comprehensive standard, not any flaw in its own reasoning.
The core allocation logic IFRIC 13 established
Total consideration ALLOCATED between: (1) goods/services sold, and (2) award credits, based on RELATIVE FAIR VALUE
ℹ️ This exact allocation LOGIC is preserved, generalised, within IFRS 15's own performance obligation allocation model.

Scope & Applicability (Historical)

What IFRIC 13 covered
IFRIC 13 applied to customer loyalty award CREDITS granted as PART of a sales transaction, entitling customers to future free/discounted goods or services – historically significant for airlines, retailers, hotels, and credit card reward programmes.
Key definitions (historical)
Award credits
Points, miles, or vouchers granted to customers as part of a sale, redeemable for future goods/services/discounts.
Redemption rate
The estimated proportion of granted award credits expected to ultimately be redeemed by customers – a key measurement input, concept preserved in current practice.

Award Credits as a Separately Identifiable Component (Historical)

The conceptual ancestor of a "performance obligation"
IFRIC 13 required identifying award credits as a SEPARATE component with its OWN fair value, distinct from the initial sale – the DIRECT conceptual precursor to IFRS 15's own "distinct performance obligation" concept.
4

Allocating Consideration – Relative Fair Value (Historical, with worked example)

Historical illustration
Example 1: Retail loyalty points allocation
A retailer sold goods for $10,000, granting 1,000 loyalty points to the customer. The points had a fair value (based on what a customer would pay to acquire them separately, adjusted for expected redemption rates) of $800.
Under IFRIC 13, of the $10,000 total consideration, $800 was ALLOCATED to the award credits (deferred as a liability until redemption/expiry), with the REMAINING $9,200 recognised as revenue immediately for the goods sold – an allocation methodology that IFRS 15's current five-step model would reach a SIMILAR outcome for, now framed as allocating transaction price across distinct performance obligations based on standalone selling prices.

Recognising Revenue on Award Redemption (Historical)

Deferred until redemption or expiry
The DEFERRED consideration allocated to award credits was recognised as revenue only WHEN the credits were REDEEMED (or expired unredeemed), based on the proportion of TOTAL expected redemptions occurring in the period – a mechanism CONCEPTUALLY preserved within IFRS 15's own revenue recognition timing principles for options granting a material right.
6

Third-Party Loyalty Programme Operators (Historical)

A principal vs agent-adjacent consideration
Where award credits entitled customers to goods/services from a THIRD PARTY (e.g. a coalition loyalty programme), IFRIC 13 addressed whether the ISSUING entity acted as PRINCIPAL or AGENT in relation to the award credits – a question CONCEPTUALLY related to IFRS 15's own current principal-vs-agent framework.
7

How This Guidance Became Part of IFRS 15

A generalisation, not a replacement of flawed logic
When the IASB developed IFRS 15 as a SINGLE, comprehensive revenue standard applicable across ALL industries, IFRIC 13's multi-element allocation approach was RECOGNISED as directly foreshadowing the desired general model. IFRS 15's five-step framework GENERALISED IFRIC 13's loyalty-specific logic into a broadly applicable "distinct performance obligation" and "material right" concept, applicable to loyalty programmes AND countless other multi-element arrangements. IFRIC 13 was formally WITHDRAWN upon IFRS 15 becoming effective.
8

Common Application Sectors

Where the "material right" analysis is most frequently applied
The retail, airline, hospitality, and banking/credit card sectors operate extensive loyalty programmes globally, making the "material right" analysis under IFRS 15 (the direct descendant of IFRIC 13's logic) genuinely significant in current practice – the analytical approach and worked example above remain fully applicable, simply reframed under IFRS 15's own performance obligation terminology.
