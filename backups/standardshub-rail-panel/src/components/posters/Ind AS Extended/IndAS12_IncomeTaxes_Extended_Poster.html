Conceptual Framework Linkage

Identical balance sheet approach to IAS 12
Ind AS 12 applies the IDENTICAL principle as IAS 12 — deferred tax is recognised for TEMPORARY DIFFERENCES between the carrying amount of assets/liabilities and their TAX BASE, using a balance-sheet-focused (not income-statement-focused) methodology.
HOW IND AS 12 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Faithful representation — recognising the future tax consequences of transactions already reflected in the financial statements — identical to IAS 12MAT credit — a genuinely Indian addition — Appendix A to Ind AS 12 recognises MAT credit as a DEFERRED TAX ASSET when it is probable the entity will pay normal income tax (rather than MAT) during the specified carry-forward period and utilise the credit — a concept ARISING FROM India's specific tax law structure, with NO direct IAS 12 counterpart
This MAT credit treatment is one of the FEW Ind AS standards with a genuinely SUBSTANTIVE addition beyond its IFRS counterpart, driven by a uniquely Indian tax mechanism rather than a general accounting divergence.
The core temporary difference formula — identical to IAS 12
Temporary difference = Carrying amount − Tax base
ℹ️ IDENTICAL formula to IAS 12 — taxable temporary differences generate deferred tax LIABILITIES; deductible temporary differences generate deferred tax ASSETS (subject to recoverability).

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 12 covers
Ind AS 12 applies to the accounting for income taxes, including current tax and deferred tax — IDENTICAL scope to IAS 12, with Appendix A's MAT credit guidance layered on top for Indian applicability.
Key definitions you must know
Tax base
IDENTICAL definition to IAS 12 — the amount attributed to an asset/liability for tax purposes.
MAT (Minimum Alternate Tax)
A minimum tax levied under Section 115JB of the Income Tax Act, 1961 on companies with low taxable income relative to book profit, generating a creditable excess under Section 115JAA.

The Balance Sheet Approach — Temporary Differences

Identical to IAS 12
Taxable temporary differences (e.g. accelerated tax depreciation) and deductible temporary differences (e.g. provisions not yet tax-deductible) are IDENTIFIED and MEASURED identically to IAS 12.

Deferred Tax Assets & Liabilities (with worked example)

Identical recognition criteria to IAS 12
Example 1: Deferred tax on a provision
An Indian company recognises a ₹20,00,000 warranty provision for accounting purposes, which is NOT tax-deductible until actually paid, with a corporate tax rate of 25%.
A DEFERRED TAX ASSET of ₹5,00,000 (₹20,00,000 × 25%) is recognised, since the provision creates a DEDUCTIBLE temporary difference expected to reverse when the warranty is paid and becomes tax-deductible — an IDENTICAL calculation to how IAS 12 would treat the same fact pattern globally.

Measurement — Tax Rates & Substantively Enacted Law

Identical to IAS 12
Deferred tax is measured at tax rates EXPECTED to apply when the asset is realised/liability settled, based on rates (and tax laws) ENACTED or SUBSTANTIVELY ENACTED by the reporting date — IDENTICAL to IAS 12, relevant when the Indian Finance Act introduces rate changes.

MAT Credit Entitlement — The Genuine Indian Addition (with worked example)

The single most important Ind AS 12-specific addition
Under India's tax law, companies pay the HIGHER of normal income tax or MAT (calculated on book profit). The EXCESS paid as MAT over normal tax generates a CREDIT that can be carried forward and set off against normal tax in FUTURE years (subject to a statutory time limit). Appendix A to Ind AS 12 requires this MAT credit to be recognised as a DEFERRED TAX ASSET when it is PROBABLE that the future economic benefit (i.e., future normal tax liability against which the credit can be utilised) will be realised — a recognition test analogous in SPIRIT to IAS 12's own deferred tax asset recoverability test, but applied to a tax mechanism that simply does NOT EXIST under IFRS in most jurisdictions.
Example 2: Recognising a MAT credit asset
An Indian company pays MAT of ₹30,00,000 in a year where its normal tax liability would have been only ₹18,00,000, generating a MAT credit of ₹12,00,000. Management projects sufficient future normal tax liabilities within the statutory carry-forward period to utilise this credit.
The ₹12,00,000 MAT credit is recognised as a DEFERRED TAX ASSET (specifically, "MAT credit entitlement") under Appendix A to Ind AS 12 — a recognition mechanism with NO direct IAS 12 counterpart, since MAT itself is a specifically Indian tax construct.

Uncertain Tax Treatments

Identical to IAS 12 (via Appendix C, aligned with IFRIC 23)
Uncertainty over income tax treatments is assessed using probability-weighted or most-likely-amount approaches, considering whether the tax authority is likely to accept the treatment — IDENTICAL to IAS 12's own Appendix C (converged with IFRIC 23).
