Conceptual Framework Linkage

The old world: a binary on/off-balance-sheet test
ASC 840's central feature – the one ASC 842 was specifically designed to FIX – was a binary CLASSIFICATION test that determined whether a lease appeared on the LESSEE's balance sheet at all. Leases meeting specific criteria were CAPITAL leases (on balance sheet, asset + liability); everything else was an OPERATING lease, disclosed only in the notes with NO balance sheet recognition – enabling substantial "off-balance-sheet financing" that became a major source of criticism, ultimately driving the joint FASB-IASB lease project that produced ASC 842 and IFRS 16.
2

Scope

Leases of property, plant, and equipment
ASC 840 applied to agreements conveying the RIGHT to use property, plant, or equipment for a stated period in exchange for consideration – covering both LESSEE and LESSOR accounting.
3

Lease Classification – the Capital Lease Criteria

Meeting ANY ONE of four bright-line criteria required capital lease treatment
CriterionBright-line test
Transfer of ownershipOwnership transfers to the lessee by the end of the lease term
Bargain purchase optionThe lease contains an option to purchase at a price low enough that exercise is reasonably assured
Lease term testLease term is 75% or more of the asset's remaining economic life
Present value testPresent value of minimum lease payments is 90% or more of the asset's fair value
Meeting ANY ONE of these four RIGID, BRIGHT-LINE criteria triggered CAPITAL lease treatment; meeting NONE of them meant OPERATING lease treatment – a genuinely binary, all-or-nothing outcome that created strong incentives to structure leases just outside these thresholds (e.g. a lease term of 74% of economic life, deliberately avoiding capital lease treatment).
4

Capital Lease Accounting (Lessee)

Asset and liability recognised, similar to a financed purchase
A CAPITAL lease was recorded by the lessee as an ASSET and a corresponding LIABILITY, both at the PRESENT VALUE of minimum lease payments (capped at fair value) – the asset was then DEPRECIATED, and the liability amortised using the EFFECTIVE INTEREST method, producing a FRONT-LOADED total expense pattern (higher interest expense early, declining over time) – economically, the lease was treated much like a FINANCED PURCHASE of the underlying asset.
5

Operating Lease Accounting (Lessee) – the Off-Balance-Sheet Result

No balance sheet recognition, straight-line rent expense
An OPERATING lease resulted in NO asset or liability recognition on the lessee's balance sheet – lease payments were simply expensed on a STRAIGHT-LINE basis over the lease term (regardless of the actual cash payment pattern), with future minimum lease payment commitments disclosed only in the NOTES. This was the central criticism that led to ASC 842 – enormous aggregate lease commitments (retail store leases, aircraft leases, etc.) sat entirely OFF the face of the balance sheet.
6

Lessor Accounting

Sales-type, direct financing, and operating lease categories
Lessors classified leases into SALES-TYPE leases (where the lessor is effectively a manufacturer/dealer, recognising profit at lease inception), DIRECT FINANCING leases (a pure financing arrangement with no dealer profit), or OPERATING leases (the lessor retains substantially all risks/rewards, continues carrying the asset, and recognises rental income over time) – largely mirroring, in a broad classification sense, the criteria used for lessee capital lease classification.
7

Sale-Leaseback Transactions

Deferred gain recognition, contingent on leaseback classification
In a SALE-LEASEBACK transaction, an entity sold an asset and simultaneously leased it back – under ASC 840, any GAIN on the sale was generally DEFERRED and amortised over the leaseback term (rather than recognised immediately), particularly where the leaseback qualified as a CAPITAL lease, reflecting that the transaction was substantively more like a FINANCING arrangement than a genuine sale.
8

Worked Example – Capital Lease Classification

Applying the bright-line tests
Example: A 6-year equipment lease
A lessee enters a 6-year lease for equipment with a 7-year remaining economic life and a $2,000,000 fair value. The present value of minimum lease payments is $1,850,000. There's no ownership transfer or bargain purchase option.
Lease term test: 6 ÷ 7 = 85.7% ≥ 75% → CRITERION MET. Present value test: $1,850,000 ÷ $2,000,000 = 92.5% ≥ 90% → CRITERION MET. Since AT LEAST ONE criterion is met (in fact two), this is classified as a CAPITAL LEASE – recognised on the lessee's balance sheet as an asset and liability at $1,850,000.
9

Worked Example – Operating Lease Straight-Line Expense

Smoothing uneven payments
Example: An escalating-payment operating lease
A 3-year operating lease has annual payments of $100,000, $110,000, and $120,000 in years 1, 2, and 3 respectively (total: $330,000).
Total lease payments $330,000 ÷ 3 years = $110,000 straight-line rent expense per year, regardless of the actual cash paid each year. In year 1, the entity expenses $110,000 but pays only $100,000, recognising a $10,000 deferred rent LIABILITY; in year 3 the reverse occurs.

ASC 840 vs IAS 17 – Comparison

Comparison – ASC 840 vs IAS 17 (both now superseded)
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 840)IFRS (IAS 17)
Classification testFour RIGID bright-line criteria (75%, 90% thresholds)Similar concept ("finance" vs "operating" lease) but more PRINCIPLES-BASED, no fixed bright-line percentages
Off-balance-sheet operating leasesPermitted – no lessee balance sheet recognitionAlso permitted under IAS 17 – broadly converged outcome (the shared flaw both ASC 842 and IFRS 16 were designed to fix)
Both legacy standards shared the SAME fundamental flaw (off-balance-sheet operating leases) – which is precisely why the FASB-IASB pursued a JOINT lease project, though it ultimately produced two standards with a genuine remaining divergence (see the ASC 842 poster, Section 6).
