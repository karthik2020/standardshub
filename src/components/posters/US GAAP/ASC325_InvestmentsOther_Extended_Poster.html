Conceptual Framework Linkage

A catch-all for investment types that don't fit the main investment Topics
Most investments fall neatly into debt securities (ASC 320), equity securities (ASC 321), or equity-method investments (ASC 323). ASC 325 exists to house the remaining investment types that DON'T fit those categories cleanly – most notably investments in life insurance contracts and retained beneficial interests in securitised financial assets – ensuring these genuinely different economic arrangements aren't forced into a measurement model designed for ordinary securities.
2

Scope – a Residual Topic, Narrowed by ASU 2016-01

What remains here today
ASC 325 today primarily addresses two specific subtopics: INVESTMENTS IN INSURANCE CONTRACTS (325-30) – most commonly company-owned or bank-owned life insurance policies – and BENEFICIAL INTERESTS IN SECURITIZED FINANCIAL ASSETS (325-40), which governs retained interests an entity holds after transferring financial assets into a securitisation structure.
3

Why This Topic Shrank

The old "cost method investments" subtopic is gone
Before ASU 2016-01, ASC 325 included a significant subtopic (325-20) governing COST METHOD investments – equity securities without a readily determinable fair value, held at cost. ASU 2016-01 ELIMINATED the cost method entirely and replaced it with the MEASUREMENT ALTERNATIVE now housed in ASC 321 (see the ASC 321 poster) – moving that entire body of guidance out of ASC 325 and substantially narrowing this Topic's remaining scope to the two subtopics described in Section 2.
4

Investments in Insurance Contracts (325-30)

Cash surrender value as the measurement basis
An entity holding a life insurance policy on an employee or other insured individual (a common arrangement for funding executive benefits, often called "company-owned life insurance" or COLI) generally recognises the CASH SURRENDER VALUE of the policy as an asset – the amount that would be realised if the policy were surrendered at the reporting date, adjusted for other amounts due under the contract (e.g. loans against the policy). Changes in cash surrender value are recognised in NET INCOME.
5

Beneficial Interests in Securitized Financial Assets (325-40)

Interests retained after a securitisation transfer
When an entity TRANSFERS financial assets into a securitisation vehicle (e.g. a pool of receivables or mortgages) but RETAINS a beneficial interest in the resulting structure (such as a subordinated tranche or servicing asset), ASC 325-40 governs the SUBSEQUENT measurement of that retained interest – generally applying a model similar to the AFS debt security approach (fair value, with credit losses assessed under ASC 326), reflecting the retained interest's exposure to the underlying pool's credit performance.
6

Worked Example – Company-Owned Life Insurance

Recognising cash surrender value growth
Example: An executive benefit funding policy
An entity holds a life insurance policy funding a deferred compensation arrangement. The policy's cash surrender value was $1,200,000 at the start of the year and $1,340,000 at year end, with no policy loans outstanding.
The entity recognises the $140,000 increase in cash surrender value in NET INCOME: Debit Cash surrender value of life insurance $140,000 / Credit Income (or gain) from life insurance $140,000 – reported as an ASSET on the Balance Sheet at its current cash surrender value.
7

Worked Example – a Retained Beneficial Interest

A subordinated tranche retained after securitisation
Example: Retained interest in a receivables securitisation
An entity securitises a pool of trade receivables, transferring the senior interests to third-party investors while retaining a subordinated residual interest valued at $2,000,000 at inception, exposed to first-loss credit risk on the underlying pool.
The retained $2,000,000 beneficial interest is recognised as an asset and subsequently measured at FAIR VALUE, with credit loss exposure assessed under ASC 326 – reflecting both interest-rate-driven fair value movements and the entity's genuine exposure to underlying pool credit deterioration, given its subordinated (first-loss) position.

ASC 325 vs IFRS – No Single Equivalent Topic

Comparison – ASC 325 vs IFRS
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 325)IFRS equivalent(s)
Dedicated Topic for miscellaneous investmentsYes – a specific residual TopicNo single equivalent – cash surrender value life insurance investments and retained securitisation interests are analysed under IFRS 9's general financial instrument classification framework instead
Life insurance cash surrender value as a named measurement basisExplicitly codifiedNo specifically named equivalent measurement concept – treated under IFRS 9's general fair value/amortised cost principles as applicable
ASC 325 is a good example of the Codification's tendency toward highly SPECIFIC, narrowly scoped Topics for particular fact patterns, where IFRS more often relies on applying GENERAL principles from a broader standard (here, IFRS 9) to the same underlying economics.

Cash surrender value
The amount an entity would realise if a life insurance policy were surrendered at the reporting date, the measurement basis for company/bank-owned life insurance investments.
Retained beneficial interest
An interest an entity keeps in a securitisation structure after transferring the underlying financial assets, exposed to the pool's credit performance.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Securitisation
A structuring transaction pooling financial assets (e.g. receivables or mortgages) and issuing interests in that pool to investors.
Subordinated interest
An interest in a securitisation structure that absorbs losses before more senior interests, typically retained by the originating entity.
