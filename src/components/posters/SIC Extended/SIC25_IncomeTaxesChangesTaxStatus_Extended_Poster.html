1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Tax consequences should follow the item that caused them
A change in an entity's tax status (e.g. converting from a partnership to a corporation, or a shareholder base change that alters applicable tax rates) can trigger significant remeasurement of deferred tax balances. IAS 12's general principle is that the tax effect of a transaction or event is recognised in the SAME place as that transaction or event itself. SIC-25 confirms this "backward tracing" principle applies even when the trigger is a tax STATUS change rather than an ordinary transaction.
Avoiding an artificial profit-or-loss distortion
If ALL tax consequences of a status change were dumped into profit or loss regardless of where the underlying item originally sat, a revaluation surplus recognised years earlier in OCI could see its associated deferred tax swing through current-period profit or loss – an outcome that would misrepresent current performance. SIC-25's exception preserves consistency between where an item's tax effect is recognised and where the item itself was originally recognised.
2

Scope & Applicability

What SIC-25 covers
SIC-25 addresses the income tax consequences of a CHANGE IN THE TAX STATUS of an entity or its shareholders that does NOT result from a change in the owners' contribution to the entity's net assets (i.e. not itself a capital transaction).
TYPICAL FACT PATTERNS
Conversion of an unincorporated entity (e.g. partnership) into a corporationAn entity's shares becoming publicly listed (or being delisted), changing the applicable tax regimeReorganisation between a parent and its subsidiary altering the group's tax status
Key definitions
Change in tax status
An event that alters the tax treatment applicable to an entity or its shareholders, other than a change resulting from an owners' contribution to net assets.
Backward tracing
The principle of recognising the tax effect of a transaction or event in the same component of the financial statements (profit or loss, OCI, or equity) as the underlying transaction or event itself.
3

What Counts as a "Change in Tax Status"

A trigger distinct from ordinary transactions
SIC-25's trigger event is a change in tax status ITSELF – not simply a new transaction that happens to have tax consequences under an unchanged tax regime. Examples include a legal reorganisation changing the entity's classification for tax purposes, or a listing event changing the applicable tax rate or regime for the entity or its shareholders – as opposed to, say, an ordinary asset revaluation, which is addressed by IAS 12's general tax allocation principles directly rather than by SIC-25.
4

The Backward-Tracing Principle

The mechanism SIC-25 applies
Where a change in tax status affects the measurement of DEFERRED (or current) tax balances, the resulting tax consequence is allocated to the SAME COMPONENT of the financial statements – profit or loss, OCI, or equity – as the transaction or event that ORIGINALLY gave rise to the underlying temporary difference or item, even though that original transaction may have occurred in a prior period.
5

The Default – Profit or Loss

Most tax status change effects land here
The current and deferred tax consequences of a change in tax status are included in PROFIT OR LOSS for the period, UNLESS those consequences relate to transactions and events that resulted, in the same or a different period, in a DIRECT CREDIT OR CHARGE to OCI or to equity – in which case Section 6's exception applies instead.
6

The Exception – Following the Original Item

Where the tax effect is redirected
To the extent the tax status change's consequences relate to an item ORIGINALLY recognised in OCI (such as a revaluation surplus, or certain foreign currency translation differences) or directly in EQUITY, the RELATED tax effect of the status change is ALSO recognised in OCI or equity respectively – consistent with where the underlying item sits, not simply defaulted to profit or loss because the status change itself is a separate triggering event.
7

Worked Example – Entity Conversion

Partnership converting to a corporation
Example: Change in applicable tax rate on conversion
A partnership, previously taxed on a pass-through basis, converts to a corporation, becoming subject to a 25% corporate tax rate directly at the entity level for the first time. This conversion requires the entity to recognise deferred tax on temporary differences that previously did not generate an entity-level deferred tax balance, totalling $1,200,000. None of the underlying temporary differences relate to items originally recognised in OCI or equity.
The full $1,200,000 deferred tax effect is recognised in PROFIT OR LOSS for the period of conversion, since none of the underlying items were originally recognised outside profit or loss – the DEFAULT treatment in Section 5 applies.
8

Worked Example – Tax Effect Tied to a Revaluation

The exception in action
Example: Deferred tax on a historical revaluation surplus
An entity's shares become publicly listed, changing the applicable tax rate from 20% to 28%. The entity holds property previously revalued upward, with a $3,000,000 revaluation surplus recognised in OCI in a prior period, against which a deferred tax liability was recognised (also through OCI at the time). The tax status change requires remeasuring that deferred tax liability at the new 28% rate, increasing it by $240,000.
Because the ORIGINAL revaluation surplus was recognised in OCI, the $240,000 incremental deferred tax effect arising from the tax status change is ALSO recognised in OCI – reducing the revaluation surplus further – rather than being charged to profit or loss, per Section 6's exception.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES

SIC-25 vs US GAAP

Comparison – SIC-25 vs US GAAP (ASC 740)
FeatureIFRS (SIC-25)US GAAP (ASC 740)
Backward tracing generallyApplied to tax status changes specifically via SIC-25, and to tax rate/law changes more broadly under IAS 12US GAAP generally does NOT use backward tracing for tax LAW or RATE changes – the effect of a tax rate change is recognised entirely in profit or loss from continuing operations, regardless of where the original item sits (a well-known US GAAP/IFRS divergence)
Tax status changes specificallyBackward tracing applies per SIC-25Tax status changes are treated similarly to rate changes – generally recognised in income from continuing operations, without the same backward-tracing exception
This is a genuinely significant divergence: IFRS's backward-tracing approach for tax rate and status changes (via IAS 12 and SIC-25) contrasts with US GAAP's general rule that such effects flow through profit or loss regardless of the original item's location – producing materially different OCI/profit-or-loss splits for identical fact patterns.

Quick-reference glossary
Temporary difference
A difference between the carrying amount of an asset or liability and its tax base, the underlying driver of deferred tax under IAS 12 that SIC-25's tracing rule applies to when a tax status change occurs.
Owners' contribution to net assets
A capital transaction, such as a share issue, explicitly EXCLUDED from SIC-25's definition of a "change in tax status" – genuine capital transactions are addressed under other guidance, not SIC-25.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Deferred tax
Tax recognised in respect of temporary differences that will reverse in future periods, subject to remeasurement whenever applicable tax rates or an entity's tax status changes.
Direct credit or charge to equity
An amount recognised outside profit or loss, directly within a component of equity – the trigger condition for SIC-25's backward-tracing exception to apply.
