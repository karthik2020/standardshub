Conceptual Framework Linkage

Extending ASPE's optional-consolidation philosophy to associates
ASPE Section 3051 governs the ACCOUNTING for INVESTMENTS in EQUITY and DEBT instruments — WHERE they FALL outside the SCOPE of Section 3856 (FINANCIAL Instruments) OR Section 1591 (SUBSIDIARIES). GENUINELY distinctively, THIS Section EXTENDS the SAME "COST method as a GENUINE alternative" philosophy we SAW under Section 1591 (see the COMPANION poster) even TO investments WHERE significant INFLUENCE exists (Section 4).
2

Scope

Equity and debt instruments not otherwise governed
This Section applies to INVESTMENTS in EQUITY and DEBT instruments GENERALLY, EXCLUDING investments in SUBSIDIARIES (SECTION 1591), interests in JOINTLY controlled ENTERPRISES (SECTION 3056), and FINANCIAL instruments WITHIN the SPECIFIC scope OF Section 3856.

4

Significant Influence — Equity or Cost Method, a Genuine Choice

Neither method is mandatory
FOR investments WHERE significant INFLUENCE exists, an ENTERPRISE elects EITHER the EQUITY method (ADJUSTING the INVESTMENT for ITS share OF the investee'S profit OR loss) OR the COST method (CARRYING the INVESTMENT at COST, recognizing INCOME only TO the extent OF dividends RECEIVED) — a GENUINE, unrestricted CHOICE between the TWO.
5

THE DISTINCTIVE POINT — Contrasting With IAS 28's Mandatory Equity Method

A meaningful, practically significant divergence
INTERNATIONALLY, IAS 28 MANDATES the EQUITY method FOR associates (SIGNIFICANT-influence investments), WITH NO cost-method ALTERNATIVE permitted. ASPE, BY contrast, PERMITS the COST method as a GENUINELY equal ALTERNATIVE — CONSISTENT with the SAME cost-BENEFIT philosophy WE saw under SECTION 1591'S subsidiary ACCOUNTING choice. THIS REINFORCES a BROADER pattern RUNNING throughout ASPE'S GROUP-and-investment accounting: PRIVATE enterprises are GENERALLY given the OPTION to AVOID the ADDED complexity OF reflecting an INVESTEE'S underlying PERFORMANCE, WHERE the INCREMENTAL benefit TO their OWN specific USER base is GENUINELY limited.
6

Investments in Subsidiaries — Cross-Referenced to Section 1591

The same three-way choice, in a different Section
WHERE an INVESTMENT amounts TO a SUBSIDIARY (i.e. CONTROL exists), the ACCOUNTING is GOVERNED by SECTION 1591'S own THREE-way choice AMONG consolidation, THE cost method, and THE equity method — SEE the COMPANION poster FOR the FULL detail.
7

Portfolio Investments Without Significant Influence

Fair value for quoted investments, cost or elected fair value for unquoted
PORTFOLIO investments QUOTED in an ACTIVE market ARE measured AT fair VALUE through NET income (a MANDATORY treatment FOR quoted INSTRUMENTS). UNQUOTED portfolio INVESTMENTS are MEASURED at COST, UNLESS the ENTERPRISE elects TO measure them AT fair VALUE instead.
8

Worked Example — Electing the Cost Method for a Significant-Influence Investment

A simple, dividend-driven approach for a 30% stake
Example: A minority strategic investment
A private company holds a 30% interest (significant influence) in another private enterprise, originally acquired for $900,000. The investee earns $400,000 of net income during the year and pays the investor a $60,000 dividend. The investor elects the cost method.
The INVESTOR'S own FINANCIAL statements SHOW the INVESTMENT at ITS ORIGINAL $900,000 COST (UNCHANGED), with INVESTMENT income OF $60,000 (the DIVIDEND received) — the INVESTEE'S own $400,000 net INCOME is NOT reflected WITHIN the INVESTOR'S statements, GIVEN the COST method ELECTION.
9

Worked Example — a Quoted Portfolio Investment at Fair Value

Mandatory fair value for actively traded instruments
Example: A holding in a publicly traded company
A private company holds a small, 2% stake in a publicly traded company, purchased for $150,000. By year end, the quoted market value has risen to $185,000.
The COMPANY MEASURES the investment AT its QUOTED fair VALUE of $185,000, RECOGNIZING a $185,000 − $150,000 = $35,000 gain DIRECTLY within NET income — MANDATORY, GIVEN the INVESTMENT is QUOTED in an ACTIVE market.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Significant influence
The ability to affect the strategic operating, investing, and financing policies of an investee, without amounting to control or joint control.
Portfolio investment
An investment where neither control nor significant influence exists, measured at fair value if quoted, or cost (or elected fair value) if unquoted.
