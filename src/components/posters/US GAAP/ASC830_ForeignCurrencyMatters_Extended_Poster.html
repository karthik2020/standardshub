Conceptual Framework Linkage

Two distinct problems, two distinct mechanisms
Foreign currency accounting actually solves TWO different problems: (1) converting TRANSACTIONS or records kept in one currency into an entity's OWN functional currency (REMEASUREMENT), and (2) converting an entire foreign entity's functional-currency financial statements into the PARENT's reporting currency for consolidation (TRANSLATION). ASC 830 keeps these two mechanisms genuinely distinct, each with its own rules.
Converged core mechanics, one genuinely different structural response to hyperinflation
ASC 830 and IAS 21 share closely aligned functional currency determination and translation mechanics — including a rare case of BOTH frameworks recycling the cumulative translation adjustment upon disposal. The standout divergence is how each framework handles HIGHLY INFLATIONARY economies (Sections 7-8) — a genuinely different structural response to the same underlying problem.
2

Scope & Applicability

Any entity with foreign currency transactions or foreign operations
ASC 830 applies to entities with TRANSACTIONS denominated in a currency other than their own functional currency, and to entities with FOREIGN OPERATIONS (subsidiaries, branches, equity method investees) whose financial statements must be translated for consolidation or combination with the reporting entity's own statements.
3

Functional Currency Determination

The primary economic environment an entity operates in
An entity's FUNCTIONAL CURRENCY is the currency of the PRIMARY ECONOMIC ENVIRONMENT in which it primarily GENERATES and EXPENDS cash — assessed using indicators such as the currency that most influences sales prices, the currency of the entity's cost base, the currency of financing, and the extent of intercompany transactions. This is a broadly similar indicator-based determination process to IAS 21's own functional currency assessment — a converged core concept.
4

Remeasurement — When the Functional Currency Differs From the Recording Currency

The "temporal method"
Where an entity's BOOKS are kept in a currency DIFFERENT from its own FUNCTIONAL currency, REMEASUREMENT converts those records into the functional currency: MONETARY items (cash, receivables, payables) are remeasured at the CURRENT exchange rate; NONMONETARY items (inventory, PP&E, at historical cost) are remeasured at the HISTORICAL rate in effect when acquired; and the resulting remeasurement gain/loss flows THROUGH NET INCOME — the "TEMPORAL METHOD." This mirrors IAS 21's own approach when an entity's records aren't kept in its functional currency.
5

Translation — When the Functional Currency Differs From the Reporting Currency

Current rate method for consolidation purposes
Where a foreign entity's FUNCTIONAL currency DIFFERS from the PARENT's REPORTING currency, TRANSLATION converts the foreign entity's entire functional-currency financial statements for consolidation: assets and liabilities are translated at the CURRENT (period-end) rate; equity accounts at HISTORICAL rates; income statement items at the rates in effect at the transaction dates (or a suitably WEIGHTED AVERAGE rate as a practical expedient) — the resulting TRANSLATION ADJUSTMENT is recognised in OCI, NOT net income. Converged with IAS 21's own translation mechanics.
6

The Cumulative Translation Adjustment (CTA)

Deferred in OCI, recycled upon disposal — genuinely converged
The accumulated TRANSLATION adjustment (the "CTA") sits within OCI, and is NOT reclassified to net income UNTIL the entity SELLS or SUBSTANTIALLY COMPLETELY LIQUIDATES the foreign entity generating it — at which point the CUMULATIVE CTA balance related to that entity IS recycled into net income as part of the gain/loss on disposal. This is CONVERGED with IAS 21's own CTA treatment — both frameworks genuinely recycle CTA upon disposal, a notable area of alignment given some other "permanent OCI, no recycling" treatments discussed elsewhere in this library (e.g. pension remeasurements under IAS 19).
7

Highly Inflationary Economies

A functional currency override, not a restatement mechanism
If a foreign entity's economy is deemed HIGHLY INFLATIONARY — generally, CUMULATIVE inflation over the preceding THREE years approaching or exceeding roughly 100% — the foreign entity's FUNCTIONAL CURRENCY is DEEMED to be the REPORTING currency of its PARENT. There is effectively NO separate functional currency for that entity anymore; its records are directly REMEASURED into the parent's reporting currency using the TEMPORAL METHOD (Section 4), as if that were the functional currency from the start — remeasurement gains/losses flow through NET INCOME, not OCI.
8

IAS 29's Contrasting Price-Level Restatement Approach

A genuinely different mechanical solution to the same problem
IAS 21 itself does NOT have an equivalent "highly inflationary functional currency override." Instead, IFRS addresses hyperinflation through a SEPARATE standard, IAS 29 (Financial Reporting in Hyperinflationary Economies): an entity whose functional currency IS hyperinflationary must RESTATE its financial statements for the CHANGES IN THE GENERAL PRICE LEVEL (using a general price index) BEFORE translation occurs — essentially adjusting the foreign entity's OWN statements to a CONSISTENT current-purchasing-power basis first, and ONLY THEN translating the restated amounts at the CURRENT exchange rate. This is a genuinely DIFFERENT mechanical approach: US GAAP simply SWITCHES functional currency and remeasures using the temporal method; IFRS instead RESTATES for price-level changes within the hyperinflationary entity's own functional currency, then translates normally — solving the same underlying distortion problem through entirely different machinery.

Worked Example — Functional Currency Remeasurement

The temporal method in action
Example: A branch keeping records in a non-functional currency
A US entity's foreign branch keeps its books in local currency (LC), but the branch's functional currency has been determined to be USD (its primary economic environment is USD-driven). The branch holds LC 500,000 cash (current rate: LC 10 = $1) and LC 1,000,000 of equipment acquired when the rate was LC 8 = $1.
Cash (MONETARY item): remeasured at the CURRENT rate = LC 500,000 ÷ 10 = $50,000. Equipment (NONMONETARY item): remeasured at the HISTORICAL rate = LC 1,000,000 ÷ 8 = $125,000 — NOT the current rate, since it's a nonmonetary item carried at historical cost. Any resulting remeasurement gain/loss on monetary items flows THROUGH NET INCOME.
10

Worked Example — Translation With CTA

Consolidating a foreign subsidiary
Example: Translating a Euro-functional subsidiary into USD
A US parent's European subsidiary has EUR-functional net assets. At the start of the year, net assets translated to $10,000,000 (rate: €1 = $1.10). By year end, net assets (adjusted for the year's activity) translate to $11,500,000 using the year-end rate of €1 = $1.15.
The subsidiary's assets/liabilities are translated at the CURRENT (year-end) rate; the resulting TRANSLATION ADJUSTMENT — reflecting the Euro's strengthening against the dollar over the year — is recognised in OCI as part of the CUMULATIVE TRANSLATION ADJUSTMENT, NOT in net income. This CTA balance remains in OCI UNTIL the parent eventually SELLS or SUBSTANTIALLY LIQUIDATES the subsidiary, at which point it is RECLASSIFIED into net income as part of the gain/loss on disposal.
11

Worked Example — the Highly Inflationary Override

Switching from OCI translation to net income remeasurement
Example: A subsidiary's economy becomes highly inflationary
A US parent's subsidiary operates in a country whose cumulative 3-year inflation rate has just crossed the highly inflationary threshold, triggering a change in the subsidiary's designated functional currency.
The subsidiary's functional currency is now DEEMED to be USD (the parent's reporting currency) — going forward, the subsidiary's records are REMEASURED (not translated) using the TEMPORAL METHOD, with remeasurement gains/losses flowing THROUGH NET INCOME rather than OCI. Under IAS 29's contrasting approach, an equivalent IFRS reporter would instead RESTATE the subsidiary's own local-currency financial statements for general price-level changes FIRST, and only then translate the restated amounts at the current exchange rate — a genuinely different sequence and mechanism producing a different measurement path to address the same hyperinflation distortion.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Temporal method
The remeasurement method using current rates for monetary items and historical rates for nonmonetary items, with gains/losses through net income.
Highly inflationary economy
An economy with cumulative three-year inflation approaching or exceeding approximately 100%, triggering a functional currency override under US GAAP.
