Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why lease classification was a substance-over-form exercise
A lease transfers the RIGHT to use an asset, but not always legal ownership. The Conceptual Framework's substance-over-form principle asks whether the SUBSTANCE of a specific lease transfers substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership – if so, the Framework's asset/liability recognition criteria are met, even without a change in legal title.
HOW IAS 17 MAPPED TO THE FRAMEWORK
Substance over form – a finance lease was recognised as an ASSET and LIABILITY of the lessee, because the lessee bore substantially all risks/rewards of ownership, REGARDLESS of legal title remaining with the lessor Faithful representation – treating a finance lease as merely an operating expense (off-balance-sheet) would materially understate a lessee's assets and liabilities The historic "bright-line" weakness – IAS 17's classification test relied heavily on JUDGEMENT-BASED indicators rather than a single quantitative threshold, which critics argued allowed structuring of operating leases specifically to keep them off-balance-sheet
The perceived weakness of IAS 17's operating lease "off-balance-sheet" treatment – allowing genuinely significant long-term obligations to remain undisclosed on the face of the SOFP – was the PRIMARY driver behind IFRS 16's eventual replacement of the entire lessee accounting model.
Measurement principles applied by IAS 17
IAS 17 measured finance leases similarly to a purchase financed by debt, while operating leases were treated as a simple rental expense.
Lease typeMeasurement approach
Finance lease (lessee)Asset and liability recognised at the LOWER of fair value and present value of minimum lease payments
Operating lease (lessee)NO asset or liability recognised; lease payments expensed straight-line over the lease term
ℹ️ Qualitative characteristics in action: the finance/operating BINARY split reflected an attempt to balance faithful representation against cost constraints – but the binary nature of the test (rather than a spectrum) is precisely what IFRS 16 later abandoned for lessees.
2

Scope & Applicability (Historical)

What IAS 17 covered
IAS 17 applied to accounting for all leases, other than specific exclusions (leases to explore for or use minerals/oil/natural gas/similar non-regenerative resources; licensing agreements for films, videos, plays, patents, copyrights; and lease agreements to use land classified as investment property under IAS 40, measured at fair value).
This general scope structure, and most of its exclusions, was carried forward largely unchanged into IFRS 16.
Key definitions you must know
Finance lease
A lease that transfers substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership of an asset – title may or may not eventually be transferred.
Operating lease
A lease OTHER than a finance lease – the lessor retains substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership.
Minimum lease payments
The payments over the lease term the lessee is/can be required to make, excluding contingent rent, costs for services, and taxes to be paid/reimbursed to the lessor.

Finance vs Operating Lease Classification

Classification indicators – judgement based, not bright-line
Whether a lease is a finance or operating lease depended on the SUBSTANCE of the transaction, not merely its legal form. IAS 17 provided INDICATORS, individually or in combination, that would normally lead to a lease being classified as a finance lease.
The lease TRANSFERS OWNERSHIP of the asset to the lessee by the end of the lease term The lessee has the OPTION to purchase the asset at a price expected to be sufficiently lower than fair value at the date the option becomes exercisable, such that it is reasonably certain the option will be exercised (a "bargain purchase option") The lease term is for the MAJOR PART of the economic life of the asset, even if title is not transferred At inception, the present value of minimum lease payments amounts to at least SUBSTANTIALLY ALL of the fair value of the leased asset The leased assets are of such a specialised nature that ONLY the lessee can use them without major modifications
These indicators were deliberately NOT bright-line percentage tests (e.g. no fixed "75% of economic life" or "90% of fair value" threshold was mandated by IAS 17 itself, though such thresholds were common as PRACTICAL rules of thumb in various jurisdictions) – genuine judgement of the overall substance was always required.

Lessee Accounting – Finance Leases (with worked example)

Initial and subsequent measurement
Initial recognition = LOWER of fair value of the leased asset and the present value of minimum lease payments
The asset was then DEPRECIATED consistently with owned assets (IAS 16/38), and the LIABILITY apportioned between finance charge and reduction of the outstanding liability, using the EFFECTIVE INTEREST METHOD (constant periodic rate of return on the remaining balance).
Worked example – finance lease amortisation
Example 1: Finance lease liability reduction
A lessee recognises a finance lease liability of $100,000 at an implicit interest rate of 8%. Annual lease payments of $25,046 are made in arrears.
Year 1 interest = 100,000 × 8% = $8,000
Year 1 principal reduction = 25,046 − 8,000 = $17,046
Closing liability = 100,000 − 17,046 = $82,954
This effective interest allocation mechanic is IDENTICAL to how IFRS 16 now measures ALL lease liabilities (both what were previously finance AND operating leases) for lessees.

Lessee Accounting – Operating Leases (Historical)

Off-balance-sheet treatment – the feature IFRS 16 eliminated
Lease payments under an operating lease were recognised as an EXPENSE on a STRAIGHT-LINE basis over the lease term (unless another systematic basis was more representative of the time pattern of the user's benefit) – NO asset or liability was recognised on the lessee's SOFP.
✕ This complete absence of any SOFP recognition for operating leases is precisely what IFRS 16 abolished – under IFRS 16, virtually ALL leases (with narrow short-term/low-value exemptions) now result in a right-of-use asset and lease liability for the lessee, eliminating the finance/operating distinction entirely from lessee accounting.
6

Lessor Accounting – Finance Leases

A net investment in the lease, replacing the physical asset
A lessor recognised assets held under a finance lease as a RECEIVABLE, at an amount equal to the NET INVESTMENT IN THE LEASE – the leased asset itself was DERECOGNISED from the lessor's own SOFP. Finance income was recognised based on a pattern reflecting a CONSTANT PERIODIC RATE OF RETURN on the lessor's net investment.
This lessor accounting model was carried forward LARGELY UNCHANGED into IFRS 16 – one of the clearest continuities between the old and new standards (Section 11).
7

Lessor Accounting – Operating Leases

Retaining the asset, recognising rental income
Under an operating lease, the lessor CONTINUED to recognise the leased asset on its own SOFP (per IAS 16/IAS 40/IAS 38, depending on the asset type), depreciating it in the normal way. Lease income was recognised on a STRAIGHT-LINE basis over the lease term, unless another systematic basis better represented the pattern of benefit.
This lessor operating lease model was ALSO carried forward essentially unchanged into IFRS 16 – lessor accounting overall changed far less than lessee accounting when the new standard was introduced.
8

Sale and Leaseback Transactions (Historical)

Treatment depended on the resulting leaseback's classification
If a sale and leaseback resulted in a FINANCE lease, any excess of sale proceeds over the carrying amount was NOT immediately recognised as income by the seller-lessee – it was DEFERRED and amortised over the lease term. If the resulting leaseback was an OPERATING lease, and the transaction was established at FAIR VALUE, any gain/loss was recognised IMMEDIATELY (subject to specific rules if the sale price was above or below fair value).
IFRS 16 fundamentally changed this area too – since virtually all leases now create a lessee liability, sale-and-leaseback gain recognition under IFRS 16 depends on the specific proportion of the asset's rights transferred, a materially different (and generally more restrictive) mechanism than IAS 17's deferred-gain approach for finance leasebacks.

Disclosure Requirements (Historical)

Required disclosures
LESSEE DISCLOSURES
For finance leases: net carrying amount of leased assets by class, a reconciliation of minimum lease payments to their present value, and future minimum sublease income For operating leases: total future minimum lease payments under non-cancellable leases, analysed by maturity band
LESSOR DISCLOSURES
For finance leases: a reconciliation of gross investment to the present value of minimum lease payments receivable, and unearned finance income For operating leases: future minimum lease payments receivable, analysed by maturity band, and a general description of leasing arrangements

Critical Judgments & Estimates (Historical)

Where management judgement mattered most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Overall assessment of whether a lease's SUBSTANCE transferred substantially all risks/rewards of ownership Whether an interest rate implicit in the lease could be reliably determined, or the lessee's incremental borrowing rate should be used instead
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Fair value of the leased asset and estimated useful/economic life, feeding into the classification indicators Unguaranteed residual value estimates used by lessors in calculating the net investment in the lease
⚠️ The judgement-heavy, indicator-based classification test was widely criticised for enabling "lease structuring" – deliberately drafting lease terms to fall just short of the finance lease indicators, keeping obligations off-balance-sheet – a key motivation for IFRS 16's wholesale reform.

IAS 17 vs IFRS 16 – What Changed

Comparison – the transition from IAS 17 to IFRS 16
FeatureIAS 17 (superseded)IFRS 16 (current)
Lessee modelDUAL model – finance leases on-balance-sheet, operating leases off-balance-sheetSINGLE model – virtually ALL leases create a right-of-use asset and lease liability (narrow short-term/low-value exemptions only)
Lessee classification testIndicator-based finance vs operating judgement, prone to structuringNo lessee classification needed at all – the dual model is eliminated entirely for lessees
Lessor modelDual model (finance/operating), based on risk/reward transferLARGELY UNCHANGED – lessors still classify leases as finance or operating, using very similar indicators to IAS 17
Sale and leasebackFinance leaseback gains deferred; operating leaseback gains often immediateGain recognition now depends on the proportion of rights transferred, a more restrictive, formula-driven approach
Effective dateWithdrawn for periods beginning on/after 1 January 2019Effective for periods beginning on/after 1 January 2019
The MOST significant change was entirely on the LESSEE side – IAS 17's off-balance-sheet operating lease treatment, long criticised by analysts and regulators for understating corporate leverage, was completely abolished. Lessor accounting, by contrast, changed comparatively little.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Net investment in the lease
The lessor's gross investment in a finance lease, discounted at the interest rate implicit in the lease.
Interest rate implicit in the lease
The discount rate causing the aggregate present value of minimum lease payments and unguaranteed residual value to equal the fair value of the leased asset plus initial direct costs.
Unguaranteed residual value
The portion of the estimated residual value of a leased asset the realisation of which is NOT assured, or is guaranteed only by a party related to the lessor.
Initial direct costs
Incremental costs directly attributable to negotiating and arranging a lease, EXCLUDING such costs incurred by manufacturer/dealer lessors.
Contingent rent
A portion of lease payments not fixed in amount, based instead on a future factor other than the passage of time (e.g. percentage of sales, future price indices) – excluded from minimum lease payments.
Non-cancellable lease
A lease cancellable only upon the occurrence of some remote contingency, with permission of the lessor, if the lessee enters a new lease for the same/equivalent asset, or upon payment of an additional amount such that continuation is reasonably certain at inception.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position (balance sheet) SOPL Statement of profit or loss (income statement) OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
