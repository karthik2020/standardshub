1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Sharing in an affiliate's economic performance, without full consolidation
ASBJ Statement No. 16 governs the EQUITY METHOD (持分法) — a measurement approach applied to investments in ENTITIES over which the investor has SIGNIFICANT INFLUENCE (typically affiliates and certain joint ventures) but NOT control. Rather than consolidating the investee LINE-BY-LINE (as with a subsidiary), the investor recognises its PROPORTIONATE SHARE of the investee's profit or loss (and OTHER equity changes) as an ADJUSTMENT to the CARRYING AMOUNT of a SINGLE investment line.
2

Scope

Investments in affiliates and certain joint ventures within consolidated statements
This Statement applies to investments in AFFILIATES (関連会社) — and certain JOINT VENTURE arrangements — within an entity's CONSOLIDATED financial statements, where the investor holds SIGNIFICANT INFLUENCE but not control.
3

Significant Influence — the Threshold for Equity Method Application

Generally 20%–50% voting rights, or other qualitative indicators
SIGNIFICANT INFLUENCE is generally PRESUMED where the investor holds BETWEEN 20% and 50% of the investee's VOTING rights — though the assessment also considers QUALITATIVE indicators such as BOARD representation, PARTICIPATION in policy-making, MATERIAL transactions between the parties, INTERCHANGE of managerial personnel, or the provision of ESSENTIAL technical information — broadly MIRRORING IAS 28's own significant influence indicators internationally.
4

Mechanics — Adjusting the Investment for the Investor's Share of Profit/Loss

A single-line adjustment, not full line-by-line consolidation
The investment is initially recorded at COST, and SUBSEQUENTLY adjusted EACH period to reflect the investor's PROPORTIONATE SHARE of the investee's profit or loss (INCREASING or DECREASING the investment's carrying amount, with a CORRESPONDING amount recognised in the investor's OWN Statement of Income as "equity in earnings of affiliates," 持分法による投資損益) — as well as its share of OTHER equity changes (e.g. the investee's OWN Valuation and Translation Adjustments movements).
5

Elimination of Unrealised Profits on Transactions With Affiliates

Eliminated to the extent of the investor's ownership interest
Where the investor TRANSACTS with its equity-method affiliate (e.g. selling inventory that REMAINS unsold with the affiliate at the reporting date), any UNREALISED profit arising from that transaction is ELIMINATED, but only to the EXTENT of the investor's OWNERSHIP percentage in the affiliate — reflecting that the investor has NOT genuinely realised profit on the PORTION of the transaction effectively conducted with ITSELF, through its proportionate interest.
6

A DISTINCTIVE FEATURE — Equity Method Applies Only in Consolidated Statements

Individual (non-consolidated) statements use cost, not equity method
A GENUINELY DISTINCTIVE feature of J-GAAP: the EQUITY METHOD is applied ONLY within an entity's CONSOLIDATED financial statements — in the entity's OWN INDIVIDUAL (non-consolidated, parent-only) financial statements, investments in affiliates (like investments in subsidiaries) are HELD AT COST, subject to impairment, under ASBJ Statement No. 10 (see the companion poster), NOT remeasured using the equity method. This CONTRASTS with SOME other jurisdictions applying IFRS, where IAS 27 offers entities the OPTION (though not a requirement) to apply the equity method in SEPARATE financial statements as well — under J-GAAP, this OPTION simply does NOT exist; the equity method is RESERVED exclusively for the consolidated reporting perspective.
7

Worked Example — Recognising the Investor's Share of Affiliate Profit

Increasing the investment carrying amount and recognising equity in earnings
Example: A 30% investment in an affiliate
A company holds a 30% interest in an affiliate (significant influence, no control), initially recorded at ¥900,000,000. The affiliate reports net profit of ¥200,000,000 for the year.
The investor recognises its SHARE of the affiliate's profit: 30% × ¥200,000,000 = ¥60,000,000 — INCREASING the investment's carrying amount to ¥960,000,000 (¥900,000,000 + ¥60,000,000), with a MATCHING ¥60,000,000 recognised as "equity in earnings of affiliates" in the investor's CONSOLIDATED Statement of Income.
8

Worked Example — Eliminating an Unrealised Intercompany Profit

Adjusting for the investor's own proportionate interest
Example: Inventory sold to an affiliate, remaining unsold at year end
A company (30% affiliate ownership) sells goods to the affiliate at a ¥50,000,000 profit margin. At year end, the affiliate has NOT yet resold these goods to outside third parties.
The investor ELIMINATES its PROPORTIONATE share of the unrealised profit: 30% × ¥50,000,000 = ¥15,000,000 — REDUCING the recognised equity-method profit accordingly, since this PORTION of the profit is considered UNREALISED from the CONSOLIDATED group's own economic perspective.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether significant influence genuinely exists, particularly near the 20% voting rights threshold or based on qualitative indicators alone
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Fair value of identifiable assets/liabilities at acquisition, where the investment includes an implicit goodwill-like premium

Quick-Reference Glossary

Equity method (持分法)
A measurement approach adjusting an investment's carrying amount for the investor's proportionate share of the investee's profit/loss and other equity changes.
Significant influence
The power to participate in an investee's financial and operating policy decisions without full control, generally presumed at 20%–50% voting rights.
