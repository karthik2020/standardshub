Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why a SINGLE control model was needed
The Conceptual Framework defines a reporting entity's boundary by what it genuinely CONTROLS. Before IFRS 10, control assessments differed across standards and were often based purely on VOTING RIGHTS percentages — allowing entities to structure arrangements (e.g. "structured entities" with minimal voting rights but genuine economic control) to avoid consolidation. IFRS 10 introduced a SINGLE, substance-based control model applicable to ALL types of investees.
HOW IFRS 10 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Substance over form — control is assessed based on POWER and RETURNS, not merely legal voting percentages, capturing arrangements that gave genuine control WITHOUT majority voting rights Faithful representation — consolidating only what is GENUINELY controlled ensures the group accounts represent the true economic entity, avoiding both under- and over-consolidation Comparability — a SINGLE control model across all investee types (subsidiaries, structured entities, etc.) replaced multiple different consolidation triggers previously scattered across IAS 27 and SIC interpretations
IFRS 10 was issued DIRECTLY in response to the 2008 financial crisis, where numerous "off-balance-sheet" special purpose/structured entities were revealed to be effectively controlled by their sponsors despite minimal formal voting rights — a major consolidation loophole IFRS 10 was designed to close.
The three-element control test, at a glance
An investor controls an investee when it is exposed to variable returns AND has the ability to affect those returns through power.
Control = Power over the investee + Exposure (rights) to variable returns + Ability to USE power to affect the amount of returns
ℹ️ ALL THREE elements must be present SIMULTANEOUSLY — missing any one means control (and hence consolidation) does NOT exist.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRS 10 covers
IFRS 10 establishes principles for the presentation and preparation of consolidated financial statements when an entity controls one or more OTHER entities. A PARENT (an entity controlling one or more subsidiaries) presents consolidated financial statements, with LIMITED exceptions (e.g. certain intermediate parents meeting specific conditions, or investment entities per Section 7).
IFRS 10's control model applies UNIVERSALLY — to traditional operating subsidiaries AND to "structured entities" (special purpose vehicles) where voting rights are not the dominant factor.
Key definitions you must know
Control of an investee
An investor controls an investee when it is exposed, or has rights, to variable returns from its involvement, and has the ability to affect those returns through power over the investee.
Power
Existing rights that give the current ability to direct the RELEVANT ACTIVITIES — the activities that significantly affect the investee's returns.
Structured entity
An entity designed so that voting/similar rights are NOT the dominant factor in deciding who controls it (e.g. voting rights relate only to administrative tasks, with relevant activities directed by contractual arrangements).
3

The Control Model — Three Elements Working Together

Reassessment is continuous, not one-off
An investor REASSESSES whether it controls an investee if facts and circumstances indicate CHANGES to one or more of the three control elements — control is NOT a static, one-time assessment made only at initial investment.
Power — over the RELEVANT ACTIVITIES (Section 4) Returns — exposure or rights to VARIABLE returns from involvement (Section 5) Linkage — the ability to USE power to AFFECT the amount of those returns (Section 6)
This continuous reassessment requirement means a change in an investee's governance structure, contractual arrangements, or even market conditions can trigger a NEW control conclusion mid-relationship, without any new investment being made.
4

Power Over the Investee (with worked example)

Beyond simple majority voting
Power arises from RIGHTS — which can include voting rights, rights to appoint/remove key management, rights to direct transactions for the investor's benefit, or OTHER contractual arrangements. POTENTIAL voting rights (e.g. convertible instruments, options) are considered ONLY if SUBSTANTIVE — the holder must have the PRACTICAL ABILITY to exercise them.
Worked example — de facto control without majority voting
Example 1: Control with less than 50% voting rights
An investor holds 45% of an investee's voting rights. The remaining 55% is held by thousands of small, widely dispersed shareholders, none individually holding more than 1%, and historical attendance at shareholder meetings has never exceeded 30% of total voting rights.
Despite holding LESS than 50%, the investor's 45% stake is likely SUFFICIENT in practice to direct the relevant activities, given the extreme DISPERSION of the remaining shares and historically low voting turnout — this is a classic "DE FACTO CONTROL" scenario requiring consolidation, even without a legal majority.
5

Exposure to Variable Returns

"Returns" is deliberately broad
Returns can be POSITIVE ONLY, NEGATIVE ONLY, or BOTH — and can include dividends, remuneration for servicing an investee's assets/liabilities, fees, tax benefits, residual interests on liquidation, cost savings, and other benefits NOT available to other interest holders. The key requirement is that returns VARY based on the investee's PERFORMANCE, not that they are guaranteed or fixed.
Even a SERVICE PROVIDER earning purely fixed, market-rate fees for managing an investee (with no additional variable return) generally does NOT have the requisite "exposure to variable returns" — a critical distinction for asset managers and similar fee-based arrangements (see Section 6's decision maker analysis).
6

Linkage Between Power and Returns (with worked example)

Principal vs agent — the decision maker analysis
Where an investor holds decision-making rights, it must determine whether it acts as a PRINCIPAL (exercising power for its OWN benefit — controls the investee) or as an AGENT (exercising power on BEHALF of others — does NOT control the investee), considering the scope of its decision-making authority, rights held by other parties, its remuneration, and its exposure to variability of returns from OTHER interests it holds.
Example 2: Fund manager — agent, not principal
An asset management company manages an investment fund, earning a market-based fixed management fee (1% of assets under management) plus a modest performance fee. The fund's other investors hold substantive KICK-OUT RIGHTS, able to remove the manager without cause via a simple majority vote. The manager holds no direct equity interest in the fund.
The manager is acting as an AGENT, not a principal — its remuneration is at market rates commensurate with services provided, its exposure to variable returns is limited, and OTHER investors hold substantive removal rights. The manager does NOT control, and therefore does NOT consolidate, the fund.

Investment Entities Exception

Fair value through profit or loss, instead of consolidation
An entity qualifying as an "INVESTMENT ENTITY" (obtains funds from investors to provide investment management services, commits to an exit strategy, and measures/evaluates investment performance on a fair value basis) does NOT consolidate its subsidiaries — instead, it measures them at FAIR VALUE THROUGH PROFIT OR LOSS, per IFRS 9. This is a DELIBERATE, narrow exception to IFRS 10's general consolidation requirement, reflecting the Framework's relevance principle: for a genuine investment fund, fair value information is MORE relevant to users than a full line-by-line consolidation of underlying portfolio companies.
8

Loss of Control

Derecognition and remeasurement at fair value
A parent LOSES control when it no longer meets the three control elements (e.g. selling shares, dilution through a new share issue, or losing contractual rights). On loss of control, the parent DERECOGNISES the subsidiary's assets/liabilities and any NCI, RECOGNISES the fair value of consideration received, and recognises any RETAINED interest at its FAIR VALUE at the date control is lost — with the resulting gain/loss recognised in profit or loss.
The mandatory fair value remeasurement of any RETAINED interest is a genuinely significant recognition event — the retained stake is treated as if NEWLY acquired at fair value on the loss-of-control date, not simply continuing at its previous consolidated carrying amount.
9

Consolidation Procedures

Uniform accounting policies and full line-by-line combination
Combine LIKE items of assets, liabilities, equity, income, expenses, and cash flows of the parent with those of its subsidiaries, line by line OFFSET the parent's investment in each subsidiary against the parent's portion of equity in each subsidiary (established under IFRS 3 at acquisition) ELIMINATE in full intragroup assets/liabilities, equity, income, expenses, and cash flows relating to transactions BETWEEN entities in the group Ensure the group applies UNIFORM accounting policies for like transactions, adjusting subsidiary financial statements where necessary

INTERESTS IN SUBSIDIARIES

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether de facto control exists absent legal majority voting rights Principal vs agent classification for fund managers and similar decision makers Whether an entity qualifies for the investment entities exception
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Fair value of a retained interest on loss of control Fair value of consideration received or receivable on disposal of a subsidiary
⚠️ Structured entities and complex financing arrangements often require EXTENSIVE judgement — the control assessment can hinge on subtle contractual details rather than headline ownership percentages.

IFRS vs US GAAP

Comparison — IFRS 10 vs US GAAP
FeatureIFRS (IFRS 10)US GAAP
Consolidation modelSINGLE control model applied to all investee typesTWO separate models: the VOTING INTEREST model AND a SEPARATE "variable interest entity" (VIE) model for entities lacking sufficient equity or where equity holders lack typical control characteristics
Structured entitiesAssessed under the SAME single control model as any other investeeAssessed under the SEPARATE, more complex VIE consolidation model (ASC 810)
Investment entitiesFair value exception exists, similar underlying rationaleSimilar investment company accounting exists under ASC 946, broadly converged in spirit
The MOST significant difference is architectural — IFRS 10 applies ONE unified control model universally, while US GAAP maintains TWO SEPARATE consolidation models (voting interest and VIE), a source of persistent complexity and divergence between the two frameworks.

Quick-reference glossary
De facto control
Control existing despite holding less than 50% of voting rights, due to the extreme dispersion of remaining shareholders and historical voting patterns.
Kick-out rights
Substantive rights held by other parties to remove a decision maker without cause — a key factor in the principal/agent assessment.
Relevant activities
Activities of the investee that significantly affect the investee's returns — the activities over which "power" must exist for control to be established.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOPL Statement of profit or loss OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
