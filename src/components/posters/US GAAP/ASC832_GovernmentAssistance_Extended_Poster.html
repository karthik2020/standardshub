ASC 832 – Government Assistance
Extended reference poster · US GAAP · Established by ASU 2021-10 · A DISCLOSURE-ONLY standard – no recognition or measurement guidance provided · USD examples
THE DEFINING FEATURE: ASC 832 is a disclosure standard only. It does NOT prescribe how a business entity should recognize or measure government assistance in its financial statements – that determination is left entirely to existing GAAP (often applied by analogy, since no specific US GAAP recognition model exists for government assistance to business entities). This stands in sharp contrast to IAS 20 / ASBE 16, which are full recognition-and-measurement standards.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Transparency about government assistance, without a prescribed accounting model
ASC 832 REQUIRES business ENTITIES to DISCLOSE information ABOUT certain TYPES of government ASSISTANCE they RECEIVE – but, GENUINELY unlike MOST other ASC topics, it DOES NOT establish ANY recognition, MEASUREMENT, or PRESENTATION requirements FOR that assistance. THE standard EXISTS purely to IMPROVE transparency, LEAVING the UNDERLYING accounting TREATMENT to WHATEVER other GAAP guidance an ENTITY reasonably APPLIES.
2

Scope – Business Entities, Not Not-for-Profits

A gap that specifically targeted for-profit entities
ASC 832 applies to BUSINESS entities that ACCOUNT for a TRANSACTION with a GOVERNMENT by APPLYING a GRANT or CONTRIBUTION accounting MODEL by ANALOGY (e.g. ANALOGIZING to IAS 20 internationally, or to the CONTRIBUTIONS guidance in ASC 958-605) – REFLECTING that, BEFORE this Standard, US GAAP had NO SPECIFIC authoritative GUIDANCE for BUSINESS entities receiving GOVERNMENT assistance (NOT-for-profit entities ALREADY had DEDICATED contribution ACCOUNTING guidance under ASC 958).
3

What Falls Outside the Scope

Transactions already governed by other specific GAAP
TRANSACTIONS ALREADY within the SCOPE of OTHER specific TOPICS – SUCH as INCOME tax ACCOUNTING (ASC 740) or REVENUE from CONTRACTS with GOVERNMENT customers (ASC 606) – REMAIN governed by THOSE topics, and are OUTSIDE ASC 832'S scope.
4

No Recognition or Measurement Guidance – By Design

A deliberate, narrow scope limitation
THE FASB DELIBERATELY chose to LIMIT ASC 832 to DISCLOSURE ONLY – NOT attempting to PRESCRIBE a SINGLE recognition or MEASUREMENT model for GOVERNMENT assistance. THIS was a NARROWER, faster-to-ISSUE response, RATHER than UNDERTAKING the FAR more SUBSTANTIAL project of DEVELOPING a COMPREHENSIVE recognition MODEL (SUCH as IAS 20's OWN deferred-income APPROACH, or ASBE 16'S equivalent) FROM scratch.
5

How Entities Fill the Gap in Practice

Diversity in practice remains, even after ASC 832
IN the ABSENCE of DEDICATED recognition GUIDANCE, entities COMMONLY analogize TO one of SEVERAL existing MODELS – MOST frequently IAS 20 (RECOGNIZING assistance as DEFERRED income, MATCHED to related COSTS or amortized OVER an asset's USEFUL life), or the NOT-for-profit CONTRIBUTIONS guidance in ASC 958-605. BECAUSE ASC 832 does NOT mandate ANY single approach, GENUINE diversity in PRACTICE remains EVEN after its ADOPTION – the STANDARD improves TRANSPARENCY about WHICH policy an ENTITY has chosen, WITHOUT standardizing the CHOICE itself.

7

Why Disclosure-Only Guidance Was Chosen

Responding to a specific, urgent transparency gap
THE PROJECT that BECAME ASC 832 GAINED urgency FOLLOWING large-SCALE government ASSISTANCE programs (SUCH as PANDEMIC-related RELIEF measures), WHICH highlighted a GENUINE gap in TRANSPARENCY about HOW businesses were ACCOUNTING for, and REPORTING, government ASSISTANCE received. THE FASB PRIORITIZED closing this DISCLOSURE gap QUICKLY, RATHER than WAITING for a FULL recognition-and-MEASUREMENT project to CONCLUDE.
8

Effective Date and Early Application

A single effective date for all entities
ASC 832 is EFFECTIVE for FISCAL years BEGINNING after DECEMBER 15, 2021, for ALL entities (WITH NO distinction BETWEEN public and PRIVATE companies, UNLIKE the STAGGERED effective DATES common ELSEWHERE in US GAAP) – EARLY application IS permitted.
9

Worked Example – Disclosing a Payroll Tax Credit Program

Disclosure without a prescribed recognition model
Example: A refundable payroll tax credit
A company receives $1,200,000 through a government payroll tax credit program during the year, recognized as a reduction of payroll tax expense within operating expenses.
The company DISCLOSES the NATURE of the PROGRAM, the FACT that it RECOGNIZED the $1,200,000 as a REDUCTION of PAYROLL tax EXPENSE, the SIGNIFICANT terms and CONDITIONS of the PROGRAM (e.g. ELIGIBILITY requirements), and ANY commitments or CONTINGENCIES – but ASC 832 ITSELF does NOT dictate WHETHER this $1,200,000 SHOULD have been RECOGNIZED as a REDUCTION of expense, OTHER income, or ANOTHER treatment.
10

Worked Example – Disclosing a Cash Grant Analogized to IAS 20

A company's own chosen accounting policy, made transparent
Example: A capital investment grant
A manufacturer receives a $5,000,000 government cash grant tied to the construction of a new production facility, which the company elects to account for by analogy to IAS 20 – recognizing the grant as deferred income, amortized over the facility's 20-year useful life.
The company DISCLOSES that it APPLIED an IAS 20-BASED accounting POLICY by ANALOGY (SINCE no SPECIFIC US GAAP recognition MODEL exists), the $5,000,000 amount, the DEFERRED income LINE item on the BALANCE Sheet, and the $250,000 ANNUAL amortization ($5,000,000 ÷ 20 YEARS) recognized WITHIN the INCOME Statement – WITH ASC 832 REQUIRING this TRANSPARENCY, but NOT itself REQUIRING the IAS 20 ANALOGY specifically.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Government assistance
Assistance provided by a government to a business entity, including cash grants, tax credits, and other forms of support, subject only to ASC 832's disclosure requirements.
Disclosure-only standard
A standard requiring transparency about a topic without prescribing its recognition, measurement, or presentation treatment.
