Conceptual Framework Linkage

Convergence, not adoption — why Ind AS exists as a separate standard
India did NOT adopt IFRS directly — it issued Ind AS, a SEPARATE but CONVERGED set of standards under Indian company law, numbered to MIRROR the corresponding IFRS/IAS (Ind AS 101 ≈ IFRS 1, Ind AS 16 ≈ IAS 16, etc.). This "converged, not adopted" approach let India retain SPECIFIC carve-outs addressing local legal, tax, and regulatory realities (e.g. the Companies Act 2013, Indian tax law) while remaining broadly comparable to IFRS globally.
HOW IND AS 101 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Comparability, tempered by local context — Ind AS 101 pursues the SAME comparability objective as IFRS 1, but permits carve-outs where full IFRS convergence would conflict with Indian company law or create disproportionate transition costs for Indian first-time adopters Faithful representation within a national legal framework — several Ind AS 101 carve-outs (e.g. specific treatment of exchange differences) reflect genuine differences in India's regulatory/tax environment, not weaker principles A transitional standard, just like IFRS 1 — the underlying PURPOSE (a one-time bridge from previous Indian GAAP to Ind AS) is identical to IFRS 1's purpose for global IFRS transitions
Ind AS became MANDATORY for specified classes of Indian companies in a PHASED manner starting FY 2016-17, with applicability based on net worth/listing status thresholds — Ind AS 101 governed EACH company's individual transition to the new regime.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 101 covers
Ind AS 101 applies to an entity's FIRST Ind AS financial statements, and interim financial reports (if any) for part of the period covered by those first Ind AS financial statements — mirroring IFRS 1's own scope, but referencing "previous Indian GAAP" (typically the Companies (Accounting Standards) Rules, 2006) as the transition starting point.
"Previous GAAP" for MOST Indian first-time adopters specifically means the earlier Indian Accounting Standards (AS) notified under the Companies Act, NOT a foreign national GAAP — a meaningful practical distinction from a typical global IFRS 1 transition.
Key definitions you must know
Date of transition to Ind AS
The beginning of the earliest period for which an entity presents full comparative information under Ind AS.
Previous GAAP (Indian context)
Typically the Companies (Accounting Standards) Rules, 2006 (the pre-Ind AS Indian GAAP) applied immediately before Ind AS adoption.
MCA (Ministry of Corporate Affairs)
The Indian government ministry responsible for notifying Ind AS under the Companies Act, 2013 — the national standard-setting authority equivalent to the IASB's global role.
3

The Opening Ind AS Balance Sheet

The four-step core requirement — identical to IFRS 1
An entity's opening Ind AS Balance Sheet is prepared at the date of transition. The entity shall: RECOGNISE all assets/liabilities required by Ind AS; NOT recognise items Ind AS does not permit; RECLASSIFY items to their correct Ind AS category; and MEASURE all recognised assets/liabilities per applicable Ind AS.
⚠️ Resulting adjustments are recognised DIRECTLY IN RETAINED EARNINGS (or another appropriate equity category) at the transition date — identical treatment to IFRS 1.
4

General Principle — Retrospective Application

The default rule — identical to IFRS 1
Except as specifically provided by the optional exemptions/exceptions, an entity applies the SAME accounting policies throughout all periods presented, using the LATEST version of each Ind AS effective at the reporting date — mirroring IFRS 1's default rule exactly.
5

Optional Exemptions — Business Combinations (with worked example)

The same core exemption as IFRS 1
A first-time adopter MAY elect NOT to apply Ind AS 103 retrospectively to past business combinations before the transition date — IDENTICAL in structure to IFRS 1's own business combinations exemption, requiring consistent treatment across ALL pre-transition acquisitions if elected.
Example 1: Electing the business combinations exemption
An Indian company acquired a subsidiary in 2012 under previous Indian GAAP, recognising goodwill of ₹8 crore using pooling-of-interests-adjacent methods then permitted. On Ind AS transition (1 April 2016), the company elects the business combinations exemption.
The ₹8 crore goodwill carrying amount is carried FORWARD into the opening Ind AS Balance Sheet largely UNCHANGED (subject to specific required adjustments, e.g. aligning recognition criteria), avoiding a costly full retrospective restatement of the 2012 acquisition under Ind AS 103's acquisition method.
6

Optional Exemptions — Deemed Cost

Fair value or previous GAAP revaluation as deemed cost — same as IFRS 1
A first-time adopter MAY elect to measure PPE, investment property, or intangible assets at fair value at the transition date, using that fair value as DEEMED COST — identical to IFRS 1. India's MCA ALSO specifically permits using the PREVIOUS GAAP CARRYING AMOUNT itself (under Indian AS, often based on historical cost) as deemed cost, WITHOUT requiring a fresh valuation exercise — a genuinely practical INDIAN-SPECIFIC relief given the scale and cost of valuing PPE across India's large first-time-adopter population (Section 7 covers this in more detail).
7

India-Specific Exemptions (with worked example)

Carve-ins unique to Ind AS 101
Deemed cost using previous GAAP carrying value — Ind AS 101 EXPLICITLY permits using the previous Indian GAAP net block (carrying value) of PPE, intangible assets, and investment property AS deemed cost on transition, WITHOUT needing a fair value exercise — a relief NOT present in this exact form under IFRS 1 Long-term foreign currency monetary items — a (now largely time-expired, but historically significant) option allowing exchange differences on long-term foreign currency loans used to acquire depreciable assets to be CAPITALISED into the asset's cost, rather than expensed through profit or loss as IAS 21/Ind AS 21 would otherwise require Cumulative translation differences — SAME as IFRS 1, deemed zero at transition if elected
Example 2: Using previous GAAP carrying value as deemed cost
An Indian manufacturing company's factory building has a previous Indian GAAP net book value of ₹15 crore at the transition date (1 April 2016), based on historical cost less accumulated depreciation. No independent fair valuation is obtained.
The company elects to use the ₹15 crore previous GAAP carrying value DIRECTLY as deemed cost — avoiding the cost and effort of an independent valuation exercise, a PRACTICAL relief that proved genuinely important given the SCALE of India's first-time Ind AS transition across thousands of companies simultaneously.
8

Mandatory Exceptions

Broadly identical to IFRS 1
Estimates, derecognition of financial assets/liabilities, hedge accounting, non-controlling interests, and classification of financial assets are all subject to the SAME "no hindsight" mandatory exceptions as IFRS 1 — this is one of the MOST closely aligned areas between the two standards.
9

Disclosure & Reconciliations

Required disclosures — identical structure to IFRS 1
Reconciliation of EQUITY reported under previous Indian GAAP to Ind AS, at both the transition date and end of the last previous-GAAP period Reconciliation of TOTAL COMPREHENSIVE INCOME reported under previous Indian GAAP to Ind AS Explanation of material adjustments to the previous GAAP cash flow statement, if presented
