Conceptual Framework Linkage

Identical classification and ECL philosophy to IFRS 9
Ind AS 109 applies the IDENTICAL Conceptual Framework logic as IFRS 9 — classification via business model + SPPI cash flow tests, and FORWARD-LOOKING expected credit loss provisioning replacing the old incurred loss model. India's convergence carries this forward WITHOUT substantive modification to the core accounting model.
HOW IND AS 109 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Faithful representation — classification reflects genuine business purpose and cash flow characteristics — IDENTICAL to IFRS 9 Timeliness/relevance — the ECL model recognises credit deterioration earlier than an incurred loss approach — IDENTICAL to IFRS 9 A genuinely DUAL credit provisioning landscape for Indian banks — RBI has historically prescribed its OWN specific asset classification and provisioning norms (NPA-based) for banks, and has been separately working toward its own ECL-aligned "Expected Credit Loss" framework FOR BANKS specifically, creating a genuinely distinctive Indian regulatory transition dynamic that most other jurisdictions applying IFRS 9 do not face in quite the same way
Ind AS 109 itself is applied by Indian NBFCs (Non-Banking Financial Companies) directly, generally WITHOUT the same RBI provisioning overlay complexity full commercial banks face — banks themselves have had a MORE GRADUAL, phased Ind AS 109 implementation given RBI's own parallel regulatory considerations.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 109 covers
Ind AS 109 applies to ALL financial assets/liabilities — IDENTICAL scope to IFRS 9, with the SAME exclusions (subsidiary/associate/JV interests, employee benefit plans, most insurance contracts, share-based payment transactions).
Ind AS 109 is genuinely central to accounting for Indian NBFCs, given their core lending business — arguably one of the MOST consequential Ind AS standards for this sector.
Key definitions you must know
Business model / SPPI
IDENTICAL definitions and tests to IFRS 9.
NBFC
Non-Banking Financial Company — an RBI-regulated Indian lending/financial services entity, subject to Ind AS 109 for its financial statements, alongside RBI's own prudential norms (though generally less extensively overlaid than full banks).

Classification — Business Model & SPPI Tests

Identical two-part test to IFRS 9
The business model test (hold to collect, hold to collect and sell, other) and the SPPI (solely payments of principal and interest) cash flow characteristics test are IDENTICAL to IFRS 9, applied by Indian entities without modification.

Expected Credit Losses — The Three-Stage Model (with worked example)

Identical staging model to IFRS 9
Example 1: An Indian NBFC's ECL staging
An Indian NBFC's vehicle loan of ₹8,00,000 has a 12-month ECL of ₹15,000 at initial recognition (Stage 1). By the next reporting date, the borrower has missed two consecutive EMI payments, indicating a significant increase in credit risk (Stage 2), with lifetime ECL estimated at ₹85,000.
The loan moves from Stage 1 to Stage 2 — loss allowance increases from ₹15,000 to ₹85,000, an incremental charge of ₹70,000 recognised immediately in profit or loss — an IDENTICAL mechanism to IFRS 9's own three-stage model globally.

RBI's Parallel Provisioning Framework for Banks/NBFCs (with worked example)

A genuinely distinctive dual system, in transition
RBI has historically mandated its OWN asset classification (Standard, Sub-standard, Doubtful, Loss — the "NPA" categories) and PROVISIONING norms for banks, based on DAYS-PAST-DUE thresholds, largely INDEPENDENT of Ind AS 109's forward-looking ECL model. RBI has SEPARATELY been working on its own "Expected Credit Loss" (ECL)-based provisioning framework specifically for banks, intended to align more closely with the Ind AS 109 philosophy — but as of this poster's preparation, this convergence remains an ACTIVE, ongoing regulatory transition, not yet fully complete for all bank categories.
Example 2: Two parallel provisioning calculations for the same loan
A commercial bank's corporate loan of ₹50,00,000 is 95 days past due. Under RBI's existing NPA norms (90+ days past due threshold), it is classified as "Sub-standard," requiring a specific minimum provisioning percentage. SEPARATELY, under Ind AS 109's ECL model, the bank calculates its own forward-looking lifetime ECL based on its internal risk models.
The bank must calculate BOTH figures — RBI's regulatory provisioning requirement (for prudential/regulatory capital purposes) AND its Ind AS 109 ECL provision (for its Ind AS-compliant financial statements) — these TWO figures are NOT always identical, requiring careful reconciliation, a genuinely DISTINCTIVE feature of the Indian banking sector's dual reporting obligations that most other IFRS 9 jurisdictions do not face in quite the same acute form.
⚠️ This DUAL provisioning requirement (RBI NPA norms + Ind AS 109 ECL) represents one of the MOST significant, practically consequential Indian-specific complexities in the entire Ind AS/IFRS comparison — an active area of regulatory convergence still evolving.

Financial Liabilities Classification

Identical to IFRS 9
Most liabilities at amortised cost; own credit risk changes on fair-value-option liabilities presented in OCI unless creating an accounting mismatch — IDENTICAL to IFRS 9's own treatment.
7

Derecognition

Identical to IFRS 9
The risks/rewards and control-based derecognition test is IDENTICAL to IFRS 9 — genuinely relevant for Indian NBFCs engaging in loan securitisation/assignment transactions, a common Indian financing structure.
8

Hedge Accounting

Identical principles-based model to IFRS 9
The economic relationship test, replacing the old 80-125% bright line, is IDENTICAL to IFRS 9 — relevant for Indian companies hedging foreign currency exposure (given significant import/export activity) and commodity price risk.

Disclosure Requirements

Identical to IFRS 9, supplemented by Ind AS 107
Classification, ECL staging movements, hedge accounting disclosures — governed alongside Ind AS 107's own detailed disclosure requirements, IDENTICAL in structure to the IFRS 9/IFRS 7 combination globally.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Business model determination — identical to IFRS 9 Reconciling RBI's regulatory provisioning approach with Ind AS 109's ECL model, for banks specifically
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Probability-weighted ECL calculations, incorporating Indian macroeconomic scenarios (GDP growth, inflation, monsoon-dependent agricultural cycles for rural lending portfolios)
⚠️ Indian NBFCs with significant RURAL or agricultural lending portfolios face genuinely DISTINCTIVE ECL modelling challenges given India's monsoon-dependent economic cycles affecting borrower repayment capacity — a practical consideration less prominent in many other IFRS 9 jurisdictions.
