SIC-15 – Operating Leases–Incentives
Historical reference note · Issued 1998 · Withdrawn 2019
⚠ WITHDRAWN – Superseded by IFRS 16 Leases, effective for periods beginning 1 January 2019
What SIC-15 addressed
SIC-15 addressed how both a LESSEE and a LESSOR should account for INCENTIVES in the negotiation of a new or renewed OPERATING lease – for example, rent-free periods, up-front cash payments to the lessee, contributions to the lessee's relocation or fit-out costs, or the lessor assuming a lessee's pre-existing lease obligations on another property.
The conclusion it reached
Lease incentives, whatever their nature or form, and REGARDLESS of the timing of the associated cash payments, should be recognised as an INTEGRAL PART of the net consideration agreed for the use of the leased asset – spread on a STRAIGHT-LINE basis (or another systematic basis, if more representative of the time pattern of the user's benefit) over the LEASE TERM, rather than recognised entirely at the point the incentive was received or paid.
Where this guidance lives today
Superseded byIFRS 16 Leases (2016)
Why withdrawnIFRS 16 replaced IAS 17 and abolished the operating/finance lease distinction for LESSEES entirely (via the single right-of-use asset model); lease incentives are now factored directly into the initial measurement of the right-of-use asset and lease liability
Current statusFor lessees, incentives now reduce the initial measurement of the right-of-use asset under IFRS 16; for lessors (who retain an operating/finance distinction), the underlying SIC-15 spreading logic broadly persists within IFRS 16's lessor accounting requirements
