IFRIC 3 – Emission Rights (WITHDRAWN)
Extended reference poster · Withdrawn 2005 · No direct successor interpretation – an enduring IFRS gap area · Retained for historical/comparative reference
🚫 WITHDRAWN INTERPRETATION – NO DIRECT REPLACEMENT – IFRIC 3 was withdrawn by the IASB only months after issuance, following widespread criticism that its required mixed-measurement approach (cost-based liability, fair-value-based asset/grant) created ARTIFICIAL profit/loss volatility for participants in cap-and-trade emission schemes. Unlike most withdrawn interpretations, NO direct successor was issued – emission rights accounting REMAINS an area of diversity in practice under IFRS today. This poster is retained for historical/comparative reference.

Conceptual Framework Linkage (Historical)

A mixed-measurement approach that proved unworkable
IFRIC 3 required emission allowances to be recognised as INTANGIBLE ASSETS (under IAS 38, at cost or fair value depending on how acquired), while the OBLIGATION to deliver allowances for emissions made was recognised as a PROVISION (under IAS 37, at the market price of allowances needed to settle it) – mixing TWO different measurement bases for what was economically a single, connected scheme.
WHY THIS CREATED A CONCEPTUAL PROBLEM
Measurement mismatch – allowances received for FREE from government were recognised at fair value with an OFFSETTING deferred income grant, while the emissions LIABILITY was measured at CURRENT market price – creating volatility even when a participant was perfectly "matched" (emissions = allowances held)No genuine faithful representation – the mismatch did not reflect any real economic gain/loss, undermining IFRIC 3's own claim to improve financial reporting relevance
This mismatch was the CENTRAL reason for withdrawal – a rare case of an interpretation being pulled specifically because its OWN internal logic produced counter-intuitive, criticised results.

Scope & Applicability (Historical)

What IFRIC 3 covered
IFRIC 3 addressed accounting for CAP-AND-TRADE emission rights schemes, where a government grants (or auctions) tradable allowances entitling the holder to emit up to a specified quantity, with participants required to deliver allowances matching actual emissions.
Relevant to entities in jurisdictions with mandatory emissions trading schemes (e.g. the EU Emissions Trading Scheme).
Key definitions (historical)
Cap-and-trade scheme
A regulatory mechanism capping total emissions, allocating/auctioning tradable allowances to participants.
Emission allowance
A tradable right to emit a specified quantity of a pollutant, historically treated as an intangible asset under IFRIC 3.
3

The Required Accounting Model (Historical)

A theoretically consistent but practically criticised approach
Each element (asset, grant, liability) was measured under the "correct" existing IFRS standard applicable to ITS nature – theoretically sound in isolation, but producing an ARTIFICIAL mismatch when combined, since the standards involved (IAS 38, IAS 20, IAS 37) were never designed to interact as a single coherent scheme.
4

Emission Allowances as Intangible Assets (Historical, with worked example)

Historical measurement illustration
Example 1: The volatility problem illustrated
A company received 1,000 emission allowances FREE from its government at the start of the year, when the market price was €20/allowance. By year-end, the company had emitted exactly 1,000 units (fully matched to its free allocation), but the market price of allowances had risen to €35.
Under IFRIC 3: the intangible asset was recognised at €20,000 (fair value at grant), with a MATCHING €20,000 deferred income liability. The EMISSIONS PROVISION, however, was measured at the year-end market price: 1,000 × €35 = €35,000. This created a €15,000 LOSS in P&L – despite the company being PERFECTLY matched (no shortfall or surplus of allowances) – the exact counter-intuitive result that led to IFRIC 3's withdrawal.
5

The Liability for Emissions Made (Historical)

Provision measured at current market price
The obligation to deliver allowances for ACTUAL emissions made during the period was measured as a PROVISION under IAS 37, at the market price of allowances needed for settlement – the source of the mismatch illustrated in Example 1.
6

Government-Granted Allowances (Historical)

Fair value recognition with a deferred income offset
Allowances granted BELOW fair value (including for nil consideration) were recognised at FAIR VALUE, with the difference between consideration paid and fair value treated as a government grant under IAS 20, released to income over the compliance period.
7

Why It Was Withdrawn – The Volatility Problem

A genuine standard-setting failure, honestly acknowledged
Constituents (particularly companies subject to the EU Emissions Trading Scheme) strongly criticised IFRIC 3 for producing ECONOMICALLY MEANINGLESS P&L volatility, as illustrated above. The IASB WITHDREW IFRIC 3 in June 2005 – just months after its March 2005 issuance – acknowledging the measurement mismatch problem, but did NOT issue a replacement interpretation, leaving emission rights accounting an area the IASB has periodically revisited (e.g. through the now-discontinued Emissions Trading Schemes research project) without a comprehensive standard ever being finalised.
8

Current Practice Diversity

An enduring gap, addressed differently across jurisdictions
In the ABSENCE of specific IFRS guidance, entities today apply general principles (IAS 38, IAS 20, IAS 37, or in some cases IAS 2) by ANALOGY, resulting in DIVERSITY in practice globally. Entities generating or trading emission/carbon credits must therefore develop and consistently apply an accounting policy by reference to the Conceptual Framework and analogous standards, disclosing the basis for their approach given the absence of a dedicated standard.
