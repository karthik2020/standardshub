Conceptual Framework Linkage

An association that manages, but doesn't economically "own," common property for itself
A Common Interest Realty Association (CIRA) – a homeowners association, condominium association, or housing cooperative – exists to MANAGE common property on behalf of its MEMBER owners, who collectively hold the real economic interest. This raises a genuinely UNUSUAL question: should the association itself CAPITALISE common property (roads, clubhouses, shared infrastructure) as ITS OWN asset, given it doesn't hold that property for its OWN economic benefit in the way a typical entity would? ASC 972 addresses this through a distinctive, EXPLICIT accounting POLICY choice (Section 3).
2

Scope

Homeowners associations, condominium associations, and housing cooperatives
ASC 972 applies to CIRAs – associations of property owners formed to manage and maintain COMMON property and enforce community rules, funded through member ASSESSMENTS.
3

Common Property – the Capitalization Policy Choice

A genuinely unusual, explicit either/or accounting policy election
ASC 972 EXPLICITLY PERMITS a CIRA to EITHER capitalise common property (roads, recreational facilities, shared infrastructure) as its OWN asset (depreciated like normal PP&E), OR to NOT capitalise it at all – reflecting the reasoning that, since the association typically CANNOT sell or otherwise economically benefit from the common property independently of its MEMBERS' collective ownership interest, capitalisation may not always provide meaningful information. This is a genuinely UNUSUAL feature – DIVERGENT industry practice exists, with SOME associations capitalising common property and others NOT, both considered ACCEPTABLE under ASC 972, PROVIDED the POLICY is applied CONSISTENTLY and disclosed.
4

Regular vs Special Assessments – Revenue Recognition

Ongoing operating dues vs one-time capital assessments
REGULAR assessments (ongoing periodic dues covering routine operating costs) are recognised as REVENUE over the period they relate to, similar to ordinary membership dues. SPECIAL assessments (one-time charges levied for a SPECIFIC capital purpose, e.g. a major roof replacement) are recognised as revenue based on the SPECIFIC purpose and TIMING of the underlying capital project, often DEFERRED until the related expenditure is actually incurred.
5

Replacement Fund (Reserve) Accounting

Funding studies supporting major future repair/replacement needs
Many CIRAs maintain a REPLACEMENT (reserve) FUND – accumulated resources set aside for FUTURE major repairs and replacements of common property components (roofs, roads, pools) – often informed by a periodic "RESERVE STUDY" or "FUNDING STUDY" estimating future capital needs and the contribution levels required to meet them. Reserve fund contributions and related restricted cash/investments are typically presented SEPARATELY from operating fund resources, given their DIFFERENT intended purpose and often legally or contractually RESTRICTED nature.
6

Worked Example – the Common Property Capitalization Choice Illustrated

Two acceptable outcomes for the same underlying facts
Example: A community clubhouse and pool
A homeowners association owns a $2,500,000 clubhouse and pool complex, used exclusively by association members.
Under Policy A (capitalization): the association records the $2,500,000 clubhouse/pool as a PP&E ASSET, depreciated over its useful life, appearing on the association's balance sheet.
Under Policy B (non-capitalization): the SAME $2,500,000 clubhouse/pool is simply NOT recorded as an asset at all on the association's balance sheet, reflecting the view that the association doesn't hold it for its OWN independent economic benefit. BOTH approaches are ACCEPTABLE under ASC 972, provided the elected policy is applied CONSISTENTLY and clearly disclosed.
7

Worked Example – a Special Assessment

Deferred until the related capital project occurs
Example: A special roof replacement assessment
A condominium association levies a $300,000 special assessment on its members to fund an upcoming roof replacement project expected to begin in 6 months.
The $300,000 collected is initially recorded as DEFERRED revenue (or a restricted fund balance) – recognised as REVENUE (and the roof replacement expenditure capitalised, if the association's policy capitalises common property, or expensed if not) as the roof replacement PROJECT actually PROCEEDS, rather than recognised immediately upon collection.

No Dedicated IFRS CIRA Standard – Discussion

General principles apply, without an explicit policy-choice mechanism
IFRS has NO dedicated CIRA industry standard, and – notably – does NOT provide the SAME explicit "either capitalise or don't" POLICY CHOICE that ASC 972 offers for common property. An IFRS-reporting association would need to apply GENERAL asset recognition principles (control, probable future economic benefit) to determine whether common property should be recognised – likely arriving at a MORE consistent, less policy-dependent answer than ASC 972's genuinely unusual explicit dual-option approach.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Common property
Shared infrastructure and facilities managed by a CIRA on behalf of its members, which may or may not be capitalized as an asset under an elected accounting policy.
Special assessment
A one-time charge levied on members for a specific capital purpose, generally deferred until the related expenditure is incurred.
Replacement fund (reserve fund)
Accumulated resources set aside for future major repairs and replacements of common property, informed by periodic funding studies.
