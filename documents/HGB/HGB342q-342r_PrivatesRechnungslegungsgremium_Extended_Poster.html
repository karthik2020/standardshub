HGB §§342q–342r — Privates Rechnungslegungsgremium; Rechnungslegungsbeirat
Private accounting standard-setting body; accounting advisory council — the DRSC's own statutory foundation
✅ ACTIVE — Book 3, Section 5 (formerly §342/§342a, renumbered 22.6.2023)
A DELIBERATE GAP-FILL: this is the exact provision underlying the entire DRS library — the DRSC (Deutsches Rechnungslegungs Standards Committee) exists as a legally recognised standard-setter ONLY because §342q authorises the government to recognise it. Every DRS standard covered in the previous batch of posters ultimately traces its legal legitimacy back to this single provision.

History — the 2023 Renumbering, and the Deeper 1998 Origin

The oldest content in the newest position
§§342q–342r were originally enacted as §§342–342a by the 1998 KonTraG (Gesetz zur Kontrolle und Transparenz im Unternehmensbereich) — genuinely among the OLDEST provisions this poster series has covered, predating even the BilMoG-era reforms discussed throughout the HGB portion of this project. When the 2023 law inserted the new §§342–342p Ertragsteuerinformationsbericht bloc at the §342 position (covered in an earlier poster in this series), this original content was renumbered forward to §342q/§342r rather than being touched substantively — confirmed by the explicit legislative annotation "Bisheriger § 342 zu § 342q geworden gem. Gesetz v. 19.6.2023."

§342q — the Recognition Model

Four delegated functions
Developing recommendations for applying the principles of group accounting (Grundsätze über die Konzernrechnungslegung) Advising the Bundesministerium der Justiz on accounting-related legislative projects Representing Germany in international standard-setting bodies Developing interpretations of international accounting standards within the meaning of §315e HGB — the IFRS-permission provision covered in the earlier HGB poster series
Independence as a condition of recognition
§342q(1) sentence 2 imposes a genuinely important constraint: ONLY an institution whose own constitution guarantees that its recommendations are developed and adopted INDEPENDENTLY and EXCLUSIVELY by accounting professionals, through a process that genuinely involves the interested professional public, may be recognised at all — a due-process and independence requirement built directly into the statutory recognition mechanism itself.
3

The DRSC Itself — Recognised in 1998, Still the Sole Holder

One standardisation agreement, still governing today
The Deutsches Rechnungslegungs Standards Committee (DRSC) was recognised by the Bundesministerium der Justiz (BMJ) under a Standardisierungsvertrag (standardisation agreement) dated 3 September 1998 — the SAME legal mechanism, and the SAME body, that has produced every DRS standard this poster series has now documented in full, from DRS 1 through DRS 28. §342q's own recognition-by-contract model means the DRSC's authority is not self-derived, but flows entirely from this ongoing governmental recognition.
4

§342r — the Fallback Rechnungslegungsbeirat

A contingency mechanism, not the DRSC's own governing body
§342r establishes a Rechnungslegungsbeirat (accounting advisory council) at the BMJ itself, as a FALLBACK mechanism should no private body currently hold §342q recognition — ensuring German group-accounting standard-setting could continue functioning even in a scenario where the DRSC's own recognition lapsed or was withdrawn, rather than leaving a genuine institutional vacuum. Given the DRSC has held continuous recognition since 1998, this fallback has never needed to be activated in practice, but its presence in the statute is itself a deliberate structural safeguard.

Worked Example — Tracing a DRS Standard's Legal Authority Back to §342q

Why DRS 20 carries the weight it does, despite being a "mere recommendation"
Recall from the earlier DRS 20 poster: DRS 20 is technically an Empfehlung (recommendation), not a directly binding statute in the way an HGB paragraph itself is.
§342q(1) Nr.1 is PRECISELY the provision authorising the DRSC to develop such recommendations at all, and German courts and practice have long treated DRSC compliance as strong, though rebuttable, evidence of GoB (Grundsätze ordnungsmäßiger Buchführung) compliance for group accounts — meaning DRS 20's own practical authority, though formally non-binding, traces its ENTIRE legitimacy back to this single statutory recognition mechanism established in §342q.
6

Relationship to IFRS

A structurally similar delegation model to the IASB's own private-standard-setter status
§342q's own recognition-by-contract model is conceptually similar to how the IASB itself operates as a private standard-setter whose authority is given legal FORCE only through separate governmental/regulatory adoption mechanisms (such as the EU's own IFRS endorsement process, referenced repeatedly throughout the earlier HGB poster series' coverage of §315e). Both the DRSC and the IASB are, at root, private bodies whose practical authority depends entirely on external governmental recognition rather than any inherent legislative power of their own.
