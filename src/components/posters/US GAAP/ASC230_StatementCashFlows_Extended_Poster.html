Conceptual Framework Linkage

Cash, the one thing accrual accounting can obscure
Net income under accrual accounting can diverge substantially from cash actually generated or consumed. ASC 230 exists to give users a clean, direct view of an entity's CASH INFLOWS AND OUTFLOWS during a period – one of the few areas where US GAAP and IFRS terminology (and much of the underlying mechanics) are FULLY aligned, since the Statement of Cash Flows is named identically in both frameworks.
A statement immune to most estimation judgement
Unlike accrual-based statements, cash flow reporting is comparatively free of estimation uncertainty – cash either moved or it didn't. ASC 230's main judgement areas are therefore CLASSIFICATION questions (which category a cash flow belongs in) rather than measurement questions, making the classification choices in Sections 4–8 the heart of this Topic's practical complexity.
2

Scope & Applicability

A required primary statement for nearly all entities
ASC 230 requires a Statement of Cash Flows as part of a complete set of financial statements for essentially all business entities. Certain entities are exempt or subject to modified requirements (e.g. certain investment companies, employee benefit plans covered by their own industry Topics in the 900s), but the general-purpose requirement is broad and closely mirrors IAS 7's own universal applicability.

4

Direct vs Indirect Method – Operating Activities

Both methods permitted, a genuine free choice
As with IAS 7, an entity may present operating cash flows using EITHER the DIRECT method (showing major classes of gross cash receipts and payments) OR the INDIRECT method (starting from net income and adjusting for non-cash items and working capital changes). US practice overwhelmingly favours the INDIRECT method – the direct method is rarely used in practice despite being explicitly encouraged (though not required) by the FASB, a preference pattern also broadly mirrored in IFRS practice.
5

The Mandatory Reconciliation Requirement

A stricter requirement than IAS 7
If an entity uses the DIRECT method, ASC 230 REQUIRES it to ALSO separately disclose a reconciliation of net income to net cash flow from operating activities (i.e. the INDIRECT method calculation) – typically in the notes. IAS 7 imposes no equivalent dual-disclosure requirement – an IFRS preparer using the direct method is not required to also present the indirect reconciliation. This makes the direct method meaningfully more costly to adopt under US GAAP than under IFRS, which likely contributes to the direct method's continued rarity in US practice.

Classification of Interest & Dividends

One of the most heavily tested US GAAP/IFRS differences
ItemUS GAAPIFRS (IAS 7)
Interest paidOperating (fixed)Operating OR financing (choice, consistent)
Interest receivedOperating (fixed)Operating OR investing (choice, consistent)
Dividends receivedOperating (fixed)Operating OR investing (choice, consistent)
Dividends paidFinancing (fixed)Operating OR financing (choice, consistent)
⚠️ US GAAP has NO CHOICE here at all – all four items follow the single fixed classification shown above. IFRS gives entities a genuine classification election (applied consistently period to period) – meaning two IFRS preparers with identical cash flows can validly report different operating cash flow totals, something impossible under US GAAP's rigid classification.
7

Restricted Cash (ASU 2016-18)

Included in the cash reconciliation, not treated as an investing transfer
ASU 2016-18 requires entities to include RESTRICTED CASH and restricted cash equivalents together with unrestricted cash and cash equivalents when reconciling the beginning-of-period and end-of-period totals shown in the Statement of Cash Flows. Before this amendment, transfers between cash and restricted cash were sometimes presented as investing (or operating) activity cash flows – the amendment eliminated that presentation, treating changes in restricted cash purely as part of the OVERALL cash reconciliation rather than as separate line-item cash flows.
8

Bank Overdrafts

A further classification divergence from IFRS
Under US GAAP, bank overdrafts are generally treated as a FINANCING activity (short-term borrowing), NOT netted against cash and cash equivalents. Under IAS 7, by contrast, bank overdrafts REPAYABLE ON DEMAND that form an INTEGRAL PART of an entity's cash management may be included as a COMPONENT of cash and cash equivalents – meaning IFRS can, in specific circumstances, show a NEGATIVE cash and cash equivalents balance that US GAAP would never present the same way.
9

Noncash Investing & Financing Activities

Disclosed supplementally, excluded from the statement
Significant NONCASH investing and financing transactions – such as acquiring an asset by assuming a directly related liability, converting debt to equity, or acquiring an asset via a finance lease – are DISCLOSED SEPARATELY (either on the face of the statement or in the notes), but EXCLUDED from the body of the statement itself, since no cash actually moved. This treatment is fully aligned with IAS 7's own noncash transaction disclosure requirement.

Worked Example – Indirect Method Reconciliation

Building operating cash flow from net income
Example: Adjusting net income for non-cash items and working capital
Net income for the year is $5,000,000. Depreciation expense was $800,000. Accounts receivable increased by $300,000; inventory decreased by $150,000; accounts payable increased by $200,000.
Net cash from operating activities = $5,000,000 + $800,000 (add back non-cash depreciation) − $300,000 (increase in receivables uses cash) + $150,000 (decrease in inventory frees cash) + $200,000 (increase in payables defers cash outflow) = $5,850,000.
11

Worked Example – Interest/Dividend Classification

The same cash flows, classified differently under each framework
Example: Interest paid and dividends received
An entity pays $1,200,000 of interest on its debt and receives $400,000 of dividend income from an equity investment during the year.
Under US GAAP: both amounts are OPERATING cash flows – no choice available.
Under IFRS (IAS 7): the entity COULD classify the $1,200,000 interest paid as either operating OR financing, and the $400,000 dividends received as either operating OR investing – an IFRS preparer electing financing/investing classification for both items would report OPERATING cash flow $1,600,000 HIGHER than a US GAAP preparer with the identical underlying cash flows.
12

Worked Example – Restricted Cash Reconciliation

Combining restricted and unrestricted cash
Example: Post-ASU 2016-18 reconciliation
At the start of the year, an entity holds $2,000,000 unrestricted cash and $500,000 restricted cash (held as loan collateral). By year end, unrestricted cash is $2,800,000 and restricted cash is $500,000 (unchanged).
The Statement of Cash Flows reconciles a COMBINED opening balance of $2,500,000 to a combined closing balance of $3,300,000 – the $500,000 restricted cash is included throughout, and NO separate "transfer to restricted cash" line item is presented, since the restricted amount did not change during the year.

ASC 230 vs IAS 7 – the Interest/Dividend Divergence

Comparison – ASC 230 vs IAS 7
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 230)IFRS (IAS 7)
Interest/dividend classificationFixed – no choice (Section 6)Genuine classification choice, consistently applied
Direct method reconciliationMandatory even under the direct methodNot required if the direct method is used
Bank overdraftsFinancing activity; not netted against cashMay be included within cash and cash equivalents if repayable on demand and integral to cash management
Restricted cashExplicitly included in the cash reconciliation (ASU 2016-18)Less explicitly codified; generally similar substance-based inclusion in practice, without an equivalent dedicated amendment
The interest/dividend classification difference is arguably the single most frequently tested US GAAP/IFRS divergence in professional exams covering both frameworks, given how directly and mechanically it changes reported operating cash flow for identical underlying transactions.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Indirect method
A presentation of operating cash flows starting from net income and adjusting for non-cash items and changes in working capital – the dominant method in both US and IFRS practice.
Restricted cash
Cash whose use is limited by contract or regulation (e.g. held as loan collateral), included together with unrestricted cash in the Statement of Cash Flows reconciliation per ASU 2016-18.
