Ind AS 34 – Interim Financial Reporting
Extended reference poster · Converged with IAS 34 · SEBI LODR quarterly reporting – a genuine Indian regulatory driver · Worked examples
🇮🇳 IND AS – CONVERGED WITH IAS 34, WITH A GENUINE REGULATORY-FORMAT INTERACTION – Ind AS 34's recognition/measurement principles for interim periods mirror IAS 34 closely. IAS 34 itself does NOT mandate interim reporting – this is left to jurisdictional regulation. In India, SEBI's LODR (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations MANDATE quarterly financial results for listed companies, using a SPECIFIC prescribed format (Regulation 33) that layers additional presentation requirements on top of Ind AS 34's own principles. Conceptual Framework Linkage

Same interim principles as IAS 34, mandatory Indian trigger
Ind AS 34 shares IAS 34's core content – it prescribes the MINIMUM content of an interim financial report and the recognition/measurement principles to be applied, treating each interim period as part of a LARGER annual period (the "integral" view for most items). Where Ind AS 34 genuinely diverges in PRACTICE is that SEBI's LODR Regulations MANDATE quarterly reporting for Indian listed companies using a SPECIFIC prescribed format – a legal trigger and presentation overlay IAS 34 itself does not impose (since IAS 34 leaves the DECISION of whether to require interim reporting to local regulation).
HOW IND AS 34 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Timeliness – more frequent reporting provides users with more current information – identical rationale to IAS 34SEBI LODR format overlay – Regulation 33 prescribes SPECIFIC line items and a standardised format for Indian listed company quarterly results, going beyond IAS 34's own more flexible minimum content requirements
This is NOT a divergence in Ind AS 34's own accounting PRINCIPLES from IAS 34 – it is a genuinely Indian REGULATORY trigger and presentation overlay, comparable in character to Schedule III's role for Ind AS 1.
The integral view – identical to IAS 34
Each interim period = an integral part of the larger annual period (for most recognition/measurement purposes)
ℹ️ IDENTICAL conceptual approach to IAS 34.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 34 covers
Ind AS 34 prescribes minimum content and recognition/measurement principles for interim financial reports – IDENTICAL scope to IAS 34. Neither standard itself MANDATES interim reporting; this is left to jurisdictional regulation.
In India, SEBI's LODR Regulations are the operative TRIGGER for Ind AS 34's mandatory application for listed companies.
Key definitions you must know
Interim period
IDENTICAL definition to IAS 34 – a financial reporting period shorter than a full financial year.
SEBI LODR Regulation 33
The specific SEBI regulation prescribing the format and timeline for Indian listed companies' quarterly financial results.

Minimum Components of an Interim Report

Identical minimum components to IAS 34
Condensed balance sheet, condensed P&L statement, condensed statement of changes in equity, condensed cash flow statement, and selected explanatory notes – IDENTICAL minimum requirements to IAS 34 (though SEBI LODR's own format may require additional specific line items).

The Discrete vs Integral Approach (with worked example)

Identical integral view to IAS 34
Example 1: Annual bonus accrual across quarters
An Indian company expects to pay an annual performance bonus of ₹40,00,000, expected to accrue evenly based on cumulative annual performance across all four quarters.
₹10,00,000 (₹40,00,000 ÷ 4) is recognised as an EXPENSE in EACH quarter, treating each interim period as an INTEGRAL part of the annual period – an IDENTICAL approach to how IAS 34 would treat the same annual bonus accrual globally, rather than a purely "discrete period" approach that would ignore the annual context.
5

Recognition & Measurement Principles

Identical to IAS 34
The SAME accounting policies applied in annual financial statements are applied in interim reports – IDENTICAL to IAS 34, with NO separate "interim-only" recognition/measurement basis (other than specific items like income tax, discussed via estimated ANNUAL effective tax rate).
6

Seasonal, Cyclical, or Occasional Revenue

Identical to IAS 34
Revenue that is SEASONAL or CYCLICAL within a financial year is recognised as/when it OCCURS, NOT anticipated or deferred to smooth results across interim periods – IDENTICAL to IAS 34, relevant for Indian sectors with pronounced seasonality (e.g. agriculture-linked FMCG, festive-season retail).
7

SEBI LODR Quarterly Reporting – The Indian Regulatory Driver

The single most important practical Indian consideration
SEBI's LODR Regulations REQUIRE every Indian listed company to submit QUARTERLY financial results within a PRESCRIBED timeline (typically 45 days of quarter-end, 60 days for the final quarter), using Regulation 33's SPECIFIC format – which includes particular line items (e.g. EPS, segment information extracts) beyond Ind AS 34's own more flexible minimum content list. This is a genuinely Indian REGULATORY trigger and presentation template, operating ALONGSIDE (not modifying) Ind AS 34's own recognition/measurement principles.
8

Materiality for Interim Reporting

Identical to IAS 34
Materiality is assessed in relation to INTERIM period financial data (not annual data), which can result in MORE items being disclosed at the interim level relative to their annual significance – IDENTICAL principle to IAS 34.
