Conceptual Framework Linkage

A pure "obligating event" question
IAS 37 recognises a provision only where a PRESENT obligation exists as a result of a PAST event. IFRIC 6 is entirely about pinning down what that past event actually is, in a fact pattern where two plausible candidates exist — and the answer determines whether a liability is recognised years earlier or is instead spread out over future periods as the entity keeps operating.
Faithful representation over front-loaded caution
Recognising the full liability at the moment historical equipment was manufactured — before any current obligation to fund its disposal actually exists — would overstate liabilities relative to the entity's true present legal position. IFRIC 6's conclusion ties recognition to the event that ACTUALLY creates the obligation: ongoing participation in the market during the relevant measurement period.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRIC 6 covers
IFRIC 6 addresses the accounting for the obligation to participate in the cost of waste management for "historical" household equipment — i.e. products sold before a specified cut-off date — under waste-management schemes modelled on the EU's Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment (WEEE) Directive, where producers who continue to sell into the market fund the waste management costs of historical equipment collectively, typically in proportion to market share.
TYPICAL FACT PATTERN
A producer sold household electronics before a legislative cut-off date establishing collective producer responsibilityThe producer continues to sell into the same market after the cut-off dateNational law allocates historical waste management costs based on producers' market share DURING a defined future measurement period
Key definitions
Historical waste
Household equipment put on the market before the specified cut-off date, whose waste management costs are funded collectively by producers who participate in the market after that date.
Measurement period
The defined future period (e.g. a calendar year) during which market share is measured for the purpose of allocating historical waste management costs among current market participants.
3

The Question IFRIC 6 Answers

A deceptively simple timing question
Should a producer recognise its share of historical waste management costs (a) when the historical household equipment was ORIGINALLY manufactured or sold, or (b) progressively, as the producer PARTICIPATES in the market during the relevant future measurement period? The two answers produce dramatically different recognition timing and liability size at any given reporting date.
4

Two Candidate Obligating Events

Weighing the alternatives
Candidate obligating eventWhy it was considered, and why rejected
Manufacture/sale of the historical equipmentSuperficially attractive — the waste physically originates from these units. REJECTED because, at that date, no law yet existed creating a present obligation on the producer specifically; the future law's cost-allocation mechanism does not attach to the individual units sold, but to market participation later
Participation in the market during the measurement periodACCEPTED — under most such schemes, a producer's allocated share of historical waste costs is calculated by reference to its market share DURING the measurement period, not by reference to units it originally sold; a producer who exits the market before the measurement period incurs no obligation at all
5

The Conclusion — Market Participation (with worked example)

The obligating event
IFRIC 6 concludes the obligating event is PARTICIPATION IN THE MARKET during the measurement period, NOT the manufacture or sale of the historical equipment. A liability is therefore recognised progressively as the entity participates in the market, and NOT up front at the point historical equipment was originally sold, nor as new products are currently manufactured.
Worked example — progressive liability build-up
Example: Market-share-based allocation
A national scheme allocates $10,000,000 of historical waste management costs among producers based on market share during the calendar year. By 30 June, a producer holds a 15% market share for the year to date and expects to hold approximately 15% for the full year.
A provision of approximately $1,500,000 (15% × $10,000,000) is built up progressively over the measurement period as the entity participates in the market — NOT recognised in full at the start of the year, and not linked to the specific historical units the producer itself originally sold.
6

Measuring the Liability

Estimation mechanics
Because the final allocation depends on FULL-PERIOD market share, which is only known with certainty at the end of the measurement period, entities must ESTIMATE their expected market share as the period progresses and true up the provision accordingly — consistent with IAS 37's general measurement principles for provisions based on the best estimate of the expenditure required to settle the obligation at the reporting date.
7

Why This Reasoning Generalises Beyond WEEE

A template for "levy-style" obligations generally
IFRIC 6's core reasoning — that the obligating event for a cost allocated based on a future activity measure is PARTICIPATION IN that activity, not some earlier triggering event — has proven influential well beyond the specific WEEE fact pattern. The same analytical structure underpins how entities analyse other market-share-based or activity-based levies and contributions imposed by government or industry schemes.
8

Interaction with New vs Historical Equipment

A narrower scope than it first appears
IFRIC 6 addresses ONLY the treatment of historical (pre-cut-off) equipment costs. Waste management obligations relating to NEW equipment sold after the cut-off date are typically the direct, individual responsibility of the producer that sold those units, and are accounted for under general IAS 37 principles at the point of sale, without IFRIC 6's market-participation reasoning being needed.

Quick-reference glossary
Obligating event
The past event that creates a present legal or constructive obligation, leaving an entity no realistic alternative to settling it — the central concept IFRIC 6 works through for this fact pattern.
Cut-off date
The date separating "historical" equipment (funded collectively based on market participation) from "new" equipment (funded directly by the individual producer that sold it).

Quick-Reference Glossary

Collective producer responsibility
A regulatory model under which current market participants jointly fund waste management costs for products sold before a specified date, in proportion to market share, rather than each producer bearing the cost of its own historical units individually.
Levy
A payment to a government or government-like body imposed under legislation, whose obligating event must be identified under IAS 37 principles — IFRIC 6's reasoning is a specific early instance of this broader levy-accounting question.
