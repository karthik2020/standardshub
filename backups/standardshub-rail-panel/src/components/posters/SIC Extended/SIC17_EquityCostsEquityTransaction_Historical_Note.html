SIC-17 — Equity—Costs of an Equity Transaction
Historical reference note · Issued 2000 · Withdrawn 2003
⚠ WITHDRAWN — Superseded by the 2003 revision of IAS 32 Financial Instruments: Presentation, effective for periods beginning 1 January 2005
What SIC-17 addressed
SIC-17 addressed how the TRANSACTION COSTS of an equity transaction — such as share issue costs, underwriting fees, and stock exchange listing costs — should be accounted for, including how to distinguish costs directly attributable to the equity transaction from more general costs that would have been incurred regardless.
The conclusion it reached
Transaction costs of an equity transaction should be accounted for as a DEDUCTION FROM EQUITY (net of any related tax benefit), to the extent they are INCREMENTAL costs directly attributable to the equity transaction that otherwise would have been avoided. Costs relating to an ABANDONED equity transaction, by contrast, are EXPENSED, since no equity instrument is ultimately issued to deduct the costs against.
Where this guidance lives today
Superseded byIAS 32 Financial Instruments: Presentation (revised 2003)
Why withdrawnThe equity transaction cost treatment was incorporated directly into the main standard's text (IAS 32.37–38), making a standalone interpretation unnecessary
Current statusThe underlying principle remains good law today — it simply now sits in IAS 32 itself rather than in a separate SIC
