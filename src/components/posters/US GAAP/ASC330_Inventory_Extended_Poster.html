Conceptual Framework Linkage

Cost, capped by recoverability
Inventory is generally carried at COST — the price actually paid to acquire or produce it — but a Conceptual-Framework-level concern arises if that cost genuinely exceeds what the inventory can realistically be sold for. ASC 330 exists to ensure inventory is never carried above its recoverable amount, while still anchoring measurement in historical cost as the primary basis.
The single most famous US GAAP/IFRS accounting difference
ASC 330's permission of the LIFO cost formula, against IAS 2's explicit prohibition of it, is arguably THE most widely known specific accounting difference between the two frameworks — taught in virtually every introductory comparative accounting course, and still fully live and unresolved today (Section 5).
2

Scope & Applicability

All entities holding inventory
ASC 330 applies to inventory held by all entities for sale in the ordinary course of business, in the process of production for such sale, or to be currently consumed in producing goods/services for sale — the same general scope IAS 2 covers.
3

Cost Basis — the Move to LCNRV (ASU 2015-11)

A deliberate convergence step toward IAS 2
ASU 2015-11 (effective 2017) simplified inventory measurement for MOST entities, moving from the old "LOWER OF COST OR MARKET" (LCM) test — a complex three-way comparison involving replacement cost, net realisable value, and net realisable value less a normal profit margin — to "LOWER OF COST AND NET REALISABLE VALUE" (LCNRV) — a SIMPLER, single comparison. This was a DELIBERATE step toward aligning with IAS 2's own long-standing NRV-based approach.
4

A Partial Convergence — LIFO/Retail Method Still Use LCM

The convergence has a carve-out
ASU 2015-11's simplification to LCNRV applies ONLY to inventory measured using FIFO or the WEIGHTED-AVERAGE cost method. Inventory measured using LIFO or the RETAIL INVENTORY METHOD CONTINUES to use the OLDER, more complex "lower of cost or market" (LCM) test — meaning US GAAP inventory measurement today is genuinely SPLIT: LCNRV for FIFO/average-cost inventory, but LCM for LIFO/retail-method inventory. This is a nuance often missed — the 2015 convergence was only PARTIAL.
5

LIFO — the Headline US GAAP/IFRS Divergence

Permitted here, explicitly prohibited under IFRS
US GAAP explicitly PERMITS the LAST-IN-FIRST-OUT (LIFO) cost formula — assuming the most recently acquired inventory items are sold FIRST, leaving older (often lower-cost, during inflation) costs in ending inventory. IAS 2 explicitly PROHIBITS LIFO entirely — no IFRS preparer may use it, regardless of circumstances. LIFO remains attractive in the US primarily for its TAX benefits during inflationary periods (lower reported taxable income, since more recent, higher costs are matched against current revenue) — a tax-driven motivation IFRS's prohibition removes entirely for international preparers.
6

The LIFO Reserve Disclosure

A distinctly US disclosure concept
Entities using LIFO must disclose the "LIFO RESERVE" — the DIFFERENCE between the inventory's LIFO-based carrying amount and what it would have been under FIFO (or current cost) — enabling users to adjust LIFO-based figures for comparability with FIFO-reporting entities (including virtually all IFRS preparers, given LIFO's prohibition there). This is a genuinely US-GAAP-specific disclosure concept, since IFRS has no comparable need for it.
7

LIFO Liquidation

A distortive event unique to LIFO reporting
If inventory quantities DECLINE during a period, older, LOWER-cost LIFO "layers" from prior years are liquidated and matched against CURRENT revenue — producing an artificially INFLATED gross margin relative to current replacement costs. This "LIFO LIQUIDATION" phenomenon is a well-known LIFO-specific distortion that simply cannot arise under IFRS, since LIFO itself is prohibited there.

Full Absorption Costing

Converged with IAS 2
Manufacturing inventory cost includes direct materials, direct labour, and an ALLOCATED portion of both variable and fixed PRODUCTION OVERHEADS (full absorption costing) — the SAME approach IAS 2 requires, allocating fixed overheads based on NORMAL production capacity, with abnormal amounts of waste/idle facility expense expensed as incurred rather than capitalised, again converged with IAS 2.

Worked Example — LCNRV Application

Writing inventory down to net realisable value
Example: FIFO inventory below cost
A FIFO-costed inventory item has a cost of $80 per unit. Its estimated selling price is $95, with estimated costs to complete and sell of $20.
Net realisable value = $95 − $20 = $75. Since $75 (NRV) is LOWER than $80 (cost), the inventory is written down to $75 per unit — a $5 per-unit write-down recognised as an expense, applying the simplified LCNRV test (no three-way "market" comparison, since this is FIFO-costed inventory).
11

Worked Example — LIFO Reserve Disclosure

Reconciling LIFO to FIFO for comparability
Example: Disclosing the gap between reported and FIFO-equivalent inventory
An entity's inventory is reported at $12,000,000 under LIFO. Management determines the same inventory would have been valued at $15,500,000 under FIFO.
The entity discloses a LIFO reserve of $3,500,000 ($15,500,000 − $12,000,000) — allowing a user comparing this entity to a FIFO (or IFRS) reporter to add back $3,500,000 to inventory, and make a corresponding adjustment to historical cost of goods sold, for a more comparable, apples-to-apples analysis.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Lower of cost and net realisable value (LCNRV)
The simplified inventory write-down test applicable to FIFO and weighted-average costed inventory since ASU 2015-11.
Lower of cost or market (LCM)
The older, three-way comparison test (replacement cost, NRV, NRV less normal profit margin) still required for LIFO and retail-method inventory.
