Conceptual Framework Linkage

Where a completed, audited Jahresabschluss finally reaches the public
Every provision this series has covered so far — recognition, measurement, presentation, consolidation, audit — culminates in a single practical act: FILING the finished, audited documents where the public can actually see them. §§325–329 govern exactly this: what must be filed, by when, in what form, and what happens if a company simply doesn't.
HOW §§325–329 MAP TO GERMAN ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES
Gläubigerschutz, made publicly actionable — creditors and counterparties can only rely on a company's own accounts if those accounts are genuinely, publicly ACCESSIBLE, not merely prepared and audited internally Proportionality — §326/§327's size-dependent relief is the SAME cost-benefit theme running throughout this entire series, now applied to the disclosure step specifically

Scope

Every Kapitalgesellschaft (and §264a-caught partnership), scaled by size
§325 binds every Kapitalgesellschaft and §264a-caught partnership established earlier in this series — with the ACTUAL extent of what must be disclosed, and how quickly, scaling directly with the §267 size classification and §264d capital-market-oriented status established in earlier posters.
3

§325(1) — What Must Be Filed, and Where

The Jahresabschluss, Lagebericht, audit opinion, and the profit-appropriation decision
§325(1) requires the Jahresabschluss, Lagebericht, the Bestätigungsvermerk (or note of its refusal, per §322), and — where the Jahresabschluss itself contained only a PROPOSAL for profit appropriation — the actual resolved decision once available, to be submitted ELECTRONICALLY to the body operating the Unternehmensregister (company register). §325(1a) extends the parallel obligation to the Konzernabschluss and Konzernlagebericht.
4

The 2022 DiRUG Reform — From Bundesanzeiger to Unternehmensregister

A genuine digitalisation-driven platform change, not merely a rename
The DiRUG (Gesetz zur Digitalisierung des Registerrechts), effective 1 August 2022, restructured HOW disclosure actually happens: filings for fiscal years beginning after 31 December 2021 go DIRECTLY to the Unternehmensregister via a dedicated electronic platform, rather than routing through the Bundesanzeiger-Verlag as an intermediary publisher, as had been the case for years beforehand. Many practitioners (and older commentary) still colloquially refer to "filing with the Bundesanzeiger" — but the legally accurate description since August 2022 is "filing with the Unternehmensregister."
⚠️ This is a genuinely practical point worth getting right: someone researching "Bundesanzeiger Offenlegung" today is researching the CURRENT Unternehmensregister-based process under a legacy name, not a separate or superseded system.

§325(1a) and (4) — the Two Filing Deadlines

12 months ordinarily, 4 months for capital-market-oriented issuers
§325(1a) sets the GENERAL deadline at TWELVE MONTHS after the balance sheet date — a genuinely generous window compared to the §264(1) three-month PREPARATION deadline established much earlier in this series, since disclosure is a separate, later step from preparation itself. §325(4) sets a considerably SHORTER, FOUR-MONTH deadline specifically for capital-market-oriented corporations (per §264d), reflecting capital markets' own need for considerably faster public access to a listed company's results — subject to the narrow §327a carve-back discussed in Section 13.

Worked Example — Calculating an Ordinary Company's Filing Deadline

A full calendar year's grace, running from the balance sheet date itself
Example 1: A medium-sized GmbH's ordinary filing deadline
A medium-sized GmbH, not capital-market-oriented, has a 31 December fiscal year-end.
Its Jahresabschluss for the year ending 31 December must be FILED with the Unternehmensregister no later than 31 December of the FOLLOWING year — the full 12-month window §325(1a) allows, measured directly from the balance sheet date itself (not rounded to a calendar-year boundary the way §257's own retention period calculation worked).
7

Worked Example — the Shorter Deadline for a Listed Company

A dramatically compressed window for capital-market-oriented issuers
Example 2: A listed AG's filing deadline
A capital-market-oriented AG has the SAME 31 December fiscal year-end.
Its filing deadline falls just FOUR MONTHS later — 30 April of the following year — a considerably tighter window than the ordinary 12-month deadline, DESPITE the underlying preparation, audit, and adoption process being at least as complex as for the non-listed company in Example 1.
8

§325(2a) — Permanent Deposit Filing for Large Corporations (Corrected)

A specific filing-mechanics option, not an IFRS supplementary statement
§325(2a) permits LARGE Kapitalgesellschaften (per §267(3)) to submit their Jahresabschluss, together with the Bestätigungsvermerk (or note of its refusal), in German, via "dauerhafte Hinterlegung" — permanent electronic deposit — to the Unternehmensregister, as an alternative filing MECHANISM under the specific conditions §325(1a) and (4) otherwise set. This is genuinely a procedural/technical filing-format provision, NOT a substantive accounting-standards election — it does not permit or require preparing accounts under any framework other than HGB itself.
✅ As flagged at the top of this poster, an earlier draft of the previous §316–324a poster incorrectly described this provision as an IFRS-based supplementary statement. That characterisation has been corrected.

§325a — Disclosure by Branches of Foreign Corporations

German transparency for a foreign parent's local branch presence
§325a requires German BRANCHES of foreign-domiciled corporations to disclose certain documents — typically the foreign parent's own accounts, prepared under ITS home jurisdiction's law, translated into German where necessary — ensuring German counterparties dealing with a local branch presence still have meaningful transparency into the underlying foreign company, even though that company itself is not a German Kapitalgesellschaft bound by the rest of this Section.
10

§326 — Relief for Small and Micro-Corporations

Abbreviated disclosure for small companies; an even lighter option for micro-corporations
§326(1) permits SMALL corporations to disclose only the ABBREVIATED §266(1) Bilanz format established earlier in this series (letters/Roman numerals only), and to omit the GuV and certain Anhang items from PUBLIC disclosure entirely — though the company must still PREPARE a full Jahresabschluss for internal and shareholder purposes; it is specifically the PUBLICLY DISCLOSED version that is abbreviated. §326(2) gives MICRO-corporations (per §267a) an even lighter option still: rather than fully public online disclosure, they may choose "Hinterlegung" (deposit) — the Bilanz is held on file and made available only ON REQUEST, rather than freely, immediately accessible to any member of the public online.
11

Worked Example — the Micro-Corporation Hinterlegung Option

Meeting minimum transparency without free, unrestricted public browsing
Example 3: A micro-corporation electing deposit over full disclosure
A Kleinstkapitalgesellschaft, meeting the €450,000/€900,000/10-employee thresholds established earlier in this series, wishes to limit how freely its balance sheet can be browsed by the general public online.
The company elects "Hinterlegung" under §326(2) rather than ordinary public disclosure — its Bilanz is held on file with the Unternehmensregister, accessible to anyone who specifically REQUESTS it, but not presented for free, casual public browsing the way an ordinarily-disclosed company's accounts would be — a genuine, if narrow, privacy-conscious relief reserved specifically for the smallest company category.
12

§327 — Relief for Medium-Sized Corporations

Narrower relief than small companies receive, but still genuine
§327 permits MEDIUM-sized corporations to combine or omit certain GuV line items in their DISCLOSED version — echoing the SAME §276 GuV combination relief established earlier in this series for the PREPARED statement itself, now applied specifically to the disclosed/filed version — and to limit certain Anhang disclosures, though considerably more narrowly than the small-company relief under §326.
13

§327a — a Narrow Carve-Back for Debt-Only Issuers

A specific exception to the "capital-market-oriented is always large" override
§327a supplies a narrow carve-back for capital-market-oriented corporations that issue ONLY debt securities (bonds), not equity — since bondholders' own investor-protection needs differ from equity investors', such companies may qualify for CERTAIN of the same size-based reliefs otherwise entirely unavailable to capital-market-oriented companies (recall §267's own "always large" override, and §325(4)'s own shorter deadline, established earlier in this series) — a genuinely narrow exception carved specifically out of that broader override.
14

§328 — Form, Format, and Content of Disclosed Documents

What's disclosed must be exactly what was audited
§328 requires disclosed documents to correspond PRECISELY to the documents the audit opinion was actually based on — a company cannot disclose a "tidied up" or otherwise different version than what the auditor examined — and requires the Bestätigungsvermerk (or note of its qualification/refusal) to be reproduced in FULL alongside the disclosed statements, ensuring readers see the genuine audit outcome rather than a selectively-presented summary.
15

§329 — the Formal Completeness Check

A checklist review, not a substantive quality audit
§329 requires the Unternehmensregister operator to conduct a FORMAL completeness check on filed documents — verifying that the required elements are actually PRESENT, not independently assessing the SUBSTANTIVE quality or accuracy of what was filed (that remains the auditor's own job under §§316–324a). Where deficiencies are identified, the operator notifies the Bundesamt für Justiz (Federal Office of Justice), the enforcement authority responsible for pursuing fine (Ordnungsgeld) proceedings against non-compliant companies.
16

Worked Example — a Deficient Filing Triggering Enforcement

Missing the deadline carries genuine, escalating financial consequences
Example 4: A company that simply misses its filing deadline
A medium-sized GmbH fails to file its Jahresabschluss within the 12-month §325(1a) deadline, and does not respond to an initial notice.
The Bundesamt für Justiz opens Ordnungsgeld (administrative fine) proceedings — beginning at a minimum threshold and capable of ESCALATING with repeated non-compliance — under §335. Unlike the individual-accounts §316(3) voidness consequence for skipping a mandatory AUDIT, a late or missing DISCLOSURE filing triggers a financial penalty mechanism rather than invalidating the underlying Jahresabschluss itself.

Relationship to IFRS

Comparison — HGB §§325–329 vs IFRS
Exactly as with the earlier retention-period poster, IFRS itself has NO equivalent public-disclosure/filing-deadline mandate — WHERE and WHEN audited accounts must be publicly filed is governed entirely by local company or securities law, wherever IFRS is adopted, with IFRS remaining silent on the question. §§325–329 sit at the SAME "legal infrastructure" layer this series has repeatedly identified beneath any accounting standard, IFRS included — even a group using §315e's IFRS permission for its Konzernabschluss STILL relies on this Subsection's own HGB filing mechanics to actually get its accounts in front of the public.
