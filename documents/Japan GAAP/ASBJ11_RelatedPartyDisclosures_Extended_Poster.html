Conceptual Framework Linkage

Transparency for transactions that may not reflect arm's-length terms
ASBJ Statement No. 11 requires disclosure of TRANSACTIONS and outstanding BALANCES with RELATED PARTIES — parties who may be able to INFLUENCE, or be INFLUENCED by, the reporting entity in ways that could affect the TERMS of a transaction. Because such relationships CAN result in transactions on terms DIFFERENT from what unrelated parties would negotiate, TRANSPARENT disclosure allows readers to assess the entity's financial position and performance with FULL knowledge of these relationships.
2

Scope

All companies with related party transactions or relationships
This Statement applies to ALL companies with RELATED PARTY relationships or transactions, requiring disclosure REGARDLESS of whether a price was actually charged for the transaction.

4

What Must Be Disclosed

Nature, terms, and amounts of transactions and balances
For each MATERIAL related party relationship or transaction, disclosure includes: the NATURE of the relationship; a DESCRIPTION of the transaction(s); the TRANSACTION amount; the ACCOUNTING policy/pricing method applied; OUTSTANDING balances at the reporting date and their terms; and any provision for doubtful accounts related to those balances.
5

Quantitative Materiality Thresholds — a J-GAAP-Specific Feature

More precisely specified thresholds than IFRS's general materiality concept
A DISTINCTIVE feature of J-GAAP's implementation guidance is the use of MORE PRECISELY SPECIFIED quantitative thresholds for determining WHICH related party transactions require disclosure — for example, thresholds expressed as a PERCENTAGE of net assets or a SPECIFIC yen amount for particular transaction types (e.g. loans, guarantees) — providing PREPARERS with more CONCRETE, less subjectively-judged guidance than IFRS's more GENERALLY stated, principles-based "materiality" concept under IAS 24.
6

Worked Example — Identifying a Related Party Transaction

A loan between a company and its affiliate
Example: An intercompany loan to an equity-method affiliate
A company lends ¥300,000,000 to an affiliate accounted for under the equity method, at an interest rate below the affiliate's normal market borrowing rate.
This is a RELATED PARTY TRANSACTION requiring disclosure — the AFFILIATE relationship, the ¥300,000,000 loan amount, the BELOW-MARKET interest rate charged, and the outstanding BALANCE at the reporting date must ALL be disclosed, since the terms may not reflect what an UNRELATED lender would have offered.
7

Worked Example — Applying a Materiality Threshold

Determining whether a transaction crosses the disclosure threshold
Example: A small routine purchase from a director-controlled company
A company purchases office supplies worth ¥800,000 during the year from a small company controlled by one of its directors. The applicable quantitative threshold for this transaction type is ¥10,000,000.
Since the ¥800,000 transaction FALLS BELOW the specified ¥10,000,000 threshold, it does NOT require separate disclosure under this Statement's guidance — illustrating how the MORE PRECISE, quantitative threshold approach gives preparers CLEARER practical guidance than a purely qualitative materiality judgement would.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Related party
A party able to influence, or be influenced by, the reporting entity in ways that could affect transaction terms, including group entities, key individuals, and entities they control.
Quantitative materiality threshold
A specific, precisely stated numerical threshold (e.g. a percentage of net assets or a fixed yen amount) determining which related party transactions require disclosure.
