Conceptual Framework Linkage

Preserving what §§238–256a required to be created
Having established WHAT must be recorded (§§238–241a), HOW the annual accounts must be prepared (§§242–245), WHAT must be recognised (§§246–251), and HOW it must be measured (§§252–256a), HGB now turns to a genuinely practical question: for how LONG must all of this documentation be preserved, and under what circumstances can a third party — a court, a counterparty in litigation — compel it to be PRODUCED?
HOW §§257–263 MAP TO GERMAN ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES
Nachprüfbarkeit (verifiability) — retention exists precisely so the traceability §238 demands remains POSSIBLE for years after the fact, not merely at the moment of original entry Gläubigerschutz (creditor protection) — courts and counterparties need genuine access to reliable records long after a transaction occurred, particularly in insolvency or dispute scenarios
This is a NARROWER, more procedural Titel than the two preceding anchor posters — it does not introduce new recognition or measurement philosophy, but rather operationalises how long, and in what form, everything already required elsewhere must survive.

Scope

Every Kaufmann bound by the preceding Titel's obligations
§§257–263 apply to the SAME population bound by §§238–256a — the retention obligation is simply the natural continuation of the bookkeeping, accounts-preparation, recognition, and measurement obligations already established, extending their reach forward in TIME rather than introducing a new category of obligated party.
3

§257(1) — What Must Be Retained

Four named categories
Opening balance sheets, annual accounts, group management reports, and the instructions/organisational documents needed to understand them Commercial letters RECEIVED (empfangene Handelsbriefe) Copies of commercial letters SENT (abgesandte Handelsbriefe) Accounting vouchers (Belege für Buchungen) — the underlying source documents supporting each individual entry
4

§257(2) — Defining a Handelsbrief

Only documents concerning a specific business transaction
§257(2) defines a Handelsbrief (commercial letter) narrowly — only correspondence that relates to the PREPARATION, CONCLUSION, or PERFORMANCE of a specific commercial transaction. General marketing material, internal policy memoranda unconnected to any specific transaction, or purely social correspondence between business contacts do NOT qualify, even if exchanged between merchants in a commercial context.
5

§257(3) — Electronic and Image-Based Retention

Permitted, subject to conditions ensuring the record remains trustworthy
§257(3) permits most documents to be retained as image or data storage media (microfilm, scanned images, electronic files) PROVIDED the reproduction (a) conforms to the original in content, (b) is available and legible within a reasonable time throughout the retention period, and (c) can be reproduced at any time. Opening balance sheets and Jahresabschlüsse historically required RETENTION in original paper form (or as an electronic original meeting stricter integrity conditions) — reflecting their heightened evidentiary and legal significance relative to ordinary correspondence.
ℹ️ Digitalisation-friendly amendments in recent years have progressively relaxed the historical paper-original requirement, allowing purely electronic preparation and retention under specific integrity safeguards — reflecting a broader, ongoing modernisation trend across German commercial law.
6

§257(4) — the Two Retention Periods: 10 Years and 6 Years

The most consequential provision in this Titel
PeriodDocument categories
10 yearsOpening balance sheets, Jahresabschlüsse, Konzernabschlüsse, Lageberichte, Konzernlageberichte, and accounting vouchers (Buchungsbelege)
6 yearsReceived commercial letters, copies of sent commercial letters, and other documents relevant for taxation purposes not already covered by the 10-year category
The heaviest evidentiary items — the finished accounts themselves, and the underlying vouchers proving each entry — get the LONGEST retention period; ordinary correspondence gets a somewhat shorter one.

Worked Example — Classifying Documents Into the Two Retention Periods

Sorting a company's records by category
Example 1: A company's year-end document set
A GmbH's records for the past fiscal year include: (a) the signed Jahresabschluss itself, (b) a supplier invoice supporting a specific booked expense, (c) a routine order-confirmation email exchanged with a customer, and (d) a general newsletter the company received from an industry association.
(a) the Jahresabschluss: 10-year retention. (b) the supplier invoice (a Buchungsbeleg supporting a specific entry): 10-year retention. (c) the order-confirmation email (a Handelsbrief relating to a specific transaction): 6-year retention. (d) the general industry newsletter: NOT subject to §257 at all — it is neither a Handelsbrief (no specific transaction) nor an accounting voucher.
8

§257(5) — When the Retention Clock Actually Starts

The end of the calendar year, not the exact date of the document itself
§257(5) provides that the retention period begins at the END of the CALENDAR YEAR in which the last entry was made in the underlying books, the inventory was drawn up, the opening balance sheet or annual accounts were prepared, the commercial letter was received or sent, or the voucher was created — NOT from the exact date of the document itself. This "rounds up" every document's retention clock to a common, predictable annual boundary, considerably simplifying administration.
9

Worked Example — Calculating a Retention Period's Expiry Date

Rounding to the calendar year boundary
Example 2: Determining exactly when a document may be destroyed
A company receives a supplier invoice (a 10-year-retention Buchungsbeleg) on 14 March of Year 1, books the corresponding entry the same day, and the invoice is fully paid and closed out with no further bookings against it.
The retention period begins at the END of the calendar year the LAST entry was made — 31 December of Year 1 — and runs for 10 FULL years from that point, expiring at the end of Year 11 (31 December). The invoice may therefore be destroyed FROM 1 January of Year 12 onward — nearly 11 full years after its original 14 March date, given the calendar-year rounding mechanic.
10

The GoBD — Tax Authority Guidance on Electronic Retention

Administrative guidance bridging commercial retention law and tax practice
The GoBD (Grundsätze zur ordnungsmäßigen Führung und Aufbewahrung von Büchern, Aufzeichnungen und Unterlagen in elektronischer Form sowie zum Datenzugriff) is administrative guidance issued by the German tax authorities elaborating in considerable technical detail on how the §257(3) electronic retention conditions must be satisfied in PRACTICE for tax purposes — covering matters like unalterable audit trails, indexing/searchability requirements, and data-access rights for tax auditors. This is yet another instance of the SAME commercial/tax linkage pattern first introduced via §140 AO in the earlier poster on §§238–241a — commercial retention law and tax administrative practice are never fully separable in the German system.
11

§258 — Court-Ordered Production in Litigation

A court may compel production, on its own motion or upon application
In an ordinary legal dispute, §258 allows a court to order a party's commercial books produced for inspection — either on the court's OWN initiative or upon a party's application — typically taking place at the location the books are actually kept, with the parties (or their authorised representatives) present, and relevant entries extracted for the court's own record.
12

§259 — the Proportionality Relief: Producing Only an Extract

A narrower alternative to producing the entire books
§259 provides that, where appropriate, only an EXTRACT of the relevant entries need be produced and communicated to the requesting party, rather than the entire set of books — a genuinely proportionality-oriented relief, protecting the confidentiality of unrelated commercial information while still satisfying the litigating party's legitimate evidentiary need.
13

§260 — Full Production in Inheritance, Property, and Dissolution Disputes

A narrower, but more thorough, category of disputes requiring the full books
§260 addresses a NARROWER category of disputes — inheritance division (Erbteilung), community-of-property disputes (Gütergemeinschaft), and partnership/company dissolution disputes (Gesellschaftsteilung) — where the court may order the FULL books produced (not merely an extract), given that in these specific dispute types, the parties' collective entitlement to a complete, unfiltered picture of the underlying assets and liabilities is considered to outweigh the confidentiality interest §259's extract-only relief otherwise protects.
14

Worked Example — Extract vs Full Production, Side by Side

The same underlying books, two different production obligations depending on the dispute type
Example 3: An ordinary contract dispute vs a partnership dissolution
Company A is involved in an ordinary commercial dispute with a supplier over a disputed invoice, and the supplier's counsel seeks production of Company A's books to verify certain entries. Separately, Company A's OWN two partners are dissolving their partnership and disagree about each partner's respective capital account balances.
In the SUPPLIER dispute, §258/§259 apply — the court may well limit production to an EXTRACT of the specific entries relevant to the disputed invoice, protecting the rest of Company A's books from unnecessary exposure. In the PARTNERSHIP dissolution, §260 applies instead — the FULL books must be produced to BOTH partners, given their joint entitlement to a complete picture of the partnership's affairs as they wind down their shared enterprise.
15

§261 — Production of Electronically Retained Documents

Making stored image/data records actually readable for production purposes
Where documents have been lawfully retained on image or data storage media under §257(3), and production is ordered under §§258–260, §261 requires the retaining party to produce READABLE reproductions of the stored records — at its OWN expense — or to provide the technical means necessary to make the records readable. Where requested, the retaining party must also furnish printouts or copies, again at its own expense.
16

Worked Example — Who Pays to Make Electronic Records Readable

The retaining party bears the cost of accessibility
Example 4: Producing scanned invoices for a court
A company retained its supplier invoices exclusively as scanned images, per §257(3), having since discarded the paper originals. A court orders production of specific invoices under §258 in an ongoing dispute.
The company must produce READABLE reproductions of the scanned invoices — and if the requesting party asks for physical printouts, the company must supply them — with the company itself bearing the COST of this production, per §261. The mere fact that the records exist only in electronic form does not shift this cost onto the requesting party.
17

§§262–263 — Repealed Provisions

No current content
Both §262 and §263 are currently marked "(weggefallen)" — repealed, with no substantive content remaining. Their original subject matter (from earlier versions of the Handelsgesetzbuch) has since been restructured elsewhere or removed entirely as German commercial law evolved.

Consequences of Premature Destruction

A direct link to the criminal-law exposure introduced in the first poster of this series
As flagged in the companion poster on §§238–241a, destroying, damaging, or concealing commercial books or records BEFORE the §257(4) retention period has expired can itself constitute the criminal offence of Bankrott under §283 StGB, INDEPENDENT of any underlying insolvency — the retention obligation this Titel establishes is not merely an administrative housekeeping matter, but one with genuine criminal-law teeth.
🚫 A company that discards records the moment it BELIEVES a dispute has concluded, without confirming the §257(4) period has genuinely expired, risks precisely this exposure — retention periods should be calculated conservatively, not assumed.

Relationship to IFRS — Another Structural Absence

Comparison — HGB §§257–263 vs IFRS
Exactly as with §§238–241a, IFRS has NO equivalent record-retention mandate whatsoever — retention periods, in an IFRS-only jurisdiction, are governed entirely by LOCAL company, tax, or evidence law, with IFRS itself remaining silent on how long underlying records must survive. This Titel is, once again, part of the LEGAL INFRASTRUCTURE layer HGB supplies beneath any accounting standard — IFRS included — rather than a topic IFRS itself ever addresses directly.
A GERMAN company preparing IFRS consolidated accounts (per §315e) STILL relies on §§257–263 to determine how long its underlying records must be kept — the two frameworks operate at entirely different LAYERS, not in competition with one another on this particular question.
