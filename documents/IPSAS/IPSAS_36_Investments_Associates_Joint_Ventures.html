1

Objective, Scope & Quantifiable Ownership Interests (paras. 1–5)

Objective & scope
Prescribes the accounting for investments in associates and joint ventures, and sets out the requirements for applying the EQUITY METHOD. Applies to all entities that are investors with SIGNIFICANT INFLUENCE over, or JOINT CONTROL of, an investee — WHERE the investment leads to a QUANTIFIABLE OWNERSHIP INTEREST.
A quantifiable ownership interest includes formal equity structures (share capital, property trust units) AND other reliably measurable interests (e.g. partnership interests) — but NOT where the equity structure is too poorly defined to measure reliably (para. 4).
Worked example — an "investment" that ISN'T an ownership interest
A public sector entity substantially funds a hospital OWNED and OPERATED by a charity
The contribution is non-exchange in nature — it lets the public entity participate in hospital operations, and the charity is accountable for public monies received.
This does NOT constitute an ownership interest — the charity could seek alternative funding and prevent the public entity from participating. The public entity is not exposed to the RISKS, nor does it enjoy the REWARDS, incidental to true ownership (para. 5).
2

Definitions (para. 8)

The three relationship types
Associate
An entity over which the investor has SIGNIFICANT INFLUENCE.
Joint arrangement
An arrangement of which two or more parties have JOINT CONTROL.
Joint control
The AGREED sharing of control by way of a binding arrangement — exists ONLY when decisions about relevant activities require UNANIMOUS consent of the controlling parties.
Joint venture
A joint arrangement where the parties with joint control have rights to the NET ASSETS of the arrangement.
The method, and the influence threshold
Equity method
Initial recognition at COST, adjusted thereafter for the investor's share of the investee's POST-ACQUISITION change in net assets/equity — the investor's surplus/deficit includes its share of the investee's surplus/deficit.
Significant influence
The POWER to participate in financial and operating policy DECISIONS of another entity — but NOT control or joint control of those policies.
3

Significant Influence — the 20% Presumption (paras. 10–15)

The rebuttable voting-power threshold (para. 11)
≥20% of voting power (direct or indirect) → PRESUMED significant influence, unless clearly demonstrated otherwise
<20% of voting power → PRESUMED NO significant influence, unless clearly demonstrated otherwise
A substantial or majority ownership by ANOTHER investor does NOT automatically preclude the entity from also having significant influence.
Evidence of significant influence (para. 12) & potential voting rights
Board (or equivalent) representation Participation in policy-making, including dividend/distribution decisions Material transactions with the investee Interchange of managerial personnel Provision of essential technical information
CURRENTLY exercisable/convertible potential voting rights (warrants, options, convertible instruments) — even those held by OTHER entities — are considered in the significant-influence assessment; management's INTENTIONS and financial ABILITY to exercise are explicitly excluded from that judgment (paras. 13–14). Significant influence can be LOST with or without any ownership change — e.g. an associate becomes subject to another government's control, a court, or an administrator (para. 15).
4

The Equity Method — Core Mechanics (paras. 16–21)

The running balance
Carrying amount = cost + cumulative share of post-acquisition surplus/deficit − distributions received ± share of other net-assets/equity changes
Investor's share of investee's surplus/deficit → investor's OWN surplus or deficit Distributions received → REDUCE the investment's carrying amount (never revenue) Share of investee net-assets/equity changes NOT run through surplus/deficit (e.g. PP&E revaluation, foreign exchange translation) → investor's OWN net assets/equity
Why NOT just recognize dividends as revenue?
Distributions received often bear LITTLE RELATION to the investee's actual performance. Since the investor has joint control or significant influence, it genuinely has an interest in the investee's performance — the equity method extends the investor's OWN financial statements to capture that interest, giving more informative reporting of both net assets/equity and surplus or deficit (para. 17).
Unless classified held-for-sale (IPSAS 44), the investment (or any retained portion) is classified as a NON-CURRENT asset (para. 21). IPSAS 41 does NOT apply to equity-method interests — except LONG-TERM interests that, in substance, extend the net investment (§10), which ARE subject to IPSAS 41 first (paras. 20, 20A).
5

Application & Exemptions from the Equity Method (paras. 22–25)

Default: equity method required — exemptions mirror IPSAS 35's
An entity with joint control or significant influence applies the equity method UNLESS exempt. Exemption applies where the entity is a controlling entity ALREADY exempt from consolidation (IPSAS 35 para. 5) — or, for a non-controlling entity, the SAME four conditions as IPSAS 35's consolidation exemption (informed/non-objecting owners; no public market trading; no securities filing; a compliant ultimate/intermediate controlling entity) (para. 23).
The fair value election for venture capital & similar vehicles
Investment held by/through a venture capital organization, mutual fund, unit trust, or investment-linked insurance fund → MAY elect fair value through surplus or deficit (IPSAS 41) instead of the equity method
Election made SEPARATELY for each associate/joint venture, at INITIAL recognition — an investment entity will, by definition, have made this election for all its investments (para. 24) Where only a PORTION is held via such a vehicle, that portion may be separately fair-valued while the REMAINDER stays on the equity method (para. 25) Where the portion is held via an INVESTMENT ENTITY specifically (not just any venture capital vehicle), fair value MUST be used for that portion — no election, it's mandatory (para. 25)
6

Classification as Held for Sale & Discontinuing the Equity Method (paras. 25A–27)

Held for sale — partial or full
Portion meeting IPSAS 44 held-for-sale criteria → apply IPSAS 44 to THAT portion; any RETAINED portion continues on the equity method until the held-for-sale portion is actually disposed of (para. 25A) After disposal → any retained interest goes to IPSAS 41, UNLESS it's still an associate/joint venture, in which case the equity method continues No longer meets held-for-sale criteria → equity method resumes RETROSPECTIVELY from the original classification date; prior-period statements are amended accordingly (para. 25B)
Discontinuing the equity method — three destinations (para. 26)
What it becomesTreatment
A controlled entityIPSAS 40 (public sector combination) + IPSAS 35 (consolidation)
A retained financial assetFair value under IPSAS 41 — that fair value becomes the deemed initial recognition value; gain/loss = (retained-interest fair value + disposal proceeds) − carrying amount at discontinuation, to surplus or deficit
Net assets/equity amounts previously recognizedSame basis as if the investee had directly disposed of the related assets/liabilities
Associate ↔ joint venture reclassification (still equity-accounted either way): the equity method CONTINUES uninterrupted — NO remeasurement of the retained interest (para. 27).
7

Changes in Ownership Interest — Reduced but Still Significant/Joint (para. 28)

Partial disposal WITHOUT losing the classification
Ownership interest reduced, but STILL classified as an associate or joint venture → transfer directly to accumulated surplus/deficit any proportion of gain/loss PREVIOUSLY recognized in net assets/equity relating to that reduction — but only if IPSAS would ALSO require that transfer on an outright disposal of the underlying assets/liabilities
This mirrors the "recycling" logic seen elsewhere in this library (e.g. revaluation surplus realized progressively or on disposal) — a partial reduction in the equity-accounted interest triggers a PROPORTIONATE recycling of any accumulated net-assets/equity amount attributable to that specific slice.

Equity Method Procedures — Upstream/Downstream Transactions & Uniform Policies (paras. 29–34B, 37–40)

Eliminating unrealized profit — but only to the unrelated-investor share
DirectionElimination rule
Upstream (associate/JV → investor)The investor's SHARE of the associate/JV's gain is eliminated
Downstream (investor → associate/JV, non-operation assets)Recognized only to the extent of UNRELATED investors' interests in the associate/JV
Downstream (assets constituting an "operation," per IPSAS 40)Recognized IN FULL (para. 34A) — a 2017 IPSAS 40 refinement
Impairment/net-realizable-value LOSSES are never eliminated this way — downstream losses recognized IN FULL by the investor; upstream losses recognized to the investor's SHARE (para. 32). Non-monetary contributions lacking commercial substance (IPSAS 45 sense) → gain/loss treated as UNREALIZED, eliminated against the investment itself, never shown as deferred gains/losses on the SOFP (para. 33).
Uniform policies, investment entities, and cumulative preference shares
Associate/JV policies CONFORMED to the investor's own for equity-method purposes — UNLESS the associate/JV is itself an INVESTMENT ENTITY, in which case its fair value measurement of ITS OWN controlled entities is RETAINED, not adjusted (paras. 37–39) Cumulative preference shares held by OTHER parties, classified as equity → investor's share of surplus/deficit computed AFTER adjusting for those dividends, WHETHER OR NOT declared (para. 40)
An economic entity's share = the AGGREGATE holding of the controlling entity and its controlled entities — other, unrelated associates'/JVs' holdings are ignored (para. 30).

Acquisition Accounting & Reporting Date Alignment (paras. 35–36)

The fair value difference on acquisition
Goodwill embedded within the investment's carrying amount → NEVER amortized (mirrors IPSAS 26's treatment for consolidated goodwill) Excess of the investor's share of NET FAIR VALUE over cost (a "bargain purchase") → included as REVENUE in the investor's share of the associate/JV's surplus or deficit, in the acquisition period Post-acquisition adjustments (e.g. depreciation based on acquisition-date fair values, or impairment of PP&E/goodwill) flow through the investor's share of surplus/deficit each period (para. 35)
Reporting date mismatches — the same two options as consolidation
Where the associate/JV's reporting date differs from the investor's, the investor EITHER obtains additional financial information as of its own date, OR uses the associate/JV's most recent statements adjusted for significant intervening transactions/events (para. 36) — precisely mirroring IPSAS 35's consolidation reporting-date rule (§9 of that Standard's poster).

The Deficit Floor — When Losses Exceed the Investment (paras. 41–42)

Stop recognizing losses at zero — with a broader "interest" definition
Investor's share of the investee's deficit ≥ its INTEREST in the associate/JV → STOP recognizing further deficit share
"Interest" here is BROADER than just the equity-method carrying amount — it includes any LONG-TERM interests that, in substance, extend the net investment (e.g. preference shares, long-term receivables/loans NOT expected to be settled in the foreseeable future) — but explicitly EXCLUDES ordinary trade receivables/payables, and secured long-term receivables with adequate collateral. Losses beyond the ordinary-share investment are absorbed against these other components in REVERSE seniority order (i.e., last-priority-on-liquidation items absorb losses first) (para. 41).
Below zero — legal/constructive obligations only, and resumption
Once the interest reaches ZERO, further deficits are provided for (a LIABILITY recognized) ONLY to the extent the entity has incurred LEGAL or CONSTRUCTIVE obligations, or made payments, on the associate/JV's behalf If the associate/JV LATER reports surpluses, the investor resumes recognizing its share only AFTER that share of surpluses equals the UNRECOGNIZED share of prior deficits (para. 42)

Impairment of the Net Investment (paras. 43–48)

Loss events — objective evidence required
Impairment exists ONLY where a loss event AFTER initial recognition has an impact on estimated future cash flows that can be RELIABLY ESTIMATED — losses expected from FUTURE events, however likely, are never recognized (para. 44A).
Significant financial difficulty of the associate/JV Breach of contract (default, payment delinquency) The investor granting a concession it wouldn't otherwise consider, for financial-difficulty reasons Probable bankruptcy or financial reorganization Disappearance of an active market DUE TO the associate/JV's financial difficulties
⚠️ NOT, by itself, evidence of impairment: a market disappearing merely because the instrument is no longer publicly traded; a credit rating downgrade; or a fair value decline — though any of these MAY count when considered alongside other information (para. 44B). A significant or PROLONGED fair value decline below cost IS objective evidence (para. 44C).
Which impairment Standard applies, and how value in use is estimated
Impairment indicated → apply IPSAS 26 (cash-generating), and POSSIBLY IPSAS 21 (non-cash-generating) as well (para. 45) Value in use for a cash-generating investment = EITHER the investor's share of the PV of the associate/JV's own future cash flows (including eventual disposal proceeds), OR the PV of expected distributions plus disposal proceeds — both methods give the SAME result under consistent assumptions (para. 46) Recoverable amount is assessed for EACH associate/JV individually, UNLESS it doesn't generate cash inflows largely independent of the entity's other assets (para. 48)

Separate Financial Statements & Transitional Provisions (paras. 49–50)

Separate statements — cross-referenced back to IPSAS 34
An investment in an associate or joint venture in the entity's OWN separate financial statements is accounted for per IPSAS 34 para. 12 — cost, IPSAS 41 fair value, or the equity method, applied consistently by category (para. 49).
Transition from proportionate consolidation — routed to IPSAS 37
The specific transitional mechanics for an entity moving FROM proportionate consolidation TO the equity method (or from the equity method to accounting for a joint OPERATION's assets/liabilities directly) are set out in IPSAS 37, not repeated here (para. 50) — reflecting the close structural relationship between these two Standards.

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Comparison with IAS 28

Comparison — IPSAS 36 vs IAS 28 (Amended 2011)
IPSAS 36 is drawn primarily from IAS 28 (Amended 2011), Investments in Associates and Joint Ventures, with amendments to 31 December 2014.
TopicDifference
Terminology"Net assets/equity," "economic entity," "controlling entity," "controlled entity," "revenue" (IPSAS 36) vs "equity," "group," "parent," "subsidiary," "income" (IAS 28)
Quantifiable ownership interest requirementIPSAS 36 applies ONLY where a quantifiable ownership interest exists (§1); IAS 28 has no equivalent explicit requirement, though it's unlikely equity accounting would apply without one anyway
Retained interest measurement when fair value is precludedIPSAS 36 permits using CARRYING AMOUNT as deemed cost when the applicable financial instruments Standard precludes fair value measurement; IAS 28 always requires fair value
Investment entity associate/JV fair value retentionIPSAS 36 REQUIRES retaining the investment entity's own fair value measurement (§8); IAS 28 only PERMITS it

Statement / Process Mapping

Decision map
1. Does the investment give a QUANTIFIABLE OWNERSHIP INTEREST? If no → outside scope entirely (§1) 2. Is there SIGNIFICANT INFLUENCE (≥20% voting power presumption) or JOINT CONTROL? → apply the equity method, unless exempt (§3, §5) 3. Is the investment held via a venture capital vehicle, mutual fund, or investment entity? → fair value election (or mandatory fair value) instead (§5) 4. Track the running carrying amount: cost + share of surplus/deficit − distributions ± other net-assets/equity share (§4) 5. Has the investor's share of losses REACHED the full interest? → stop recognizing further losses at zero, absorb only legal/constructive obligations beyond that (§10) 6. Is there OBJECTIVE EVIDENCE of impairment? → apply IPSAS 26 (and possibly IPSAS 21) to determine any impairment loss (§11) 7. Has the relationship CHANGED — control gained, held-for-sale, disposal, or reclassification between associate/JV? → route to the corresponding discontinuation/continuation mechanics (§6, §7)

Quick-Reference Glossary

Associate
An entity over which the investor has significant influence.
Significant influence
The power to participate in financial and operating policy decisions of another entity, without control or joint control.
Joint control
The agreed sharing of control by binding arrangement, existing only when relevant-activity decisions require unanimous consent.
Joint venture
A joint arrangement where the parties with joint control have rights to the net assets of the arrangement.
Equity method
Initial recognition at cost, adjusted for the investor's share of the investee's post-acquisition net assets/equity change.
Upstream / downstream transaction
Sales from the associate/JV to the investor (upstream), or from the investor to the associate/JV (downstream).
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOFPerf Statement of financial performance NA/E Net assets/equity (statement of changes)
