Conceptual Framework Linkage

Book-tax differences that will reverse — and cost or benefit the entity later
Financial reporting and tax law often measure the SAME transaction differently, creating TEMPORARY DIFFERENCES that will REVERSE in future periods — generating future tax consequences the entity must recognise TODAY. ASC 740 governs this recognition, using the same fundamental ASSET/LIABILITY (balance-sheet) approach IAS 12 uses.
Converged on the core method, sharply divergent on specific mechanics
ASC 740 and IAS 12 share the SAME foundational temporary-difference, asset/liability approach — but diverge meaningfully on deferred tax asset presentation mechanics (Section 4), and MOST significantly on the entire STRUCTURE used for uncertain tax positions (Sections 5–6), one of the most heavily tested comparative income tax topics.
2

Scope & Applicability

All income taxes, domestic and foreign
ASC 740 applies to the accounting for INCOME TAXES — both domestic and foreign — for all entities subject to income tax, addressing current tax expense, deferred tax assets/liabilities, valuation allowances, uncertain tax positions, and intraperiod allocation.
3

Temporary Differences — the Asset/Liability Method

Converged core method
Deferred tax assets and liabilities are recognised for TEMPORARY DIFFERENCES between the financial reporting carrying amount and the TAX BASIS of assets and liabilities, using ENACTED (Section 9) tax rates expected to apply when the differences REVERSE — the SAME balance-sheet/asset-liability approach IAS 12 requires, a genuinely converged core methodology.
4

Deferred Tax Asset Recognition — the Valuation Allowance Approach

A gross-with-contra-account mechanic, vs IAS 12's net approach
US GAAP recognises the FULL GROSS deferred tax asset, then reduces it via a separate VALUATION ALLOWANCE if it is MORE LIKELY THAN NOT (over 50%) that some or all of the DTA will NOT be realised — a TWO-STEP, gross-asset-plus-contra-account presentation. IAS 12 instead uses a NET, ONE-STEP approach: a deferred tax asset is recognised ONLY TO THE EXTENT it is PROBABLE that sufficient future taxable profit will be available to utilise it — there is NO separate "valuation allowance" contra-account concept; the DTA is simply recognised DIRECTLY at its net realisable amount. The underlying RECOGNITION THRESHOLD ("more likely than not" vs "probable") is quite SIMILARLY worded and substantively close — the genuine difference here is largely a PRESENTATION/BOOKKEEPING mechanic (gross-with-contra vs net-direct), though it does affect how movements are disclosed and tracked period to period.
5

Uncertain Tax Positions — the Two-Step Framework

A highly specific, distinctly US GAAP structure
ASC 740's uncertain tax position guidance (originating from the former FIN 48 interpretation) uses a DISTINCTIVE TWO-STEP framework: Step 1 — RECOGNITION: a tax benefit is recognised ONLY if it is MORE LIKELY THAN NOT (based on the position's TECHNICAL MERITS) that the position will be SUSTAINED upon examination by the tax authority. Step 2 — MEASUREMENT: if recognition is warranted, the benefit is measured as the LARGEST amount that is GREATER THAN 50% LIKELY to be realised upon ULTIMATE SETTLEMENT — a CUMULATIVE PROBABILITY assessment across a range of possible outcomes, essentially finding the largest amount for which the cumulative probability of realization exceeds 50%.
6

IFRIC 23's Contrasting Measurement-Only Approach

No separate recognition threshold — straight to measurement
IFRS addresses uncertain tax treatments through IFRIC 23 (Uncertainty over Income Tax Treatments) — which does NOT impose a separate, distinct RECOGNITION threshold the way ASC 740's "more likely than not" Step 1 does. Instead, IFRIC 23 goes STRAIGHT to MEASUREMENT: an entity uses EITHER the SINGLE MOST LIKELY AMOUNT, OR an EXPECTED VALUE (probability-weighted sum of possible outcomes), WHICHEVER method BETTER PREDICTS the resolution of the uncertainty — essentially assuming the tax authority will examine the position with full knowledge of all relevant information, and measuring accordingly. This is a genuinely DIFFERENT STRUCTURAL approach: US GAAP's explicit two-gate (recognise, THEN measure) process versus IFRS's single measurement-focused step, without an equivalent separate recognition threshold — one of the most heavily tested income tax structural differences.
7

The Indefinite Reversal Exception

Broadly converged in substance, differently worded
US GAAP permits NOT recognising a deferred tax liability on undistributed earnings of a foreign subsidiary (or other outside basis differences) if the parent has demonstrated both the ABILITY and the INTENTION to indefinitely REINVEST those earnings rather than repatriate them (narrowed in practical significance, though not eliminated, by the 2017 US tax reform's mandatory repatriation transition tax and GILTI provisions). IAS 12 has a BROADLY SIMILAR exception for outside basis differences related to subsidiaries/branches/associates/joint ventures, where the parent controls the timing of reversal and it is PROBABLE the difference will NOT reverse in the FORESEEABLE FUTURE — genuinely converged in substance, even though drafted with somewhat different specific conditions and terminology.
8

Intraperiod Tax Allocation

Allocating total tax expense across the statement
Total income tax expense/benefit for the period is ALLOCATED among continuing operations, discontinued operations, OCI items, and direct equity adjustments — using a specific "WITH-AND-WITHOUT" incremental approach for items outside continuing operations. IAS 12 addresses a broadly similar allocation issue (splitting tax between profit or loss, OCI, and equity), with SOMEWHAT different specific mechanics for certain backwards-tracing exceptions — broadly converged conceptually, differing in some mechanical detail.
9

Enacted vs Substantively Enacted Tax Rates

A genuine, practically significant timing difference
US GAAP requires using ENACTED tax rates — tax law that has been SIGNED into law. IAS 12 permits using rates that have been ENACTED OR SUBSTANTIVELY ENACTED (e.g. a rate that has passed all necessary legislative steps except purely FORMAL/ceremonial final steps, common in parliamentary systems with royal assent or similar procedures). This means IFRS can sometimes reflect an ANTICIPATED rate change EARLIER than US GAAP, where a jurisdiction's legislative process has multiple stages — a genuine, PRACTICALLY significant difference in TIMING for measuring deferred tax balances around a rate change.

Worked Example — Valuation Allowance vs IAS 12 Net DTA Recognition

Same net result, different bookkeeping presentation
Example: A DTA where only 60% is expected to be realised
An entity has a gross deferred tax asset of $5,000,000 related to net operating loss carryforwards. Management concludes it is more likely than not (probable) that only $3,000,000 of this asset will ultimately be realised.
Under US GAAP: Debit Deferred tax asset $5,000,000 (gross) / Credit Valuation allowance $2,000,000 / Credit [other] $3,000,000 — presenting the GROSS $5,000,000 asset and a SEPARATE $2,000,000 contra-account, netting to $3,000,000.
Under IAS 12: the entity would simply recognise a deferred tax asset of $3,000,000 DIRECTLY — the SAME net $3,000,000 economic outcome, but with NO gross asset or separate valuation allowance ever appearing in the accounting records.

Worked Example — the Uncertain Tax Position Two-Step Approach

Applying the cumulative-probability measurement test
Example: A tax position with a range of possible sustained amounts
An entity takes a $1,000,000 tax deduction. Management concludes it is MORE LIKELY THAN NOT the position will be sustained upon examination (Step 1 passed). Assessing the possible SETTLEMENT outcomes: a 55% cumulative probability of sustaining the full $1,000,000, an additional 25% cumulative probability (80% total) of sustaining at least $700,000, and a 15% cumulative probability (95% total) of sustaining at least $400,000.
Applying Step 2 — find the LARGEST amount with a CUMULATIVE probability exceeding 50%: the $1,000,000 amount alone already has a 55% cumulative probability, which EXCEEDS 50% — so the entity recognises the FULL $1,000,000 tax benefit, since even the largest, least-conservative outcome clears the 50% cumulative threshold on its own.
12

Worked Example — Enacted vs Substantively Enacted Rate Difference

A pending rate change awaiting only formal assent
Example: A tax rate reduction passed but not yet formally signed
A jurisdiction's legislature has passed a bill reducing the corporate tax rate from 25% to 21%, with only a purely ceremonial final head-of-state assent step remaining (a formality that has never historically been withheld) before the year-end reporting date. That final assent occurs shortly AFTER year end.
Under US GAAP: since the rate change is NOT yet formally ENACTED at year end, deferred taxes are measured using the OLD 25% rate.
Under IAS 12: the rate change may be considered SUBSTANTIVELY ENACTED at year end (having passed all substantive legislative steps), permitting deferred taxes to be measured using the NEW 21% rate ALREADY at year end — producing a genuinely different measurement of the SAME deferred tax balances for the identical facts.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Temporary difference
A difference between the financial reporting carrying amount and tax basis of an asset or liability that will result in taxable or deductible amounts in future years.
Substantively enacted (IFRS term)
A tax rate that has passed all substantive legislative steps except purely formal/ceremonial final steps, permitting its use under IAS 12 even before formal enactment.
