1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Solving the over-disclosure problem for group subsidiaries
IFRS 19 addresses a widely-felt COST-BENEFIT problem: subsidiaries within a group that ALREADY reports under full IFRS often had to maintain a SEPARATE set of disclosures (or a second set of accounting records entirely, if using local GAAP or the IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard) purely to satisfy statutory filing requirements — even though users of a WHOLLY-OWNED, non-publicly-accountable subsidiary's financial statements have MUCH narrower information needs than users of a publicly listed parent.
HOW IFRS 19 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Cost constraint on useful financial reporting — full IFRS disclosure volume is DISPROPORTIONATE to the information needs of a non-publicly-accountable subsidiary's limited user baseComparability preserved — IFRS 19 changes DISCLOSURE only, never RECOGNITION or MEASUREMENT — eligible entities apply the SAME accounting policies as full IFRS, ensuring group reporting consistency
IFRS 19 is a DISCLOSURE-ONLY standard, working ALONGSIDE (not instead of) all other IFRS Accounting Standards — it never changes HOW an item is recognised or measured, only WHAT must be disclosed about it.
The core mechanism
Eligible entity applies: FULL recognition/measurement/presentation (other IFRS Standards) + REDUCED disclosure (IFRS 19, replacing other standards' own disclosure sections)
ℹ️ An eligible entity's disclosure requirements come EXCLUSIVELY from IFRS 19, not from the disclosure sections of the other standards it otherwise fully applies.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRS 19 covers
IFRS 19 specifies the reduced disclosure requirements an ELIGIBLE entity may apply INSTEAD of the disclosure requirements in other IFRS Accounting Standards — a VOLUNTARY election, effective for periods beginning on/after 1 January 2027, with early application permitted.
Key definitions you must know
Public accountability
Having debt/equity instruments traded in a public market, OR holding assets in a FIDUCIARY capacity for a broad group of outsiders (e.g. banks, insurers) — an entity WITHOUT either characteristic may be eligible.
Intermediate parent
A subsidiary that is itself also a parent of further subsidiaries — assesses its OWN eligibility separately in its separate financial statements.
3

Eligibility Criteria (with worked example)

Two conditions, assessed at the reporting date
Example 1: A subsidiary's eligibility assessment
A company is a wholly-owned subsidiary of a listed parent, which prepares CONSOLIDATED financial statements complying with IFRS Accounting Standards. The subsidiary itself has no listed debt/equity and is not a bank, insurer, or similar fiduciary institution.
This subsidiary MEETS both IFRS 19 eligibility criteria: (1) it does NOT have public accountability, and (2) its PARENT produces IFRS-compliant consolidated financial statements — making it ELIGIBLE to elect IFRS 19's reduced disclosure package in its own (consolidated, separate, or individual) financial statements.
4

What "Not Have Public Accountability" Means

A deliberately narrow exclusion
Entities applying IAS 26 (retirement benefit plans) do NOT meet the "not have public accountability" criterion and are therefore NOT eligible for IFRS 19. Similarly, entities with LISTED debt/equity, or those holding assets in a fiduciary capacity for a BROAD group of outsiders (banks, insurance companies, securities brokers), are EXCLUDED — the standard is deliberately targeted at subsidiaries whose financial statements serve a NARROW, largely group-internal user base.
5

What IFRS 19 Actually Does — Disclosure Only

Never touches recognition or measurement
An entity electing IFRS 19 continues to apply the FULL recognition, measurement, and presentation requirements of every OTHER applicable IFRS Accounting Standard — the ONLY change is that its DISCLOSURE requirements come from IFRS 19 instead of from each individual standard's own (fuller) disclosure section.
6

The Reduced Disclosure Package

A comprehensive but genuinely lighter disclosure set
Of the 34 IFRS Accounting Standards that include disclosure requirements, IFRS 19 provides a REDUCED version for 30 of them — using the IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard's own disclosure requirements as a STARTING POINT (with appropriate omissions/changes reflecting subsidiary-specific information needs), while retaining the FULL recognition/measurement basis of full IFRS rather than the IFRS for SMEs' own simplified recognition/measurement model.
7

Standards Requiring Full Disclosure — No Reduction Permitted

Three notable exceptions
The disclosure requirements for THREE standards must STILL be applied IN FULL, even by an eligible entity electing IFRS 19: IFRS 8 (Operating Segments), IFRS 17 (Insurance Contracts), and IAS 33 (Earnings per Share) — reflecting the IASB's judgement that these disclosures remain essential even for a non-publicly-accountable subsidiary's more limited user base, or (for IFRS 17) that insurance-specific complexity warrants full transparency regardless.
8

Why This Standard Matters for Groups

A genuinely significant compliance-cost reduction opportunity
MANY companies globally are subsidiaries of groups reporting under full IFRS, and currently must EITHER prepare a full second set of IFRS-compliant financial statements for group consolidation purposes, OR maintain two separate sets of accounting records (one for local statutory filing, one for group reporting). IFRS 19 removes the need to EITHER over-disclose beyond users' needs OR maintain two separate records, making it a genuinely material simplification for large multinational groups with numerous subsidiaries.

An EXPLICIT, UNRESERVED statement of compliance with IFRS Accounting Standards, specifically noting IFRS 19's adoptionReduced disclosures per IFRS 19 paragraphs 21-276, replacing (not supplementing) the disclosure sections of other standardsFull disclosure for IFRS 8, IFRS 17, and IAS 33 (no reduction available)

Quick-reference glossary
IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard
A separate, simplified IFRS-based standard with its own reduced recognition/measurement AND disclosure — distinct from IFRS 19, which retains FULL recognition/measurement.
Fiduciary capacity
Holding assets for a broad group of outsiders as a primary business (e.g. banking, insurance) — disqualifying an entity from IFRS 19 eligibility.
