Conceptual Framework Linkage – a Tax Status, Not a Distinct GAAP Model

REIT status is defined by tax law, not accounting standards
"REIT" status is FUNDAMENTALLY a TAX concept – governed by Internal Revenue Code Subchapter M requirements (e.g. a 75% real estate ASSET test, a 90% taxable income DISTRIBUTION requirement) – rather than a distinct GAAP MEASUREMENT model. A REIT's underlying real estate investments continue to be accounted for under GENERAL real estate principles (ASC 970, ASC 360), with ASC 974 addressing only a FEW genuinely REIT-specific accounting matters: organisational/offering costs and property acquisition costs.
2

Scope

Entities electing REIT tax status
ASC 974 applies to entities that HAVE elected (or intend to elect) REIT tax status, addressing the LIMITED set of accounting matters genuinely distinctive to this structure.
3

Organizational and Offering Costs

Netted against offering proceeds, similar to general stock issuance costs
Costs of a REIT's SECURITIES OFFERING (underwriting fees, legal, printing costs directly related to the offering) are treated CONSISTENTLY with general stock issuance cost principles applicable to ANY entity – CHARGED AGAINST the GROSS PROCEEDS of the offering (i.e. NETTED against additional paid-in capital), rather than expensed through the income statement. This is NOT a genuinely unique REIT rule – it simply confirms that REITs follow the SAME general offering cost treatment as other equity-issuing entities.
4

Property Acquisition Costs

Governed by general ASC 805/360 principles
Costs of ACQUIRING real estate properties are accounted for under GENERAL business combination (ASC 805, if the acquisition constitutes a business) or asset acquisition principles – with TRANSACTION costs generally EXPENSED as incurred for business combinations, or CAPITALISED as part of the asset's cost for asset acquisitions that don't meet the definition of a business – the SAME distinction and treatment ANY entity would apply, not a REIT-specific rule.
5

Funds From Operations (FFO) – a Non-GAAP Supplemental Metric

A widely used industry metric, defined by NAREIT – NOT itself a GAAP-mandated measure
"FUNDS FROM OPERATIONS" (FFO) – GENERALLY defined (by the National Association of Real Estate Investment Trusts, NAREIT) as GAAP net income, EXCLUDING gains/losses from property SALES and ADDING BACK real estate DEPRECIATION/AMORTISATION – is a WIDELY used supplemental performance metric in REIT industry practice and investor communications, reflecting the view that GAAP depreciation may not fully capture real estate's actual economic value trends (since well-maintained real estate often APPRECIATES rather than declining in value the way GAAP depreciation assumes). FFO is NOT itself a GAAP measure MANDATED by ASC 974 – it is a widely adopted, INDUSTRY-STANDARD non-GAAP supplemental disclosure, typically presented ALONGSIDE (and reconciled to) GAAP net income, not as a substitute for it.
6

Worked Example – Offering Cost Treatment

Netted against proceeds, not expensed
Example: A REIT's public offering
A REIT raises $200,000,000 gross proceeds in a public equity offering, incurring $9,000,000 of underwriting fees and other direct offering costs.
The REIT records $200,000,000 − $9,000,000 = $191,000,000 as the NET increase to additional paid-in capital – the $9,000,000 of offering costs are NETTED directly against the gross proceeds, NOT recognised as an expense in the income statement.
7

Worked Example – an FFO Reconciliation From Net Income

A common supplemental disclosure, reconciled to GAAP net income
Example: Reconciling GAAP net income to FFO
A REIT reports GAAP net income of $40,000,000, which includes a $12,000,000 gain on the sale of a property and $28,000,000 of real estate depreciation and amortisation expense for the period.
FFO = $40,000,000 (net income) − $12,000,000 (property sale gain, excluded) + $28,000,000 (depreciation/amortisation, added back) = $56,000,000 – presented as a SUPPLEMENTAL, NON-GAAP metric ALONGSIDE the $40,000,000 GAAP net income figure, with a clear reconciliation between the two.

No Dedicated IFRS REIT Standard – Discussion

REIT status is a legal/tax concept without a precise accounting-standard equivalent
IFRS has NO dedicated REIT standard – much like ASC 974 itself is a fairly THIN Topic, since "REIT" is fundamentally a TAX/LEGAL status rather than a distinct accounting model. Entities operating similarly under IFRS apply IAS 40 (Investment Property) – which offers its OWN notable POLICY CHOICE between the COST model and the FAIR VALUE model for investment property, a genuinely different, more consequential measurement choice than anything specific to REITs under US GAAP (where investment property remains at historical cost less depreciation under ASC 360, WITHOUT an equivalent fair value model option). Many countries have their OWN REIT-like tax regimes, but these are NATIONAL tax law constructs, not IFRS accounting requirements.
