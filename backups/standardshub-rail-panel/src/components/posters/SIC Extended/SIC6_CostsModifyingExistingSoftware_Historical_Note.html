SIC-6 — Costs of Modifying Existing Software
Historical reference note · Issued 1998 · Withdrawn 2004
⚠ WITHDRAWN — Superseded by revisions to IAS 16 and IAS 38, effective for periods beginning 1 January 2005
What SIC-6 addressed
SIC-6 addressed whether costs of MODIFYING existing computer software — as distinct from developing entirely new software — should be EXPENSED or CAPITALISED, and how such modification costs should be distinguished from routine maintenance.
The conclusion it reached
Costs of modifying software should be treated in the SAME way as costs of DEVELOPING new software — expenditure that enhances or extends the software's performance BEYOND its original specification could be CAPITALISED, subject to meeting the general internally generated intangible asset recognition criteria in IAS 38 (or IAS 16, where the software formed an integral part of related hardware).
Where this guidance lives today
Superseded byRevisions to IAS 16 Property, Plant and Equipment and IAS 38 Intangible Assets (2004)
Why withdrawnClarified guidance on software costs, including modifications, was incorporated directly into the main standards, making a standalone interpretation unnecessary
Current statusThe underlying principle remains good law today — it simply now sits in IAS 16/IAS 38 (and is echoed in SIC-32 for website-specific software) rather than in a separate SIC
