1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Which exchange rate reflects the real transaction?
IAS 21 requires foreign currency transactions to be translated using the exchange rate at the "date of the transaction." When consideration is paid or received IN ADVANCE of the related revenue, expense, or asset being recognised, it is not obvious whether "the transaction" occurs at the advance payment date or the later recognition date – and the two dates can carry very different exchange rates. IFRIC 22 resolves this ambiguity directly.
Locking in the rate that reflects the real economic commitment
Once an advance payment is made or received, the entity's exposure to further exchange rate movement on that specific amount is effectively FIXED – the prepayment or deferred income becomes a non-monetary item. IFRIC 22's conclusion that the transaction date is the advance payment/receipt date faithfully reflects this reality: no further currency risk arises on the item after that point.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRIC 22 covers
IFRIC 22 applies where an entity pays or receives consideration in advance, denominated in a foreign currency, and recognises a NON-MONETARY prepayment ASSET or deferred income LIABILITY before recognising the related item of revenue, expense, or non-monetary asset – clarifying which exchange rate to apply to translate that related item.
TYPICAL FACT PATTERNS
Advance payments to a foreign supplier for goods to be delivered laterDeposits received from foreign customers ahead of delivering goods or servicesDeposits paid toward the purchase of foreign property, plant, or equipment
Key definitions
Date of the transaction
Per IAS 21, the date on which a transaction first qualifies for recognition, used to determine the exchange rate for initial translation – the specific concept IFRIC 22 clarifies for advance consideration.
Advance consideration
A payment made, or receipt received, before the related revenue, expense, or non-monetary asset is recognised, resulting in a non-monetary prepayment or deferred income item.
3

The Question IFRIC 22 Answers

A precise, narrow application question
When advance foreign currency consideration is paid or received, and a related non-monetary asset (prepayment) or non-monetary liability (deferred income) is recognised, at what date should the "date of the transaction" be determined for translating the EVENTUAL revenue, expense, or asset that the advance consideration relates to – the date of the ADVANCE payment/receipt, or the (later) date the revenue/expense/asset is actually recognised?
4

The Core Conclusion – Date of Initial Recognition

The exchange rate is locked in at the advance date
The date of the transaction, for the purpose of determining the exchange rate to use, is the date of INITIAL RECOGNITION of the non-monetary prepayment asset (or deferred income liability) arising from the advance consideration – NOT the later date the related revenue, expense, or non-monetary asset is ultimately recognised. This effectively "locks in" the exchange rate applicable at the advance payment/receipt date for the related item.
5

Multiple Advance Payments or Receipts

Each instalment gets its own transaction date
If there are MULTIPLE payments or receipts in advance of the related item's recognition, the entity determines the date of the transaction for EACH payment or receipt SEPARATELY. This means a single contract settled through several advance instalments can result in the eventual revenue or expense being translated using SEVERAL DIFFERENT exchange rates, one for each instalment's own transaction date.
6

Why the Prepayment Is Non-Monetary

The classification that drives the whole conclusion
A prepayment for goods or services (or deferred income received for the same) is a NON-MONETARY item under IAS 21 – it will be settled by the delivery of goods, services, or a non-monetary asset, NOT by a fixed or determinable amount of cash. Because non-monetary items carried at historical cost are NOT subsequently retranslated for exchange rate changes, the rate applicable at INITIAL recognition of the prepayment is the rate that continues to apply when the related revenue/expense is finally recognised.

Worked Example – Single Advance Payment

Advance payment for inventory, delivered later
Example: Full advance payment, single delivery date
A USD-functional-currency entity pays a European supplier €1,000,000 in advance for goods, when the exchange rate is $1.10/€1.00 (prepayment recognised at $1,100,000). The goods are delivered two months later, when the spot rate has moved to $1.15/€1.00.
The inventory is recognised at $1,100,000 – using the exchange rate at the date the PREPAYMENT was initially recognised, NOT the $1,150,000 that a delivery-date rate would imply. The prepayment (a non-monetary asset) is simply reclassified to inventory at its existing carrying amount; no further exchange gain or loss arises on this item.
8

Worked Example – Instalment Advances

Blended rate across multiple instalments
Example: Two advance instalments at different rates
The same USD entity instead pays the €1,000,000 in two equal €500,000 instalments: the first when the rate is $1.10/€1.00, the second one month later when the rate is $1.20/€1.00. Delivery occurs after both payments.
Instalment 1: €500,000 × $1.10 = $550,000. Instalment 2: €500,000 × $1.20 = $600,000. The inventory is recognised at the SUM of the two transaction-date amounts: $1,150,000 – a blended rate determined by the two SEPARATE transaction dates, per Section 5, rather than a single rate applied to the whole €1,000,000.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Identifying precisely when a non-monetary prepayment asset or deferred income liability is first recognised, for entities with complex or staged payment termsDetermining whether multiple payments genuinely represent SEPARATE advance consideration events, each needing its own transaction date
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Generally minimal – the exchange rate is a directly observable spot rate at each advance payment/receipt date, rather than an estimated figure

IFRIC 22 vs US GAAP

Comparison – IFRIC 22 vs US GAAP (ASC 830)
FeatureIFRS (IFRIC 22)US GAAP (ASC 830)
Transaction date for advance considerationExplicitly the date of initial recognition of the prepayment/deferred income (locked-in rate)Generally reaches a SIMILAR outcome in practice – the non-monetary prepayment is not remeasured for subsequent FX changes – though without a single, equally explicit codified interpretation dedicated to this exact question
Multiple advance paymentsEach instalment gets its own transaction date, explicitly requiredSimilarly expected in practice under the general non-monetary-item translation principle, though less explicitly codified than under IFRIC 22
The practical outcome is largely converged between the two frameworks – both treat the prepayment as a non-monetary item locked in at its own historical rate – with IFRIC 22's main contribution being to remove residual diversity in IFRS practice by making the principle explicit and mandatory.

Quick-reference glossary
Monetary item
Cash and items to be received or paid in fixed or determinable amounts of money – RETRANSLATED at each reporting date under IAS 21, unlike the non-monetary prepayments IFRIC 22 addresses.
Non-monetary item
An item, such as a prepayment for goods, that will be settled by delivery of goods, services, or another non-monetary asset rather than a fixed amount of cash – NOT retranslated after initial recognition.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Spot exchange rate
The exchange rate for immediate delivery, used under IAS 21 to translate a foreign currency transaction on initial recognition – the rate IFRIC 22 anchors to the advance consideration date.
Deferred income
A non-monetary liability recognised when consideration is received in advance of the entity satisfying its performance obligation, the mirror-image counterpart to a prepayment asset.
