Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why IFRS 6 is deliberately an "interim" standard
Exploration and evaluation (E&E) expenditure sits in a uniquely uncertain position under the Conceptual Framework — at the point of spending, it is often genuinely UNCLEAR whether a future economic benefit (a commercially viable mineral deposit) will ever materialise. Rather than resolving this uncertainty with a single, fully worked-out set of rules, the IASB deliberately issued IFRS 6 as a NARROW, transitional standard permitting entities to LARGELY CONTINUE their existing accounting policies.
HOW IFRS 6 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Recognition uncertainty — E&E expenditure often cannot be reliably assessed against the Framework's normal asset recognition criteria (probable future benefit) until FAR more work/data is available — IFRS 6 acknowledges this by NOT imposing a single, prescriptive policy Cost constraint on standard-setting — the IASB explicitly chose NOT to develop a comprehensive extractive industries standard immediately, instead allowing existing diverse national practices to continue while research continued A modified impairment trigger — recognising that E&E assets' value is unusually hard to assess, IFRS 6 provides SPECIFIC impairment indicators tailored to the extractive industry, rather than relying solely on IAS 36's general indicators
IFRS 6 is UNIQUE among IFRS Standards in explicitly PRESERVING existing accounting policy diversity rather than harmonising it — a deliberate, narrow, "first step" standard the IASB always intended to revisit with more comprehensive extractive industry guidance in the future.
Measurement principles applied by IFRS 6
IFRS 6 sets a MINIMUM initial measurement requirement (cost), then largely defers to the entity's own existing accounting policy for everything else.
StageIFRS 6's treatment
Initial recognitionAt COST — a firm requirement
Subsequent measurementEntity develops its OWN accounting policy — cost model or revaluation model, applied consistently
ℹ️ Qualitative characteristics in action: allowing continued POLICY DIVERSITY for subsequent measurement trades comparability for PRACTICAL cost/reliability constraints — an explicit, deliberate compromise unique to this standard.

Scope & Applicability

What IFRS 6 covers
IFRS 6 applies to EXPLORATION AND EVALUATION EXPENDITURES an entity incurs — specifying the financial reporting for the exploration for, and evaluation of, mineral resources.
WHAT IS NOT COVERED
Expenditures incurred BEFORE the exploration for and evaluation of mineral resources (e.g. expenditures before the entity has obtained the LEGAL RIGHTS to explore a specific area) — these are accounted for under the Conceptual Framework and other applicable Standards, NOT IFRS 6 Expenditures incurred AFTER the technical feasibility and commercial viability of extracting a mineral resource are DEMONSTRABLE — these fall outside IFRS 6's scope and are accounted for under normal Standards (e.g. IAS 16, IAS 38)
IFRS 6's scope is DELIBERATELY narrow — a specific "window" of activity between obtaining exploration rights and demonstrating technical feasibility/commercial viability, EXCLUDING both the earlier pre-rights stage and the later development stage.
Key definitions you must know
Exploration and evaluation expenditures
Expenditures incurred in connection with the exploration for and evaluation of mineral resources, BEFORE the technical feasibility and commercial viability of extracting the mineral resource are demonstrable.
Exploration and evaluation assets
Exploration and evaluation expenditures recognised as assets in accordance with an entity's own accounting policy.
Mineral resources
Minerals, oil, natural gas, and similar non-regenerative resources, AFTER the entity has obtained legal rights to explore a specific area.

Recognition — A Deliberately Permissive Standard

The unique freedom IFRS 6 grants — with worked example
IFRS 6 permits an entity to DEVELOP an accounting policy for E&E assets WITHOUT specifically considering the requirements of paragraphs 11 and 12 of IAS 8 (which would otherwise require reference to the Conceptual Framework and similar IFRS requirements) — a deliberate, EXPLICIT carve-out from IAS 8's normal policy-selection hierarchy.
Example 1: Continuing a "successful efforts" policy under IFRS 6
An oil and gas entity previously used a "successful efforts" method under its local GAAP, capitalising only exploration costs for wells that turn out to be commercially successful, expensing costs for "dry holes" immediately. On IFRS adoption, the entity wishes to CONTINUE this same policy.
IFRS 6 EXPLICITLY permits this — the entity can continue applying its EXISTING accounting policy for E&E assets, without needing to justify it against the Conceptual Framework or other IFRS Standards in the way IAS 8 would normally require for a policy with no directly applicable Standard.
⚠️ This IAS 8 carve-out is UNIQUE to IFRS 6 — no other IFRS Standard grants this degree of continued flexibility, reflecting the IASB's deliberate decision to defer full harmonisation of extractive industry accounting to a later, more comprehensive project.

Measurement — Initial Cost & Accounting Policy Choice

Initial measurement at cost
E&E assets are measured at COST at initial recognition — typically comprising costs of acquiring exploration rights, topographical/geological/geochemical/geophysical studies, exploratory drilling, trenching, sampling, and activities related to evaluating technical feasibility/commercial viability
An entity's accounting policy determines which of these expenditures are RECOGNISED as E&E assets, and which are expensed — provided the resulting allocation is REASONABLE, given the circumstances.
Subsequent measurement — cost or revaluation model
After recognition, an entity applies EITHER the COST MODEL or the REVALUATION MODEL to E&E assets — consistent with IAS 16's or IAS 38's own measurement models, depending on whether the E&E asset is classified as tangible or intangible (see Section 7).
The choice of cost vs revaluation model, once made, is a genuine accounting policy applied consistently — the standard's flexibility applies most significantly to WHAT gets capitalised in the first place, not merely how it's subsequently measured.
5

Reclassification of E&E Assets

Exiting IFRS 6's scope once feasibility is demonstrated
An E&E asset shall NO LONGER be classified as such once the TECHNICAL FEASIBILITY and COMMERCIAL VIABILITY of extracting the mineral resource are DEMONSTRABLE. E&E assets shall be ASSESSED FOR IMPAIRMENT (Section 6) BEFORE reclassification, and any impairment loss recognised BEFORE the reclassified asset moves into its new category, governed by the normal applicable Standard (typically IAS 16 for tangible development assets, or IAS 38 for intangible ones).
This is a CRITICAL sequencing rule — the mandatory pre-reclassification impairment test ensures E&E assets are not carried forward at an inflated amount into the development-stage accounting regime, which follows entirely different (and typically stricter) rules.
6

Impairment — A Modified Trigger (with worked example)

IFRS 6's own indicators, replacing some of IAS 36's general ones
IFRS 6 MODIFIES the application of IAS 36 for E&E assets by specifying facts and circumstances that indicate E&E assets should be TESTED for impairment — an entity assesses E&E assets for impairment when facts/circumstances suggest the carrying amount may EXCEED recoverable amount. IFRS 6 also permits the level at which E&E assets are assessed for impairment (i.e. the CGU or group of CGUs) to be LARGER than the CGU normally required under IAS 36.
EXAMPLE IMPAIRMENT INDICATORS SPECIFIC TO IFRS 6
The period for which the entity has the right to explore in the specific area has EXPIRED, or will expire in the NEAR FUTURE, and is NOT expected to be renewed Substantive expenditure on further exploration/evaluation in the specific area is NEITHER budgeted nor planned Exploration/evaluation activities have NOT led to the discovery of commercially viable quantities, and the entity has decided to DISCONTINUE such activities in the specific area Sufficient data exists to indicate that, although a development in the specific area is likely to proceed, the carrying amount is UNLIKELY to be recovered in full from successful development or by sale
Example 2: An expiring exploration licence
An entity holds an E&E asset of $3,500,000 relating to a specific licence area. The exploration licence is due to expire in 4 months, and management has confirmed it will NOT be renewed, since geological surveys have not indicated a commercially viable deposit.
This is a CLEAR IFRS 6-specific impairment indicator (licence expiring imminently, not to be renewed) — the entity must ASSESS the E&E asset for impairment under IAS 36's recoverable amount test. Given no commercially viable deposit and no ongoing rights, recoverable amount is likely close to NIL, and a full write-off would typically be recognised.

Presentation — Tangible vs Intangible Classification

Classifying E&E assets by their NATURE
An entity classifies E&E assets as TANGIBLE or INTANGIBLE according to the NATURE of the assets acquired, applying this classification CONSISTENTLY. Some E&E assets are treated as intangible (e.g. drilling rights, licences), while others are treated as tangible (e.g. vehicles, drilling rigs used in exploration). Where a tangible asset is CONSUMED in developing an intangible asset, the amount reflecting that consumption is part of the cost of the INTANGIBLE asset — but this does NOT change the tangible asset's own classification.
Consistent classification is required across an entity's WHOLE E&E asset base, but the underlying assets themselves retain their natural tangible/intangible character; the classification exercise simply DETERMINES which subsequent measurement standard (IAS 16 or IAS 38) applies once the asset leaves IFRS 6's scope.

Interaction with Other Standards

A deliberately narrow, standalone standard
Because IFRS 6 grants such significant policy flexibility for RECOGNITION, but is silent on many other detailed matters, an entity's E&E accounting policy typically DRAWS ON established principles from IAS 16/IAS 38 for classification and subsequent measurement mechanics, while relying on IFRS 6's own specific rules for the initial recognition scope and the modified impairment trigger described above.

Disclosure Requirements

Required disclosures
GENERAL DISCLOSURES
Its accounting policies for exploration and evaluation expenditures, including the recognition of E&E assets Amounts recognised in its financial statements arising from the exploration for and evaluation of mineral resources (assets, liabilities, income and expense, operating and investing cash flows)
TREATED AS A SEPARATE CLASS
E&E assets are treated as a separate class of assets, and the ordinary disclosures required by IAS 16 or IAS 38 (as applicable) are provided for this separate class, consistent with the entity's tangible/intangible classification choice

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Which expenditures qualify for capitalisation as E&E assets, per the entity's own developed accounting policy Whether technical feasibility and commercial viability have become "demonstrable," triggering reclassification out of IFRS 6's scope Whether tangible or intangible classification is appropriate for each type of E&E asset
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Recoverable amount of E&E assets when an IFRS 6-specific impairment indicator arises The appropriate level (CGU or larger grouping) at which E&E assets are tested for impairment
⚠️ Because IFRS 6 permits genuine policy diversity, COMPARABILITY between different extractive entities can be significantly LIMITED — two companies with economically similar exploration activities may report materially different E&E asset balances, purely due to differing (both entirely IFRS 6-compliant) capitalisation policies.

IFRS vs US GAAP — Key Differences

Comparison — IFRS 6 vs US GAAP
FeatureIFRS (IFRS 6)US GAAP
Accounting policy flexibilityDeliberately BROAD — permits continuation of virtually any pre-existing E&E accounting policyMore PRESCRIPTIVE — US GAAP specifies detailed methods (e.g. "successful efforts" or "full cost" method) for oil and gas specifically, under ASC 932
IAS 8 policy hierarchy overrideExplicitly EXEMPTED from the normal IAS 8 policy-selection hierarchyNo comparable concept, since US GAAP is itself the single source of authoritative guidance with its own specific extractive industry rules
Impairment testing levelMay use a LARGER grouping than the normal CGUSpecific full-cost ceiling test or successful-efforts impairment rules apply, differing in mechanics from IAS 36
ComprehensivenessNarrow, transitional, deliberately incomplete standardMore comprehensive, detailed industry-specific US GAAP guidance (ASC 932) covering oil and gas activities specifically
IFRS 6 remains, by DESIGN, a narrower and less prescriptive standard than the equivalent detailed US GAAP extractive industries guidance — reflecting the IASB's original intention to revisit this area with more comprehensive standard-setting at a later stage, which (as of this poster's preparation) has not yet been completed.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Successful efforts method
An accounting policy capitalising exploration costs only for wells/projects that prove commercially successful, expensing costs for unsuccessful ("dry hole") efforts immediately.
Full cost method
An accounting policy capitalising ALL exploration costs within a defined cost centre (e.g. a country or region), regardless of individual well/project success, subject to a ceiling test.
Technical feasibility
Confirmation that a mineral resource can genuinely be extracted using currently available technology and methods.
Commercial viability
Confirmation that extracting a mineral resource is expected to be economically profitable, given estimated reserves, costs, and market prices.
Specific area
A geographical area over which an entity has legal rights to explore, the basis on which many IFRS 6 impairment indicators are assessed.
Development phase
The stage following demonstrated technical feasibility and commercial viability, at which point expenditures fall OUTSIDE IFRS 6's scope and are accounted for under IAS 16/IAS 38.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position (balance sheet) SOPL Statement of profit or loss (income statement) OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
