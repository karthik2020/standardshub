Conceptual Framework Linkage

A deliberate simplification favouring a single measurement basis
Before ASU 2016-01, equity securities classified as AFS were measured at fair value with changes in OCI – the FASB concluded this produced information LESS useful than routing all equity fair value changes directly through NET INCOME, on the basis that equity investment performance is a genuine part of an entity's current-period economic results, not a deferred item. ASC 321 reflects that conclusion: essentially ALL equity securities with a readily determinable fair value now flow through net income, full stop.
The Topic that most starkly separates from IFRS 9
ASC 321 is the primary SOURCE of the single most consequential OCI-related divergence between US GAAP and IFRS covered elsewhere in this library (in the ASC 220 poster) – IFRS 9's equity FVOCI election has NO US GAAP counterpart at all, making this Topic worth understanding in its own right as the origin of that difference.
2

Scope & Applicability

Equity securities with limited exceptions
ASC 321 applies to investments in EQUITY SECURITIES – instruments representing an ownership interest in an entity, such as common and preferred stock – EXCEPT for investments accounted for under the EQUITY METHOD (ASC 323, where the investor has significant influence) or CONSOLIDATED subsidiaries (where the investor has control), both of which fall outside ASC 321's scope and are addressed by their own dedicated Topics instead.
3

Why ASU 2016-01 Eliminated AFS-Equity Treatment

The FASB's stated rationale
The FASB concluded that DEFERRING unrealised equity security gains/losses in OCI – as the old AFS-equity model did – provided LESS decision-useful information than reflecting those changes directly in current-period net income, since an equity investment's fair value movement is a genuine element of the investor's economic performance for the period, not merely a timing difference awaiting eventual realisation the way a debt instrument's interest-rate-driven fair value movement often is.
4

General Measurement – Fair Value Through Net Income

The default (and essentially only) rule
Equity securities with a READILY DETERMINABLE fair value (e.g. actively traded on an exchange) are measured at FAIR VALUE, with ALL changes in fair value – both unrealised holding gains/losses during the period AND realised gains/losses on sale – recognised in NET INCOME. There is NO OCI classification election available, unlike debt securities under ASC 320's AFS category.
5

No OCI Option, at All

A firm, unqualified rule
Unlike IFRS 9, which permits an IRREVOCABLE election to present fair value changes on certain equity instruments in OCI (with NO subsequent recycling to profit or loss even on disposal), ASC 321 offers NO SUCH ELECTION for any equity security with a readily determinable fair value – the through-net-income treatment is mandatory, not optional, and applies uniformly regardless of the entity's investment horizon or strategic intent.
6

The Measurement Alternative – Securities Without Readily Determinable Fair Value

A practical expedient for non-marketable equity investments
For equity securities WITHOUT a readily determinable fair value (typically investments in private, non-publicly-traded companies), ASU 2016-01 introduced the MEASUREMENT ALTERNATIVE: such investments are measured at COST, MINUS impairment, PLUS OR MINUS changes resulting from OBSERVABLE PRICE CHANGES in orderly transactions for the IDENTICAL or a SIMILAR investment of the SAME issuer – a practical expedient avoiding the cost and difficulty of a full fair value measurement for genuinely illiquid, non-marketable stakes.
7

Observable Price Change Adjustments Under the Alternative

Triggered by a specific, identifiable transaction
If the entity identifies an OBSERVABLE transaction for the same or a similar security of the same issuer (e.g. a new funding round for a private company at a different implied valuation), the carrying amount under the measurement alternative is ADJUSTED to reflect that observable price, with the resulting adjustment recognised in NET INCOME – consistent with the general "through net income" philosophy applied throughout ASC 321.
8

Impairment Under the Measurement Alternative

A qualitative assessment each period
An entity applying the measurement alternative performs a QUALITATIVE assessment each reporting period to identify indicators of impairment. If a qualitative assessment indicates impairment IS likely, the entity estimates the security's fair value and, if that fair value is LESS than carrying amount, recognises an impairment loss in NET INCOME for the difference – a simplified, indicator-driven approach rather than the full fair value remeasurement other securities are subject to.
9

Worked Example – Fair Value Change Through Net Income

A publicly traded equity investment
Example: Marketable equity securities remeasured at year end
An entity holds publicly traded shares purchased for $2,000,000. At year end, the fair value of the holding is $2,300,000.
The $300,000 unrealised gain is recognised ENTIRELY in NET INCOME for the period: Debit Equity investment $300,000 / Credit Unrealised gain on equity securities (net income) $300,000 – NO portion goes through OCI, regardless of the entity's investment intent or holding period.
10

Worked Example – the Measurement Alternative

A private company stake, adjusted by a new funding round
Example: A minority stake in a private start-up
An entity holds a minority equity stake in a private company, originally purchased for $500,000, with no readily determinable fair value. Eighteen months later, the private company completes a new funding round at a valuation implying the entity's stake is now worth $750,000 – an observable transaction for a similar (same-class) security of the same issuer.
The entity ADJUSTS the carrying amount from $500,000 to $750,000, recognising the $250,000 increase in NET INCOME – applying the measurement alternative's observable-price-change mechanic, without needing to perform a full independent fair value valuation.

ASC 321 vs IFRS 9 – the FVOCI Election Divergence

Comparison – ASC 321 vs IFRS 9's equity instrument treatment
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 321)IFRS 9
Default measurementFair value through NET INCOME, mandatoryFair value through profit or loss (FVTPL), the default
OCI election available?NEVER – no such election existsYES – an IRREVOCABLE election for equity instruments not held for trading, deferring gains/losses in OCI
Recycling on disposal, if FVOCI electedNot applicableNEVER recycled to profit or loss, even on sale – a genuinely "permanent OCI" outcome under IFRS 9
Non-marketable equity practical expedientThe measurement alternative (cost + observable price changes)No directly equivalent named alternative – IFRS 9 still generally requires fair value estimation even for unquoted equity, though with practical guidance on when cost may approximate fair value
⚠️ This remains one of the most consequential earnings-volatility differences between the two frameworks – a US GAAP preparer holding a long-term strategic equity stake will see 100% of its fair value swings hit net income every period, while an IFRS preparer holding an economically identical stake CAN elect to shield those swings from profit or loss entirely, via the FVOCI election with no recycling.

Quick-reference glossary preview
Readily determinable fair value
A fair value that can be reliably established, typically from an active market quote – the threshold determining whether an equity security uses the fair value model or the measurement alternative.
Measurement alternative
A practical expedient measuring non-marketable equity securities at cost, adjusted for impairment and observable price changes, rather than requiring a full fair value estimate.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Observable price change
A transaction price for an identical or similar security of the same issuer, used to adjust the carrying amount of an equity investment measured under the measurement alternative.
Equity security
An instrument representing an ownership interest in an entity, such as common or preferred stock, distinct from a debt security representing a creditor relationship.
