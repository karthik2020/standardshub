Conceptual Framework Linkage

Extractive costs pose a genuinely different capitalization question
Mining involves substantial up-front EXPLORATION spending long before it's known whether a commercially viable ore body even exists, followed by ongoing STRIPPING (waste removal) costs during production that provide improved future access to ore. ASC 930 addresses when these COSTS should be CAPITALISED as assets versus expensed, and how stripping costs specifically should be treated once production has begun.
IFRS 6 is a deliberately unusual, narrow standard
IFRS 6 (Exploration for and Evaluation of Mineral Resources) is famous for its deliberately NARROW, PERMISSIVE approach – rather than mandating a specific accounting policy, it largely allows entities to CONTINUE applying their EXISTING accounting policies for exploration and evaluation costs. ASC 930 takes a more CONVENTIONAL approach, requiring costs to meet normal asset recognition criteria (Section 4).
2

Scope

Mining exploration, development, and production entities
ASC 930 applies to entities engaged in the exploration, development, and production of MINERALS (metals, coal, industrial minerals) – excluding oil and gas, which is separately addressed by ASC 932.
3

Exploration and Evaluation Costs – Capitalization Criteria

Capitalised where directly attributable to a specific mineral property with probable future benefit
Costs DIRECTLY attributable to FINDING and EVALUATING specific mineral properties – geological surveys, exploratory drilling, sample analysis – are CAPITALISED as an asset if it is PROBABLE they will provide FUTURE ECONOMIC BENEFIT (i.e. lead to an economically viable ore body); costs incurred before a specific property is identified, or where NO future benefit is probable, are EXPENSED as incurred. This reflects normal GAAP asset-recognition principles, applied contextually to the mining exploration setting.
4

THE KEY DIVERGENCE – IFRS 6's Unusual "Grandfather Existing Policy" Approach

A deliberately non-prescriptive standard, unlike anything on the US GAAP side
IFRS 6 takes a genuinely UNUSUAL regulatory approach: rather than PRESCRIBING a specific method for exploration and evaluation (E&E) cost accounting, it largely permits an entity to CONTINUE applying whatever accounting policy it was ALREADY using immediately before adopting IFRS 6 – effectively "GRANDFATHERING" pre-existing diverse practice, subject only to some baseline consistency and impairment-indicator requirements. This was a deliberate, TEMPORARY compromise by the IASB, reflecting the difficulty of achieving international consensus on E&E accounting given widely varying pre-existing national practices. ASC 930 has NO equivalent "grandfather your existing policy" mechanism – US GAAP requires E&E costs to meet NORMAL asset recognition criteria (Section 3) consistently, without the same policy-continuation allowance IFRS 6 provides. This is a genuinely unusual, STRUCTURAL divergence – not a difference in specific rules, but a difference in whether a codified, CONSISTENT capitalisation policy is required AT ALL.
5

Stripping Costs During the Production Phase

Capitalised as a "stripping activity asset" where improved access provides future benefit
STRIPPING costs (removing waste/overburden material to access ore) incurred DURING the production phase are CAPITALISED if they create a PROBABLE future economic benefit in the form of IMPROVED ACCESS to the ore body – amortised over the RESERVES benefited by the specific stripping campaign, rather than expensed as a period production cost. This closely mirrors IFRIC 20's OWN "stripping activity asset" concept (also requiring capitalisation of stripping costs providing improved access, to the extent the benefit is realised as inventory) – a genuinely CONVERGED area, since IFRIC 20 was informed by pre-existing industry practice similar to that already used under US GAAP.
6

Asset Retirement Obligations (Reclamation)

Governed by the general ASC 410 framework
Legal obligations to RECLAIM mined land (restore the site to a required post-mining condition) are recognised as ASSET RETIREMENT OBLIGATIONS under the GENERAL ASC 410 framework – recognised at fair value when the obligation is incurred, with a corresponding increase to the related asset's carrying amount, accreted over time and depreciated alongside the asset.
7

Impairment of Mining Assets

Normal long-lived asset impairment principles apply
Capitalised mining assets (including stripping activity assets and E&E costs) are tested for impairment under the GENERAL long-lived asset impairment framework (ASC 360) – a two-step test comparing UNDISCOUNTED expected future cash flows to carrying amount, then measuring any impairment loss based on FAIR VALUE if the undiscounted test fails, with NO industry-specific formulaic "ceiling test" of the kind used in oil and gas accounting (ASC 932).
8

Worked Example – Stripping Cost Capitalization

Capitalising costs that improve future access
Example: An open-pit mine's deep stripping campaign
A mining company incurs $12,000,000 of stripping costs to access a NEW, deeper ore zone that will be mined over the following 4 years, well beyond the immediate benefit of current-period ore extraction.
Since the stripping activity provides IMPROVED ACCESS to a SPECIFIC, IDENTIFIABLE future ore zone (rather than merely sustaining current-period production), the $12,000,000 is CAPITALISED as a stripping activity asset, and AMORTISED over the 4-year period the newly accessed ore zone is expected to be mined – rather than expensed immediately as a current production cost.
9

Worked Example – Exploration Cost Capitalization

A specific property with probable future benefit
Example: Drilling costs on an identified claim
A company incurs $3,000,000 of exploratory drilling costs on a SPECIFIC, staked mineral claim, with preliminary results indicating a probable economically viable deposit.
Since the costs are DIRECTLY attributable to a SPECIFIC, identified property, and future economic benefit is deemed PROBABLE based on preliminary results, the $3,000,000 is CAPITALISED as an exploration and evaluation asset – to be further evaluated for eventual reclassification to development/production assets, or for impairment if subsequent results prove unfavourable.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Stripping activity asset
An asset capitalised for production-phase waste removal costs that provide probable improved future access to an identifiable ore body.
Exploration and evaluation (E&E) costs
Costs of finding and evaluating specific mineral properties, capitalised when future economic benefit is probable.
Reclamation
The legal obligation to restore mined land to a required post-mining condition, accounted for as an asset retirement obligation.
