Conceptual Framework Linkage

A genuinely new reporting entity emerges from bankruptcy
When a company emerges from Chapter 11 bankruptcy reorganization having genuinely SHED most of its old liabilities and OWNERSHIP has substantially changed hands, ASC 852 treats the emerging entity — under specific conditions — as effectively a NEW reporting entity: assets and liabilities are RESTATED to FAIR VALUE (much like a business combination), and the ACCUMULATED DEFICIT is RESET to zero. This "FRESH-START REPORTING" mechanism gives financial statement users a clean, forward-looking starting point, rather than carrying forward historical costs and an accumulated deficit built up under a capital structure that no longer exists.
2

Scope — Chapter 11 Reorganizations

A genuinely US-specific legal context
ASC 852 applies to entities undergoing reorganization under CHAPTER 11 of the US BANKRUPTCY CODE — a specific US legal proceeding allowing a financially distressed entity to restructure its debts and operations while continuing as a going concern, typically under a court-confirmed PLAN of reorganization.
3

The Two-Part Fresh-Start Eligibility Test

BOTH conditions must be met
TestRequirement
1The REORGANIZATION VALUE of the entity's assets IMMEDIATELY BEFORE confirmation of the plan is LESS THAN the total of post-petition liabilities and allowed claims — i.e. the entity was genuinely insolvent on a going-concern reorganization-value basis
2Holders of EXISTING VOTING SHARES immediately before plan confirmation receive LESS THAN 50% of the VOTING SHARES of the EMERGING entity — i.e. genuine, substantial change of CONTROL/OWNERSHIP has occurred
BOTH conditions must be satisfied for FRESH-START reporting to apply — if either condition fails (e.g. the old shareholders retain majority control of the emerged entity), the entity continues its PRE-EXISTING basis of accounting instead.
4

Fresh-Start Reporting Mechanics — Revaluation to Fair Value

A clean slate, valued as if newly created
When fresh-start reporting applies, the emerging entity's ASSETS and LIABILITIES are RESTATED to their FAIR VALUES as of the fresh-start reporting date, and the ACCUMULATED DEFICIT is RESET TO ZERO — the entity's financial statements essentially begin again, as if it were a genuinely NEW reporting entity, even though it is legally a continuation of the same company.
5

Reorganization Value and Its Allocation

Similar mechanics to a business combination purchase price allocation
The entity's total REORGANIZATION VALUE (broadly, its going-concern fair value immediately after restructuring) is ALLOCATED to individual assets and liabilities using an approach analogous to ASC 805's business combination purchase price allocation — identifiable assets and liabilities are recorded at their individual fair values, with any EXCESS reorganization value over identifiable net assets recognised similarly to GOODWILL.
6

Financial Statement Presentation — Predecessor vs Successor

A "black-line" divider between two distinct reporting periods
Financial statements are presented with a clear BLACK-LINE division between the "PREDECESSOR" company (the entity's financial statements up to and including the fresh-start date) and the "SUCCESSOR" company (the entity's financial statements from the fresh-start date forward) — reflecting that these are, in a genuine accounting sense, TWO DIFFERENT REPORTING ENTITIES, even though they represent the same continuing legal enterprise and operations.
7

Worked Example — the Fresh-Start Eligibility Test

Applying both conditions
Example: A manufacturer emerging from Chapter 11
A manufacturer's reorganization value immediately before plan confirmation is determined to be $80,000,000, while its post-petition liabilities and allowed claims total $120,000,000. Under the confirmed plan, former shareholders receive only 5% of the new voting shares, with creditors receiving the remaining 95% in exchange for their claims.
Test 1: Reorganization value ($80,000,000) < total liabilities/claims ($120,000,000) → MET. Test 2: Former shareholders retain only 5% of voting shares, well under 50% → MET. Since BOTH conditions are satisfied, the entity QUALIFIES for fresh-start reporting upon emergence.
8

Worked Example — Fresh-Start Balance Sheet Adjustment

Resetting the balance sheet
Example: Restating assets and eliminating the accumulated deficit
Immediately before fresh-start, the entity's historical-cost net assets are $50,000,000, with an accumulated deficit of $200,000,000. Its fresh-start reorganization value, allocated to identifiable assets and liabilities at fair value, totals $80,000,000.
Assets and liabilities are REVALUED to their individual fair values (aggregating to the $80,000,000 reorganization value, with any excess treated similarly to goodwill). The PRE-EXISTING $200,000,000 accumulated deficit is ELIMINATED (reset to zero) — the SUCCESSOR entity begins its life with a clean retained earnings balance of $0, even though the underlying operating business and workforce continue unchanged.

No Direct IFRS Equivalent — Discussion

A genuinely US-specific Topic, tied to a specific national legal framework
IFRS does NOT have a DEDICATED standard equivalent to ASC 852's fresh-start reporting framework — there is no direct IAS/IFRS counterpart, since the concept is fundamentally tied to CHAPTER 11 of the US BANKRUPTCY CODE, a specific national legal proceeding without a precise structural analogue in most other jurisdictions' insolvency regimes. An IFRS reporter undergoing an analogous restructuring might, in rare circumstances, apply general IFRS principles (e.g. derecognition of extinguished liabilities, fair value remeasurement where otherwise required) to achieve SOME similar economic outcomes — but WITHOUT a single, dedicated, codified "fresh-start" framework paralleling ASC 852's specific two-part eligibility test and predecessor/successor presentation convention. This makes ASC 852 one of the narrower Topics in this library with genuinely NO direct standard-level IFRS counterpart, similar in that respect to ASC 808 (Collaborative Arrangements).

Quick-Reference Glossary

Fresh-start reporting
A method of accounting in which an entity emerging from Chapter 11 restates its assets and liabilities to fair value and resets its accumulated deficit to zero, upon meeting specific eligibility conditions.
Reorganization value
The fair value of an entity's assets immediately before plan confirmation, generally equivalent to the amount a willing buyer would pay for the reorganized entity's assets immediately after restructuring.
Predecessor / successor company
The presentation convention distinguishing an entity's financial statements before (predecessor) and after (successor) the fresh-start reporting date.
