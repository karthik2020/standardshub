Conceptual Framework Linkage

Identical adjusting/non-adjusting distinction to IAS 10
Ind AS 10 applies the IDENTICAL principle as IAS 10 – events after the reporting period are classified as ADJUSTING (providing evidence of conditions existing AT the reporting date) or NON-ADJUSTING (indicative of conditions arising AFTER the reporting date).
HOW IND AS 10 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Faithful representation – only conditions existing at the reporting date should adjust the recognised amounts – identical to IAS 10Timeliness vs relevance trade-off – significant non-adjusting events are disclosed rather than recognised, balancing timely reporting with relevance – identical to IAS 10
Ind AS 10 introduced NO substantive change to this framework relative to IAS 10 – its Indian practical relevance concerns the specific timeline of board meeting approval and AGM processes under the Companies Act, 2013.
The dividend treatment – identical to IAS 10
Dividends declared AFTER the reporting date = NON-ADJUSTING event → NOT recognised as a liability at the reporting date
ℹ️ IDENTICAL to IAS 10 – a genuinely important rule, as many jurisdictions' PRIOR local GAAP frameworks (including old Indian GAAP) required dividend liability recognition at the reporting date, a practice Ind AS 10/IAS 10 both explicitly REJECT.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 10 covers
Ind AS 10 applies to the accounting/disclosure of events between the reporting date and the date financial statements are AUTHORISED FOR ISSUE – IDENTICAL scope to IAS 10.
Key definitions you must know
Adjusting event
IDENTICAL definition to IAS 10 – provides evidence of conditions existing at the reporting date.
Date of authorisation for issue
The date the board of directors (or equivalent) approves financial statements for issue – a key reference date, identical concept to IAS 10.
3

Adjusting Events (with worked example)

Identical examples/treatment to IAS 10
Example 1: Customer insolvency confirming a receivable impairment
An Indian company's major customer, already showing signs of financial distress before the March 31 year-end, is declared insolvent by the NCLT (National Company Law Tribunal) in May, before the financial statements are authorised for issue in June.
This is an ADJUSTING event – the insolvency confirms a condition (customer's deteriorating financial position) that EXISTED at the reporting date, requiring the receivable to be WRITTEN DOWN in the March 31 financial statements – an IDENTICAL treatment to how IAS 10 would classify the same fact pattern globally.
4

Non-Adjusting Events

Identical treatment to IAS 10
Events indicative of conditions arising AFTER the reporting date (e.g. a major acquisition, a natural disaster affecting facilities, significant share price declines) are NOT recognised but DISCLOSED if material – IDENTICAL to IAS 10.
5

Dividends Declared After the Reporting Period

Identical to IAS 10
Dividends proposed/declared AFTER the reporting date (even if recommended by the board before authorisation for issue) are NOT recognised as a liability at the reporting date – disclosed instead in the notes – IDENTICAL to IAS 10.
6

Going Concern Assessment

Identical to IAS 10
If management determines AFTER the reporting date that it intends to liquidate the entity or cease trading, or has no realistic alternative, financial statements are NOT prepared on a going concern basis – IDENTICAL to IAS 10, this determination itself functions as an ADJUSTING event regardless of when the intention arose.

Indian Board Meeting & AGM Timeline Context (with worked example)

A practical Indian corporate governance timing consideration
Under the Companies Act, 2013, Indian companies typically hold a board meeting to APPROVE financial statements before their AGM (Annual General Meeting), with dividends often RECOMMENDED by the board but only formally DECLARED upon shareholder approval at the AGM – occurring well AFTER the reporting date and even after the date of board approval. This reinforces why Ind AS 10's non-adjusting dividend treatment is particularly significant in the Indian corporate governance context.
Example 2: Board-recommended dividend timing
An Indian listed company's board RECOMMENDS a final dividend of ₹5 per share on May 20 (after the March 31 year-end) when approving financial statements for issue, with shareholder approval expected at the AGM in August.
The recommended dividend is NOT recognised as a liability in the March 31 financial statements – it is a NON-ADJUSTING event, disclosed in the notes, with formal declaration only occurring at the AGM – an IDENTICAL treatment to how IAS 10 would classify a comparable board-recommended dividend globally, reflecting the Indian corporate governance sequence.
8

Date of Authorisation for Issue

Identical to IAS 10
The date of authorisation for issue is typically the date of BOARD APPROVAL (not the AGM date) – IDENTICAL concept to IAS 10, marking the cut-off point for identifying subsequent events requiring evaluation.
