Conceptual Framework Linkage (Historical)

Identical percentage-of-completion philosophy to IAS 11
Ind AS 11 applied the IDENTICAL principle as IAS 11 – construction contract revenue/costs were recognised by reference to the STAGE OF COMPLETION of contract activity at the reporting date, when outcome could be estimated reliably.
HOW IND AS 11 MAPPED TO THE FRAMEWORK (HISTORICAL)
Relevance – recognising revenue as construction activity progresses, rather than only at final completion – identical rationale to IAS 11Matching – costs matched against revenue recognised in the same period – identical to IAS 11
Both Ind AS 11 and IAS 11 were REPLACED by their respective single revenue standards (Ind AS 115 / IFRS 15), which introduced a more rigorous CONTROL-based over-time recognition test in place of this dedicated construction contracts standard.
Now superseded by Ind AS 115's over-time recognition test
🚫 Ind AS 11 is NO LONGER APPLICABLE. Construction/EPC contract revenue is now recognised under Ind AS 115's five-step model, using its over-time vs point-in-time criteria (see the Ind AS 115 poster, Section 6).

Scope & Applicability (Historical)

What Ind AS 11 covered
Ind AS 11 applied to contracts specifically negotiated for the construction of an asset or combination of assets – IDENTICAL historical scope to IAS 11.
Genuinely significant historically for Indian EPC, infrastructure, and real estate companies.
Key definitions (historical)
Percentage-of-completion method
IDENTICAL definition to IAS 11 – recognising revenue/costs by reference to stage of completion.
Fixed price contract
A contract type historically covered by Ind AS 11, now assessed under Ind AS 115.

The Percentage-of-Completion Method (Historical)

Identical to IAS 11
Stage of completion was determined by methods such as the proportion of costs incurred to date over estimated total costs, surveys of work performed, or physical completion of contract work – IDENTICAL to IAS 11.
4

Contract Revenue & Costs (Historical, with worked example)

Identical measurement to IAS 11
Example 1: Historical percentage-of-completion calculation
An Indian EPC contractor's highway project had a total contract value of ₹100 crore, with ₹40 crore of costs incurred against total estimated costs of ₹80 crore.
Under the OLD Ind AS 11 methodology, stage of completion was 50% (₹40cr ÷ ₹80cr), so ₹50 crore of revenue (50% × ₹100cr) would have been recognised – an IDENTICAL calculation methodology to how IAS 11 was historically applied globally. This methodology is NO LONGER USED – Ind AS 115's over-time criteria now govern this determination.
5

Recognition of Expected Losses (Historical)

Identical to IAS 11
The ENTIRE expected loss on a contract was recognised IMMEDIATELY once foreseeable, regardless of stage of completion – IDENTICAL to IAS 11's own prudent, immediate loss recognition principle.
6

Combining & Segmenting Contracts (Historical)

Identical to IAS 11
Criteria for combining a group of contracts as one, or segmenting a single contract into separately identifiable assets, were IDENTICAL to IAS 11 – a concept that has evolved into Ind AS 115's own performance obligation identification.
7

Transition Impact for Indian EPC/Real Estate Companies

A genuinely important historical Indian transition event
India's LARGE EPC/infrastructure and real estate development sectors experienced a SIGNIFICANT transition impact moving from Ind AS 11's percentage-of-completion approach to Ind AS 115's stricter control-based over-time criteria – particularly for real estate developers, where the shift from an assumed "continuous transfer" model to Ind AS 115's more rigorous three-criteria test (see Ind AS 115 poster, Section 6) sometimes shifted revenue recognition from OVER TIME to POINT IN TIME (at possession/registration), materially altering reported revenue timing for many Indian developers.
8

Why It Was Withdrawn

Convergence with the single global revenue standard
Ind AS 11 (like IAS 11) was withdrawn as part of the broader move to a SINGLE, principles-based revenue recognition standard (Ind AS 115/IFRS 15) applicable across ALL industries, replacing the prior patchwork of industry/contract-type-specific standards.
