1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Warning users about obligations that don't yet meet the liability definition
Many contractual arrangements – such as a long-term purchase commitment – do NOT yet meet the definition of a LIABILITY, because no PAST TRANSACTION or event has occurred that obligates the entity to transfer resources (performance under the contract hasn't started). ASC 440 exists purely to ensure users are still INFORMED about these arrangements through disclosure, even though nothing is recognised on the balance sheet.
2

Scope – Disclosure Only, No Recognition

A pure disclosure Topic
ASC 440 contains NO recognition or measurement guidance whatsoever – it is entirely a DISCLOSURE Topic, requiring entities to describe UNUSED, UNRECOGNISED commitments that could materially affect their financial position, even though no liability is recognised for them.
3

What Counts as a "Commitment"

Contractual arrangements not yet meeting the liability definition
A "commitment" under ASC 440 is a contractual arrangement obligating the entity to a FUTURE transaction – most commonly UNCONDITIONAL PURCHASE OBLIGATIONS (e.g. a long-term agreement to purchase a fixed quantity of raw materials over several years), or other significant contractual arrangements not otherwise recognised as liabilities because performance under the contract has not yet occurred.
4

Unrecognized Purchase Obligations

The most common practical application
A common example: an entity signs a 10-year "take-or-pay" contract obligating it to purchase a minimum quantity of natural gas each year, regardless of whether it actually uses that quantity. Until each year's minimum purchase obligation is actually TRIGGERED, no liability exists – but the TOTAL future commitment is disclosed under ASC 440 so users understand the entity's exposure.
5

Why These Stay Off-Balance-Sheet

No past event has yet created a present obligation
A commitment differs from a genuine liability because the underlying EXECUTORY contract has not yet been performed by either party – the entity has not yet received the goods/services it has committed to purchase, and the counterparty has not yet delivered them. Since neither side has performed, no PAST event has yet created the PRESENT obligation a liability requires – consistent with the same Conceptual-Framework-level liability definition ASC 405 and IAS 37 both apply.
6

Worked Example – Disclosing an Unconditional Purchase Obligation

A multi-year raw material commitment
Example: A 5-year take-or-pay supply contract
An entity signs a 5-year contract requiring it to purchase a minimum of $2,000,000 of a raw material each year, regardless of actual usage, with no right to cancel.
NO liability is recognised on the Balance Sheet at signing – no goods have yet been delivered. Instead, the entity DISCLOSES the total future minimum purchase commitment ($10,000,000 over 5 years, with a schedule by year) in the notes, so users understand this significant future cash outflow obligation even though it isn't reflected as a recognised liability.

8

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where judgement matters most
The main judgement is determining whether a contractual arrangement is SIGNIFICANT enough to warrant disclosure, and whether it has genuinely not yet crossed the threshold into a recognised liability (as opposed to, for instance, an onerous contract that might require separate loss recognition under other guidance).
9

ASC 440 vs IFRS – Dispersed Across IAS 1/IAS 37

Comparison – ASC 440 vs IFRS equivalents
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 440)IFRS equivalent(s)
Dedicated commitments disclosure TopicYes – a standalone TopicNo single equivalent – commitment disclosures are addressed through general IAS 1 disclosure principles and, where a contract has become ONEROUS, IAS 37's onerous contract provisions
Onerous contract recognitionGenerally NOT addressed by this Topic (a liability would arise separately if the contract meets other recognition criteria)IAS 37 explicitly requires recognising a PROVISION for onerous contracts, where the unavoidable costs of meeting the obligations exceed the expected economic benefits
IAS 37's explicit onerous contract provision requirement is arguably the more consequential comparison point – a purely executory commitment that has become genuinely loss-making would be recognised as a liability under IFRS, while US GAAP has no equally explicit general onerous-contract recognition trigger outside specific Topics (e.g. lease-related guidance).

Quick-reference glossary preview
Unconditional purchase obligation
An agreement to purchase goods or services at a fixed or minimum price, enforceable regardless of the buyer's need for the goods or services.
Executory contract
A contract under which neither party has yet performed, meaning no liability or asset has yet been recognised for the arrangement.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Take-or-pay contract
A contract obligating the buyer to pay for a minimum quantity of goods (typically a commodity) whether or not delivery is actually taken.
Onerous contract
A contract where the unavoidable costs of meeting the obligations exceed the expected economic benefits – a recognition trigger under IAS 37, without an equally general counterpart under US GAAP.
