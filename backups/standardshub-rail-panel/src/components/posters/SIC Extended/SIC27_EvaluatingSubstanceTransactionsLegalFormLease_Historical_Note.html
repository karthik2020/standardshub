SIC-27 — Evaluating the Substance of Transactions Involving the Legal Form of a Lease
Historical reference note · Issued 2001 · Withdrawn 2019
⚠ WITHDRAWN — Superseded by IFRS 16 Leases, effective for periods beginning 1 January 2019
What SIC-27 addressed
SIC-27 addressed a SERIES of transactions structured to involve the LEGAL FORM of a lease — often tax-driven or fee-generating arrangements — that, taken TOGETHER, did NOT, in economic substance, involve a genuine transfer of the right to use an underlying asset. It provided indicators for evaluating whether such a series should be accounted for according to its OVERALL economic substance rather than its individual legal form.
The conclusion it reached
Where a series of transactions was, in substance, NOT a lease, entities were required to "UNBUNDLE" and account SEPARATELY for any fees received (for example, from an investor participating in the structure) and any obligations assumed in connection with providing that fee — preventing the legal "lease" label from driving accounting that misrepresented the transaction's true economic character. SIC-27 worked alongside the separately issued IFRIC 4 (which addressed the related question of whether an arrangement CONTAINS a lease at all).
Where this guidance lives today
Superseded byIFRS 16 Leases (2016)
Why withdrawnIFRS 16 replaced IAS 17 and restructured lease scope guidance, folding substance-over-form evaluation into the standard's broader definitional and scope framework; IFRIC 4 was withdrawn at the same time for closely related reasons
Current statusThe underlying substance-over-form philosophy remains a live principle throughout IFRS generally, though SIC-27's specific lease-structuring indicator checklist is no longer separately applied
