Conceptual Framework Linkage

The delivery mechanism for nearly every disclosure this series has promised
Across this entire poster series, the Anhang has been repeatedly named as the DESTINATION for disclosure obligations arising elsewhere — the §264(2) true-and-fair remedy, the §251 Haftungsverhältnisse, the §252(2) exceptional-case justification, the §256/§255(3) measurement elections. §§284–288 finally supply the GENERAL framework for what the Anhang itself must actually contain.
HOW §§284–288 MAP TO GERMAN ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES
Klarer Einblick, completed — the Anhang is the FINAL layer through which HGB's own "true and fair" concept is actually achieved, entirely through disclosure rather than departure Size-dependent proportionality — §286(2) and §288 scale disclosure burden to company size, the SAME cost-benefit theme running throughout this entire library

Scope

Every Kapitalgesellschaft, with substantial size-dependent variation
§§284–288 bind every Kapitalgesellschaft required to prepare an Anhang under §264(1) — but the ACTUAL extent of disclosure varies enormously by size, with small and (to a lesser degree) medium corporations exempted from large swathes of §285's requirements under §288.
3

§284(1) — the General Explanatory Function of the Anhang

Explaining, not merely repeating, the Bilanz and GuV
§284(1) requires the Anhang to EXPLAIN (erläutern) the items shown in the Bilanz and GuV, taking into account the specific presentation structure elected — the Anhang's role is not to duplicate the primary statements, but to provide the CONTEXT and DETAIL a reader needs to genuinely understand what those figures represent.
4

§284(2) No.1 — Disclosing Every Measurement Method Elected

The single provision tying together nearly every election this series has documented
§284(2) No.1 requires disclosure of the accounting and measurement METHODS applied to the Bilanz and GuV items — this is the SPECIFIC provision requiring disclosure of virtually every genuine election this poster series has catalogued: the LIFO/FIFO choice (§256), the borrowing cost capitalisation election (§255(3)), the deferred tax method (§274), the Gesamtkosten-/Umsatzkostenverfahren choice (§275), the goodwill amortisation period (§246(1) s.4), and the self-created intangibles capitalisation election (§248(2)).
5

Worked Example — Disclosing the LIFO Election

Making a genuinely distinctive election transparent to readers
Example 1: A company electing LIFO under §256
A company elects LIFO for measuring a specific category of raw material inventory, as legally permitted under §256 (see the earlier companion poster on §§252–256a).
The company discloses, in its Anhang, that LIFO has been applied to this SPECIFIC inventory category — a genuinely important disclosure GIVEN LIFO's own status as an unusual, minority choice among German preparers, and one with no equivalent availability under IAS 2 or ASPE, making the disclosure especially informative for any reader comparing the company against international peers.
6

§284(2) No.2 — Disclosing a Change in Measurement Method

Not merely the fact of a change, but its quantified financial effect
§284(2) No.2 requires that WHERE a measurement method deviates from the PRIOR year, this must be disclosed, and the EFFECT on the enterprise's net assets, financial position, and results of operations must be presented SEPARATELY — and, where material, INDIVIDUALLY quantified, rather than simply noted as having occurred.
7

Worked Example — Quantifying the Effect of a Method Change

A disclosed change, with its financial impact made explicit
Example 2: Switching from FIFO to weighted-average cost
A company switches its inventory measurement method from FIFO to weighted-average cost, resulting in a €180,000 lower closing inventory valuation than FIFO would have produced.
The company discloses the CHANGE in method in its Anhang, and separately quantifies that the switch REDUCED closing inventory (and correspondingly increased cost of goods sold) by €180,000 relative to what continuing under FIFO would have shown — giving readers a concrete basis for understanding how much of any year-over-year change in reported results is attributable to the METHOD change itself, rather than genuine underlying business performance.
8

§284(3) — Justifying a §252(2) Departure

Explaining WHY a general measurement principle was departed from, and its effect
§284(3) requires the REASONS for any §252(2) departure from a general measurement principle (see the earlier anchor poster on §§252–256a) to be disclosed, and its influence on the enterprise's financial position to be EXPLAINED — closing the loop on that earlier-established, deliberately narrow escape valve, ensuring it can never be exercised silently.
9

§285 — the Long List of Other Mandatory Disclosures (Selected Major Categories)

One of HGB's longest, most frequently amended provisions
§285 contains a long, numbered list of further specific mandatory disclosures — this provision has been amended repeatedly over the years (most recently in connection with various EU accounting directive transpositions), and this poster presents its MAJOR, well-established categories rather than claiming to exhaustively reproduce every current numbered sub-point.
Total liabilities with a remaining term of MORE than 5 years, and liabilities secured by collateral, with the type and form of security Total amount of financial obligations NOT recognised on the Bilanz (beyond the §251 Haftungsverhältnisse itself), where relevant to assessing the overall financial position — e.g. multi-year lease or rental commitments Breakdown of Umsatzerlöse by business segment and geographic market, where these differ materially Average number of employees during the year, by category Total remuneration paid to members of management and supervisory bodies (subject to the §286(3) relief discussed below) Loans and advances granted to, and guarantees entered into on behalf of, members of management and supervisory bodies
These are the disclosure categories most commonly encountered and cross-referenced in German accounting practice — the full current text of §285 runs to considerably more numbered sub-points than shown here.

Worked Example — Disclosing Off-Balance-Sheet Financial Commitments

A distinct category from §251's own Haftungsverhältnisse
Example 3: A multi-year equipment lease commitment
A company enters into a 5-year equipment operating lease, committing to total future payments of €600,000 — an off-balance-sheet obligation, since ordinary operating leases (recall the HGB Section 3065-equivalent operating lease treatment discussed elsewhere in this poster library's other frameworks) are not recognised as a liability.
The company discloses the €600,000 total future commitment under §285, GIVEN its relevance to assessing the company's overall financial position — a disclosure category DISTINCT from, though conceptually related to, the §251 Haftungsverhältnisse guarantee-style disclosures established earlier in this series.
11

Worked Example — Employee and Board Remuneration Disclosures

Aggregate, not individual, figures for the ordinary case
Example 4: Disclosing management board remuneration
A GmbH's management board (Geschäftsführung) receives total annual remuneration of €850,000 across three members.
The company discloses the AGGREGATE €850,000 total remuneration figure in its Anhang under §285 — INDIVIDUAL, member-by-member remuneration disclosure is generally NOT required for a non-listed Kapitalgesellschaft in the ordinary case, reflecting a more privacy-conscious posture than some other jurisdictions' executive-pay disclosure regimes.
12

§286(1) — Omission Where Disclosure Would Be Seriously Prejudicial

A genuine, if narrow, commercial-sensitivity safety valve
§286(1) permits disclosures that would be SERIOUSLY PREJUDICIAL (erheblich schädigen) to the company, or to a group company, to be OMITTED — PROVIDED this does not prevent the Anhang from still achieving an overall true and fair view. Critically, the FACT of the omission must itself be disclosed — a company cannot simply omit an item silently and leave readers unaware that something has been withheld.
13

Worked Example — Applying the §286(1) Commercial-Sensitivity Relief

Genuinely narrow, and never silent
Example 5: Withholding customer-specific pricing detail during an active negotiation
A company is mid-way through a highly sensitive contract renegotiation with a major customer, where disclosing certain granular segment-level pricing detail otherwise required under §285 would materially weaken its negotiating position.
The company OMITS the specific granular disclosure under §286(1), but explicitly NOTES in its Anhang that a disclosure has been omitted on these grounds — preserving transparency about the FACT of the omission, even while protecting the genuinely sensitive underlying commercial detail itself.
14

§286(2)–(4) — Further Omission Reliefs

Size, board remuneration, and shareholding disclosures
§286(2): further size-dependent relief for medium and small corporations regarding certain §285 items §286(3): permits omission of INDIVIDUAL management/supervisory board remuneration disclosure in specific circumstances, reflecting the aggregate-disclosure norm discussed in Example 4 above §286(4): permits omission of certain shareholding (Anteilsbesitz) disclosures where these would be seriously prejudicial, paralleling §286(1)'s general commercial-sensitivity logic
15

§287 — Repealed

No current content
§287 is currently marked "(weggefallen)" — repealed, with no substantive content remaining.
16

§288 — Size-Dependent Relief From §284/§285

A consolidated exemption, scaled by the §267 size classes
§288(1) exempts SMALL corporations from a considerable range of the §284/§285 disclosure requirements entirely — reflecting the same cost-benefit relief theme running throughout this whole poster series. §288(2) provides FURTHER, somewhat narrower relief specifically for MEDIUM-sized corporations, covering a smaller subset of items than the small-company exemption reaches.
17

Worked Example — Applying §288's Size-Based Exemption

The same underlying facts, radically different disclosure burden by size
Example 6: Two companies with identical segment data, different size classes
Company A (large, per §267's thresholds confirmed in the earlier poster) and Company B (small) both generate revenue across three distinct geographic markets in materially different proportions.
Company A must provide the FULL §285 segment/geographic revenue breakdown disclosure. Company B, relying on §288(1)'s small-company exemption, may OMIT this SAME disclosure entirely — DESPITE both companies having identical, materially significant underlying segment data, purely as a function of their different size classifications.

Critical Judgments & Practical Difficulties

Where judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether a specific disclosure would genuinely be "seriously prejudicial," justifying §286(1) omission, versus merely commercially inconvenient Whether the financial effect of a measurement method change is sufficiently material to require individual quantification under §284(2) No.2
PRACTICAL DIFFICULTIES
Tracking which specific §285 sub-items apply given a company's precise size classification, particularly near a threshold boundary Keeping pace with §285's frequent legislative amendments across successive fiscal years

HGB's APPROACH to size-based relief is structurally distinctive — rather than a wholly SEPARATE simplified standard (the IFRS for SMEs approach), HGB builds graduated relief directly INTO the same statutory provisions every company starts from, scaling obligations up or down within one unified framework.
