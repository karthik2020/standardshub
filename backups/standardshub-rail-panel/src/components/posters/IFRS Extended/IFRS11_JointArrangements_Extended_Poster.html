Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why classification follows RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS, not legal form
The Conceptual Framework's asset/liability definitions ask what an entity genuinely CONTROLS or OWES. IFRS 11 applies this directly to joint arrangements: classification depends on the parties' RIGHTS to assets and OBLIGATIONS for liabilities of the arrangement, NOT on the legal structure alone — even a separate legal vehicle can be a "joint operation" if the parties' rights/obligations relate directly to the underlying assets/liabilities.
HOW IFRS 11 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Substance over form — a separate legal vehicle does NOT automatically mean the parties only have a net equity interest; contractual terms can preserve direct rights/obligations Faithful representation — joint operators recognise THEIR OWN share of assets/liabilities directly, reflecting genuine direct exposure, rather than a single net investment figure Comparability — replacing IAS 31's proportionate consolidation CHOICE with a single mandatory equity method for joint ventures removed a major comparability weakness
IFRS 11 was issued alongside IFRS 10 and IFRS 12 as part of a coordinated "consolidation package," directly replacing IAS 31 and its criticised policy-choice structure.
The two-category structure
IFRS 11 replaced IAS 31's three structural forms with just TWO categories, each with a single mandatory accounting treatment.
TypeAccounting
Joint operationEach party recognises its OWN share of assets, liabilities, revenue, expenses directly
Joint ventureMANDATORY equity method (IAS 28) — NO proportionate consolidation option
ℹ️ Removing the IAS 31 CHOICE for joint ventures directly improved comparability, at some cost to the granularity IAS 31's proportionate consolidation once provided.

Scope & Applicability

What IFRS 11 covers
IFRS 11 applies to ALL entities that are a PARTY to a joint arrangement — an arrangement of which two or more parties have JOINT CONTROL.
A joint arrangement has two essential characteristics: parties are bound by a CONTRACTUAL ARRANGEMENT, and that contractual arrangement gives two or more parties JOINT CONTROL.
Key definitions you must know
Joint control
The contractually agreed sharing of control, which exists ONLY when decisions about relevant activities require the UNANIMOUS consent of the parties sharing control.
Joint operation
A joint arrangement whereby parties with joint control have RIGHTS to the ASSETS and OBLIGATIONS for the LIABILITIES relating to the arrangement.
Joint venture
A joint arrangement whereby parties with joint control have RIGHTS to the NET ASSETS of the arrangement.
3

Joint Control — The Threshold (with worked example)

Unanimous consent — a genuinely high bar
An entity assesses whether ALL the parties, or a GROUP of the parties, control the arrangement COLLECTIVELY (per IFRS 10's control definition), and then whether decisions about RELEVANT ACTIVITIES require the UNANIMOUS consent of those parties sharing control.
Example 1: Testing for unanimous consent
Three parties hold 40%, 35%, and 25% of the voting rights in an arrangement. The contractual agreement requires decisions about relevant activities to be approved by AT LEAST 75% of the voting rights.
NO single party can unilaterally approve decisions (none holds 75% alone), but multiple DIFFERENT combinations of two parties CAN reach 75% (e.g. 40%+35%=75%, or 40%+25%... does not reach 75% alone with only two, need to check: 40+35=75, 35+25=60, 40+25=65). Since MORE THAN ONE combination of parties could together reach the required percentage, NO single combination has EXCLUSIVE joint control — this arrangement likely does NOT meet the joint control threshold, since unanimous consent of a SPECIFIC identifiable set of parties is not established.
4

Classifying the Arrangement

Structure, legal form, contractual terms, and other facts
Classification as a joint operation or joint venture depends on the parties' RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS arising from the arrangement, assessed by considering: the STRUCTURE of the arrangement (separate vehicle or not); when structured through a separate vehicle, the LEGAL FORM of that vehicle; the terms of the CONTRACTUAL ARRANGEMENT; and, when relevant, OTHER FACTS AND CIRCUMSTANCES.
An arrangement NOT structured through a separate vehicle is ALWAYS a joint operation — the separate vehicle analysis (Section 7) only becomes necessary once a distinct legal entity/vehicle exists.
5

Joint Operations Accounting (with worked example)

Recognising the joint operator's OWN direct share
A joint operator recognises, in relation to its interest: its ASSETS, including its share of any assets held jointly; its LIABILITIES, including its share of any liabilities incurred jointly; its REVENUE from the sale of its share of output; its share of REVENUE from the sale of output by the joint operation; and its EXPENSES, including its share of any expenses incurred jointly.
Example 2: A joint operation in oil exploration
Two companies jointly operate an oil field through a contractual arrangement (no separate vehicle), each entitled to 50% of extracted oil and jointly liable for decommissioning costs. Company A's own extraction costs for the period: $2,000,000. Jointly incurred decommissioning provision: $6,000,000.
Company A recognises its OWN $2,000,000 extraction costs directly, PLUS its 50% share of the joint decommissioning liability = 6,000,000 × 50% = $3,000,000, recognised directly on ITS OWN SOFP — no separate joint venture entity accounting is needed.
6

Joint Ventures Accounting

Mandatory equity method — no alternative
A joint venturer recognises its interest in a joint venture as an INVESTMENT, and accounts for that investment using the EQUITY METHOD in accordance with IAS 28 — UNLESS the entity is exempted from applying the equity method per IAS 28's own scope exceptions (e.g. certain investment entities, or entities meeting specific parent-level exemption conditions).
This is a DIRECT departure from IAS 31 — there is NO proportionate consolidation option available for joint ventures under IFRS 11, regardless of preparer preference.

Separate Vehicle Assessment — The Key Judgement (with worked example)

When a separate vehicle can STILL be a joint operation
Even where an arrangement IS structured through a separate vehicle, it can still be a JOINT OPERATION if the LEGAL FORM, contractual terms, or OTHER FACTS/CIRCUMSTANCES indicate the parties have rights to assets and obligations for liabilities (rather than merely rights to net assets).
Example 3: A separate vehicle that is still a joint operation
Two companies establish a separate legal entity to construct and operate a pipeline. Although the pipeline is legally owned by the separate entity, the contractual arrangement specifies that the OUTPUT (transported gas capacity) is entirely allocated to the two parties in proportion to their interests, and each party is contractually obligated to fund the entity's operating costs and any cash shortfalls in that SAME proportion.
Despite the SEPARATE legal vehicle, the CONTRACTUAL TERMS give each party DIRECT rights to substantially all the output and DIRECT obligations to fund costs — this indicates the arrangement is a JOINT OPERATION, NOT a joint venture, despite the separate legal entity's existence. Each party accounts for its own share of assets/liabilities/revenue/expenses directly, per Section 5.
⚠️ This is one of the MOST heavily tested and counter-intuitive aspects of IFRS 11 — a separate legal vehicle does NOT automatically mean joint venture (equity method) treatment; the underlying CONTRACTUAL substance can still produce joint OPERATION accounting.
8

Accounting for Parties Without Joint Control

Participants who lack joint control
A party participating in, but NOT having joint control of, a joint operation ALSO accounts for its interest in the assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses relating to its involvement, IF it has rights to assets and obligations for liabilities. A party participating in, but not having joint control of, a joint VENTURE accounts for its interest per IFRS 9 (as a financial asset), UNLESS it has SIGNIFICANT INFLUENCE, in which case IAS 28's equity method applies instead.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether unanimous consent genuinely exists among an identifiable group of parties Classifying a separate-vehicle arrangement as a joint operation vs joint venture based on contractual substance
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Allocating shared assets/liabilities/costs between joint operators where contractual terms require estimation
⚠️ The separate vehicle classification judgement (Section 7) is consistently one of the most challenging and frequently misapplied areas of the entire consolidation suite.

IFRS vs US GAAP

Comparison — IFRS 11 vs US GAAP
FeatureIFRS (IFRS 11)US GAAP
Joint venture accountingMANDATORY equity method onlyEquity method is also the general approach, though proportionate consolidation is permitted in SOME specific industries (e.g. certain oil and gas/construction arrangements) under US GAAP
Classification driverRights and obligations (structure, legal form, contractual terms, other facts)Similar substance-based analysis, though with different specific guidance
US GAAP retains a NARROW proportionate consolidation option in specific industries that IFRS 11 does not permit at all — a residual divergence despite otherwise broadly similar philosophy.
