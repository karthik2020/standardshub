Conceptual Framework Linkage

The organising topic for the entire Presentation area
ASC 205 supplies the OVERALL requirements for a complete set of financial statements, plus three specialised bodies of guidance folded into the same Topic: going concern assessment, discontinued operations classification, and the liquidation basis of accounting. It plays a role broadly analogous to IAS 1, but — true to the Codification's structure — several matters IAS 1 addresses directly (e.g. classified balance sheet presentation) are instead split out into their own dedicated Topics (ASC 210, 215, 225, 230).
Faithful representation under genuine going-concern uncertainty
The going-concern subtopic in particular reflects a Conceptual Framework-level concern: financial statements prepared on a going-concern basis implicitly represent that the reporting entity will continue operating — ASC 205-40 exists to ensure that representation is EXPLICITLY tested and disclosed when substantial doubt genuinely exists, rather than left as an unstated assumption.
2

Scope & Applicability

What ASC 205 covers
ASC 205 applies to ALL entities preparing financial statements under US GAAP. The going-concern guidance (205-40) applies to EVERY reporting entity, for BOTH annual and interim periods. The discontinued operations guidance (205-20) applies whenever a component or group of components has been disposed of or is classified as held for sale. The liquidation basis guidance (205-30) applies only once liquidation becomes IMMINENT (a specifically defined threshold, see Section 7).

No "True and Fair Override" in US GAAP

A structural divergence worth knowing early
IAS 1 contains a rare "override" provision permitting departure from a specific IFRS requirement, in EXTREMELY rare circumstances, if compliance would be so misleading it conflicts with the objective of financial statements. US GAAP has NO equivalent override mechanism. An entity applying US GAAP must comply with the Codification's requirements as written — there is no codified escape hatch for a rare conflict between a specific rule and "fair presentation" in the way IAS 1 provides. This reflects a more RULES-BASED character to US GAAP relative to IFRS's more PRINCIPLES-BASED orientation, though both frameworks are, in practice, a blend of both approaches.

Going Concern (205-40)

Management's explicit, codified responsibility
ASC 205-40 (added by ASU 2014-15) requires MANAGEMENT to assess, for BOTH annual and interim periods, whether conditions or events raise SUBSTANTIAL DOUBT about the entity's ability to continue as a going concern for ONE YEAR from the date the financial statements are ISSUED (or available to be issued, for nonpublic entities) — a defined, codified "look-forward" period.
Two-step process
Step 1: Assess, based on RELEVANT conditions/events known (or reasonably knowable) as of the issuance date, without regard to management's plans, whether substantial doubt exists.
Step 2: If substantial doubt exists, evaluate whether management's PLANS to mitigate that doubt will alleviate it. Disclosure is required EITHER WAY once substantial doubt is initially identified — the required disclosures simply differ depending on whether the doubt is alleviated by the plans or not.

Discontinued Operations (205-20)

A narrowed, 2014-revised definition
A disposal of a component (or group of components) of an entity is reported as a DISCONTINUED OPERATION only if the disposal represents a STRATEGIC SHIFT that has (or will have) a MAJOR effect on the entity's operations and financial results — for example, a disposal of a major geographic area, a major line of business, or a major equity method investment. This definition (from ASU 2014-08) is deliberately NARROWER than the pre-2014 US GAAP definition, and was adopted specifically to converge more closely with IFRS 5's own "major line of business or geographical area" discontinued-operation threshold.

Liquidation Basis of Accounting (205-30)

A distinctly US GAAP concept — no direct IFRS counterpart standard
When LIQUIDATION becomes IMMINENT — defined as either (a) a liquidation plan has been APPROVED by the person(s) with authority and the likelihood is remote that execution will be blocked by other parties, or (b) a liquidation plan is being IMPOSED by other forces (e.g. involuntary bankruptcy) and it is remote that the entity will return to a going-concern basis — the entity switches from the going-concern basis to the LIQUIDATION basis of accounting. Under this basis, assets are measured at the ESTIMATED AMOUNT of cash or other consideration expected to be collected in settling/converting those assets in liquidation, and the entity ACCRUES the costs it expects to incur, and income it expects to earn, through the end of the liquidation.

Comparative Periods — ASC vs SEC Rules

Comparatives driven by SEC rules, not the Codification itself
The Codification itself does not mandate a minimum number of comparative periods for private companies (one year of comparatives is common practice but not strictly required by ASC 205 for non-SEC filers). PUBLIC companies, however, must follow SEC Regulation S-X, which requires TWO years of comparative Balance Sheets and THREE years of comparative Income Statements, Statements of Comprehensive Income, and Statements of Cash Flows in most cases — a more prescriptive comparative-period requirement than IAS 1's general one-comparative-year default.

Worked Example — Going Concern Assessment

Substantial doubt, alleviated by management's plans
Example: A covenant breach with a credible refinancing plan
At year end, an entity has breached a debt covenant, making $15,000,000 of long-term debt callable on demand, while the entity holds only $3,000,000 of unrestricted cash. Before the issuance date of the financial statements, management has SIGNED a binding refinancing agreement replacing the callable debt with new long-term financing on customary terms.
Step 1: The covenant breach and resulting demand-callable debt, considered without regard to management's plans, DOES raise substantial doubt about the entity's ability to continue as a going concern for one year from issuance.
Step 2: Because the refinancing agreement is already SIGNED and binding (not merely a plan or intention), it is probable the plans will be effectively implemented and will mitigate the conditions — substantial doubt is ALLEVIATED. Disclosure is still required describing the conditions that raised the doubt and how management's plans alleviated it, but no "going concern" qualification of the type auditors historically flagged is needed in the financial statement notes themselves (though the AUDIT report may still reference the disclosure).

Worked Example — Discontinued Operations Classification

Testing against the "strategic shift, major effect" threshold
Example: Sale of one of three reporting segments
A diversified entity with three reporting segments (Consumer Products, Industrial Equipment, and Specialty Chemicals) sells its entire Specialty Chemicals segment, which represented 35% of consolidated revenue and 40% of consolidated operating income.
The Specialty Chemicals segment represents a MAJOR LINE OF BUSINESS with a MAJOR effect on the entity's operations and financial results (35–40% of key metrics), and its disposal constitutes a genuine STRATEGIC SHIFT for the remaining two-segment entity. This QUALIFIES for discontinued operations presentation under ASC 205-20 — the segment's results are presented separately, net of tax, below income from continuing operations, for all periods presented.
11

Worked Example — Liquidation Basis Transition

Switching bases mid-year
Example: Board-approved liquidation plan
An entity's board of directors approves a plan of complete liquidation on 1 September, with no realistic possibility of the plan being blocked by shareholders or creditors. Liquidation is expected to conclude within 18 months.
Liquidation becomes IMMINENT on 1 September (board approval, remote likelihood of blockage). From that date, the entity SWITCHES to the liquidation basis: assets are remeasured to the estimated cash amount expected to be realised in liquidation (rather than historical cost or fair value under going-concern GAAP), and a liability/accrual is recognised for estimated costs to be incurred (e.g. wind-down costs, employee termination costs) through the expected 18-month liquidation period.

Disclosure Requirements

Key disclosures across the four subtopics
Going concern: principal conditions/events raising substantial doubt, management's evaluation, and (if applicable) management's plans and their effectDiscontinued operations: major classes of line items constituting the discontinued operation's pre-tax profit/loss, and major categories of related assets/liabilitiesLiquidation basis: a statement that the liquidation basis has been adopted, the liquidation plan's key assumptions, and a statement of net assets in liquidation together with a statement of changes in net assets in liquidation

ASC 205 vs IAS 1 / IFRS 5

Comparison — ASC 205 vs IAS 1 (going concern) and IFRS 5 (discontinued operations)
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 205)IFRS (IAS 1 / IFRS 5)
Going concern look-forward periodExplicitly defined: 1 year from the FINANCIAL STATEMENT ISSUANCE dateIAS 1 requires assessment for a period of at least 12 months from the END OF THE REPORTING PERIOD — a subtly different (and typically shorter) window than the US GAAP issuance-date anchor
Explicit "substantial doubt" threshold and two-step processYes — codified in detail (ASC 205-40)IAS 1 requires an assessment but is less prescriptive about a specific "substantial doubt" threshold and formal two-step process
Discontinued operations thresholdStrategic shift with a major effect on operations/results (post-2014, converged toward IFRS)IFRS 5: a component representing a separate major line of business or geographical area of operations — broadly converged following the 2014 US GAAP narrowing
Liquidation basis of accountingDedicated, codified guidance (ASC 205-30)NO dedicated IFRS standard — an entity in liquidation applies general Conceptual Framework and IAS 1 going-concern-departure principles without an equivalent specific measurement model
Override for misleading complianceNone — no override mechanism existsIAS 1 contains a rare, narrowly available true-and-fair override provision
The liquidation basis of accounting is one of the more genuinely UNIQUE features of US GAAP relative to IFRS — IFRS entities facing liquidation must work from general principles rather than a dedicated, detailed measurement model.

Quick-reference glossary preview
Imminent (liquidation)
A defined threshold under ASC 205-30 — either an approved liquidation plan with remote likelihood of blockage, or an externally imposed liquidation plan with remote likelihood of returning to going-concern status.
Substantial doubt
The defined threshold under ASC 205-40 triggering going-concern disclosure — assessed without regard to management's mitigating plans in the first instance.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Component of an entity
Operations and cash flows that can be clearly distinguished, operationally and for financial reporting purposes, from the rest of the entity — the unit of account for discontinued operations analysis.
Net assets in liquidation
The liquidation-basis equivalent of total equity — the excess of estimated liquidation-basis assets over estimated liabilities, presented in a dedicated Statement of Net Assets in Liquidation.
