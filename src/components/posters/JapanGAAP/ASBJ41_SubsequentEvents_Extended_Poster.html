Conceptual Framework Linkage

A single, dedicated standard replacing scattered auditing-committee-report guidance
ASBJ Statement No. 41 addresses events OCCURRING AFTER the balance sheet date but BEFORE the financial statements are APPROVED for issue — determining WHETHER such events should trigger an ADJUSTMENT to the financial statements THEMSELVES, or merely a DISCLOSURE. Previously, Japan's subsequent events guidance was SCATTERED across Auditing Standards Committee Reports and general accounting principles, WITHOUT a single, dedicated ACCOUNTING standard — this Statement CONSOLIDATES and SYSTEMATISES that guidance while ALIGNING it with IAS 10 internationally.
2

Scope — Consolidating Previously Scattered Guidance

All companies preparing financial statements under J-GAAP
This Statement applies to ALL companies preparing financial statements, addressing events between the BALANCE SHEET date and the date financial statements are APPROVED for issue.
3

Adjusting vs Non-Adjusting Events

Evidence of conditions at the balance sheet date, versus new conditions arising after
Event typeTreatment
Adjusting eventProvides EVIDENCE of conditions that EXISTED at the balance sheet date — the financial statements are ADJUSTED to reflect this evidence
Non-adjusting eventIndicative of conditions that AROSE AFTER the balance sheet date — DISCLOSED only, with NO adjustment to recognised amounts
This TWO-CATEGORY framework directly MIRRORS IAS 10's own adjusting/non-adjusting event distinction internationally.
4

The Approval Date — the Key Reference Point

Defining precisely when the subsequent events window closes
The subsequent events REVIEW period runs from the balance sheet date UNTIL the date the financial statements are APPROVED for issue (typically board approval, 承認日) — a PRECISE reference point this Statement seeks to CLARIFY and make CONSISTENT across preparers, addressing PRACTICAL questions such as HOW to treat information arriving from SUBSIDIARIES close to the parent's OWN approval deadline.
5

Going Concern Reassessment

A subsequent event can fundamentally change the basis of preparation
Where a subsequent event INDICATES that the GOING CONCERN basis of preparation is NO LONGER appropriate (e.g. a decision to LIQUIDATE, or cease TRADING, arising AFTER the balance sheet date), this represents the MOST SIGNIFICANT possible category of adjusting-type consequence — REQUIRING a FUNDAMENTAL change to the basis on which the ENTIRE financial statements are prepared, RATHER than a narrow, isolated adjustment.
6

Why This Standard Was Needed — Systematising Previously Dispersed Rules

Clarifying the basis for existing practice, not overhauling it
Commentary on this exposure draft has NOTED that the NEW standard is NOT expected to SUBSTANTIALLY change existing PRACTICE — Japanese companies have ALREADY been applying broadly SIMILAR principles through auditing-standards-committee guidance and general accounting PRINCIPLES. Rather, this Statement's PRIMARY value lies in providing a CLEAR, SYSTEMATIC, dedicated ACCOUNTING basis for treatment that was PREVIOUSLY scattered and less FORMALLY codified — REDUCING the risk of INCONSISTENT judgement across preparers.
7

Worked Example — an Adjusting Event

Reflecting evidence of a condition that already existed
Example: A major customer's bankruptcy confirming an existing receivable's impairment
A company's fiscal year ends March 31. On April 20 (before board approval), a major customer with an outstanding receivable at March 31 files for bankruptcy, confirming the customer had already been in serious financial distress AT the balance sheet date.
This is an ADJUSTING EVENT — the bankruptcy filing PROVIDES EVIDENCE of a condition (the customer's financial distress) that ALREADY EXISTED at March 31. The company ADJUSTS its financial statements, recognising an IMPAIRMENT of the receivable AS AT the balance sheet date.
8

Worked Example — a Non-Adjusting Event

Disclosure only, no adjustment to recognised amounts
Example: A fire destroying a warehouse after year end
The SAME company's warehouse is destroyed by a fire on April 25 — an event with NO connection to conditions existing at the March 31 balance sheet date.
This is a NON-ADJUSTING EVENT — the fire is INDICATIVE of a condition ARISING AFTER the balance sheet date. The company DISCLOSES the nature of the event and, WHERE practicable, an estimate of its FINANCIAL effect, WITHOUT adjusting the RECOGNISED amounts in the March 31 financial statements themselves.
