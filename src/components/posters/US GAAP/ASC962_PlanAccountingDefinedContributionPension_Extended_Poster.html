Conceptual Framework Linkage — No Actuarial Obligation to Measure

The plan's obligation is simply the sum of participant account balances
Unlike a DEFINED BENEFIT plan (ASC 960), a DEFINED CONTRIBUTION plan (e.g. a 401(k) plan) makes NO promise of a specific future BENEFIT amount — each participant's ultimate benefit is simply whatever their INDIVIDUAL ACCOUNT balance happens to be worth at retirement, based on contributions made and investment performance. This means ASC 962 requires NO actuarial present value obligation disclosure of the kind ASC 960 requires — there is no defined benefit PROMISE to actuarially value in the first place, since the plan's total liability is, by definition, simply the sum of all individual account balances.
2

Scope

Individual-account defined contribution plans
ASC 962 applies to the SEPARATE financial statements of DEFINED CONTRIBUTION pension plans — including 401(k), 403(b), profit-sharing, and money purchase pension plans — where benefits are based on individual participant account balances.
3

Statement of Net Assets Available for Benefits

Investments at fair value, consistent with plan accounting generally
Like ASC 960, the plan's primary statement presents INVESTMENTS at FAIR VALUE, less any liabilities, resulting in NET ASSETS AVAILABLE FOR BENEFITS — but here, this total is INHERENTLY equal (or very close, aside from minor unallocated amounts) to the SUM of all individual participant ACCOUNT BALANCES, since that's precisely what a defined contribution plan's assets represent.
4

Statement of Changes in Net Assets Available for Benefits

Contributions in, investment activity, distributions out
This statement shows EMPLOYER and EMPLOYEE contributions received, investment income/gains ALLOCATED to individual participant accounts based on their elected investment options, LESS benefit distributions/withdrawals paid to participants and administrative expenses.
5

Participant-Directed vs Nonparticipant-Directed Investment Programs

A meaningful disclosure distinction based on who controls investment choices
A PARTICIPANT-DIRECTED investment program is one where INDIVIDUAL participants choose among available investment options for their OWN account (the typical modern 401(k) structure) — plans must disclose investment information for such programs with LESS granular detail than for NONPARTICIPANT-DIRECTED programs (where the plan itself, rather than individual participants, determines the investment allocation) — reflecting that participant-directed investment CHOICES are inherently more the individual's own responsibility.
6

Worked Example — Participant Account Allocation

Allocating investment performance to individual accounts
Example: Allocating annual investment return across participant accounts
A 401(k) plan's total investments earn a 12% annual return. A participant's account balance was $50,000 at the start of the year, with $6,000 of contributions made evenly throughout the year.
The participant's account is credited with its PROPORTIONATE SHARE of the plan's overall 12% investment return, based on their account's actual balance over time (weighted for contribution timing) — resulting in an account balance that reflects BOTH their own contributions AND their allocated share of overall plan investment performance, WITHOUT any actuarial calculation being required, since the benefit is simply whatever the account is worth.
