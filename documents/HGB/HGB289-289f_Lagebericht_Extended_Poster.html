HGB §§289–289f — Lagebericht (Management Report)
Extended reference poster · Book 3, Section 2, Subsection 1, Title 6 · The forward-looking narrative report that sits deliberately outside the Jahresabschluss · German commercial law · EUR examples
REGULATORY STATUS CHECK (as of the most recent information available): Germany has NOT YET transposed the EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) into national law, despite repeated planned deadlines. The §§289b–289e non-financial statement framework described below is the ORIGINAL 2017 regime (implementing the 2014 NFRD), and REMAINS the operative law for the time being. A CSRD transposition — significantly expanding scope — is anticipated but has already slipped multiple times; verify current status before relying on this for a live filing.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

The last piece of the individual-accounts puzzle, deliberately kept separate
As established at the very start of this poster's Subsection, the Lagebericht is DELIBERATELY kept OUTSIDE the Jahresabschluss definition (§242(3)) — yet §264(1) requires it to be prepared and filed alongside the Bilanz, GuV, and Anhang regardless. §§289–289f now supply its actual content, closing out Book 3, Section 2, Subsection 1 before the poster series turns to consolidated accounts.
HOW §§289–289F MAP TO GERMAN ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES
Ein den tatsächlichen Verhältnissen entsprechendes Bild — the SAME "true and fair" formulation from §264(2) reappears here, applied to a forward-looking NARRATIVE rather than to historical figures Klarer Einblick, extended into the future — the Lagebericht is where HGB's transparency objective reaches beyond the balance sheet date entirely, into risk and opportunity

Scope

Every Kapitalgesellschaft required to prepare a Lagebericht, with additional layers for listed companies
§289's CORE content requirements apply to every Kapitalgesellschaft bound by §264(1)'s Lagebericht obligation. §289a, §289b (per its own specific size/listing criteria — see Section 11), and §289f each add FURTHER, progressively narrower layers — reaching only listed AGs/KGaAs, or large capital-market-oriented companies with more than 500 employees, respectively.
3

§289(1) — the Core Content Test

Course of business, business result, and position — presented true to actual circumstances
§289(1) requires the Lagebericht to present the COURSE OF BUSINESS — including the business result — and the company's POSITION, such that a picture corresponding to actual circumstances (den tatsächlichen Verhältnissen entsprechendes Bild) is conveyed. It must ALSO include an accurate presentation of the PRINCIPAL OPPORTUNITIES AND RISKS associated with expected future development, together with an assessment and explanation of that future development, with the MATERIAL ASSUMPTIONS underlying any forward-looking statements disclosed.
4

The Prognosebericht — a Genuinely Distinctive Forward-Looking Mandate

A mandatory forecast, embedded directly in the statutory filing itself
The forecast/outlook component of §289(1) — commonly referred to in German practice as the Prognosebericht — is a genuinely DISTINCTIVE feature relative to IFRS. IFRS itself imposes NO requirement for a forward-looking narrative report as part of the mandatory financial statements — "management commentary" exists only as a NON-MANDATORY IFRS Practice Statement, which jurisdictions and companies may adopt or ignore entirely. HGB, by contrast, makes a reasoned, assumption-disclosed FORECAST a mandatory, statutory component of every Kapitalgesellschaft's annual filing.
5

Worked Example — Drafting a Compliant Forecast Statement

Assumptions must be disclosed, not merely a bare conclusion
Example 1: A manufacturer's outlook statement
A manufacturing company expects revenue growth of approximately 6% in the coming year, based on an assumed continuation of current order backlog trends and an assumed stable raw material cost environment.
A COMPLIANT Prognosebericht states not merely "we expect 6% revenue growth" but explicitly discloses the underlying MATERIAL ASSUMPTIONS — the order backlog trend and the raw material cost stability assumption — per §289(1)'s specific requirement that assumptions underlying forward-looking statements be disclosed, allowing readers to assess the forecast's sensitivity to those assumptions proving wrong.
6

§289(2) — Further Required Content

Four named further topics, where relevant to a complete picture
Events of PARTICULAR SIGNIFICANCE occurring AFTER the fiscal year-end (subsequent events) — conceptually adjacent to IAS 10, though delivered through the Lagebericht's narrative rather than through adjusted or disclosed financial statement figures The EXPECTED development of the enterprise, with its opportunities and risks — reinforcing the Prognosebericht content from §289(1) The enterprise's RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT activities (Forschungs- und Entwicklungsbericht) — a specifically named content requirement Existing BRANCHES (Zweigniederlassungen) of the enterprise
7

Worked Example — a Post-Year-End Significant Event

Narrative disclosure, not a Bilanz adjustment
Example 2: A major acquisition agreed shortly after year-end
A company signs a definitive agreement to acquire a competitor for €12,000,000, three weeks after its fiscal year-end but before the Jahresabschluss and Lagebericht are finalised.
The acquisition, being an event of clear significance occurring AFTER the balance sheet date, is described in the Lagebericht under §289(2) — this is a NARRATIVE disclosure, distinct from the Imparitätsprinzip-driven Bilanz ADJUSTMENT mechanism covered in the earlier §§252–256a poster (which applies to information ILLUMINATING conditions that already existed AT the balance sheet date, not to genuinely new post-year-end transactions like this one).
8

§289(3)–(4) — Non-Financial Performance Indicators and Internal Controls

A narrower forerunner to §289b's fuller non-financial statement
§289(3) requires NON-FINANCIAL performance indicators relevant to understanding the business — including environmental and employee matters — to be included in the analysis TO THE EXTENT necessary for understanding the course of business, its result, and its position. §289(4) requires, specifically for CAPITAL-MARKET-ORIENTED corporations, a description of the main characteristics of the enterprise's INTERNAL CONTROL and RISK MANAGEMENT SYSTEM as it relates to the (financial) accounting process.
9

§289a — Takeover-Related Disclosures for Listed AGs

A set of disclosures unrelated to ordinary financial reporting, bundled into the Lagebericht anyway
§289a — Germany's transposition of the EU Takeover Directive's disclosure requirements — requires certain listed AGs/KGaAs to disclose, WITHIN the Lagebericht: the composition of share capital; restrictions on voting rights or share transfers; direct/indirect shareholdings exceeding 10% of voting rights; holders of shares carrying special control rights; voting control mechanisms for employee shareholdings; rules on appointing/removing management board members and amending the articles of association; the management board's powers (e.g. to issue or buy back shares); material agreements that take effect or CHANGE upon a change of control following a takeover bid; and compensation agreements with management board members or employees in the event of a takeover bid.
10

Worked Example — Disclosing a Change-of-Control Clause

Making takeover-defence-relevant financing terms visible to the market
Example 3: A financing agreement with an acceleration clause
A listed AG has a major syndicated loan agreement containing a clause allowing lenders to demand immediate repayment if a single shareholder acquires more than 30% of the company's voting shares (a classic change-of-control acceleration clause).
The AG discloses this SPECIFIC clause — its trigger threshold and its effect — under §289a, since it is exactly the kind of "material agreement that takes effect or changes upon a change of control following a takeover bid" the provision targets, giving the market visibility into a financing consequence that could materially affect the outcome of any actual takeover attempt.
11

§289b — the (Still-Current) Non-Financial Statement Obligation

Capital-market-oriented companies with more than 500 employees, per the ORIGINAL 2017 regime
§289b(1) requires a Kapitalgesellschaft meeting specific criteria — in practice, capital-market-oriented companies (and certain credit institutions/insurers) exceeding 500 employees — to EXPAND its Lagebericht with a non-financial statement (nichtfinanzielle Erklärung), covering environmental, employee, social, human rights, and anti-corruption/anti-bribery matters. §289b(2) permits this content to instead be delivered via a SEPARATE, standalone non-financial report published alongside the Lagebericht, rather than embedded within it.
⚠️ As flagged at the top of this poster: this remains the OPERATIVE regime because Germany has not yet transposed the EU's CSRD, which would substantially EXPAND this scope (to large companies generally, and eventually smaller capital-market-oriented ones) once national implementing legislation finally passes.
12

§289c — Content of the Non-Financial Statement

Business model, five topics, concepts, outcomes, risks, and KPIs
§289c requires a description of the enterprise's BUSINESS MODEL, and then, for EACH of the five named topics (environmental, employee, social, human rights, anti-corruption/bribery matters), a description of: the CONCEPTS pursued; the DUE DILIGENCE processes applied; the OUTCOMES of those concepts; the PRINCIPAL RISKS connected with the company's own operations AND with its business relationships, products, or services likely to have a materially adverse impact; how those risks are MANAGED; and relevant NON-FINANCIAL key performance indicators.
13

Worked Example — Covering the Five Mandatory Topics

A genuinely broad substantive scope, beyond ordinary financial matters
Example 4: A manufacturer's non-financial statement structure
A capital-market-oriented manufacturer with 1,200 employees prepares its non-financial statement, addressing each of the five §289c topics.
ENVIRONMENTAL: energy consumption reduction targets and outcomes. EMPLOYEE: workforce safety incident rates and training hours. SOCIAL: community engagement and local supply chain practices. HUMAN RIGHTS: supplier due diligence processes addressing labour conditions in the supply chain. ANTI-CORRUPTION: whistleblowing mechanisms and anti-bribery training completion rates — each topic addressed with its OWN concept, due diligence process, outcome, principal risks, risk management approach, and relevant KPI, exactly as §289c's structure requires.
14

§289d — Use of Recognised Frameworks

A named-framework option, increasingly exercised toward ESRS in practice
§289d permits an enterprise to base its non-financial statement on national, EU, or international frameworks (e.g. the UN Global Compact, OECD Guidelines, ISO 26000, or the Global Reporting Initiative) — and where it does so, must specify WHICH framework was used, or explain why none was used. In practice, given the anticipated CSRD transposition, an increasing number of companies now voluntarily orient their §289b statement toward the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) even ahead of any formal legal requirement to do so.
15

§289e — Omission of Prejudicial Information

A narrower cousin of §286(1), specific to non-financial content
§289e permits omission of information about IMPENDING developments or matters under negotiation where disclosure would, in management's reasoned assessment, be seriously prejudicial to the company's commercial position — PROVIDED such omission does not prevent a fair and balanced understanding of the business's development, performance, position, and the impact of its activity.
16

Worked Example — Applying the §289e Relief to an Impending Negotiation

A narrow, reasoned exception, not a general escape hatch
Example 5: Withholding detail on an in-progress supplier renegotiation
A company is mid-negotiation with a key supplier over improved labour standards in its supply chain, and disclosing the specific negotiating position it has taken in its non-financial statement would materially undermine its leverage in the ongoing talks.
The company MAY omit the specific negotiating detail under §289e, PROVIDED its overall non-financial statement still gives a fair, balanced picture of its human-rights due diligence approach generally — it cannot use §289e to omit its ENTIRE human-rights topic coverage, only the narrow, genuinely sensitive negotiating detail itself.
17

§289f — the Corporate Governance Statement

Compliance declaration, governance practices, board working methods, and diversity information
§289f requires listed AGs specifically to include (either within the Lagebericht or as a separately referenced section/webpage) a corporate governance statement covering: the German Corporate Governance Code declaration of conformity (Entsprechenserklärung, per §161 AktG); relevant governance practices applied beyond strict legal requirements; a description of the management board and supervisory board's working methods and the composition/working methods of their committees; and target/diversity-related information concerning board composition, an area that has been subject to further specific German legislative developments in recent years worth verifying for current precise mechanics.

Critical Judgments & Practical Difficulties

Where judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether a forward-looking assumption is genuinely "material" enough to require explicit disclosure under §289(1) Whether an impending matter is genuinely sensitive enough to justify §289e omission, versus merely uncomfortable to disclose
PRACTICAL DIFFICULTIES

Relationship to IFRS — a Genuinely Distinctive HGB Feature

Comparison — HGB §§289–289f vs IFRS
FeatureHGBIFRS
Mandatory forward-looking narrative reportYES — the Prognosebericht (§289(1))NO mandatory equivalent — Management Commentary is a non-mandatory Practice Statement
Non-financial/sustainability reportingStatutory, HGB-embedded (§289b–e), soon to expand via CSRD transpositionNOT an IFRS requirement — governed separately by IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards (ISSB) where adopted
Takeover-related disclosuresStatutory, embedded in the Lagebericht (§289a)Governed by securities/takeover law separately, not IFRS itself
The Lagebericht as a WHOLE is arguably the single most distinctively "non-IFRS" component of everything this poster series has covered — IFRS financial statements simply do not embed a mandatory, statutorily-defined FORECAST narrative the way HGB's Prognosebericht requires.
