Conceptual Framework Linkage

Refining the layered tax framework for a new tax measure
This revision to ASBJ Statement No. 27 (see the companion poster for the BASE standard's layered corporation/inhabitant/enterprise tax framework) was PROPOSED via exposure draft on January 9, 2026 – the SAME general period Japan's "SPECIAL CORPORATE TAX FOR DEFENSE" (see the companion Practical Solution poster) was being INTRODUCED, suggesting the revision LIKELY addresses HOW this NEW tax measure fits WITHIN the existing current income tax accounting FRAMEWORK.

Scope of the Proposed Revision

All companies subject to Japan's evolving corporate tax system
The PROPOSED revision applies to companies SUBJECT to Japan's current INCOME tax framework, LIKELY clarifying how the RECENTLY introduced special corporate tax for defense should be CLASSIFIED and RECOGNISED alongside the EXISTING layered corporation/inhabitant/enterprise tax structure.

Likely Driver – Accommodating the New Special Corporate Tax for Defense

A plausible connection between two concurrent 2026 developments
GIVEN the CLOSE timing between this exposure draft (January 2026) and the SEPARATE Practical Solution addressing the new special corporate tax for DEFENSE (issued as a TENTATIVE measure in February 2026 – see the companion poster), it is PLAUSIBLE that this revision to the CURRENT Income Taxes standard is intended to provide MORE PERMANENT, Statement-level treatment for the new defense tax – EVENTUALLY superseding or FORMALISING the tentative Practical Solution guidance issued SEPARATELY. This CONNECTION reflects the SURROUNDING timing and context RATHER than confirmed text of the FINAL revised standard.
4

The Continuing Income-Based/Size-Based Enterprise Tax Distinction

A framework this revision would most likely preserve, not replace
The EXISTING distinction between the INCOME-based portion of enterprise tax (treated as current TAX expense) and the SIZE-based "pro forma standard" portion (treated as an OPERATING expense – see the companion ASBJ Statement No. 27 poster) is a WELL-ESTABLISHED, foundational J-GAAP classification PRINCIPLE. A TARGETED revision addressing a NEW tax measure would MOST LIKELY preserve this EXISTING framework, applying its SAME underlying logic (is the new tax genuinely INCOME-based, or based on SOME other factor?) to the new defense tax MEASURE, RATHER than overhauling the framework itself.

Coordination With the Tax Effect Accounting Amendments

Likely paired with corresponding deferred tax guidance
CONSISTENT with the GENERAL ASBJ pattern of COORDINATING current tax (No. 27) and DEFERRED tax (No. 28) guidance TOGETHER (as seen with the October 2025 interim reporting package's No. 40 amendment – see the companion poster), this January 2026 Current Income Taxes exposure draft LIKELY has a CORRESPONDING companion amendment addressing any DEFERRED tax consequences the new defense tax measure MIGHT create.

Worked Example – Classifying the New Defense Tax Within Current Tax Expense

Applying the existing income-based classification logic to a new tax
Example: A company subject to the new defense tax
A company determines its special corporate tax for defense liability is calculated based on its TAXABLE income, similarly to ordinary corporation tax.
CONSISTENT with the EXISTING income-based classification LOGIC (Section 4), the company would LIKELY classify this NEW tax as CURRENT TAX EXPENSE, presented ALONGSIDE corporation tax, inhabitant tax, and the income-based portion of enterprise tax – EXTENDING the EXISTING framework to the NEW tax measure, RATHER than requiring an ENTIRELY new classification approach.
