Conceptual Framework Linkage

A cost-anchored framework for tangible capital assets
ASPE Section 3061 governs the RECOGNITION, MEASUREMENT, and DEPRECIATION of PROPERTY, plant, AND equipment – TANGIBLE assets HELD for USE in the PRODUCTION or SUPPLY of GOODS and SERVICES, for RENTAL to OTHERS, or FOR administrative PURPOSES, and EXPECTED to be USED for MORE than one PERIOD. THE Section applies the COST model EXCLUSIVELY, WITHOUT the REVALUATION alternative IAS 16 permits INTERNATIONALLY (Section 5).
2

Scope

Tangible, long-lived operational assets
This Section applies TO tangible CAPITAL assets USED in OPERATIONS, EXCLUDING agricultural PRODUCTIVE biological ASSETS (SECTION 3041), and ASSETS subject TO their OWN specific GOVERNING Sections.
3

Initial Measurement – Cost

Purchase price plus directly attributable costs
PROPERTY, plant, AND equipment is INITIALLY measured AT cost – the PURCHASE price PLUS any COSTS directly ATTRIBUTABLE to BRINGING the asset TO the LOCATION and CONDITION necessary FOR it to BE capable of OPERATING in the MANNER intended by MANAGEMENT.
4

Componentization

Separately depreciating significant components with different useful lives
WHERE a SIGNIFICANT component OF an item OF property, PLANT, and EQUIPMENT has a MATERIALLY different USEFUL life FROM the ASSET as a WHOLE (e.g. a BUILDING'S roof VERSUS its STRUCTURE), that COMPONENT is DEPRECIATED separately – CONSISTENT with the GENERAL componentization APPROACH also USED internationally.
5

THE COST MODEL ONLY – No Revaluation Option

A meaningful measurement simplification relative to IFRS
ASPE requires the COST model EXCLUSIVELY – property, PLANT, and EQUIPMENT is CARRIED at COST less ACCUMULATED depreciation AND accumulated IMPAIRMENT losses, WITH NO alternative REVALUATION model AVAILABLE. THIS stands IN contrast TO IAS 16, WHICH permits an ENTITY to ELECT a REVALUATION model (CARRYING assets at FAIR value, WITH revaluation SURPLUSES flowing THROUGH other COMPREHENSIVE income). GIVEN ASPE'S complete ABSENCE of any OCI mechanism (see the COMPANION Section 1520 poster), a REVALUATION model would HAVE nowhere APPROPRIATE to RECOGNIZE the RESULTING surplus – REINFORCING why ASPE'S cost-ONLY approach is BOTH a DELIBERATE simplification AND a STRUCTURALLY consistent OUTCOME given the FRAMEWORK'S broader DESIGN.
6

Depreciation – a Systematic and Rational Basis

Straight-line, declining balance, or units of production
DEPRECIATION is RECOGNIZED on a SYSTEMATIC and RATIONAL basis OVER the ASSET'S useful LIFE – COMMON methods INCLUDE straight-LINE, declining BALANCE, and UNITS of PRODUCTION, CHOSEN to BEST reflect the PATTERN in WHICH the ASSET'S economic BENEFITS are CONSUMED.
7

Major Inspections and Overhauls

Capitalized where recognition criteria are met
COSTS OF a MAJOR inspection OR overhaul ARE capitalized WHERE they MEET the GENERAL recognition CRITERIA (i.e. FUTURE economic BENEFITS are PROBABLE, and the COST can BE reliably MEASURED), WITH the CARRYING amount OF any PREVIOUSLY recognized inspection/OVERHAUL cost DERECOGNIZED at THAT time.
8

Subsequent Expenditures

Capitalized only if future benefits genuinely increase
SUBSEQUENT expenditures ARE capitalized ONLY where THEY increase the FUTURE economic BENEFITS the ASSET is EXPECTED to GENERATE, BEYOND its ORIGINALLY assessed STANDARD of PERFORMANCE – ROUTINE repairs and MAINTENANCE are EXPENSED as INCURRED.
9

Worked Example – Componentized Depreciation

Depreciating a roof separately from a building's structure
Example: A commercial building with a shorter-lived roof
A company acquires a commercial building for $4,000,000, with the roof component valued at $500,000 (15-year useful life) and the remaining structure valued at $3,500,000 (40-year useful life).
The company DEPRECIATES the ROOF SEPARATELY at $500,000 ÷ 15 = $33,333 per year, and the STRUCTURE at $3,500,000 ÷ 40 = $87,500 per year, FOR a TOTAL annual DEPRECIATION of $120,833 – REFLECTING each COMPONENT'S own DISTINCT useful LIFE.
10

Worked Example – Capitalizing a Major Overhaul

Recognizing a new cost while derecognizing the old
Example: A manufacturing equipment overhaul
A company performs a major overhaul on production equipment, costing $180,000, extending the equipment's useful life and meeting the general recognition criteria. The equipment's carrying amount includes $40,000 remaining from a previous overhaul, now fully superseded.
The company CAPITALIZES the NEW $180,000 overhaul COST, and DERECOGNIZES the REMAINING $40,000 carrying AMOUNT of the SUPERSEDED prior OVERHAUL, RECOGNIZING it AS an EXPENSE – NET effect on the ASSET'S carrying amount OF $180,000 − $40,000 = $140,000 increase.

Quick-Reference Glossary
