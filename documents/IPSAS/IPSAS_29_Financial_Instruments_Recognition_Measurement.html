An Unusual Status — What's Deleted, What Remains

Neither fully live nor fully withdrawn
IPSAS 29 is unlike any other Standard in this library so far. When IPSAS 41, Financial Instruments, was issued in August 2018, it absorbed almost ALL of IPSAS 29's substantive recognition and measurement content — paragraphs 11 through 79 (initial recognition, classification, subsequent measurement, reclassification, derecognition, impairment) are now simply marked [Deleted] in the current Handbook. Even the OBJECTIVE paragraph itself (para. 1) is [Deleted].
⚠️ What SURVIVES: paragraphs 80–126 — a narrow, OPTIONAL set of legacy HEDGE ACCOUNTING requirements. IPSAS 41 (para. 177) permits an entity to ELECT to continue applying IPSAS 29's hedge accounting model instead of adopting IPSAS 41's own hedge accounting requirements. This poster covers only that surviving, optional slice.
Why this matters for the poster library
Most withdrawn IPSAS in this library (6, 7, 8, 13, 15, 17, 25) have a clean redirect: the whole Standard is gone, replaced by a successor. IPSAS 29 doesn't fit that pattern — it's PARTIALLY superseded, with a genuinely optional accounting POLICY CHOICE still available to preparers today. Recognition, measurement, classification, and impairment of financial instruments are governed by IPSAS 41 for ALL entities. Hedge accounting is the one area where an entity can choose between IPSAS 41's model and this legacy model.

Scope — Applying the Legacy Hedge Accounting Option (paras. 2, 9–10)

The current scope paragraph, in full
"This Standard shall be applied by all entities to all financial instruments within the scope of IPSAS 41 IF, and to the extent that: (a) IPSAS 41 permits the hedge accounting requirements of this Standard to be applied; and (b) the financial instrument is part of a hedging relationship that qualifies for hedge accounting in accordance with this Standard" (para. 2, as amended).
Paragraphs 3–8 are [Deleted]. This is now a POINTER paragraph — it exists to define WHEN the remainder of this Standard applies, rather than to establish independent recognition/measurement scope.
Terminology borrowed from elsewhere
Terms used in the surviving hedge accounting paragraphs are defined in IPSAS 28 and IPSAS 41, not restated independently here (para. 9) — e.g. amortized cost, derecognition, derivative, effective interest method, financial liability at fair value through surplus or deficit, financial guarantee contract, firm commitment, forecast transaction, hedge effectiveness, hedged item, hedging instrument, held for trading, regular way purchase or sale, and transaction costs.
HEDGE EFFECTIVENESS is defined directly in para. 10: the degree to which changes in the fair value or cash flows of the hedged item attributable to a hedged risk are OFFSET by changes in the fair value or cash flows of the hedging instrument.

Hedge Accounting — Qualifying Conditions & Three Hedge Types (paras. 96–98)

Three types of hedging relationship (para. 96)
Fair value hedge — a hedge of exposure to changes in the fair value of a recognized asset/liability, or an unrecognized firm commitment, attributable to a particular risk and affecting surplus/deficit Cash flow hedge — a hedge of exposure to variability in cash flows attributable to a particular risk on a recognized asset/liability, or a highly probable forecast transaction, affecting surplus/deficit Hedge of a net investment in a foreign operation, as defined in IPSAS 4
A hedge of the foreign currency risk of a FIRM COMMITMENT may be accounted for as EITHER a fair value hedge or a cash flow hedge (para. 97).
Five conditions — ALL required for hedge accounting (para. 98)
Formal designation and documentation at hedge INCEPTION — hedging instrument, hedged item, nature of risk, effectiveness assessment method The hedge is expected to be HIGHLY EFFECTIVE at offsetting fair value/cash flow changes attributable to the hedged risk For cash flow hedges: the forecast transaction is HIGHLY PROBABLE, presenting an exposure that could affect surplus/deficit Hedge effectiveness can be RELIABLY MEASURED The hedge is assessed on an ONGOING basis, and determined to have ACTUALLY been highly effective throughout the reporting periods designated
4

Fair Value Hedges (paras. 99–105)

The mechanics — both sides through surplus/deficit
Gain/loss on the HEDGING INSTRUMENT → surplus or deficit
Gain/loss on the HEDGED ITEM (attributable to the hedged risk) → adjusts its carrying amount, ALSO to surplus or deficit
The two largely offset — that's the point of the hedge. Applies whether the hedged item is otherwise carried at cost, or is a financial asset at fair value through net assets/equity under IPSAS 41 para. 41 (para. 99).
Discontinuation & amortization
Discontinue PROSPECTIVELY if: the hedging instrument expires/is sold/terminated/exercised; the hedge no longer meets the para. 98 criteria; or the entity revokes the designation (para. 102) Carrying-amount adjustments to a hedged item using the effective interest method are AMORTIZED to surplus or deficit, beginning no later than when the hedged item stops being adjusted for the hedged risk (para. 103)
A clearing-counterparty NOVATION required by new laws/regulations is NOT treated as an expiration or termination of the hedging instrument, provided changes are limited to what's needed to effect that replacement (para. 102(a)) — a modern addition addressing post-financial-crisis derivatives clearing mandates.
5

Cash Flow Hedges (paras. 106–112)

The effective/ineffective split
Effective portion of the hedging instrument's gain/loss → net assets/equity (Statement of Changes in Net Assets/Equity)
Ineffective portion → surplus or deficit
The net assets/equity component is adjusted to the LESSER (in absolute terms) of the cumulative hedging-instrument gain/loss since inception, and the cumulative fair value change of the hedged item's expected future cash flows since inception (para. 107).
Reclassification when the forecast transaction occurs
Results in a FINANCIAL asset/liability → reclassify the net-assets/equity amount into surplus/deficit in the SAME period(s) the hedged cash flows affect surplus/deficit (para. 108) Results in a NON-FINANCIAL asset/liability → EITHER reclassify on the same basis, OR remove the amount and fold it into the asset/liability's initial cost — a consistent POLICY CHOICE applied to all such hedges (paras. 109–110) Forecast transaction NO LONGER expected to occur → the cumulative amount is reclassified to surplus/deficit IMMEDIATELY (para. 112(c))
Discontinuation triggers mirror §4's fair value hedge list — instrument expiry/sale/termination, no longer meeting para. 98 criteria, or revocation (para. 112).
6

Hedges of a Net Investment in a Foreign Operation (para. 113)

Accounted for like a cash flow hedge
Including hedges of a monetary item accounted for as part of the net investment (IPSAS 4). Follows the SAME effective/ineffective split as cash flow hedges:
Effective portion of the hedging instrument's gain/loss → net assets/equity, via the Statement of Changes in Net Assets/Equity Ineffective portion → surplus or deficit
On DISPOSAL of the foreign operation → the cumulative effective-portion amount is reclassified from net assets/equity to surplus or deficit, per IPSAS 4 paras. 56–57
A separate package of TEMPORARY EXCEPTIONS (paras. 113A–113N) addresses hedging relationships directly affected by INTEREST RATE BENCHMARK REFORM (e.g. LIBOR transition) — narrowly scoped relief that applies only where the reform creates genuine uncertainty about a hedged interest rate benchmark.

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Comparison with IAS 39

Comparison — IPSAS 29 vs IAS 39
IPSAS 29 was originally drawn from IAS 39, Financial Instruments: Recognition and Measurement (amendments up to 31 December 2008, plus the IASB's April 2009 Improvements to IFRS). IAS 39 was ITSELF later replaced by IFRS 9 in the private sector — a transition IPSAS mirrored via IPSAS 41, though IPSAS chose to retain a legacy hedge accounting OPTION rather than deleting IPSAS 29 outright.
TopicDifference
Concessionary loans & nil-consideration guaranteesIPSAS 29 (originally) contained additional application guidance IAS 39 lacks — a public-sector-specific need
Terminology"Statement of financial performance," "net assets/equity" (IPSAS 29) vs "statement of comprehensive income," "equity" (IAS 39)
Embedded IFRIC guidanceIFRIC 9 (Reassessment of Embedded Derivatives) and IFRIC 16 (Hedges of a Net Investment) are embedded as AUTHORITATIVE appendices — under IFRS, these are separate documents
Current retention strategyIAS 39's hedge accounting was fully superseded by IFRS 9; IPSAS retained IPSAS 29's hedge model as an explicit CHOICE alongside IPSAS 41's own hedge accounting — a structural divergence unique to the public sector standard

Statement / Process Mapping

Decision map — does IPSAS 29 even apply to this instrument?
1. Is the question about RECOGNITION, MEASUREMENT, CLASSIFICATION, or IMPAIRMENT of a financial instrument? → apply IPSAS 41. IPSAS 29 has NOTHING left to say on this (§1) 2. Is the question about HEDGE ACCOUNTING specifically? → the entity has a CHOICE: IPSAS 41's own hedge accounting model, OR this Standard's legacy model (§2) 3. Has the entity ELECTED to continue applying IPSAS 29's hedge accounting? → apply the para. 98 qualifying conditions, then route to fair value (§4), cash flow (§5), or net investment (§6) hedge mechanics as applicable 4. Does the hedging relationship still meet ALL FIVE qualifying conditions? → continue hedge accounting. If any fails → discontinue PROSPECTIVELY from that point

Quick-Reference Glossary

Hedge effectiveness
The degree to which changes in the fair value/cash flows of a hedged item attributable to a hedged risk are offset by changes in the hedging instrument.
Fair value hedge
A hedge of exposure to fair value changes in a recognized asset/liability or unrecognized firm commitment, attributable to a particular risk.
Cash flow hedge
A hedge of exposure to cash flow variability attributable to a particular risk, associated with a recognized item or a highly probable forecast transaction.
Firm commitment
A binding agreement for the exchange of a specified quantity of resources at a specified price on a specified future date(s).
Forecast transaction
An uncommitted but anticipated future transaction.
Reclassification adjustment
An amount reclassified from net assets/equity to surplus or deficit in the current period.
Financial statement key
SOFPerf Statement of financial performance NA/E Net assets/equity (statement of changes)
