Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why (almost) every lease is now on the balance sheet
The Conceptual Framework defines an asset as a resource CONTROLLED as a result of past events, from which future economic benefits are expected. IFRS 16 applies this directly: a lease conveys the RIGHT TO USE an identified asset, which is itself a resource the lessee controls – meaning virtually EVERY lease creates a genuine asset (and corresponding liability) for the lessee, abolishing IAS 17's off-balance-sheet operating lease treatment entirely.
HOW IFRS 16 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Faithful representation – recognising a right-of-use asset and lease liability for ALL leases (with narrow exemptions) ensures the SOFP reflects genuine economic obligations previously kept off-balance-sheet Comparability – a SINGLE lessee model eliminates the classification judgement (finance vs operating) that made comparing lessees under IAS 17 difficult Substance over form – the right to use an asset is a genuine resource regardless of whether legal title ever transfers
IFRS 16 was a DIRECT response to long-standing criticism that IAS 17's off-balance-sheet operating lease treatment understated corporate leverage – a criticism that persisted for decades before finally being addressed.
The single lessee model vs the retained lessor dual model
PartyModel
LesseeSINGLE model – right-of-use asset + lease liability for (almost) all leases
LessorDUAL model RETAINED – finance vs operating classification, largely unchanged from IAS 17
ℹ️ The reform focused ENTIRELY on the lessee side – lessor accounting was judged to already faithfully represent economic substance and was left substantively intact.

Scope & Applicability

What IFRS 16 covers
IFRS 16 applies to ALL leases, EXCEPT: leases to explore for minerals/oil/gas; leases of biological assets (IAS 41); service concession arrangements (IFRIC 12); licences of intellectual property granted by a lessor (IFRS 15); and rights held under licensing agreements for films, patents, copyrights (IAS 38).
A lessee MAY elect NOT to apply IFRS 16 to leases of INTANGIBLE assets (an optional scope exclusion).
Key definitions you must know
Lease
A contract, or part of a contract, that conveys the right to CONTROL the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration.
Right-of-use asset
An asset representing a lessee's right to use an underlying asset for the lease term.
Incremental borrowing rate
The rate a lessee would have to pay to borrow, over a similar term and security, funds necessary to obtain an asset of similar value – used to discount lease payments if the rate implicit in the lease is not readily determinable.

Identifying a Lease (with worked example)

The two-part test – identified asset + control over use
A contract IS (or contains) a lease if it conveys the right to CONTROL the use of an IDENTIFIED asset for a period of time. This requires: (1) an IDENTIFIED asset (explicitly or implicitly specified, without substantive substitution rights held by the supplier); and (2) the customer has the right to OBTAIN SUBSTANTIALLY ALL the economic benefits from use, AND the right to DIRECT the use of the asset throughout the period.
Example 1: Testing for a lease – substantive substitution rights
A customer contracts for the use of a specific truck for 3 years to transport goods, with the customer deciding routes, timing, and cargo. The supplier retains a theoretical CONTRACTUAL right to substitute the truck for another, but has NO PRACTICAL ability to do so economically (substitution would require significant cost/effort with no real benefit to the supplier), and has NEVER exercised this right historically.
Since the supplier's substitution right is NOT substantive (no practical ability/economic benefit), the truck IS an identified asset. The customer directs its use (routes, timing, cargo) and obtains substantially all economic benefits – this contract IS a LEASE under IFRS 16, requiring right-of-use asset and lease liability recognition by the customer (lessee).

Lessee – Initial Recognition & Measurement (with worked example)

The initial measurement formulas
Lease liability = Present value of lease payments not yet paid, discounted at the rate implicit in the lease (or the lessee's incremental borrowing rate)
Right-of-use asset = Lease liability + initial direct costs + prepaid lease payments + estimated restoration costs − lease incentives received
Worked example – initial recognition
Example 2: Initial recognition of a 5-year office lease
A lessee enters a 5-year lease with annual payments of $50,000 in arrears, discounted at an incremental borrowing rate of 6%. Present value of the 5 payments = $210,618. Initial direct costs incurred by the lessee: $8,000.
Lease liability = $210,618 (PV of payments)
Right-of-use asset = 210,618 + 8,000 = $218,618

Lessee – Subsequent Measurement (with worked example)

Depreciation and effective interest, running in parallel
The right-of-use asset is DEPRECIATED (generally straight-line) over the SHORTER of the lease term and the asset's useful life (or over the useful life if ownership transfers/a purchase option is reasonably certain to be exercised). The lease liability is measured using the EFFECTIVE INTEREST METHOD, with interest expense recognised separately from the depreciation charge.
Example 3: Year 1 subsequent measurement
Continuing Example 2: right-of-use asset $218,618, depreciated straight-line over 5 years. Lease liability $210,618 at 6%, with a $50,000 payment at the end of Year 1.
Depreciation = 218,618 / 5 = $43,724
Interest expense = 210,618 × 6% = $12,637
Liability after payment = 210,618 + 12,637 − 50,000 = $173,255
TWO separate P&L charges result: depreciation ($43,724) and interest ($12,637) – TOTAL Year 1 expense of $56,361, FRONT-LOADED compared to a straight-line operating lease expense of $50,000/year under the old IAS 17 model.

Short-Term & Low-Value Exemptions

The narrow "off-balance-sheet" survivors
A lessee MAY elect NOT to recognise a right-of-use asset and lease liability for: SHORT-TERM leases (lease term of 12 months or less, with no purchase option); and leases of LOW-VALUE underlying assets (assessed on an absolute basis when new, e.g. laptops, small office furniture – NOT dependent on the lessee's size). For these, lease payments are recognised as an EXPENSE on a straight-line (or other systematic) basis – essentially preserving IAS 17's old operating lease treatment for these two narrow categories only.
7

Lease Modifications

Separate lease vs remeasurement of the existing lease
A modification INCREASING scope AND consideration by an amount COMMENSURATE with the stand-alone price for the increase is accounted for as a SEPARATE, new lease. Other modifications (decreasing scope, or increasing scope without commensurate pricing) require REMEASUREMENT of the existing lease liability using a revised discount rate, with a corresponding adjustment to the right-of-use asset.
8

Lessor Accounting

Retained largely unchanged from IAS 17
A lessor classifies each lease as a FINANCE lease (substantially all risks/rewards transferred – derecognise the asset, recognise a net investment receivable) or an OPERATING lease (asset retained on the lessor's SOFP, lease income recognised straight-line) – using indicators essentially IDENTICAL to IAS 17's own classification test.
9

Sale and Leaseback Transactions

A materially reformed area versus IAS 17
If a sale qualifies as a genuine "sale" under IFRS 15's control-transfer test, the seller-lessee recognises a right-of-use asset for the PROPORTION of the previous carrying amount relating to the right retained, and recognises ONLY the gain/loss relating to the RIGHTS TRANSFERRED to the buyer-lessor – a materially more complex, RESTRICTIVE approach than IAS 17's deferred-gain mechanism.

Disclosure Requirements

Required disclosures
LESSEE DISCLOSURES
Depreciation charge for right-of-use assets by class, interest expense on lease liabilities Expense relating to short-term and low-value asset leases Maturity analysis of lease liabilities (similar to IFRS 7's own maturity analysis)
LESSOR DISCLOSURES
Similar to IAS 17's own disclosures – finance lease reconciliations, operating lease future minimum payments

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether a contract contains a lease (identified asset, control over use) Determining the lease term, including assessing whether extension/termination options are reasonably certain to be exercised
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Incremental borrowing rate, where the rate implicit in the lease cannot be readily determined Estimated restoration/dismantling costs included in the right-of-use asset
⚠️ Determining the LEASE TERM (including renewal options reasonably certain to be exercised) is one of the MOST frequently tested and consequential judgements under IFRS 16, directly affecting both the asset and liability measurement.

IFRS vs US GAAP

Comparison – IFRS 16 vs US GAAP (ASC 842)
FeatureIFRS (IFRS 16)US GAAP
Lessee modelSINGLE model – all leases create a right-of-use asset and liabilityDUAL model RETAINED – lessees STILL classify leases as "finance" or "operating," with DIFFERENT P&L expense patterns for each
Operating lease P&L pattern (lessee)Front-loaded (depreciation + declining interest), same as finance leasesSINGLE straight-line total lease expense for operating leases – a KEY remaining divergence from IFRS
Low-value asset exemptionAvailable (absolute value-based)NOT available under US GAAP – no direct equivalent exemption
This is one of the MOST significant remaining IFRS/US GAAP divergences – IFRS 16 abolished the lessee dual model ENTIRELY, while ASC 842 retained it, meaning IFRS and US GAAP lessees can report MATERIALLY different P&L expense patterns for economically similar leases.

Lease term
The non-cancellable period of a lease, plus periods covered by extension options reasonably certain to be exercised, and periods covered by termination options reasonably certain NOT to be exercised.
Commencement date
The date the lessor makes the underlying asset available for use by the lessee.
