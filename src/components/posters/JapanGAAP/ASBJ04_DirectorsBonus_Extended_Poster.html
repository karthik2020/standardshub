Conceptual Framework Linkage

Compensation for service rendered is an expense, not a distribution of profit
ASBJ Statement No. 4 addresses a GENUINELY significant reform in Japanese accounting practice: HOW should a company's payment of a BONUS to its DIRECTORS (役員賞与) be characterised? Under LONG-STANDING Japanese practice (rooted in the old Commercial Code), director bonuses were traditionally treated as an "APPROPRIATION OF RETAINED EARNINGS" – a DISTRIBUTION of profit, decided by the shareholders' general meeting ALONGSIDE dividends – rather than as compensation EXPENSE. This Statement ESTABLISHED that directors' bonuses must instead be recognised as an EXPENSE, in the period the related SERVICES were rendered – a fundamental, PRINCIPLE-DRIVEN change.
2

Scope

All companies paying bonuses to directors, executive officers, and similar officers
This Statement applies to ALL companies that pay BONUSES to DIRECTORS (取締役), AUDITORS/company auditors (監査役), and EXECUTIVE OFFICERS (執行役) – governing the RECOGNITION timing and classification of such payments.
3

The Old Practice – Directors' Bonus as an Appropriation of Retained Earnings

Treated like a dividend, not a cost of doing business
Under the OLD Commercial Code-era practice, a director's bonus was CONCEPTUALLY treated much like a DIVIDEND – a DISTRIBUTION of the company's ALREADY-EARNED profit, requiring approval at the shareholders' GENERAL MEETING (株主総会) ALONGSIDE the dividend resolution – appearing as an APPROPRIATION of Retained Earnings (利益剰余金の処分) rather than as an EXPENSE reducing the period's reported profit. This meant a company's REPORTED profit for the year did NOT reflect the ECONOMIC cost of director compensation actually EARNED during that year.
4

THE KEY REFORM – Recognition as an Expense in the Period of Service

Director compensation for services rendered is genuinely a cost of the business
ASBJ Statement No. 4 ESTABLISHED that a directors' bonus RELATING to services rendered DURING a given fiscal year must be recognised as an EXPENSE in the STATEMENT OF INCOME (損益計算書) FOR THAT YEAR – REGARDLESS of whether the SPECIFIC amount has yet been FORMALLY approved by the shareholders' general meeting. This reflects the PRINCIPLE that compensation for services ALREADY rendered represents a genuine ECONOMIC COST of the period, NOT a discretionary distribution of ALREADY-earned profit decided AFTER the fact – a MEANINGFUL conceptual and practical shift bringing Japanese practice CLOSER to how employee and management compensation is treated INTERNATIONALLY.
5

Recognition Timing – Accrual Before Shareholders' Meeting Approval

A liability accrued for the estimated amount, ahead of formal approval
Where a director's bonus RELATES to services rendered during the fiscal year, but has NOT yet been formally APPROVED by the shareholders' general meeting as of the balance sheet date, the company ACCRUES a LIABILITY for the ESTIMATED amount expected to be approved – recognising the related EXPENSE in the SAME period the services were rendered, rather than DEFERRING recognition until the LATER approval date.
6

Presentation in the Statement of Income

Presented as an operating expense, reducing reported profit
Directors' bonus expense is presented WITHIN the Statement of Income as an ORDINARY operating (or, depending on classification, a selling, general and administrative) EXPENSE – REDUCING the company's reported OPERATING and NET profit for the period, in CONTRAST to the OLD practice where it never touched the Statement of Income at all, appearing SOLELY as a post-profit APPROPRIATION.
7

Worked Example – Accruing a Directors' Bonus Before Approval

Recognising the expense ahead of the shareholders' meeting
Example: A fiscal year-end bonus accrual
A company's fiscal year ends March 31. Directors have rendered services throughout the year, and management estimates the board will propose (and the shareholders' meeting in June will likely approve) a total directors' bonus of ¥80,000,000 relating to the fiscal year just ended.
At March 31 (the fiscal year end), the company recognises an ¥80,000,000 EXPENSE in the Statement of Income for the year, with a corresponding ACCRUED LIABILITY on the Balance Sheet – even though the shareholders' meeting FORMALLY approving the specific amount doesn't occur until JUNE, several months LATER.
8

Worked Example – Contrasting Old vs New Treatment

The same ¥80,000,000, two very different presentations
Example: The same bonus, before and after the reform
Using the same ¥80,000,000 directors' bonus from the prior example.
Under the OLD practice: the ¥80,000,000 would NOT appear in the Statement of Income at all – it would be presented SOLELY as an APPROPRIATION of Retained Earnings, AFTER net profit is already determined, leaving REPORTED profit UNAFFECTED.
Under ASBJ Statement No. 4: the SAME ¥80,000,000 REDUCES reported operating and net profit DIRECTLY, as a genuine EXPENSE of the period – a MATERIAL difference in REPORTED profitability for companies with SIGNIFICANT director bonus arrangements.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Directors' bonus (役員賞与)
A bonus paid to directors, auditors, or executive officers, recognised as an expense in the period services are rendered under ASBJ Statement No. 4.
Appropriation of Retained Earnings (利益剰余金の処分)
The former practice of treating director bonuses as a post-profit distribution decided by the shareholders' general meeting, rather than an expense – now abandoned.
