Conceptual Framework Linkage

Sector-specific application guidance, not a competing accounting framework
A Statement of Recommended Practice (SORP) provides SECTOR-SPECIFIC guidance on how to APPLY FRS 102 (or, occasionally, other underlying standards) within a PARTICULAR industry – charities, higher/further education, registered social housing providers, limited liability partnerships (LLPs), retirement benefit (pension) schemes, and investment/authorised funds each have their OWN SORP. A SORP does NOT establish a SEPARATE accounting framework – it sits WITHIN, and supplements, the "new UK GAAP" hierarchy (FRS 100–105), addressing genuinely SECTOR-SPECIFIC transactions and presentation needs that the general standards don't fully anticipate.
2

Scope – SORP-Making Bodies and Sectors Covered

Recognised bodies developing sector guidance under FRC oversight
SORPs are developed by SORP-MAKING BODIES formally recognised by the Financial Reporting Council (FRC) – sector representative organisations with the expertise to translate general FRS 102 principles into PRACTICAL, industry-specific guidance. The MOST prominent SORPs cover CHARITIES, FURTHER AND HIGHER EDUCATION institutions, REGISTERED SOCIAL HOUSING providers, LLPs, RETIREMENT BENEFIT schemes, and INVESTMENT/AUTHORISED FUNDS.
3

Status – "Comply or Explain," Not a Separate GAAP

A recommended practice with real practical force, though not itself an FRS
A SORP is NOT itself a Financial Reporting Standard with the SAME binding status as FRS 102 – but DEPARTURE from a relevant SORP's RECOMMENDATIONS must be JUSTIFIED and DISCLOSED, giving SORPs a genuine "COMPLY OR EXPLAIN"-STYLE practical force within their sector. In PRACTICE, entities within a covered sector almost UNIVERSALLY follow the applicable SORP, given the STRONG expectation of compliance and the SORP's role in demonstrating genuine sector-appropriate "true and fair" presentation.
4

The Charities SORP – the Statement of Financial Activities (SoFA)

A distinctive combined statement, unique to charity-sector reporting
The most PROMINENT SORP – the CHARITIES SORP (FRS 102) – requires charities to present a "STATEMENT OF FINANCIAL ACTIVITIES" (SoFA) IN PLACE of a conventional Profit and Loss Account – a DISTINCTIVE statement COMBINING all incoming resources (donations, grants, trading income, investment income) and resources expended (charitable activities, raising funds, governance costs), analysed by FUND type (Section 5), arriving at a NET movement in funds for the period – reflecting the fundamentally DIFFERENT nature of charitable, non-reciprocal income compared to ordinary commercial revenue.
5

Fund Accounting – Restricted, Unrestricted, Designated, and Endowment Funds

A UK charity-sector parallel to US GAAP's donor-restriction net asset classification
Fund typeDescription
Unrestricted fundsFunds the trustees can use FREELY for any of the charity's purposes
Restricted fundsFunds a DONOR has stipulated must be used for a SPECIFIC purpose
Designated fundsUNRESTRICTED funds the TRUSTEES (not a donor) have earmarked for a specific future purpose – the charity's own internal, reversible designation
Endowment fundsFunds a donor requires to be held as CAPITAL (PERMANENT endowment, retained indefinitely) or for a SPECIFIED period (EXPENDABLE endowment), rather than spent immediately
This fund STRUCTURE echoes – but is not identical in terminology to – US GAAP's ASC 958 "net assets WITH donor restrictions" vs "net assets WITHOUT donor restrictions" classification, and its board-designated "quasi-endowment" concept (see the companion ASC 958 poster).
6

Other Prominent SORPs

Higher/further education, social housing, LLPs, and pension schemes
The FURTHER AND HIGHER EDUCATION SORP addresses university and college-specific transactions (research grants, tuition fee recognition, specific fund accounting). The REGISTERED SOCIAL HOUSING PROVIDERS SORP addresses housing association-specific matters (social housing property costing, capital grants). The LLPs SORP addresses partnership-specific equity and members' capital presentation. The PENSIONS SORP addresses retirement benefit SCHEME (not sponsoring employer) financial statements – broadly analogous in PURPOSE to the US GAAP ASC 960/962/965 plan accounting Topics, presenting a scheme's OWN net assets and actuarial liability information.

Worked Example – the Charities SORP's SoFA in Outline

Combining all resource flows into a single statement
Example: A charity's simplified SoFA
A charity receives £400,000 of unrestricted donations, £150,000 of restricted grant income for a specific project, and incurs £480,000 of charitable activity costs and £60,000 of governance costs during the year.
The SoFA presents TOTAL incoming resources of £550,000 (£400,000 unrestricted + £150,000 restricted), LESS total resources expended of £540,000 (£480,000 + £60,000), arriving at a net movement in funds of £10,000 for the year – with the movement further ANALYSED between the unrestricted and restricted fund COLUMNS, showing how each fund type individually moved.
8

Worked Example – Classifying a Donation Into the Correct Fund

Donor stipulation drives fund classification
Example: A donor-specified building appeal
A charity receives a £50,000 donation, with the donor's letter explicitly stating the funds must be used ONLY toward the charity's ongoing building renovation appeal.
Since the DONOR has stipulated a specific PURPOSE, the £50,000 is classified as a RESTRICTED FUND – it cannot be used for the charity's general purposes until (and unless) applied toward the building renovation, distinct from how the trustees' OWN internal earmarking of unrestricted funds (a designated fund) would be treated.

Relationship to IAS/IFRS and to US GAAP's ASC 958

Comparison across frameworks
FeatureUK SORPs (e.g. Charities SORP)IFRS / US GAAP
Dedicated not-for-profit standardYES – the Charities SORP built on FRS 102IFRS: NO dedicated standard. US GAAP: YES – ASC 958
Fund/net asset classificationRestricted / unrestricted / designated / endowment fundsASC 958: net assets with/without donor restrictions (simplified from a former three-tier system)
Combined activities statementStatement of Financial Activities (SoFA)ASC 958 uses a Statement of Activities, conceptually similar but not identically structured
IFRS itself has NO dedicated not-for-profit contribution accounting standard (see the ASC 958 poster's discussion of this gap) – the UK's Charities SORP, built on FRS 102, fills an analogous role to ASC 958 within the UK/Irish reporting landscape, even though the two frameworks' specific fund/net-asset terminology and statement structures differ.

The combined income, expenditure, and fund movement statement required by the Charities SORP in place of a conventional Profit and Loss Account.
Restricted fund

Designated fund
Unrestricted funds a charity's own trustees have earmarked for a specific future purpose, remaining reversible and within the charity's own discretion.
