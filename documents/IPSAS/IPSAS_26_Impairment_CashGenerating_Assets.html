Objective, Scope & Definitions (paras. 1–13)

Objective & scope
Prescribes the procedures an entity applies to determine whether a CASH-GENERATING asset is impaired, ensures impairment losses are recognized, specifies when to reverse them, and prescribes disclosures. The sister standard to IPSAS 21, which covers non-cash-generating assets.
Inventories (IPSAS 12); binding arrangement/contract-cost assets (IPSAS 47) Financial assets within IPSAS 41's scope Investment property measured at fair value (IPSAS 16) Deferred tax assets; assets from employee benefits (IPSAS 39) Biological assets at fair value less costs to sell (IPSAS 27) Held-for-sale assets/disposal groups (IPSAS 44)
Key definitions (para. 13)
Cash-generating unit
The SMALLEST identifiable group of assets held with the primary objective of generating a commercial return, generating cash inflows from continuing use that are largely independent of other assets/groups.
Recoverable amount
The HIGHER of an asset's (or CGU's) fair value less costs of disposal, and its value in use.
Value in use (cash-generating asset)
The present value of the ESTIMATED FUTURE CASH FLOWS expected from continuing use, plus disposal at the end of useful life.
ℹ️ Note the terminology shift from IPSAS 21: "recoverable AMOUNT" and "cash flows" here, vs. "recoverable SERVICE amount" and "service potential" in IPSAS 21 — reflecting the commercial vs. service-delivery orientation of each Standard.

Cash-Generating vs Non-Cash-Generating Assets (paras. 14–18A)

Same test as IPSAS 21, mirrored
Cash-generating assets are held with the PRIMARY OBJECTIVE of generating a commercial return, deployed the way a profit-oriented entity would deploy them. The default PRESUMPTION (given most public sector entities' overall objectives) is that assets are NON-cash-generating — this Standard is the exception case, not the default.
Goodwill is ALWAYS treated as cash-generating for impairment purposes — assessed exclusively under this Standard, never IPSAS 21 (para. 18A).
Worked example — a municipal parking garage
A commercially-run parking garage, currently at 25% capacity utilization
Management has determined the garage generates a commercial rate of return when usage reaches 75% capacity and above. Usage has declined without a corresponding increase in parking charges.
The garage is IMPAIRED — its carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount, reflecting a decline in the future economic benefits embodied in the asset (para. 20).

Identifying an Asset That May Be Impaired (paras. 20A–30)

The same minimum indications framework as IPSAS 21 (para. 25)
EXTERNAL SOURCES
Observable market value declines beyond normal expectations Significant adverse changes in technological, market, economic, or legal environment Increases in market interest rates affecting the discount rate and likely reducing recoverable amount materially
INTERNAL SOURCES
Evidence of obsolescence or physical damage Significant adverse changes in extent/manner of use — idle assets, restructuring/disposal plans, useful life reassessed A halt in construction before completion Internal reporting evidence the asset's ECONOMIC PERFORMANCE is worse than expected
Same intangible-asset annual test rule as IPSAS 21: indefinite-life and not-yet-available-for-use intangibles are tested at least annually regardless of indication (cross-ref).

Measuring Recoverable Amount (paras. 31–42)

Fair value less costs of disposal
Recoverable amount = HIGHER of (fair value less costs of disposal) and (value in use)
Same shortcut logic as IPSAS 21: if either amount already exceeds carrying amount, no impairment, and the other need not be determined. Fair value follows a similar evidence hierarchy: binding sale agreement → active market bid price → best available information.
Fair value ≠ value in use — a critical distinction (para. 66A)
Fair value reflects what MARKET PARTICIPANTS would pay; value in use reflects entity-SPECIFIC factors. Fair value does NOT reflect: additional value from grouping assets into a portfolio, synergies with other entity assets, legal rights/restrictions specific to the current owner, or owner-specific tax benefits/burdens.
⚠️ Unlike IPSAS 21, a genuinely FORCED SALE is treated as a valid fair value less costs of disposal reflection under IAS 36 — but IPSAS 26 explicitly does NOT treat a forced sale as reflecting fair value less costs of disposal (a key departure noted in §13).

Value in Use — Cash Flow Projections (paras. 43–70)

Five elements to reflect (para. 43)
Estimated future cash flows expected from the asset Expected variations in the amount/timing of those cash flows Time value of money (current market risk-free rate) The price for bearing the asset's inherent uncertainty Other factors (e.g. illiquidity) market participants would price in
Value in use = PV(future cash flows), using a pre-tax discount rate reflecting current market assessments of time value of money and asset-specific risk
Budget-based projection rules (para. 46)
Base projections on REASONABLE, SUPPORTABLE assumptions — greater weight to EXTERNAL evidence Use the most recent management-approved budgets/forecasts, covering a MAXIMUM of 5 years (unless a longer period is justified) — EXCLUDING cash flows from future restructurings or performance-enhancing improvements Extrapolate beyond that period using a STEADY OR DECLINING growth rate, capped at the long-term average for the relevant products/industry/market unless a higher rate is justified
Cash outflow estimates EXCLUDE financing activities and income tax (para. 63) — consistent with the pre-tax discount rate. Once an entity is COMMITTED to a restructuring (IPSAS 19), its cost savings/benefits ARE reflected in value in use going forward (para. 60).

Recognizing & Measuring an Impairment Loss (paras. 71–75)

Same recognition mechanics as IPSAS 21 (§7 of that Standard)
If recoverable amount < carrying amount → reduce to recoverable amount = IMPAIRMENT LOSS, recognized in surplus/deficit (or revaluation surplus first, if the asset is revalued)
Non-revalued asset → surplus or deficit immediately Revalued asset (IPSAS 31/45 current value model) → revaluation decrease first, to the extent of the revaluation surplus for that asset/class; excess to surplus or deficit Beyond zero → a liability is recognized only if ANOTHER standard requires it (e.g. IPSAS 19 dismantling obligations) Depreciation/amortization is adjusted PROSPECTIVELY over the remaining useful life after the loss

Cash-Generating Units — Identification & Carrying Amount (paras. 77–90)

When to step up from asset to unit
If an individual asset's recoverable amount CANNOT be determined — because its value in use can't be estimated close to fair value less costs of disposal, AND it doesn't generate largely independent cash inflows on its own — recoverable amount is determined for its CASH-GENERATING UNIT instead: the SMALLEST group of assets generating largely independent cash inflows (paras. 78–79).
✓ Active market test (para. 81): if an active market exists for an asset's OUTPUT, that asset (or group) is identified as its own CGU — even if some/all output is used INTERNALLY. Internal transfer prices are adjusted to management's best estimate of arm's-length prices when estimating cash flows.
Carrying amount of a CGU (paras. 86–90)
INCLUDES only assets directly attributable or reasonably allocable to the unit, that generate the cash inflows used in its value-in-use calculation EXCLUDES recognized liabilities — UNLESS the unit's recoverable amount can't be determined without considering that liability (e.g. a buyer would assume it on disposal) CGUs are identified CONSISTENTLY period to period unless a change is justified — disclosure required if the composition changes and an impairment loss is recognized/reversed (para. 83–84)
A non-cash-generating asset CONTRIBUTING to a cash-generating unit (e.g. a shared reception area) has a PROPORTION of its own carrying amount allocated into the CGU's carrying amount, after its own separate IPSAS 21 impairment test (para. 93, see §9).

Goodwill Allocation & Impairment Testing (paras. 90A–97H)

Allocating goodwill to CGUs
Goodwill acquired in a public sector combination is allocated, from the acquisition date, to EACH of the acquirer's CGUs (or groups of CGUs) expected to BENEFIT FROM THE SYNERGIES of the combination — irrespective of whether other assets/liabilities are also assigned to those units.
This links directly to IPSAS 40's acquisition accounting — goodwill is recognized only where it gives rise to cash inflows or reductions in the acquirer's net cash outflows.
Never reversed, once impaired
✕ One-way street: an impairment loss recognized for GOODWILL is NEVER reversed in a subsequent period. IPSAS 31 prohibits recognizing internally generated goodwill — so any apparent later increase in goodwill's recoverable amount is presumed to be internally generated goodwill, not a genuine recovery of the acquired goodwill (paras. 111A–111B).

Allocating an Impairment Loss Within a CGU (paras. 91–97)

The waterfall order (para. 91)
1. Reduce GOODWILL allocated to the unit first → 2. THEN spread the remainder pro rata across the unit's other assets, by carrying amount
No individual asset's carrying amount is reduced below the HIGHEST of: its own fair value less costs of disposal (if measurable), its own value in use (if determinable), and ZERO (para. 92) Any amount that would otherwise breach that floor is reallocated pro rata to the OTHER cash-generating assets in the unit
Non-cash-generating assets contributing to a CGU (paras. 93–95)
A contributing non-cash-generating asset is first tested SEPARATELY under IPSAS 21. A PROPORTION of its (already impairment-tested) carrying amount — reflecting how much its service potential contributes to the CGU — is then folded into the CGU's carrying amount BEFORE the CGU's own recoverable amount is estimated. That non-cash-generating asset receives NO further impairment loss beyond what IPSAS 21 already determined, and the CGU-level allocation in para. 91 excludes it entirely (spread only across cash-generating assets).
If an individual asset's recoverable amount truly cannot be determined, and its carrying amount exceeds the higher of its own fair value less costs of disposal AND the allocation-procedure result, an impairment loss is still recognized for that asset — but ONLY if the related CGU is ALSO impaired overall (para. 94).

Reversing an Impairment Loss (paras. 98–111B)

Individual asset reversal
Mirror-image indications to §3 (favorable market/technological/legal changes, favorable use changes, resumed construction, better-than-expected internal performance). A reversal is recognized ONLY if there's been a genuine CHANGE IN ESTIMATES — never merely from the passage of time / unwinding of the discount (para. 105). Capped at the never-impaired carrying amount, net of depreciation (para. 106); routed through surplus/deficit or revaluation surplus, mirroring the original loss (para. 108).
CGU-level reversal — the reverse waterfall
Reversal allocated pro rata to the unit's cash-generating assets EXCEPT goodwill — never above the lower of (a) recoverable amount, and (b) the never-impaired carrying amount
No part of a CGU-level reversal is ever allocated to a NON-cash-generating asset contributing service potential (para. 110) Goodwill impairment is NEVER reversed — see §8 (para. 111A)

Redesignation of Assets & Disclosure (paras. 112–125)

Redesignation — same rule as IPSAS 21, mirrored
Effluent treatment plant — from cash-generating to non-cash-generating
Built to treat industrial effluent at commercial rates for a nearby industrial estate, with excess capacity used to treat social-housing effluent at no charge. The industrial estate closes; the site becomes social housing.
The entity redesignates the plant as NON-CASH-GENERATING — future impairment testing moves to IPSAS 21. Redesignation itself does NOT trigger an automatic test; the ordinary indication framework applies at the next reporting date (para. 112–113).
Disclosure requirements (paras. 114–125)
Criteria used to distinguish cash-generating from non-cash-generating assets Per class of assets: impairment losses/reversals recognized in surplus/deficit (with the SOFPerformance line item), and those recognized directly in revaluation surplus Per SEGMENT (IPSAS 18 reporters): impairment losses/reversals recognized in surplus/deficit Per MATERIAL impairment loss/reversal: events/circumstances, amount, nature of the asset (or CGU description, composition, and any composition CHANGE), and whether recoverable amount is fair value less costs of disposal or value in use
A separate disclosure package applies for GOODWILL-CONTAINING CGUs where an estimate is particularly sensitive to key assumptions — requiring disclosure of those key assumptions, the period over which management projected cash flows, and the growth/discount rates used (§ paras 126A onward, cross-referenced from the Handbook's disclosure section on estimates for CGUs containing goodwill or indefinite-life intangibles).

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Comparison with IAS 36

Comparison — IPSAS 26 vs IAS 36
IPSAS 26 is drawn primarily from IAS 36 (2004), Impairment of Assets, including a May 2008 Improvements to IFRS amendment.
TopicDifference
Cash-generating vs non-cash-generating splitIPSAS 26 defines this explicitly, with commentary distinguishing the two — no IAS 36 equivalent (IAS 36 assumes all assets are commercial)
Cash-generating unit definitionModified from IAS 36 to reference "primary objective of generating a commercial return"
Corporate assetsIPSAS 26 does NOT define corporate assets or include specific requirements for them; IAS 36 does
Market capitalization indicatorIAS 36 treats net assets exceeding market capitalization as a minimum impairment indicator; IPSAS 26 has NO equivalent (public sector entities have no market capitalization)
Forced saleIPSAS 26: a forced sale is NOT a reflection of fair value less costs of disposal. IAS 36: it CAN be, if management is compelled to sell immediately
Non-cash-generating assets within a CGUIPSAS 26 has specific requirements/guidance (§9); IAS 36 has none
RedesignationIPSAS 26 has explicit redesignation guidance and a disclosure requirement for the classification criteria; IAS 36 has neither

Statement / Process Mapping

End-to-end impairment decision map
1. Is the asset CASH-GENERATING? → this Standard applies. Non-cash-generating (the default) → IPSAS 21 instead (§2) 2. Is there an impairment INDICATION at the reporting date? → estimate recoverable amount for the INDIVIDUAL asset (§3, §4) 3. Can the individual asset's recoverable amount be determined? If NOT → step up to its CASH-GENERATING UNIT (§7) 4. Does the unit contain GOODWILL? → test the goodwill-containing unit(s) at least annually regardless of indication (§8) 5. Is the unit impaired? → allocate the loss: goodwill FIRST, then pro rata to other assets, respecting individual asset floors (§9) 6. At later reporting dates, is there a reversal indication? → reverse for individual assets/CGU assets (never goodwill), capped appropriately (§10)

Quick-Reference Glossary

Cash-generating unit
The smallest identifiable group of assets held for a commercial return, generating largely independent cash inflows.
Recoverable amount
The higher of an asset's (or CGU's) fair value less costs of disposal and its value in use.
Value in use (cash-generating asset)
Present value of estimated future cash flows from continuing use and eventual disposal.
Costs of disposal
Incremental costs directly attributable to an asset's disposal, excluding finance costs and income tax.
Goodwill
Treated as cash-generating for impairment purposes; only recognized where it gives rise to cash inflows; impairment never reversed.
Corporate assets (concept, not defined in IPSAS 26)
Group/divisional assets contributing to multiple CGUs' cash flows — IAS 36 defines these; IPSAS 26 does not.
