Conceptual Framework Linkage

A thoroughly converged, cost-anchored standard
ASPE Section 3031 governs the RECOGNITION and MEASUREMENT of INVENTORIES – GOODS held FOR sale in the ORDINARY course OF business, in the PROCESS of PRODUCTION for SUCH sale, or in the FORM of materials OR supplies to BE consumed in the PRODUCTION process. THE Section is CLOSELY converged with IAS 2 internationally.
2

Scope

Goods held for sale or consumption in the ordinary course of business
This Section applies to INVENTORIES generally, EXCLUDING agricultural INVENTORIES and PRODUCTIVE biological ASSETS (governed SEPARATELY by SECTION 3041 – see the COMPANION poster).
3

What Costs Are Included

Purchase costs, conversion costs, and costs to bring inventory to its present condition
THE cost OF inventory COMPRISES all COSTS of PURCHASE (INCLUDING duties and TRANSPORT, net OF trade DISCOUNTS), costs OF conversion (DIRECT labor and a SYSTEMATIC allocation OF production overhead), and OTHER costs INCURRED in BRINGING the inventory TO its PRESENT location and CONDITION.
4

Cost Formulas – FIFO, Weighted Average, and Specific Identification

A choice among permitted formulas
FOR items THAT are ORDINARILY interchangeable, an ENTERPRISE applies FIFO (first-IN, first-OUT) or WEIGHTED average COST, CONSISTENTLY from PERIOD to period. FOR items THAT are NOT ordinarily INTERCHANGEABLE (e.g. UNIQUE, custom-MADE goods), SPECIFIC identification IS used INSTEAD.
5

No LIFO

LIFO is not a permitted cost formula
LIFO (last-IN, first-OUT) is NOT a PERMITTED cost FORMULA under ASPE – CONSISTENT with IAS 2 internationally, but IN contrast to US GAAP, WHICH continues to PERMIT LIFO as ONE of several ACCEPTABLE inventory COST methods.
6

Lower of Cost and Net Realizable Value

A ceiling on carrying value
INVENTORY is MEASURED at the LOWER of COST and NET realizable VALUE (the ESTIMATED selling PRICE in the ORDINARY course OF business, LESS estimated COSTS of COMPLETION and estimated COSTS necessary TO make the SALE) – a WRITE-down is RECOGNIZED whenever COST exceeds NET realizable VALUE.
7

Reversal of a Previous Write-Down

Permitted where net realizable value recovers, up to original cost
WHERE the CIRCUMSTANCES that PREVIOUSLY caused inventory TO be written DOWN below COST no LONGER exist, OR where THERE is clear EVIDENCE of an INCREASE in NET realizable VALUE, the WRITE-down is REVERSED (LIMITED to the EXTENT of the ORIGINAL write-DOWN, so the CARRYING amount NEVER exceeds ORIGINAL cost) – CONSISTENT with IAS 2'S own REVERSAL permission internationally.
8

Worked Example – Calculating Weighted Average Cost

Blending purchases at different prices
Example: A retailer's weighted average inventory cost
A company begins the month with 500 units at $12 each ($6,000). It purchases 300 more units at $14 each ($4,200) during the month.
WEIGHTED average COST per UNIT = ($6,000 + $4,200) ÷ (500 + 300) = $10,200 ÷ 800 = $12.75 per unit, APPLIED to ALL subsequent SALES and ENDING inventory VALUATION until the NEXT purchase.
9

Worked Example – Reversing a Prior Write-Down

Recovering value, limited to the original cost ceiling
Example: A recovering commodity price
A company's inventory, originally costing $200,000, was written down to a net realizable value of $170,000 in the prior period due to a temporary price decline. By the current period end, prices have recovered, and net realizable value is now $230,000.
The company REVERSES the PRIOR write-DOWN, RESTATING the INVENTORY to its ORIGINAL $200,000 COST – NOT the FULL $230,000 net REALIZABLE value, SINCE the REVERSAL is CAPPED at the ORIGINAL cost basis.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Net realizable value
Estimated selling price in the ordinary course of business, less estimated costs of completion and costs necessary to make the sale.
Specific identification
A cost formula assigning actual, individually tracked costs to items that are not ordinarily interchangeable.
Financial statement key (ASPE terminology)
BS Balance Sheet IS Income Statement SRE Statement of Retained Earnings / Statement of Changes in Equity SCF Cash Flow Statement
