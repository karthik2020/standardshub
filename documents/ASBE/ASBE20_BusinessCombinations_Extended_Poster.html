Conceptual Framework Linkage

Two fundamentally different accounting models, depending on control history
ASBE No. 20 governs the RECOGNITION and MEASUREMENT of BUSINESS COMBINATIONS — SPLIT into TWO fundamentally DIFFERENT categories DEPENDING on WHETHER the COMBINING entities were UNDER common CONTROL both BEFORE and AFTER the COMBINATION (see the COMPANION ASBE No. 2 poster for the PREDECESSOR-cost treatment) or NOT (WHERE the ACQUISITION method — CLOSELY converged with IFRS 3 — applies).

Not under common controlACQUISITION method — FAIR value MEASUREMENT, GOODWILL (or BARGAIN purchase GAIN) recognised
3

The Acquisition Method — Combinations Not Under Common Control

Identifying the acquirer, measuring net assets at fair value
FOR combinations NOT under COMMON control, the ACQUISITION method APPLIES — IDENTIFYING the ACQUIRER, DETERMINING the ACQUISITION date, and RECOGNISING the IDENTIFIABLE assets ACQUIRED, liabilities ASSUMED, and any NON-CONTROLLING interest at FAIR VALUE at the ACQUISITION date — CONSISTENT with IFRS 3's OWN acquisition method internationally.
4

Measuring and Recognising Goodwill

Consideration transferred plus NCI, less net identifiable assets
GOODWILL is MEASURED as the EXCESS of (a) the CONSIDERATION transferred PLUS the AMOUNT of any NON-CONTROLLING interest, OVER (b) the FAIR value of the ACQUIREE'S identifiable NET assets ACQUIRED — CONSISTENT with IFRS 3's OWN goodwill CALCULATION internationally.
5

Goodwill — Impairment-Only, Genuinely Aligned With IFRS 3

No systematic amortisation — a point of genuine international alignment
GOODWILL recognised UNDER this Standard is MEASURED at COST LESS accumulated IMPAIRMENT losses (see the COMPANION ASBE No. 8 poster) — WITH NO systematic AMORTISATION, TESTED for IMPAIRMENT AT LEAST annually — CONSISTENT with the IMPAIRMENT-only approach applied UNDER IFRS 3/IAS 36 internationally. This is a GENUINE point of ALIGNMENT with IFRS, in CONTRAST to Japan GAAP'S mandatory GOODWILL amortisation requirement (see the COMPANION ASBJ Statement No. 21 poster EARLIER in this SERIES).
6

Negative Goodwill (Bargain Purchase Gain)

Recognised immediately, after reassessment
WHERE the FAIR value of the ACQUIREE'S net IDENTIFIABLE assets EXCEEDS the CONSIDERATION transferred (a "BARGAIN purchase"), the ACQUIRER first RE-ASSESSES whether it has CORRECTLY identified ALL assets ACQUIRED and LIABILITIES assumed, and CORRECTLY measured THEIR fair VALUES — WITH any REMAINING excess recognised IMMEDIATELY as a GAIN in PROFIT or LOSS, CONSISTENT with IFRS 3's OWN treatment internationally.
7

Acquisition-Related Costs — Expensed as Incurred

Not capitalised into the cost of the combination
COSTS the ACQUIRER incurs in CONNECTION with a BUSINESS combination (LEGAL fees, DUE diligence COSTS, advisory FEES) are EXPENSED as INCURRED — EXCEPT costs of ISSUING debt or EQUITY securities, WHICH are accounted FOR under THEIR own RESPECTIVE standards — CONSISTENT with IFRS 3'S own treatment internationally.
8

Step Acquisitions

Remeasuring a previously held interest to fair value
WHERE an ACQUIRER already HELD an EQUITY interest in the ACQUIREE BEFORE obtaining CONTROL (a "STEP acquisition"), the PREVIOUSLY held INTEREST is REMEASURED to FAIR value at the ACQUISITION date, WITH the RESULTING difference RECOGNISED in PROFIT or LOSS — CONSISTENT with IFRS 3'S own step-acquisition TREATMENT internationally.
9

Common Control Combinations — Recap and the Restatement Practice

Comparative financial statements often restated as if combined from the start
AS discussed in the COMPANION ASBE No. 2 poster, COMBINATIONS UNDER common CONTROL use a PREDECESSOR-cost method, WITH NO goodwill RECOGNISED. A GENUINELY distinctive ADDITIONAL feature of THIS treatment: COMPARATIVE financial STATEMENTS are OFTEN RESTATED as IF the COMBINATION had OCCURRED from the BEGINNING of the EARLIEST period PRESENTED (an "AS-IF pooling" restatement APPROACH) — a PRACTICE without a DIRECT parallel under IFRS 3, WHICH has NO equivalent common-CONTROL exception REQUIRING comparable RESTATEMENT.
10

The Measurement Period

Retrospective adjustments within a limited window
WHERE the INITIAL accounting for a BUSINESS combination is INCOMPLETE by the END of the REPORTING period in WHICH the combination OCCURS, PROVISIONAL amounts are USED, WITH retrospective ADJUSTMENTS permitted WITHIN a SPECIFIED "measurement PERIOD" following the ACQUISITION date — CONSISTENT with IFRS 3's OWN measurement period CONCEPT internationally.
11

Worked Example — Recognising Goodwill in an Arm's-Length Acquisition

The standard goodwill calculation
Example: An acquisition of an unrelated company
A company acquires 100% of an unrelated target for RMB 600,000,000 cash. The target's identifiable net assets have a fair value of RMB 480,000,000.
GOODWILL = RMB 600,000,000 − RMB 480,000,000 = RMB 120,000,000, recognised on the BALANCE Sheet, SUBJECT to ANNUAL impairment TESTING, with NO systematic AMORTISATION.
12

Worked Example — a Bargain Purchase Gain

Recognising a gain rather than goodwill
Example: An acquisition below the target's net asset fair value
A company acquires 100% of a distressed but fundamentally sound target for RMB 200,000,000. After reassessment, the target's identifiable net assets have a fair value of RMB 235,000,000.
The company recognises a BARGAIN purchase GAIN of RMB 235,000,000 − RMB 200,000,000 = RMB 35,000,000, recognised IMMEDIATELY in PROFIT or LOSS.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Bargain purchase gain
A gain recognised where the fair value of an acquiree's identifiable net assets exceeds the consideration transferred, after reassessment.
Measurement period
The window following an acquisition date during which provisional business combination accounting may be retrospectively adjusted as new information emerges.
