Conceptual Framework Linkage

A collision between two defensible principles
IAS 34 treats each interim period as an integral part of the annual period, generally favouring the SAME recognition and measurement principles used at year end. But IAS 36 permanently prohibits reversing a goodwill impairment loss once recognised. IFRIC 10 exists because, without guidance, an entity could argue an interim impairment "washes out" by year end if conditions recover — a result IAS 36's own annual-period logic was designed to prevent.
Consistency between interim and annual periods
IFRIC 10's conclusion ensures an impairment loss recognised in ANY interim period within a financial year is treated exactly as it would be if that same loss had been recognised at the year end — consistent, comparable, and immune to which specific quarter the underlying trigger happened to be identified in.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRIC 10 covers
IFRIC 10 addresses the interaction between IAS 34 and the impairment requirements of IAS 36 for goodwill, and (historically) IAS 39 for investments in equity instruments and financial assets carried at cost. It applies to any entity that reports both interim AND annual financial statements applying IFRS.
ASSETS IN SCOPE OF THE NO-REVERSAL RULE
GoodwillInvestments in equity instrumentsFinancial assets carried at cost because their fair value cannot be reliably measured
Key definitions
Interim period
A financial reporting period shorter than a full financial year, per IAS 34 — typically a quarter or half-year.
Impairment loss
The amount by which the carrying amount of an asset or cash-generating unit exceeds its recoverable amount, per IAS 36.
3

The Core Question

What IFRIC 10 actually decides
If an entity recognises an impairment loss on goodwill or a relevant equity/cost-measured financial asset in an interim period, and the underlying indicators that triggered the loss REVERSE by the next reporting date (interim or annual), may the entity reverse the previously recognised impairment loss? IFRIC 10's answer is a firm NO.
4

The No-Reversal Rule for Goodwill

Interim impairment losses on goodwill are permanent
An entity shall NOT reverse an impairment loss recognised in a previous interim period in respect of goodwill, even if no loss, or a smaller loss, would have been recognised had the impairment assessment been performed only at the end of a subsequent reporting period. This directly extends IAS 36's absolute prohibition on reversing goodwill impairment into the interim-reporting context.
5

The No-Reversal Rule for Equity Instruments

Historically extended to certain financial assets too
The same no-reversal logic historically applied to impairment losses on investments in equity instruments, and on financial assets carried at cost because fair value could not be reliably measured, under the former IAS 39 impairment model — such losses, once recognised through profit or loss in an interim period, could not be reversed even if the equity instrument's value later recovered.
6

Worked Example — Quarterly Impairment

Goodwill impairment recognised in Q2, recovering by Q4
Example: No reversal permitted despite recovery
A cash-generating unit carries goodwill of $3,000,000. At the end of Q2, a recoverable amount assessment identifies an impairment loss of $500,000, which is recognised in the Q2 interim financial statements, reducing goodwill to $2,500,000. By Q4, the same cash-generating unit's recoverable amount has recovered such that no impairment would have been indicated had the assessment first been performed at that date.
The $500,000 impairment loss recognised in Q2 CANNOT be reversed in Q4 or at year end. Goodwill remains carried at $2,500,000 in the annual financial statements, notwithstanding the year-end recoverable amount assessment showing no impairment on a stand-alone basis.
7

What IFRS 9 Changed in Practice

The equity-instrument leg is now largely academic
Under IFRS 9, equity instruments are generally measured at fair value, with changes recognised either in profit or loss, or — if an irrevocable FVOCI election is made — in other comprehensive income, with NO subsequent recycling to profit or loss even on disposal. Since IFRS 9 does not use an impairment model for equity instruments in the way IAS 39 did, IFRIC 10's equity-instrument guidance has little remaining practical application; its GOODWILL guidance, however, remains fully operative and is the interpretation's primary ongoing relevance today.
8

Assets Where Reversal IS Permitted

A narrow, specific prohibition — not a blanket rule
IFRIC 10's no-reversal conclusion is specific to goodwill (always) and to the historical IAS 39 equity/cost-measured asset categories. It does NOT prevent reversal of impairment losses on OTHER assets within IAS 36's scope — such as property, plant and equipment or intangible assets other than goodwill — which remain eligible for impairment reversal in a LATER period (interim or annual) if the recoverable amount genuinely recovers, subject to IAS 36's own reversal ceiling (capped at the carrying amount that would have applied had no impairment been recognised).

Disclosure Requirements

What IFRIC 10 layers onto IAS 34/IAS 36
Nature and amount of any impairment loss recognised or reversed during an interim period, consistent with IAS 34's general disclosure requirements for significant eventsExplanation where an interim impairment loss on goodwill has not been reversed despite an apparent later recovery in indicators, to aid user understanding of the no-reversal principle's effect

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether impairment indicators genuinely exist at an interim date, given entities are not required to perform a full annual-style impairment test every quarter unless indicators are present
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit at the interim date, often based on less complete information than at year end

IFRIC 10 vs US GAAP

Comparison — IFRIC 10 vs US GAAP (ASC 270 / ASC 350)
FeatureIFRS (IFRIC 10)US GAAP
Reversal of goodwill impairment generallyAlways prohibited, at both interim and annual levelAlso always prohibited under ASC 350 — goodwill impairment is never reversed under US GAAP either
Explicit interim-specific interpretationYes — IFRIC 10 directly addresses the interim/annual interactionASC 270 embeds a similar "integral part of the annual period" philosophy without a dedicated interpretation on this specific reversal question, largely because the underlying no-reversal rule is already absolute at any reporting date
On goodwill specifically, the two frameworks reach a highly converged outcome; IFRIC 10's distinct contribution is making explicit that the SAME absolute rule applies even to a loss recognised only in an interim period, closing a gap IAS 34's general "consistency with annual principles" language did not fully resolve on its own.

Quick-reference glossary
Cash-generating unit (CGU)
The smallest identifiable group of assets that generates cash inflows largely independent of other assets, the level at which goodwill impairment is tested under IAS 36.
Recoverable amount
The higher of an asset's (or CGU's) fair value less costs of disposal and its value in use — compared against carrying amount to determine impairment under IAS 36.

Quick-Reference Glossary

No-reversal principle
The IAS 36 rule, extended by IFRIC 10 to interim periods, that once a goodwill impairment loss is recognised it can never be written back, regardless of subsequent recovery in the underlying indicators.
Integral view of interim reporting
IAS 34's underlying philosophy that each interim period is a component of the full financial year and should generally apply the same recognition and measurement principles as annual reporting — the conceptual basis IFRIC 10 relies on.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOPL Statement of profit or loss OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
