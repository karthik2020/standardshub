ASC 430 – Deferred Revenue
US GAAP · Subtopic: 430-10 Overall · A narrow, largely-absorbed Topic since ASC 606's contract liability model took over most substantive content
⚠ LARGELY ABSORBED INTO ASC 606 – Most deferred revenue guidance today lives within the contract liability concept in ASC 606, not here
What ASC 430 originally addressed, and what changed
Before the 2014 revenue recognition overhaul, ASC 430 served as a general placeholder Topic for DEFERRED (unearned) REVENUE – amounts received or receivable from customers before the related goods or services had been delivered. Since ASC 606 (effective 2018) introduced the concept of a CONTRACT LIABILITY – an entity's obligation to transfer goods or services to a customer for which the entity has already received (or is entitled to receive) consideration – the SUBSTANTIVE recognition and measurement guidance for what was once called "deferred revenue" now lives within ASC 606 itself, not in this Topic.
Why ASC 430 still exists as a shell
ASC 430 remains in the Codification largely as a NAMING/CROSS-REFERENCE point – connecting the older, more familiar "deferred revenue" terminology still widely used in practice and financial statement captions to its now-authoritative treatment under ASC 606's contract liability model. There is little INDEPENDENT substantive content left in ASC 430 itself; virtually all recognition, measurement, and disclosure requirements for amounts received in advance of performance are governed by ASC 606.
Where this stands today, and the IFRS comparison
Current substantive homeASC 606 – contract liability guidance governs recognition, measurement, and derecognition of amounts received before performance
Typical balance sheet caption"Deferred revenue" or "Contract liabilities" – either caption is commonly seen in practice, both referring to the same ASC 606 concept
IFRS comparisonIFRS 15 (the converged counterpart to ASC 606, from the same joint FASB-IASB project) uses the identical "contract liability" concept – this is one of the most closely converged areas in the entire Codification, since both standards share a common origin and near-identical wording for this specific concept
