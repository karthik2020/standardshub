Conceptual Framework Linkage

Translating transactions and foreign operations into the reporting currency
ASBE No. 19 governs HOW an ENTERPRISE INCLUDES foreign CURRENCY transactions and FOREIGN operations WITHIN its OWN financial STATEMENTS, and HOW it TRANSLATES financial STATEMENTS into a PRESENTATION currency – BROADLY converged with IAS 21 internationally, sharing the SAME functional-CURRENCY concept and MONETARY/non-monetary DISTINCTION.
2

Functional Currency

The currency of the primary economic environment
An ENTERPRISE'S FUNCTIONAL currency is DETERMINED by the PRIMARY economic ENVIRONMENT in WHICH it OPERATES – GENERALLY the currency that MAINLY influences SALES prices, LABOUR, material, and OTHER costs – CONSISTENT with IAS 21's OWN functional currency concept.
3

Initial Recognition – Spot Rate at Transaction Date

Translating a foreign currency transaction on first recognition
A FOREIGN currency TRANSACTION is INITIALLY recorded by APPLYING the SPOT exchange RATE at the DATE of the TRANSACTION (or a RATE that CLOSELY approximates IT, such as an AVERAGE rate for a PERIOD, WHERE exchange RATES do not FLUCTUATE significantly).
4

Monetary Items – Retranslated at Each Reporting Date

Exchange differences recognised in profit or loss
FOREIGN currency MONETARY items (CASH, receivables, PAYABLES) are RETRANSLATED at the CLOSING rate at EACH reporting DATE, WITH the RESULTING exchange DIFFERENCES recognised in PROFIT or LOSS for the PERIOD – CONSISTENT with IAS 21's OWN treatment internationally.
5

Non-Monetary Items – Historical Cost vs Fair Value

The measurement basis determines the translation treatment
NON-MONETARY items MEASURED at HISTORICAL cost are NOT retranslated – they REMAIN at the exchange RATE prevailing at the TRANSACTION date. NON-MONETARY items MEASURED at FAIR value are TRANSLATED using the exchange RATE at the DATE the fair VALUE was DETERMINED, WITH the resulting DIFFERENCE treated CONSISTENTLY with HOW other fair VALUE gains/losses on THAT item are treated (EITHER profit or LOSS, or OTHER comprehensive INCOME, depending on the ITEM'S own measurement CATEGORY).
6

Translating Foreign Operations for Consolidation

Assets/liabilities at closing rate, income/expenses at transaction-date or average rate
WHEN translating a FOREIGN operation'S financial STATEMENTS for CONSOLIDATION purposes, ASSETS and LIABILITIES are TRANSLATED at the CLOSING rate, WHILE INCOME and EXPENSES are TRANSLATED at the exchange RATE at the DATES of the TRANSACTIONS (or an AVERAGE rate as a PRACTICAL approximation) – WITH the RESULTING exchange DIFFERENCES recognised WITHIN OTHER COMPREHENSIVE INCOME, RATHER than PROFIT or loss.
7

Recycling on Disposal of a Foreign Operation

Cumulative translation differences transferred to profit or loss
ON DISPOSAL of a FOREIGN operation, the CUMULATIVE amount of EXCHANGE differences PREVIOUSLY recognised WITHIN other COMPREHENSIVE income (RELATING to that OPERATION) is RECLASSIFIED to PROFIT or LOSS – CONSISTENT with IAS 21'S own RECYCLING requirement internationally, and (NOTABLY) CONSISTENT with the SAME "eventual FULL recycling" philosophy DISCUSSED in the COMPANION JMIS Modification No. 2 poster EARLIER in this SERIES.
8

China's Managed Floating Exchange Rate Regime – a Contextual Note

A macro/regulatory backdrop, not an accounting divergence
CHINA operates a MANAGED FLOATING exchange RATE regime for the RENMINBI, WITH the PEOPLE'S Bank of CHINA setting a DAILY reference RATE and PERMITTING trading WITHIN a SPECIFIED band. THIS is a MACROECONOMIC and REGULATORY backdrop RATHER than an ACCOUNTING-standard-LEVEL divergence – the ACCOUNTING mechanics THEMSELVES (spot RATE at transaction DATE, closing RATE for monetary ITEMS) apply CONSISTENTLY regardless of the UNDERLYING currency REGIME.
9

Worked Example – Retranslating a Monetary Item

Recognising an exchange gain on a foreign currency receivable
Example: A USD-denominated export receivable
A company recognises a USD 1,000,000 export receivable when the exchange rate is USD 1 = RMB 7.10 (carrying amount RMB 7,100,000). At the reporting date, the exchange rate has moved to USD 1 = RMB 7.20.
The receivable is RETRANSLATED to USD 1,000,000 × 7.20 = RMB 7,200,000, with an EXCHANGE GAIN of RMB 7,200,000 − RMB 7,100,000 = RMB 100,000 recognised in PROFIT or LOSS for the period.
10

Worked Example – Recycling Cumulative Translation Differences on Disposal

Moving accumulated OCI into profit or loss upon sale
Example: Disposing of a wholly-owned overseas subsidiary
A company sells its wholly-owned overseas subsidiary, which had accumulated RMB 25,000,000 of foreign currency translation gains within other comprehensive income over its years of ownership.
Upon DISPOSAL, the company RECLASSIFIES the FULL RMB 25,000,000 cumulative TRANSLATION gain from OTHER comprehensive INCOME into PROFIT or LOSS, RECOGNISING it as PART of the OVERALL gain or LOSS on disposal of the SUBSIDIARY.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Functional currency
The currency of the primary economic environment in which an enterprise operates, determining the basis for foreign currency transaction accounting.
Foreign currency translation reserve
A component of other comprehensive income accumulating exchange differences arising from translating foreign operations, recycled to profit or loss upon disposal.
