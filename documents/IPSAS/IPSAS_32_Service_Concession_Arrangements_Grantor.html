1

Objective, Scope & Out-of-Scope Arrangements (paras. 1–7)

Objective
Prescribes the accounting for service concession arrangements BY THE GRANTOR — a public sector entity. This Standard fills a genuine gap: it addresses only the GRANTOR's side; the OPERATOR's accounting is addressed by the relevant international/national standard dealing with service concession arrangements (typically IFRIC 12) (para. 7).
A "grantor" is typically a government or other public sector entity granting a private (or public) operator the right to provide public services using an asset.
What falls outside scope (para. 6)
Arrangements that do NOT involve delivery of public services Arrangements with service/management components where the asset is NOT controlled by the grantor — e.g. outsourcing, ordinary service contracts, or privatization
Within scope: arrangements where the OPERATOR provides public services related to the service concession asset ON BEHALF of the grantor (para. 5). The scope deliberately MIRRORS IFRIC 12's recognition criteria, so both parties to the same arrangement apply symmetric principles — minimizing the risk that an asset is accounted for by BOTH parties, or by NEITHER.
2

Definitions — Grantor, Operator, Asset & Arrangement (para. 8)

The parties
Grantor
The entity that grants the right to use the service concession asset to the operator.
Operator
The entity that USES the service concession asset to provide public services, SUBJECT TO the grantor's control of the asset.
The arrangement and the asset
Service concession arrangement
A binding arrangement where (a) the operator uses the asset to provide a public service on the grantor's behalf for a specified period, and (b) the operator is compensated for its services over that period.
Service concession asset
An asset used to provide public services, EITHER provided by the operator (constructed/developed/acquired, or an existing operator asset) OR provided by the grantor (an existing asset, or an upgrade to one).
3

Recognition of a Service Concession Asset — the Control Test (paras. 9–10)

Two conditions — BOTH required (except whole-of-life assets)
The grantor CONTROLS or REGULATES what services the operator must provide with the asset, to WHOM, and at what PRICE The grantor CONTROLS — through ownership, beneficial entitlement, or otherwise — any SIGNIFICANT RESIDUAL INTEREST in the asset at the end of the arrangement
ℹ️ Whole-of-life exception: for an asset used for its ENTIRE useful life within the arrangement, ONLY the first condition (control/regulation of services) needs to be met — there's no meaningful "end of arrangement" residual interest to assess (para. 10). This mirrors IFRIC 12's approach precisely, by deliberate design (§1) — a CONTROL-based test, not a risks-and-rewards test, because the grantor's primary interest is service delivery, not economic return.
4

Initial Measurement & Reclassification of Existing Grantor Assets (paras. 11–13)

Initial measurement — fair value, with an exception
Service concession asset (new, operator-provided) → initially measured at FAIR VALUE
After initial recognition (or reclassification), the asset is subsequently accounted for under IPSAS 31 (if intangible) or IPSAS 45 (if PP&E), as appropriate (para. 13).
Reclassifying the grantor's OWN existing asset
Where an EXISTING grantor asset (or an upgrade to one) meets the para. 9/10 conditions, the grantor RECLASSIFIES it as a service concession asset — continuing to account for it under IPSAS 31 or IPSAS 45 as appropriate, rather than remeasuring it at fair value from scratch (para. 12).
This reclassification mechanic is distinct from initial recognition of a NEW operator-provided asset — no new fair value measurement event occurs merely from the reclassification itself.
5

Recognition & Measurement of the Corresponding Liability (paras. 14–17)

The matching-liability principle
Recognize a service concession asset → ALSO recognize a LIABILITY, initially measured at the SAME amount, adjusted for any other consideration exchanged
EXCEPTION: no liability arises merely from RECLASSIFYING an existing grantor asset (§4) — unless the operator provides ADDITIONAL consideration as part of that reclassification (para. 14).
Two models, driven by the nature of consideration (para. 17)
Financial liability model — the grantor MAKES PAYMENTS to the operator Grant of a right to the operator model — the grantor compensates by OTHER MEANS: the right to earn revenue from third-party users, or access to another revenue-generating asset (e.g. a private hospital wing, an adjacent parking facility)
These two models can be COMBINED for a single arrangement — see §8, Dividing the Arrangement.
6

Financial Liability Model (paras. 18–23)

When it applies — an UNCONDITIONAL obligation
Applies where the grantor has an unconditional obligation to pay cash/another financial asset for the asset's construction, development, acquisition, or upgrade. Unconditional includes guaranteeing SPECIFIED/DETERMINABLE amounts, OR a SHORTFALL guarantee (topping up user-fee revenue to a determinable floor) — even if payment is contingent on the asset meeting quality/efficiency standards (para. 19).
IPSAS 28, IPSAS 30, and the derecognition requirements of IPSAS 41 apply to this financial liability, except where IPSAS 32 itself provides specific guidance (para. 20).
Splitting the payment into three components (paras. 21–23)
Each payment = reduction of the LIABILITY + a FINANCE CHARGE + a charge for SERVICES provided by the operator
The finance charge AND the services charge are both recognized as EXPENSES Where asset and service components are SEPARATELY identifiable → allocate by relative FAIR VALUES Where they are NOT separately identifiable → use ESTIMATION techniques instead
7

Grant of a Right to the Operator Model (paras. 24–26)

The liability is unearned revenue, not a financial obligation
No unconditional cash obligation, but the operator is granted a right to earn third-party revenue (or access another revenue-generating asset) → the liability = the UNEARNED PORTION of exchange revenue
Granting the operator this right IS regarded as a REVENUE-generating transaction for the grantor — but because the right is effective over the WHOLE arrangement period, the grantor does NOT recognize that revenue immediately. Instead, revenue is recognized ACCORDING TO THE ECONOMIC SUBSTANCE of the arrangement, reducing the liability progressively (paras. 25–26).
This deliberately avoids the term "performance obligation" (used in ED 43) — IPSAS 32 governs an EXCHANGE transaction, and "performance obligation" risked confusion with IPSAS 23's non-exchange terminology.
8

Dividing the Arrangement (paras. 27–28)

A blended arrangement is split into two, not forced into one model
Grantor pays PARTLY via financial liability and PARTLY via grant of a right → account for EACH PART separately
The TOTAL amount initially recognized for the combined liability is the SAME amount determined under para. 15 (i.e., matching the service concession asset's measurement, adjusted for other consideration) Each part of that total liability is THEN accounted for under its own model — the financial-liability mechanics (§6) for the cash-obligation portion, and the grant-of-a-right mechanics (§7) for the revenue-right portion
This is common in real-world arrangements — e.g. a toll-road concession where the grantor makes some availability payments AND grants the operator toll-collection rights.
9

Other Liabilities, Commitments, Contingent Liabilities/Assets & Other Revenues (paras. 29–30)

Routine cross-references, not new mechanics
Other liabilities, commitments, contingent liabilities, and contingent assets arising from a service concession arrangement are accounted for under the ORDINARY Standards — IPSAS 19 (provisions/contingencies), IPSAS 28, IPSAS 30, and IPSAS 41 (financial instruments) — IPSAS 32 does not create bespoke rules for these (para. 29).
Other revenue streams
Revenues from a service concession arrangement OTHER than the unearned-revenue liability release described in §7 (paras. 24–26) are accounted for under IPSAS 47, Revenue — the general revenue Standard, not a special IPSAS 32 mechanism (para. 30).
10

Presentation & Disclosure (paras. 31–33)

Required disclosures, per arrangement (para. 32)
A DESCRIPTION of the arrangement SIGNIFICANT TERMS affecting the amount, timing, and certainty of future cash flows — concession period, re-pricing dates, re-pricing/renegotiation basis Nature and extent of: rights to use specified assets; rights to expect specified operator services; the CARRYING AMOUNT of recognized service concession assets (including reclassified existing assets); rights to receive assets at arrangement end; renewal/termination options; other rights/obligations (e.g. major overhauls); and obligations to provide asset access CHANGES in the arrangement occurring during the period
Individual vs aggregate disclosure, and interaction with IPSAS 31/45
Disclosures are provided INDIVIDUALLY for each material arrangement, or in AGGREGATE for arrangements of a similar nature (e.g. toll collections, telecommunications, water treatment) — para. 33. This is IN ADDITION to the ordinary class-of-asset disclosures required by IPSAS 31/45; a service concession asset subset may sit WITHIN a broader IPSAS 45 asset class (e.g. a toll bridge reported with "bridges" generally, but ALSO with other toll roads reported in aggregate for IPSAS 32 purposes).

Relationship to IFRIC 12 — No Direct IAS/IFRS Standard Equivalent

A deliberate mirror of an Interpretation, not a Standard
IPSAS 32 has no equivalent IAS/IFRS STANDARD — instead it deliberately MIRRORS IFRIC 12, Service Concession Arrangements, an IFRS INTERPRETATION that addresses only the OPERATOR's accounting. IFRIC 12 provided no guidance for the grantor at all — the gap IPSAS 32 was built to fill (Basis for Conclusions, BC1).
The IPSASB deliberately used IFRIC 12's SAME recognition criteria (control over services and residual interest) so that both parties to one arrangement apply SYMMETRIC principles — minimizing double-counting or under-counting of the asset (BC2, BC15) IPSAS 32 rejected a RISKS-AND-REWARDS approach (too subjective for public sector service delivery, where the grantor's primary interest is SERVICE POTENTIAL, not economic return) and a RIGHTS-AND-OBLIGATIONS approach (too significant a departure from existing practice) in favor of the CONTROL-based test (BC12–BC15) Terminology was deliberately changed FROM ED 43's "performance obligation" TO "liability," to avoid confusion with IPSAS 23's unrelated non-exchange-transaction usage of "performance obligation" (BC21–BC23) Unlike IFRIC 12 (silent on the point), IPSAS 32 provides EXPLICIT guidance on the interest rate to use for the financial liability model's finance charge — the operator's cost of capital, if determinable, or another rate appropriate to the arrangement's specific terms (BC28)

Statement / Process Mapping

End-to-end decision map
1. Does the arrangement involve the operator delivering PUBLIC SERVICES using an asset, on the grantor's behalf? If no (outsourcing, ordinary service contracts) → outside scope entirely (§1) 2. Does the grantor control/regulate the SERVICES (what, to whom, at what price), AND control any significant RESIDUAL interest at arrangement end (or just the services test, for whole-of-life assets)? → recognize a service concession ASSET (§3) 3. Is this a NEW operator-provided asset, or an EXISTING grantor asset meeting the test? → fair value initial measurement, or reclassification, respectively (§4) 4. Recognize a matching LIABILITY (except on mere reclassification without extra consideration) (§5) 5. Does the grantor have an UNCONDITIONAL cash obligation? → FINANCIAL LIABILITY model (§6). Does the grantor instead grant REVENUE RIGHTS? → GRANT OF A RIGHT model (§7). Both? → DIVIDE the arrangement and apply each model to its portion (§8) 6. Route all OTHER liabilities/contingencies/revenues to the ordinary Standards — IPSAS 19, 28, 30, 41, and 47 (§9)

Quick-Reference Glossary

Grantor
The entity that grants the operator the right to use the service concession asset.
Operator
The entity using the service concession asset to provide public services, subject to the grantor's control.
Service concession arrangement
A binding arrangement where the operator uses an asset to provide public services on the grantor's behalf, compensated over the arrangement period.
Service concession asset
An asset used to provide public services under a service concession arrangement.
Financial liability model
Applies where the grantor has an unconditional obligation to pay cash or another financial asset to the operator.
Grant of a right to the operator model
Applies where the grantor compensates the operator through revenue rights rather than cash payments.
