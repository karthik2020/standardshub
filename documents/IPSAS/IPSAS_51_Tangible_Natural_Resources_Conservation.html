Objective, Scope & Out-of-Scope Items (paras. 1–5)

Objective — the newest Standard in the entire Handbook
Establishes principles for reporting relevant information about the nature, amounts, timing, and uncertainties arising from TANGIBLE NATURAL RESOURCES HELD FOR CONSERVATION — issued January 2026, a genuinely novel topic with no direct predecessor anywhere in the accrual IPSAS body of literature.
Sets out recognition, measurement, and presentation requirements, and requires disclosures enabling users to evaluate the nature/risks of these resources AND their effect on financial position, performance, and cash flows (para. 2).
A genuinely residual scope — everything else has its own Standard already
Inventories (IPSAS 12) · Investment property (IPSAS 16) Biological assets under active biological transformation for sale (IPSAS 27) Service concession assets (IPSAS 32) · Leases (IPSAS 43) Assets classified held for sale (IPSAS 44) PP&E used in production/supply/rental/administration (IPSAS 45) Exploration and evaluation assets (IPSAS 50) Obligations relating to these resources — governed by IPSAS 19 instead (para. 5)
2

Classification — the Primary-Purpose Test & Unit of Account (paras. 6–7, AG2–AG4)

One resource, potentially multiple units of account
A resource may consist of MULTIPLE units of account, each classified INDIVIDUALLY based on the entity's primary intended reason for holding THAT unit. Only units meeting the §3 definition fall within this Standard's scope — others are accounted for under whichever IPSAS matches their own primary purpose (para. 6).
Two required characteristics, and the heritage-asset distinction
The asset must be NATURALLY OCCURRING It must be held PRIMARILY FOR CONSERVATION
⚠️ IPSAS 45 heritage assets (historic buildings, monuments, works of art) can overlap conceptually — preserved for present/future generations due to rarity or significance — but are NOT naturally occurring, so they fail this Standard's first characteristic and stay within IPSAS 45 (§16 in that Standard's own poster now explicitly excludes environmental/natural heritage features as a consequential amendment) (para. AG4).
3

Definitions (paras. 8–9, AG5–AG13)

The core definition — deceptively simple
Tangible natural resource held for conservation
A naturally occurring tangible asset that is managed to PREVENT its degradation.
"Naturally occurring" = living and non-living components of the Earth constituting the biophysical/geophysical environment (para. AG5). "Conservation" (used interchangeably with "preservation" elsewhere) may mean simply restricting access, or actively managing growth/restoration where a resource needs rehabilitation (paras. AG6–AG7).
What "degradation" covers — and doesn't
Depletion from active use/consumption, even by a THIRD PARTY Destruction (e.g. forest fires, deliberate or accidental) Other decline events, such as pollution (para. AG10)
ℹ️ NATURAL population deaths from age/disease within a growing population are generally NOT degradation (para. AG11). An INCREASE in service potential from natural growth is a possible conservation outcome — but not a REQUIRED one (para. AG12).

Recognition — the Three-Part Test (paras. 10–13, AG19–AG24)

The criteria (para. 10)
PROBABLE that service potential will flow to the entity The entity CONTROLS the resource as a result of PAST EVENTS The resource can be measured RELIABLY
Note: "service potential" ONLY — not "economic benefits" — since these resources are held purely for OPERATIONAL capacity (Basis for Conclusions BC23), unlike most other tangible-asset Standards in this library which reference both.
Control indicators — no single one decisive (para. AG22–AG23)
Legal ownership Ability to grant/deny/restrict access The means to achieve the entity's objectives with the resource An enforceable right to the resource's service potential
⚠️ Control may exist even where SOME indicators fail — and may be ABSENT even where MOST are satisfied. Legal ownership alone is not decisive; substance over form governs where ownership and service-potential rights diverge.
5

Subsequent Expenditures — Maintenance vs. Incremental Service Potential (paras. 14–16)

Day-to-day maintenance — always expensed
Costs that don't create INCREMENTAL service potential — day-to-day maintenance (rangers protecting a resource, chemical treatment) and research (IPSAS 31 sense) — are expensed as incurred (para. 14).
Expansion — capitalized, regardless of prior recognition status
Acquiring ADDITIONAL parcels of land to expand a conservation area IS capitalized — it generates incremental service potential likely to flow to the entity (para. 15). This applies EVEN IF the underlying resource was never initially recognized (e.g. because its cost/deemed cost couldn't be measured reliably) — each subsequent expenditure is independently tested against the §4 recognition criteria (para. 16).
6

Initial Measurement — Deemed Cost vs. Cost (paras. 17–23)

Two paths, split by transaction type
NOT an orderly-market transaction (incl. non-exchange) → DEEMED COST = current OPERATIONAL value, per IPSAS 46 (para. 17)
EXCHANGE transaction (rare in practice for these resources) → COST — purchase price + directly attributable costs (paras. 19–20)
Deemed-cost measurement at initial recognition does NOT constitute a revaluation — the §8 revaluation mechanics apply only to LATER elective revaluations (para. 18).
Directly attributable costs, and the capitalization cutoff
Employee benefit costs directly from acquisition/development (incl. rehabilitation) Site preparation, initial delivery/handling, professional fees (para. 21)
Capitalization STOPS once the item is in the location/condition necessary to be conserved as management intends (para. 22) — the same cutoff logic used throughout IPSAS 45.
7

Subsequent Measurement — Historical Cost & Current Value Models (paras. 24–26)

One policy per class, borrowed directly from IPSAS 46/45's structure
Choose HISTORICAL COST or CURRENT VALUE — applied to an ENTIRE class, per IPSAS 46
If current value ceases to be reliably measurable on a continuing basis → fall back to historical cost. If it LATER becomes reliably measurable again and the entity elects current value → treated as a REVALUATION (para. 24).
The two models
Historical cost model — cost/deemed cost, less accumulated depreciation (if applicable) and accumulated impairment (para. 25) Current value model — revalued amount = current OPERATIONAL value at the revaluation date, less subsequent accumulated depreciation/impairment (para. 26)
8

Revaluation Mechanics (paras. 27–30, AG25–AG29)

Frequency, and the class-level offsetting rule — identical structure to IPSAS 45
Revalue with SUFFICIENT REGULARITY that carrying amount doesn't materially differ from current operational value — volatile resources may need ANNUAL revaluation; stable/mature ones every three to five years (para. 27, AG25). Individual-asset increases/decreases WITHIN a class are offset against each other; NEVER across different classes (para. 30).
Increases, decreases & recycling
INCREASE → revaluation surplus (net assets/equity) — UNLESS reversing a prior decrease for the SAME class previously in surplus/deficit (para. 28) DECREASE → surplus or deficit — UNLESS offsetting an existing revaluation surplus credit for that SAME class (para. 29) Revaluation surplus may transfer to accumulated surplus/deficit on derecognition, or progressively as depreciated — NEVER through surplus or deficit itself (para. AG29)

Depreciation — the Indefinite-Life Rebuttable Presumption (paras. 31–40, AG34–AG39)

A REVERSED presumption from IPSAS 45 — genuinely distinctive
REBUTTABLE PRESUMPTION: tangible natural resources held for conservation have INDEFINITE useful lives — the opposite default from IPSAS 45's PP&E (finite-life presumption)
These resources typically aren't used/consumed the way ordinary tangible assets are (para. 31). "Indefinite" still doesn't mean "infinite" — conclusions must rest on objective evidence, not optimistic projections, and should NOT depend on planned future expenditure beyond current-condition maintenance (para. 33) — the same guardrail IPSAS 45 applies to its own indefinite-life PP&E.
Worked examples of finite life, and living-resource population logic
Land encroached by rising sea levels, facing severe continual flooding risk within a foreseeable period → FINITE life (para. AG37) A conserved geological feature erodes over a geological timescale so long there's no foreseeable service-potential limit → INDEFINITE (para. AG36) Living resources: conserved at POPULATION level, not individual-organism level — a STABLE or GROWING population generally supports indefinite life; a DECLINING endangered population may support finite life instead (para. AG39)
Residual value, useful life, and depreciation method are all reviewed AT LEAST annually — changes are IPSAS 3 accounting estimate changes (paras. 38, 40).

Impairment & Derecognition (paras. 41–46)

Impairment — a straight cross-reference to IPSAS 21
No bespoke impairment mechanics — apply IPSAS 21's non-cash-generating-asset framework directly (para. 41). Indefinite-life resources are reviewed ANNUALLY for impairment indicators (para. 42) — the same annual-review discipline seen throughout this library for indefinite-life assets.
Derecognition — the familiar three triggers
Gain/loss = net disposal proceeds − carrying amount → surplus or deficit at derecognition
On DISPOSAL, when control CEASES, or when NO service potential is expected (para. 43) Deferred consideration → recognized at cash price equivalent; the difference is interest revenue under IPSAS 47 (para. 46)
11

Reclassification — Into and Out of Scope (paras. 47–50)

A bidirectional test, following the IPSAS 16 reclassification pattern
If a resource (or specific part) is NO LONGER primarily held for conservation → reclassify based on its NEW primary use. If a resource within ANOTHER Standard's scope becomes primarily held for conservation → reclassify INTO this Standard (para. 47). Up to the reclassification date, the entity keeps applying the PREVIOUS Standard, considering whether the change itself indicates impairment under IPSAS 21 or 26 (para. 48).
Carrying amount carries over as deemed cost
Net carrying amount immediately BEFORE reclassification (plus revaluation surplus, if identifiable) becomes the COST for subsequent accounting under the newly-applicable Standard
A resource meeting IPSAS 44's held-for-sale criteria is reclassified there instead (para. 50) — routing back into this library's own IPSAS 44 poster.

Presentation — Display & Core Disclosures (paras. 51–58)

Display — its own SOFP line item
Displayed as a SEPARATE line item on the statement of financial position, per IPSAS 1 para. 88 — now amended to add subsection (ba) for this exact purpose
Core disclosures for RECOGNIZED resources (para. 54–57)
Narrative description — nature/type, location/quantity, significant unit-of-account judgments, significance to entity objectives, and the judgments supporting asset recognition Measurement model and basis for gross carrying amount If depreciable: WHY, method, residual value, useful lives/rates, gross carrying amount and accumulated depreciation A full RECONCILIATION — additions, disposals/held-for-sale, revaluation/impairment increases-decreases, depreciation, reclassifications Restrictions on title/use, pledged-as-security amounts, other stewardship responsibilities For revalued classes: effective date, independent valuer involvement, revaluation surplus movement, and the SUM of all individual revaluation surpluses/deficits within the class

Disclosure — Current Value Measurement, Unrecognized Resources & the Rare/Endangered Carve-Out (paras. 59–71)

Current value measurement — the familiar fair value hierarchy package
Mirrors the fair value disclosure packages seen throughout this library: measurement techniques/inputs, observable vs. unobservable basis, Level 3 reconciliations, valuation process descriptions, and sensitivity narratives (paras. 62–64) — presented in TABULAR format unless another format suits better (para. 67).
Unrecognized resources, and a genuinely distinctive conservation-specific carve-out
Resources meeting the asset definition but failing reliable measurement → disclose qualitative info (nature, location, quantity), the measurement DIFFICULTIES, and SIGNIFICANCE to entity objectives (para. 68) Stewardship-only resources (unrecognized) → explain the nature of stewardship rights/responsibilities and the legislation establishing them (para. 71)
⚠️ Rare/endangered exemption: disclosing LOCATION, QUANTITY, or CARRYING AMOUNT of a rare or endangered resource could itself facilitate further endangerment (e.g. poaching coordinates) — an entity MAY withhold that specific detail, disclosing only the general nature of the resource plus the fact AND reason disclosure was limited (paras. 60, 69, AG42–AG43). A genuinely novel disclosure carve-out with no real precedent elsewhere in this library.

Worked Example — Determining Current Operational Value of a Conservation Forest (Illustrative Example 3)

A government entity obtains a 5,000-hectare conservation woodland via a non-exchange transaction
Two acceptable valuation approaches, both permitted under IPSAS 46 — market approach vs. cost approach
Case A — Market approach (external valuer): Uses historical purchase data from comparable conservation forests, adjusted for location/size differences, species differences, and average plantation age — cross-checked against physically similar TIMBER-PRODUCTION forests (with purpose differences factored in) as a reasonableness check. Result: CU28 million.

Case B — Cost approach (develop a similar asset): Estimates the cost to GROW a comparable forest from scratch — a ~20-year growth period, CU4,000/hectare forestation costs (site prep, seedlings, present value of 20 years' labor/maintenance), plus CU1,500/hectare for land with similar characteristics. Result: 5,000 ha × (CU4,000 + CU1,500) = CU27.5 million.
Both approaches land within ~2% of each other (CU28.0m vs. CU27.5m) — a useful cross-check on estimate reasonableness. Given the estimation uncertainty inherent in either method, the entity must ALSO consider IPSAS 1 paras. 140–148's key-source-of-estimation-uncertainty disclosures, layered on top of this Standard's own §13 current-value disclosure package.

Worked Example — Modified Retrospective vs. Full Retrospective Transition (Illustrative Example 5)

Case A — resource still controlled at transition (historical cost model elected)
Resource A: CU50m current value when control was originally obtained (decades ago, unrecognized under the old framework); CU70m current value by 20X2, held continuously through 20X4 initial IPSAS 51 application
Modified retrospective (para. 74(a)): recognize at CU70m (current value AT the date of initial application, Jan 1, 20X4), adjusting opening accumulated surplus/deficit by CU70m.

Full retrospective under IPSAS 3 (para. 74(b)): recognize at the ORIGINAL CU50m (value when control was first obtained) — since the entity elected the historical cost model, the SUBSEQUENT CU70m increase is never reflected. Carrying amount and opening accumulated surplus/deficit both stay at CU50m.
Case B — resource sold before transition
Same facts, except Resource A was sold in 20X2 for CU70m cash — unrecognized at the time, so the ENTIRE proceeds hit accumulated surplus/deficit as a CU70m gain under the old framework
Modified retrospective: no adjustment at all — the resource wasn't controlled at the Jan 1, 20X4 application date, so it's simply outside scope.

Full retrospective: recognize CU50m at original acquisition (reflected in 20X3 opening accumulated surplus/deficit); the 20X2 sale gain becomes only CU20m (CU70m proceeds − CU50m carrying amount) instead of the original CU70m. Net result: accumulated surplus/deficit at Jan 1, 20X3 is STILL CU70m either way (CU50m initial recognition + CU20m gain) — the retrospective method changes WHERE the value shows up (asset vs. gain), not the total.

Control Assessment — Water & Living Resources (Implementation Guidance IG11–IG17)

Water — free-flowing is (almost) never controllable
Oceans, seas, lakes, rivers — their physical movement generally CAN'T be controlled by any single entity (IG13). Water in a MANAGED environment (e.g. an underground aquifer) CAN be controlled where the entity has legal ownership conferring enforceable service-potential rights, the MEANS to actively manage volume/flow, and the ability to RESTRICT others' access (IG14).
Living resources — mobility complicates everything
Plants (immobile) are generally EASIER to demonstrate control over than animals/fish that can freely roam across jurisdictional boundaries (IG15). Control requires the ability to DIRECT the resource's conservation AND restrict others' access — physical barriers, legislation, or security personnel (IG16). An UNFETTERED ability to sell a living resource is a strong control indicator — but ALSO a strong sign the item is really inventory (IPSAS 12) or a biological asset (IPSAS 27) rather than genuinely within THIS Standard's scope (IG17).

Effective Date & Transition (paras. 72–78)

The most future-dated Standard in this entire library
Periods beginning on/after January 1, 2028 — earlier application permitted, with disclosure if elected (para. 72)
Deliberately NOT required to be adopted alongside IPSAS 50 or the IPSAS 12 stripping-costs amendments — the Board found no technical interdependency requiring simultaneous adoption (Basis for Conclusions BC44).
Two transition methods — a genuine choice
Modified retrospective — recognize qualifying resources at deemed cost (current operational value) AS AT the date of initial application; cumulative effect adjusts opening accumulated surplus/deficit. Resources already DERECOGNIZED before that date need NOT be considered (para. 74(a)) Full retrospective — apply IPSAS 3's ordinary retrospective restatement instead (para. 74(b))
If current value can't be reliably measured at initial application → the same §13 disclosure-only treatment applies as for ongoing unrecognized resources (para. 76). Reclassified assets carry over at their pre-reclassification carrying amount, incl. any identifiable revaluation surplus (para. 77).

Relationship to GFSM 2014 — No IAS/IFRS Equivalent

A genuinely original IPSASB Standard — no IFRS counterpart exists
IPSAS 51 has NO IFRS Standard drawn-from relationship — IFRS has never addressed conservation-held natural resources as a distinct asset category. Instead, the Handbook benchmarks it against the Government Finance Statistics Manual 2014 (GFSM 2014), joining IPSAS 22, 42, 48, and 50 as Standards compared to statistical frameworks rather than IFRS.
TopicComparison
Recognition/measurement basicsALIGNED — both require recognition/measurement, though GFSM 2014 doesn't report these resources SEPARATELY, folding them into the appropriate broader asset category
Scope breadthIPSAS 51 covers ONLY conservation-held resources; GFSM 2014 covers natural resources more BROADLY
Presentation of value changesIPSAS 51: net assets/equity OR surplus/deficit, as appropriate. GFSM 2014: "other economic flows" — a distinct statistical category
SOFP displayIPSAS 51 REQUIRES a separate line item (§12); GFSM 2014 folds these resources into the appropriate nonfinancial-asset category on the balance sheet
Measurement convergenceGFSM 2014 uses MARKET VALUE throughout; IPSAS 51's current operational value may closely APPROXIMATE this, but preparers should check for adjustments (ownership transfer costs, exit-price assumptions)

Statement / Process Mapping

Master decision map
1. Determine the UNIT OF ACCOUNT — is the resource naturally occurring AND held PRIMARILY for conservation? If not → route to whichever IPSAS matches its actual primary purpose (§1, §2) 2. Apply the THREE recognition criteria — probable service potential, control from past events, reliable measurement. Fails reliable measurement? → disclose only, don't recognize (§4, §13) 3. Measure initial cost/deemed cost — current operational value for non-exchange events, cost for exchange transactions (§6) 4. Choose ONE subsequent measurement policy per CLASS — historical cost, or current value (§7, §8) 5. Assess useful life — REBUTTABLE PRESUMPTION of INDEFINITE life; depreciate only if genuinely finite (§9) 6. Test for impairment under IPSAS 21; on disposal/loss of control/no remaining service potential → derecognize (§10) 7. Primary purpose CHANGES? → reclassify, carrying amount becomes deemed cost under the new Standard (§11)

Quick-Reference Glossary

Tangible natural resource held for conservation
A naturally occurring tangible asset that is managed to prevent its degradation.
Class of tangible natural resources held for conservation
A grouping of resources of similar nature, shown as a single disclosure item.
Degradation
A reduction in the overall service potential embodied in a tangible natural resource held for conservation.
Current operational value
The amount the entity would pay for the remaining service potential of an asset held primarily for operational capacity.
Naturally occurring
The living and non-living components of the Earth constituting the biophysical and geophysical environment.
Endangered / rare resources
Resources seriously at risk of extinction, or uncommon/scarce/infrequently encountered, respectively.
