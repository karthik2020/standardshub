HGB §§341q–341y — Zahlungsbericht: Ergänzende Vorschriften für bestimmte Unternehmen des Rohstoffsektors
Extended reference poster · Book 3, Section 4, Subsection 3 (entire Subsection) · Anti-corruption payment transparency for extractive and forestry companies · German commercial law · EUR examples
A GENUINELY DIFFERENT KIND OF TRANSPARENCY MECHANISM: unlike everything else this poster series has documented, §§341q–341y aren't about a company's OWN financial position at all — they require large extractive and forestry companies to publicly report their PAYMENTS TO GOVERNMENTS, country by country and project by project, specifically to combat resource-sector corruption. Introduced by the 2015 BilRUG, implementing Chapter 10 of the EU Accounting Directive.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Transparency aimed outward, at governments, not inward, at the company's own accounts
Every provision this poster series has documented protects someone's understanding of a COMPANY's own financial position — creditors, shareholders, the public. §§341q–341y do something genuinely different: they require transparency about payments a company makes TO governments, specifically because extractive-sector payments to resource-rich governments have historically been a major global corruption vector, particularly in developing countries where oil, gas, mining, and logging revenues can otherwise disappear from public accountability entirely.
HOW §§341Q–341Y MAP TO GERMAN ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES

Scope — §341q, and the Inlandsemittent Extension

Large extractive and forestry companies — and issuers regardless of corporate form
§341q applies this Subsection to large Kapitalgesellschaften and PIEs active in the mineralgewinnende Industrie (mineral-extracting industry — oil, gas, coal, and mining) OR in Holzeinschlag in Primärwäldern (logging of primary/old-growth forests). Notably, certain Inlandsemittenten (domestic securities issuers, per WpHG) that issue securities but are NOT themselves organised as a Kapitalgesellschaft are ALSO required, through CORRESPONDING application of §§341r–341v, to prepare a Zahlungsbericht or Konzernzahlungsbericht — ensuring the obligation reaches genuinely comparable issuers regardless of their own specific legal form, the SAME anti-avoidance logic this series has documented repeatedly.
3

§341r — Key Definitions: Staatliche Stelle, Projekt, and the Seven Payment Categories

The vocabulary underlying the entire reporting framework
§341r defines the terms this Subsection's substantive provisions depend on: "staatliche Stelle" (governmental entity — the payment recipient, at national, regional, or local level, including bodies controlled by such entities); "Projekt" (project — the operational activities governed by a single contract, licence, lease, concession, or similar legal agreement forming the basis for payment obligations to a government); and, at §341r Nr.3 Buchstabe a-g, SEVEN specific payment categories the report must track — including production entitlements, income/production/profit-based taxes (excluding consumption-type taxes like VAT), royalties, dividends, signature/discovery/production bonuses, licence and similar fees, and infrastructure-improvement payments.
4

§341s — the Obligation and Its Exemptions

Mandatory for qualifying companies, with size and duplicate-avoidance exemptions
§341s establishes the actual obligation to prepare a Zahlungsbericht (payment report), with specific exemptions — most notably where a subsidiary's own extractive/forestry payments are ALREADY fully captured within a parent's own Konzernzahlungsbericht (Section 12), echoing the SAME duplicate-avoidance theme this series has documented repeatedly (§264(3), §291/§292, §315b(2)).
5

§341t(1) — Core Content, Including the "Nil Report" Mechanism

Only extractive/forestry-related payments — nothing else — with a genuine nil-activity confirmation
§341t(1) requires the Kapitalgesellschaft to state which payments it made during the reporting period to governmental entities SPECIFICALLY in connection with its extractive/forestry activity — OTHER payments may NOT be included at all, keeping the report tightly focused. Where a company subject to this obligation made NO reportable payments to any governmental entity during a period, the report need only confirm that extractive or primary-forest-logging activity WAS carried out, WITHOUT payments being made — a genuine "nil report" mechanism distinguishing "we had no reportable activity" from "we had activity, but paid nothing."

Worked Example — a Genuine Nil Report

Confirming activity occurred, without needing to report zero-value line items
Example 1: An exploration-stage company with no payment obligations yet
A mining company conducts early-stage exploration activity in a specific country during the reporting period, but has not yet triggered any of the specific §341r payment categories — no royalties, taxes, or licence fees have yet become due under its exploration permit's own terms.
The company's Zahlungsbericht need only state that mineral-extracting activity WAS carried out during the period, WITHOUT any payments being made — a simple, genuine confirmation rather than a report full of zero-value line items, while still keeping the company's activity itself visible and accounted for within the transparency framework.
7

§341t(2) — the Direct-Payment-Only Rule

Reporting only where the company itself made the payment
§341t(2) confines the reporting obligation to governmental entities the company paid DIRECTLY — even where that recipient governmental entity subsequently passes the payment on to a further entity, the REPORTING company need not trace or report that DOWNSTREAM redistribution itself, keeping the reporting burden anchored to the company's own direct payment relationships.
8

§341t(5)–(6) — In-Kind Payments, and a Genuine Anti-Avoidance Rule

Value over form, and a genuine prohibition on artificial structuring
§341t(5) requires payments made AS in-kind benefits (Sachleistungen — e.g., providing infrastructure or goods in place of a cash payment) to be considered by their VALUE and, where relevant, their volume, with the report explaining HOW that value was determined. §341t(6) provides the Subsection's own explicit anti-avoidance rule: reference is made to the SUBSTANCE of a payment or activity, not its FORM, and payments/activities may NOT be artificially split or combined with the aim of circumventing this Subsection's application — a genuinely direct anti-avoidance provision, more explicit than most substance-over-form principles this series has documented elsewhere.
9

Worked Example — Valuing an In-Kind Payment

Infrastructure provided instead of cash, still fully captured
Example 2: Building a road in place of a cash infrastructure payment
As part of its mining concession terms, a company constructs a public access road for the host government, valued at €2,400,000, INSTEAD of making an equivalent cash infrastructure-improvement payment.
The company's Zahlungsbericht reports this AS a €2,400,000 infrastructure-improvement payment under §341t(5), with an explanation of how the €2,400,000 valuation was determined — ensuring the transparency framework captures the payment's genuine ECONOMIC substance, REGARDLESS of the fact that no cash actually changed hands.
10

§341u — Structuring the Report: Country by Country, Project by Project

A genuinely granular, two-dimensional reporting structure
§341u(1) requires the Zahlungsbericht to be structured BY COUNTRY, with each country's own relevant governmental entities identified precisely enough (official designation, plus location/region) to allow UNIQUE attribution. §341u(3)-(4) then require, WHERE a payment relates to a specific project, the total amounts by EACH of the §341r payment categories to be shown for that project specifically — EXCEPT where a payment obligation is imposed on the company WITHOUT reference to any specific project (such as general corporate income tax), which can instead be reported at the country level without needing project-level attribution.
11

Worked Example — Building a Multi-Country, Multi-Project Payment Report

Two dimensions of granularity, applied only where genuinely relevant
Example 3: A company operating two mining projects in one country, plus general tax obligations
A mining company operates two separate, licence-based projects within the same country: Project Alpha, paying €800,000 in royalties and €300,000 in licence fees to the national mining authority, and Project Beta, paying €450,000 in royalties to the same authority. The company also pays €1,200,000 in general corporate income tax to the national tax authority, unrelated to any specific project.
The report shows, for the mining authority: Project Alpha — €800,000 royalties, €300,000 licence fees; Project Beta — €450,000 royalties — each broken out SEPARATELY by project and payment category. The €1,200,000 general income tax, having NO specific project attribution, is instead reported simply at the COUNTRY level against the tax authority, without needing to be artificially allocated across Project Alpha or Beta.
12

§341v — the Konzernzahlungsbericht

The same reporting logic, consolidated across the entire group
§341v requires a parent Kapitalgesellschaft to prepare a Konzernzahlungsbericht (group payment report), consolidating information about ALL payments to governmental entities made by INCLUDED group companies in connection with their own extractive or primary-forest-logging activity, with §§341s–341u applying CORRESPONDINGLY. The parent need NOT further break payments down by which specific raw material or commodity they relate to — a genuine simplification at the consolidated level, avoiding an unnecessary additional layer of granularity beyond what the country/project structure already provides.
13

Worked Example — Consolidating Group-Wide Extractive Payments

A single, unified group-wide picture of extractive-sector payments
Example 4: A parent consolidating two subsidiaries' own extractive payments
A mining group's parent company has two subsidiaries, each independently making payments to different governmental entities in different countries in connection with their own separate extractive operations.
The parent's OWN Konzernzahlungsbericket consolidates BOTH subsidiaries' payment information into a single group-wide report, structured by country and project per §§341s–341u's corresponding application — and, per §341v(5), the qualifying subsidiaries are THEMSELVES exempted from separately preparing their own individual Zahlungsberichte, since their information is already fully captured at the group level, consistent with the duplicate-avoidance theme flagged in Section 4.
14

§341w–341y — Disclosure and Enforcement

The same disclosure and enforcement architecture, applied to a new document type
§341w requires the Zahlungsbericht/Konzernzahlungsbericht to be disclosed, broadly paralleling the general §325 disclosure framework this series has documented in detail. §341x supplies administrative fine (Bußgeld) consequences for non-compliance, while §341y supplies coercive fine (Ordnungsgeld) provisions, mirroring the §334/§335 architecture already established for the general corporate disclosure regime — ensuring this genuinely distinct transparency mechanism is backed by the SAME enforcement seriousness as the rest of Book 3.
15

The Broader Policy Context — EITI and Global Extractive Transparency

Part of a genuinely global transparency movement, not a purely German innovation
§§341q–341y implement Chapter 10 of the EU's own 2013/34/EU Accounting Directive, itself part of a broader, genuinely GLOBAL movement toward extractive-sector payment transparency — paralleling the Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative (EITI), a voluntary international standard many resource-rich countries themselves participate in, and comparable mandatory disclosure requirements introduced elsewhere (such as under the US Dodd-Frank Act). The shared underlying policy objective across all these initiatives: extractive-sector payments to governments have historically been a significant global corruption vector, particularly in resource-rich developing countries where such revenues can otherwise disappear from meaningful public accountability entirely.

Critical Judgments & Practical Difficulties

Where judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Determining whether a specific payment genuinely relates to a distinct "Projekt" or should instead be reported at the country level, particularly for obligations with ambiguous project attribution
PRACTICAL DIFFICULTIES
Valuing genuinely complex in-kind payments (infrastructure, goods, services) with sufficient precision and a defensible, disclosed methodology
17

Relationship to IFRS

A regulatory transparency mechanism sitting outside IFRS entirely
IFRS itself has no direct equivalent — payment-to-government transparency reporting is a REGULATORY disclosure requirement (implementing a specific EU Directive chapter) rather than a financial accounting standard, and applies REGARDLESS of whether a company's own financial statements follow HGB or, via §315e, EU-IFRS. A company's choice of accounting framework for its Jahresabschluss/Konzernabschluss has NO bearing on whether this genuinely separate reporting obligation applies.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Zahlungsbericht
Payment report — the standalone document disclosing extractive/forestry payments to governmental entities.
Staatliche Stelle
Governmental entity — the payment recipient, at any level of government, or bodies controlled by such entities.
Projekt
The operational activities governed by a single contract, licence, lease, or concession forming the basis for payment obligations to a government.
Mineralgewinnende Industrie
Mineral-extracting industry — oil, gas, coal, and mining activity, one of the two triggering business types.
Holzeinschlag in Primärwäldern
Logging in primary/old-growth forests — the second triggering business type.
Sachleistung
A payment made in kind rather than cash, reported by its value under §341t(5).
