Conceptual Framework Linkage

A narrow, historically-rooted exception rather than a comprehensive framework
Agricultural products have LONG been recognised as a special case in accounting – once HARVESTED, crops and animals often have a READY market, a REASONABLY ASSURED price, negligible marketing costs, and are largely INTERCHANGEABLE, making the traditional historical-cost model less meaningful. ASC 905 addresses this through a NARROW, specific exception (Section 4) – but does NOT attempt a comprehensive remeasurement framework for agricultural assets more broadly.
A major structural gap compared to IAS 41
IAS 41 (Agriculture) takes a FUNDAMENTALLY different, far more comprehensive approach – requiring virtually ALL biological assets to be remeasured to FAIR VALUE at EVERY reporting date, throughout their entire growth cycle, with changes flowing through PROFIT OR LOSS. US GAAP has NO equivalent standard-wide biological asset fair value model – this is one of the most SUBSTANTIAL remaining measurement-basis divergences anywhere in the Codification (Section 5).
2

Scope – Agricultural Producers and Cooperatives

Crops, livestock, and agricultural cooperative entities
ASC 905 applies to entities engaged in AGRICULTURAL PRODUCER activities (growing crops, raising livestock) and to AGRICULTURAL COOPERATIVES (entities owned by and operated for the benefit of their member-producers, e.g. grain elevators, dairy cooperatives).
3

General Measurement – Historical Cost Remains the Default

Growing crops and immature animals generally follow ordinary cost accumulation
As a GENERAL rule, growing crops and IMMATURE (not-yet-harvested/not-yet-mature) animals continue to be accounted for at ACCUMULATED HISTORICAL COST – the costs of seed, feed, labor, and other direct/allocable costs are accumulated as the crop grows or the animal matures, much like WORK-IN-PROCESS inventory in a manufacturing context. There is NO general requirement to remeasure growing crops or immature livestock to fair value during their growth phase under US GAAP.
4

The Market Value (Net Realizable Value) Exception

A narrow exception at, or near, the point of harvest/completion
Certain agricultural products – MOST commonly at or near HARVEST or completion of growth – may be stated at SALES (MARKET) PRICE, LESS DISPOSAL COSTS, even ABOVE cost (recognising a gain BEFORE actual sale), PROVIDED specific criteria are met: (a) the product has a RELIABLE, READILY DETERMINABLE market price; (b) the product has RELATIVELY INSIGNIFICANT and PREDICTABLE disposal costs; and (c) the product is available for IMMEDIATE delivery. This "completed-production" or "market price" method is a narrow, LONG-STANDING exception to the normal lower-of-cost-(or-market) inventory principle, applicable mainly to certain grains, some precious metals, and select commodities that meet these specific criteria.
5

THE KEY DIVERGENCE – No Comprehensive Biological Asset Fair Value Model

IAS 41's mandatory, comprehensive fair value remeasurement – with no US GAAP equivalent
IAS 41 requires an entity to measure BIOLOGICAL ASSETS (living plants and animals) – and, in most cases, AGRICULTURAL PRODUCE at the point of harvest – at FAIR VALUE LESS COSTS TO SELL, at EVERY reporting date, THROUGHOUT the ENTIRE growth cycle (not merely at or near harvest), with ALL changes in fair value (both from PRICE changes and from PHYSICAL growth/transformation) recognised DIRECTLY in PROFIT OR LOSS as they occur – a genuinely COMPREHENSIVE, MANDATORY fair value model (subject to a narrow cost-based exception only where fair value cannot be reliably measured). US GAAP has NO PARALLEL, standard-wide biological asset fair value framework – ASC 905's narrow market-value exception (Section 4) applies only NARROWLY, generally near completion/harvest, under SPECIFIC qualifying criteria, and growing crops/immature livestock are otherwise carried at HISTORICAL COST throughout their growth phase. This represents one of the MOST SUBSTANTIAL remaining measurement-basis divergences between the two frameworks anywhere in this library – a company's DAIRY HERD, ORCHARD, or STANDING TIMBER could be measured on an ENTIRELY different basis (continuously remeasured fair value vs accumulated historical cost) purely depending on which framework applies.
6

Cooperative-Specific Accounting

Patronage dividends and marketing arrangements
Agricultural COOPERATIVES have their own specific accounting considerations – notably PATRONAGE DIVIDENDS (distributions to member-producers based on the volume of business each member transacted with the cooperative, rather than on ownership shares as such), and specific guidance on accounting for MARKETING arrangements between the cooperative and its member-producers.
7

Worked Example – the Market Value Method for a Harvested Crop

Recognising a gain before sale, under narrow criteria
Example: Harvested wheat with a reliable market price
A grain producer harvests wheat with an accumulated cost of $400,000. The wheat has a well-established commodity market price, insignificant and predictable transportation/handling costs of $15,000, and is available for immediate delivery to buyers. Its market value less disposal costs is $460,000.
Since the wheat meets ALL the specific qualifying criteria (reliable market price, insignificant/predictable disposal costs, available for immediate delivery), the producer may state the wheat at $460,000 – recognising a $60,000 gain ($460,000 − $400,000) BEFORE actual sale, under the narrow market value exception.
8

Worked Example – Contrasting IAS 41's Growing-Crop Fair Value Model

The same orchard, two fundamentally different measurement bases
Example: A maturing apple orchard, not yet harvested
An entity owns an apple orchard, with trees still several months from harvest. Accumulated historical cost (fertilizer, labor, water) to date is $2,000,000. An appraiser estimates the trees' fair value less costs to sell, reflecting current market conditions and the trees' growth stage, at $2,650,000.
Under US GAAP (ASC 905): the orchard remains at its $2,000,000 ACCUMULATED HISTORICAL COST – the trees are not yet harvested, so the narrow market value exception does NOT apply; NO remeasurement gain is recognised.
Under IAS 41: the orchard (a biological asset) is remeasured to its $2,650,000 FAIR VALUE LESS COSTS TO SELL, recognising a $650,000 gain DIRECTLY in profit or loss – EVEN THOUGH the trees are still growing and no harvest or sale has occurred.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Market value (completed-production) method
A narrow exception allowing certain harvested agricultural products meeting specific criteria to be stated at market price less disposal costs, even above cost.
Biological asset
A living plant or animal – the subject of IAS 41's comprehensive fair value remeasurement model, with no equivalent standard-wide framework under US GAAP.
Patronage dividend
A distribution by an agricultural cooperative to its member-producers based on the volume of business each member transacted with the cooperative.
