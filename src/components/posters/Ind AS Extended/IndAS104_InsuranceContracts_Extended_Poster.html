Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why India's insurance accounting sits in a distinctive, dual-track position
India's insurance SECTOR is regulated by IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India), which prescribes its OWN detailed financial statement formats and actuarial reserving requirements for LICENSED insurers under the Insurance Act, 1938 and IRDAI regulations. Ind AS 104 was notified as India's converged version of IFRS 4, but its APPLICATION to insurance COMPANIES themselves has been repeatedly DEFERRED by the MCA — meaning most Indian insurers continue preparing statutory financial statements under IRDAI's own framework rather than full Ind AS. Ind AS 104 remains directly relevant primarily to NON-insurance entities holding insurance contracts, or for LIMITED purposes.
HOW IND AS 104 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Pragmatic sequencing, similar to IFRS 4's original rationale — just as IFRS 4 was a deliberate interim step pending comprehensive global reform, India's DEFERRAL of full Ind AS for insurers reflects a similar pragmatic sequencing, prioritising REGULATORY (IRDAI) stability for a systemically important, heavily supervised sector Faithful representation, safeguarded by liability adequacy — the liability adequacy test (Section 5) provides the SAME minimum reliability floor as under IFRS 4, wherever Ind AS 104 IS applied A standard in genuine TRANSITION — India has been actively working toward an Ind AS 117 (IFRS 17-equivalent) implementation roadmap for insurers, meaning Ind AS 104's current relevance is itself somewhat TEMPORARY, similar to how IFRS 4 itself was always understood as interim globally
This is a genuinely IMPORTANT and DISTINCTIVE feature of the Ind AS landscape — unlike Ind AS 101/102/103 (converged with CURRENT IFRS), Ind AS 104 reflects convergence with an OLDER, SUPERSEDED global standard (IFRS 4), since India has not yet completed its transition to an IFRS 17 equivalent.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 104 covers
Ind AS 104 applies to insurance contracts an entity ISSUES (including reinsurance contracts held), and financial instruments with discretionary participation features — IDENTICAL scope structure to IFRS 4.
In PRACTICE, its most common Indian application is for NON-insurance entities holding certain insurance-linked instruments, or IRDAI-regulated entities to the (limited) extent Ind AS applies to them.
Key definitions you must know
IRDAI
Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India — the statutory body regulating and supervising India's insurance sector, prescribing its own financial statement formats for licensed insurers.
Liability adequacy test
Identical concept to IFRS 4 — a minimum reliability check ensuring recognised insurance liabilities are not understated using current cash flow estimates.

Insurance Risk — The Classification Gateway (with worked example)

Identical test to IFRS 4
A contract qualifies as an "insurance contract" only if it transfers SIGNIFICANT insurance risk — the SAME test as IFRS 4, with contracts lacking significant insurance risk instead accounted for under Ind AS 109 (the Ind AS equivalent of IFRS 9).
Example 1: Testing significant insurance risk under Ind AS 104
An Indian non-insurance holding company holds a group credit-life policy on behalf of employees, paying out a fixed sum assured on death, materially exceeding the policy's cash surrender value.
This clearly transfers SIGNIFICANT insurance risk, qualifying for Ind AS 104 treatment (with the underlying insurer, a licensed Indian insurance company, itself reporting under IRDAI's framework for its own statutory purposes) — an identical analysis to how IFRS 4 would classify the same contract globally.
4

Continuation of Existing Accounting Policies

Identical interim mechanism to IFRS 4
Where Ind AS 104 applies, an entity may CONTINUE its existing insurance accounting policies without full IAS 8/Ind AS 8 justification — the SAME explicit carve-out as IFRS 4, subject to the liability adequacy test and prohibited practices (Section 7).
5

The Liability Adequacy Test

Identical mechanism to IFRS 4
An entity assesses, at each reporting date, whether recognised insurance liabilities are ADEQUATE using CURRENT cash flow estimates — any deficiency recognised IMMEDIATELY in profit or loss, mirroring IFRS 4's own minimum reliability floor exactly.
6

IRDAI's Regulatory Role in Indian Insurance Accounting (with worked example)

A parallel, and in practice DOMINANT, reporting framework
IRDAI prescribes DETAILED financial statement formats (per the IRDAI (Preparation of Financial Statements and Auditor's Report of Insurance Companies) Regulations), actuarial VALUATION methodologies for insurance liabilities, and SOLVENCY margin requirements — a comprehensive REGULATORY accounting framework that, for LICENSED Indian insurers, effectively takes PRECEDENCE over Ind AS 104 in practical day-to-day statutory reporting.
Example 2: IRDAI format vs Ind AS 104 in practice
A licensed Indian life insurance company prepares its statutory annual financial statements using IRDAI's prescribed Revenue Account, Profit and Loss Account, and Balance Sheet formats, with insurance liabilities valued per IRDAI actuarial regulations.
The insurer's PRIMARY statutory reporting obligation is to IRDAI's framework, NOT Ind AS 104 directly — Ind AS 104 becomes most directly relevant for entities OUTSIDE the IRDAI-regulated insurance sector holding insurance-type contracts, or in the context of India's ONGOING roadmap toward eventually notifying a comprehensive Ind AS 117 for insurers themselves.
7

Prohibited Practices

Identical prohibitions to IFRS 4
Ind AS 104 prohibits catastrophe and equalisation provisions for risks NOT yet incurred — an IDENTICAL prohibition to IFRS 4, since these represent future, not present, obligations under the Conceptual Framework's basic liability definition.
8

Embedded Derivatives & Unbundling

Identical treatment to IFRS 4
Embedded derivatives requiring separation are bifurcated and measured at fair value per Ind AS 109; unbundling of deposit components is permitted but not required, IDENTICAL to IFRS 4's own treatment.

Disclosure Requirements

Required disclosures — identical structure to IFRS 4
