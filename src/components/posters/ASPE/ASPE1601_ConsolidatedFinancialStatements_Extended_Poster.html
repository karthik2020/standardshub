1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Combining a group's financial statements into one set of accounts
ASPE Section 1601 governs HOW a PARENT enterprise, HAVING elected CONSOLIDATION under SECTION 1591, PREPARES consolidated FINANCIAL statements — COMBINING the PARENT'S and SUBSIDIARIES' individual FINANCIAL statements INTO a SINGLE, unified SET presenting the GROUP as ONE economic ENTITY. THE underlying MECHANICS closely PARALLEL IFRS 10'S OWN consolidation PROCEDURES internationally.
2

Scope — Applied Once Consolidation Is Elected

The implementation standard following Section 1591's policy choice
This Section applies to ENTERPRISES that HAVE elected, UNDER Section 1591, to CONSOLIDATE their SUBSIDIARIES — WHERE an ENTERPRISE instead ELECTS the COST or EQUITY method, THIS Section'S consolidation MECHANICS do NOT apply.
3

Combining Financial Statements Line by Line

Assets, liabilities, income, and expenses added together
CONSOLIDATED financial STATEMENTS combine the PARENT'S and EACH subsidiary'S assets, LIABILITIES, equity, INCOME, and EXPENSES ON a LINE-by-line basis — PRESENTING the GROUP as IF it WERE a SINGLE economic ENTITY, RATHER than a COLLECTION of LEGALLY separate COMPANIES.
4

Elimination of Intragroup Balances and Transactions

Removing transactions within the group entirely
INTRAGROUP balances, TRANSACTIONS, income, and EXPENSES (INCLUDING unrealized PROFITS and losses ARISING from intragroup TRANSACTIONS, such AS inventory sold BETWEEN group MEMBERS that REMAINS unsold at YEAR end) are ELIMINATED in FULL — ENSURING the GROUP'S consolidated RESULTS reflect ONLY transactions WITH parties OUTSIDE the group.
5

Uniform Accounting Policies Across the Group

Adjusting subsidiary statements to match group policy
CONSOLIDATED financial STATEMENTS are PREPARED using UNIFORM accounting POLICIES for LIKE transactions and EVENTS ACROSS the ENTIRE group — WHERE a SUBSIDIARY applies DIFFERENT accounting POLICIES from the GROUP'S own, APPROPRIATE adjustments ARE made to THAT subsidiary'S financial STATEMENTS when PREPARING the CONSOLIDATED statements.
6

Aligning Reporting Dates

Coterminous year-ends where practicable
WHERE PRACTICABLE, a SUBSIDIARY'S reporting DATE is ALIGNED with the PARENT'S own REPORTING date — WHERE this IS not PRACTICABLE, the SUBSIDIARY'S own MOST recent FINANCIAL statements are USED, WITH adjustments MADE for the EFFECTS of SIGNIFICANT transactions or EVENTS occurring IN the intervening PERIOD between the TWO reporting DATES.
7

Loss of Control — Deconsolidation

Removing a former subsidiary's assets and liabilities
UPON losing CONTROL of a SUBSIDIARY, the PARENT derecognizes the SUBSIDIARY'S assets (INCLUDING any GOODWILL) and LIABILITIES at THEIR carrying AMOUNTS as OF the date CONTROL is LOST, derecognizes the CARRYING amount of ANY non-CONTROLLING interest, and RECOGNIZES any RESULTING gain OR loss.
8

Labeling Requirements

Every statement clearly identified as consolidated
EACH primary FINANCIAL statement MUST be LABELED as "CONSOLIDATED" — CLEARLY signaling to READERS that the STATEMENTS present the GROUP as a WHOLE, RATHER than the PARENT'S own SEPARATE, non-CONSOLIDATED position.
9

Worked Example — Eliminating an Intragroup Sale

Removing unrealized profit still sitting in inventory
Example: Unsold intragroup inventory
A parent sells inventory to its subsidiary for $500,000, recording a $120,000 profit on the sale. At year end, the subsidiary has resold none of this inventory to outside customers.
The CONSOLIDATED financial STATEMENTS ELIMINATE the FULL $120,000 UNREALIZED profit — SINCE, from the GROUP'S perspective, NO sale to an OUTSIDE party has YET occurred — REDUCING both CONSOLIDATED revenue/PROFIT and the CARRYING amount OF the INVENTORY.
10

Worked Example — Deconsolidation Upon Loss of Control

Recognizing a gain or loss when a subsidiary is sold
Example: Selling a controlling stake
A parent sells its entire 80% controlling interest in a subsidiary for $3,200,000 cash. At the date control is lost, the subsidiary's net assets have a consolidated carrying amount of $2,700,000, with a related non-controlling interest carrying amount of $540,000.
The PARENT derecognizes the SUBSIDIARY'S $2,700,000 net ASSETS and the $540,000 non-CONTROLLING interest, RECOGNIZING a GAIN of $3,200,000 − ($2,700,000 − $540,000) = $1,040,000 on DECONSOLIDATION.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Intragroup elimination
The removal of balances, transactions, and unrealized profits arising between members of a consolidated group.
Deconsolidation
The derecognition of a former subsidiary's assets, liabilities, and non-controlling interest upon the parent's loss of control.
Financial statement key (ASPE terminology)
BS Balance Sheet IS Income Statement SRE Statement of Retained Earnings / Statement of Changes in Equity SCF Cash Flow Statement
