HGB §331 — Unrichtige Darstellung (Misrepresentation)

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Everything this series has documented, seen from the criminal-law side
Every recognition rule, measurement principle, and disclosure requirement this poster series has covered assumes GOOD FAITH preparation. §331 addresses the opposite scenario: what happens when someone in a position of responsibility deliberately gets it wrong — not through error or genuine disagreement about a judgement call, but through intentional misrepresentation or concealment. This is the sharpest possible enforcement mechanism protecting everything else this series has built.
HOW §331 MAPS TO GERMAN ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES
Gläubigerschutz, given criminal teeth — creditors and shareholders rely on the SAME documents this series has covered; §331 exists specifically to deter deliberate abuse of that reliance Klarer Einblick, as a criminal-law floor — every "true and fair view" provision this series has documented (§243(2), §264(2), §297) has §331 standing behind it as the ultimate, criminal-law-backed guarantee against deliberate distortion

Scope — a Sonderdelikt, Not a General Offence

Only those in a specific position of responsibility can commit this offence
§331 is a Sonderdelikt — a "special offence" that can only be committed by someone holding a SPECIFIC status: members of a Kapitalgesellschaft's management board or executive body, members of its supervisory board, or (for the formation-related offence) the company's founders. An outside party with no such role cannot directly commit this particular offence, though they might separately face liability under general criminal law provisions for fraud or similar conduct.
3

§331 Nr.1 — Misrepresentation in the Core Financial Statements

Every major document this series has covered, named directly
§331 Nr.1 criminalises management board, executive body, or supervisory board members who misrepresent or conceal the company's (or, for a group, the Konzern's) circumstances in the opening balance sheet, the Jahresabschluss, the Lagebericht, the Konzernabschluss, or the Konzernlagebericht — literally naming, as the objects of potential falsification, the SAME documents this entire poster series has spent so many posters documenting how to prepare correctly.

Worked Example — Falsifying a Provision to Improve Reported Results

Deliberately omitting a known, mandatory provision
Example 1: Deliberately omitting a known onerous-contract provision
Recall the onerous contract example from the earlier §§246–251 poster — a company genuinely knows a supply contract has become loss-making, mandating an €80,000 Rückstellung under §249(1). The company's management board, aware of this, deliberately OMITS the provision from the Jahresabschluss specifically to present a more favourable net income figure ahead of a planned refinancing.
This is NOT merely a §249 measurement error — the DELIBERATE, knowing omission, done specifically to present a more favourable picture, satisfies §331 Nr.1's misrepresentation element, exposing the responsible board members to imprisonment of up to three years or a fine, ON TOP OF whatever civil consequences (director liability, audit qualification, contract rescission) might separately follow.
5

§331 Nr.2 — Misrepresentation to the Shareholders' Meeting

Beyond the formal statements themselves — oral or informal presentations too
§331 Nr.2 extends the SAME prohibition to presentations or overviews of the company's ASSET POSITION given directly to the shareholders' or general meeting — capturing misrepresentation that occurs OUTSIDE the formal, written Jahresabschluss/Lagebericht themselves, such as in a board's own oral or supplementary presentation to shareholders at the annual meeting.
6

Worked Example — a False Asset-Position Presentation

A misleading verbal presentation, independent of the written accounts themselves
Example 2: Overstating cash reserves at the annual meeting
At the annual shareholders' meeting, a management board member verbally presents the company's cash position as "comfortably strong" and specifically overstates available liquid reserves by a material amount, KNOWING this to be false, in order to discourage shareholders from pressing for a planned dividend cut.
Even though this occurs in a VERBAL presentation rather than the written Jahresabschluss itself, the deliberate misrepresentation of the company's asset position directly TO the shareholders' meeting satisfies §331 Nr.2 independently — the offence is not limited to the formal WRITTEN documents alone.
7

§331 Nr.3 — Misrepresentation in a Capital Increase Context

Protecting the integrity of a specific, high-stakes corporate action
§331 Nr.3 addresses AG/KGaA management board members who, in the disclosures or explanations given specifically in connection with a capital increase (Kapitalerhöhung) examination or similar corporate action, misrepresent or conceal material facts — protecting the integrity of a specific, high-stakes moment where new investors are relying on accurate representations to decide whether to contribute fresh capital.
8

Worked Example — Overstating Contributed Assets in a Capital Increase

Misrepresenting the value of a non-cash contribution
Example 3: Overstating a contributed patent's value
During a capital increase, a management board member knowingly overstates the value of a patent being contributed as a non-cash (Sacheinlage) contribution in exchange for new shares, in the formal disclosures accompanying the capital increase examination.
The DELIBERATE overstatement of the contributed patent's value in this specific corporate-action context satisfies §331 Nr.3 — new and existing shareholders are entitled to rely on an accurate valuation when the company's own capital structure is being genuinely changed through this process.
9

§331 Nr.4 — Misrepresentation in Company Formation Documents

Extending backward to the company's very founding
§331 Nr.4 extends this same protection backward to a company's own FORMATION — founders, or members of its executive/supervisory bodies, who misrepresent or conceal the circumstances of the company's formation in the documents that process requires (such as a founders' report, Gründungsbericht) commit the same category of offence.
10

Worked Example — a False Founders' Report

Falsifying the record at the very moment a company comes into existence
Example 4: Concealing a related-party discount in a founders' report
A company's founders knowingly conceal, in the formation-related Gründungsbericht, that a significant asset contributed to the new company was purchased from a related party at a materially inflated price shortly beforehand — information that would have been genuinely relevant to assessing the fairness of the formation itself.
The deliberate concealment of this material formation circumstance satisfies §331 Nr.4 — the criminal exposure attaches EVEN AT the company's founding, before it has ever prepared an ordinary Jahresabschluss at all.
11

The Mental Element — Why Genuine Negligence Alone Is Not Enough

Intent, not mere error, is what §331 targets
§331's core verbs — "unrichtig wiedergeben oder verschleiern" (misrepresent or conceal) — carry a genuine INTENT requirement (Vorsatz) under standard German criminal law principles, not mere negligence. A genuinely mistaken measurement judgement, an honest disagreement about a difficult accounting estimate, or an oversight caught and corrected does NOT, by itself, satisfy §331 — the offence targets DELIBERATE falsification or concealment, distinguishing it sharply from the negligence-based Ordnungsgeld/Bußgeld administrative consequences (§334, §335) covered separately elsewhere in this Subsection.
12

Penalty Structure — up to Three Years, or a Fine

Genuine imprisonment exposure, not merely a financial consequence
§331's offences generally carry a penalty of imprisonment (Freiheitsstrafe) of up to THREE YEARS, or a fine (Geldstrafe) — a genuinely severe consequence relative to the purely financial Ordnungsgeld and civil liability mechanisms this series has otherwise documented, reflecting how seriously deliberate financial statement falsification is treated within German criminal law specifically.

Critical Judgments & Practical Difficulties

Where judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Distinguishing a genuine, good-faith accounting judgement call (even one later shown to be wrong) from deliberate misrepresentation carrying the necessary criminal intent
PRACTICAL DIFFICULTIES
Proving the DELIBERATE, knowing nature of a specific misstatement in a criminal proceeding, as distinct from establishing that an error simply occurred
14

Relationship to IFRS — a Layer IFRS Simply Doesn't Address

Criminal enforcement sits entirely outside any accounting standard
IFRS itself has NO equivalent provision — it is an accounting standard, not a criminal statute, and simply does not legislate consequences for deliberate falsification. Criminal liability for financial statement fraud, in an IFRS jurisdiction, is governed ENTIRELY by that jurisdiction's own separate general criminal or securities law (comparable in spirit to statutes like the US Sarbanes-Oxley Act's own criminal provisions), never by IFRS itself. §331 is once again part of the "legal infrastructure" layer this series has repeatedly identified sitting BENEATH any accounting standard — genuinely necessary for the whole system to function, but structurally outside what IFRS as a standard-setter could ever address directly.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Unrichtig wiedergeben
To misrepresent — presenting circumstances inaccurately, one of §331's two core prohibited acts.
Verschleiern
To conceal — deliberately obscuring circumstances, §331's other core prohibited act.
Sonderdelikt
A special offence that can only be committed by someone holding a specific defined status or role — here, management/supervisory board members or founders.
Vorsatz
Intent — the mental element §331 requires; genuine negligence alone does not satisfy the offence.
Gründungsbericht
Founders' report — a company-formation document whose falsification triggers §331 Nr.4.
Freiheitsstrafe
Imprisonment — the penalty §331 exposes offenders to, up to three years, alongside or instead of a fine.
