1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

A single, unified standard covering all non-monetary exchanges
ASBE No. 7 governs EXCHANGES of NON-MONETARY assets BETWEEN two PARTIES – WHETHER with NO monetary CONSIDERATION at ALL, or with ONLY a MINOR monetary "TOP-UP" (BOOT) PAYMENT alongside the NON-MONETARY exchange ITSELF. GENUINELY DISTINCTIVELY, this SINGLE standard COVERS ALL types of non-MONETARY assets – INVENTORIES, fixed ASSETS, intangible ASSETS, and LONG-term equity INVESTMENTS – RATHER than being SCATTERED across MULTIPLE separate ASSET-specific standards, as the EQUIVALENT guidance IS internationally.
2

Scope – All Types of Non-monetary Assets

A unified framework spanning multiple asset categories
This Standard applies to EXCHANGES INVOLVING inventories (ASBE No. 1), fixed ASSETS (ASBE No. 4), intangible ASSETS (ASBE No. 6), and LONG-term equity INVESTMENTS (ASBE No. 2) – WHENEVER TWO parties EXCHANGE such assets DIRECTLY, RATHER than through a CONVENTIONAL cash SALE and separate CASH purchase.
3

Monetary vs Non-monetary Consideration

A quantitative threshold for the "boot" payment
An EXCHANGE QUALIFIES as a NON-MONETARY exchange (RATHER than an ORDINARY monetary SALE/purchase TRANSACTION) WHERE any MONETARY consideration ("BOOT") INVOLVED represents ONLY a MINOR PORTION of the TOTAL fair VALUE of the assets EXCHANGED – GENERALLY assessed AGAINST a SPECIFIED percentage THRESHOLD of the transaction's TOTAL fair value.
4

The Commercial Substance Test

Does the exchange genuinely change the entity's future cash flows?
An EXCHANGE has "COMMERCIAL SUBSTANCE" WHERE the RISK, timing, or AMOUNT of the entity's FUTURE cash FLOWS is EXPECTED to CHANGE SIGNIFICANTLY as a RESULT of the exchange – for EXAMPLE, where the CONFIGURATION (risk, timing, AMOUNT) of cash FLOWS from the ASSET received DIFFERS materially FROM that of the ASSET given up. This TEST DETERMINES WHICH of the TWO measurement APPROACHES applies (Sections 5 and 6).
5

Measurement Where Commercial Substance Exists – the Fair Value Model

Recognising a gain or loss on exchange
WHERE the EXCHANGE HAS commercial SUBSTANCE AND the FAIR value of EITHER the asset RECEIVED or the asset GIVEN up can be MEASURED reliably, the ASSET received is MEASURED at the FAIR VALUE of the asset GIVEN UP (ADJUSTED for any BOOT paid or RECEIVED) – WITH the DIFFERENCE between this FAIR value and the CARRYING amount of the asset GIVEN up RECOGNISED as a GAIN or LOSS on exchange.
6

Measurement Where Commercial Substance Is Absent – the Carrying Amount Model

No gain or loss recognised
WHERE the EXCHANGE LACKS commercial SUBSTANCE, or WHERE neither asset's FAIR value can be MEASURED reliably, the ASSET received is MEASURED at the CARRYING AMOUNT of the asset GIVEN up (ADJUSTED for any BOOT paid or RECEIVED) – WITH NO gain or LOSS recognised on the EXCHANGE itself.
7

A Genuinely Broader Standard Than Its International Counterparts

One standard, rather than several scattered provisions
INTERNATIONALLY, the SAME general "COMMERCIAL SUBSTANCE" test APPEARS SEPARATELY within IAS 16 (fixed ASSETS) and IAS 38 (intangible ASSETS), WITHOUT a SINGLE, UNIFIED standard COVERING ALL non-MONETARY asset TYPES together (INVENTORIES exchanges, for EXAMPLE, are addressed DIFFERENTLY under IFRS 15's OWN non-cash CONSIDERATION guidance). ASBE No. 7's CHOICE to CONSOLIDATE the EQUIVALENT principles INTO a SINGLE, standalone STANDARD spanning ALL asset TYPES is a GENUINELY notable STRUCTURAL feature, EVEN though the UNDERLYING commercial-SUBSTANCE measurement LOGIC itself is BROADLY converged.
8

Worked Example – an Exchange With Commercial Substance

Recognising a gain on exchange
Example: Swapping a delivery vehicle for a piece of production equipment
A company exchanges a delivery vehicle (carrying amount RMB 500,000, fair value RMB 650,000) for a piece of production equipment owned by another company, with no cash changing hands. The exchange genuinely changes the company's future cash flow profile (moving from logistics use to manufacturing use).
Since the exchange HAS commercial SUBSTANCE and FAIR value is RELIABLY measurable, the company RECOGNISES the NEW equipment at RMB 650,000 (the FAIR value of the VEHICLE given up), and a GAIN on exchange of RMB 650,000 − RMB 500,000 = RMB 150,000.
9

Worked Example – an Exchange Without Commercial Substance

Carrying the new asset at the old asset's book value
Example: An exchange lacking genuine economic substance
Two related companies within the same group exchange similar pieces of office equipment (carrying amount RMB 200,000 each), with no meaningful change expected in either company's future cash flows.
Since the exchange LACKS commercial SUBSTANCE, the RECEIVING company measures the NEW equipment at the CARRYING amount of the equipment given UP – RMB 200,000 – with NO gain or loss recognised on the exchange.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Commercial substance
A condition met where an exchange is expected to significantly change the risk, timing, or amount of an entity's future cash flows, determining whether fair value or carrying amount measurement applies.
Boot
A minor monetary payment accompanying an otherwise non-monetary asset exchange.
