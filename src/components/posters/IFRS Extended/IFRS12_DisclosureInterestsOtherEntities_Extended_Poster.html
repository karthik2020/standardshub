Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why "off-balance-sheet" exposures still need disclosure
The Conceptual Framework's relevance objective requires users to understand risk exposures that may NOT be fully captured by consolidation alone – particularly for structured entities an investor is involved with but does NOT control. IFRS 12 exists purely to give users this fuller picture, consolidating (pun intended) ALL "interests in other entities" disclosures into ONE comprehensive standard.
HOW IFRS 12 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Relevance – users need to understand the nature and extent of an entity's interests, and associated risks, even where NOT consolidated Faithful representation – disclosing significant judgements underlying control/joint control/significant influence conclusions helps users assess the RELIABILITY of consolidation decisions A pure disclosure standard – like IFRS 7 and IFRS 8, IFRS 12 introduces no new recognition/measurement rules
IFRS 12 was issued as part of the SAME 2011 consolidation package as IFRS 10 and IFRS 11 – directly responding to financial crisis-era criticism that entities' true exposure to "shadow" or off-balance-sheet vehicles was poorly disclosed.
The four interest categories covered
IFRS 12 consolidates disclosure requirements across every type of "other entity" interest into a single standard.
Interest typeKey disclosure focus
SubsidiariesNCI, restrictions, consolidated structured entities
Joint arrangements & associatesNature, financial effects, summarised financials
Unconsolidated structured entitiesNature, extent, and risks of involvement
ℹ️ Combining what were once scattered disclosures (across IAS 27, IAS 28, IAS 31) into ONE standard improves understandability and completeness.

Scope & Applicability

What IFRS 12 covers
IFRS 12 applies to entities with an interest in a SUBSIDIARY, a JOINT ARRANGEMENT, an ASSOCIATE, or an UNCONSOLIDATED STRUCTURED ENTITY – requiring disclosure of information enabling users to evaluate the NATURE of, and RISKS associated with, its interests, and the EFFECTS of those interests on its financial position, performance, and cash flows.
IFRS 12's scope DELIBERATELY extends to unconsolidated structured entities – arrangements an entity has NO controlling stake in, but which nonetheless carry disclosure-worthy risk exposure.
Key definitions you must know
Structured entity
An entity designed so that voting/similar rights are NOT the dominant factor in deciding who controls it (per IFRS 10).
Interest in another entity
Contractual and non-contractual involvement exposing an entity to variability of returns from the other entity's performance.
Sponsor of a structured entity
An entity involved in the design/creation of a structured entity, even without a current interest, whose name is associated with that entity.

Significant Judgements & Assumptions

Disclosing the reasoning, not just the conclusion
An entity discloses information about SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS AND ASSUMPTIONS made in determining: that it has CONTROL of another entity (IFRS 10); that it has JOINT CONTROL of an arrangement, or SIGNIFICANT INFLUENCE over another entity (IFRS 11/IAS 28); and the TYPE of joint arrangement (joint operation or joint venture) when structured through a separate vehicle.
This is a UNIQUE disclosure requirement – it's not enough to simply STATE the consolidation conclusion; the entity must explain the REASONING, particularly for borderline cases (e.g. de facto control, principal/agent analysis).

Interests in Subsidiaries (with worked example)

NCI and consolidated structured entity disclosures
Disclosures include the composition of the group, interests NCI hold in group activities/cash flows, and – for EACH subsidiary with MATERIAL NCI – summarised financial information (dividends paid to NCI, summarised assets/liabilities/revenue/profit/cash flows).
Example 1: Material NCI disclosure trigger
A parent holds an 80% interest in a subsidiary that represents 15% of the group's total consolidated assets, with the 20% NCI stake considered material at the group level.
Because NCI in this subsidiary is MATERIAL, the parent must disclose SUMMARISED financial information for that subsidiary specifically (its own assets, liabilities, revenue, profit, and dividends paid to NCI) – a subsidiary-specific disclosure, not merely an aggregate NCI figure.
5

Interests in Joint Arrangements & Associates

Nature, extent, and financial effects
For EACH material joint venture and associate: name, principal place of business, proportion of ownership, and whether the investment is measured at equity method or fair value. SUMMARISED financial information is required (assets, liabilities, revenue, profit/loss, other comprehensive income) – for joint ventures, additionally disaggregated by cash and cash equivalents, current/non-current assets/liabilities, and depreciation/interest/tax expense.
6

Unconsolidated Structured Entities (with worked example)

The disclosure that closes the "shadow banking" gap
For structured entities NOT consolidated, an entity discloses qualitative/quantitative information about its interests, including the NATURE, PURPOSE, size, and activities of the structured entity, and HOW it is financed. Where the entity has SPONSORED a structured entity in which it holds NO interest at the reporting date, it discloses how it determined which structured entities it sponsored, and income from those entities during the period.
Example 2: Sponsored securitisation vehicle
A bank sponsors (but does not control or hold an equity interest in) a securitisation vehicle that purchases and repackages loan receivables originated by the bank, generating servicing fee income for the bank.
Even with NO consolidation and NO current equity interest, IFRS 12 requires disclosure of the bank's SPONSORSHIP relationship, the nature/purpose of the vehicle, and the servicing fee income earned – precisely the kind of "shadow" exposure IFRS 12 was designed to surface.
7

Restrictions on Access to Group Assets

Understanding what the group cannot freely use
An entity discloses SIGNIFICANT RESTRICTIONS on its ability to access or use group assets, and settle group liabilities (e.g. those arising from borrowing arrangements, regulatory requirements, or NCI protective rights) – helping users understand that consolidated total assets are NOT always freely available to the parent for group-wide use.
8

Consolidated Structured Entities

Support provided, and support NOT contractually required
Where an entity has PROVIDED financial or other support to a CONSOLIDATED structured entity WITHOUT being contractually required to (e.g. a discretionary bailout), it discloses the TYPE and AMOUNT of support provided, and the reasons – a disclosure specifically designed to surface implicit, non-contractual support arrangements that could otherwise go unnoticed.
9

Investment Entities Disclosures

Additional disclosures for the IFRS 10 investment entity exception
An entity qualifying as an investment entity (Section 7 of the IFRS 10 poster) discloses significant judgements/changes supporting that determination, and information about each unconsolidated subsidiary it measures at fair value instead of consolidating.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Materiality of an individual subsidiary's NCI, triggering subsidiary-specific summarised disclosure Identifying which unconsolidated structured entities the entity has "sponsored"
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Maximum exposure to loss from unconsolidated structured entity interests
⚠️ IFRS 12's disclosures can be extensive and data-intensive, particularly for groups with numerous structured entities or complex NCI arrangements.

IFRS vs US GAAP

Comparison – IFRS 12 vs US GAAP
FeatureIFRS (IFRS 12)US GAAP
Consolidated disclosure standardSINGLE comprehensive standard covering all interest typesDisclosures are SPREAD across multiple topics (ASC 810 for VIEs, ASC 323 for equity method), no single consolidated standard
Unconsolidated VIE/structured entity disclosureExtensive, including sponsorship relationshipsAlso required under ASC 810, broadly similar in substance
IFRS 12's single-standard structure is somewhat MORE STREAMLINED than the equivalent scattered US GAAP guidance, though the underlying disclosure PHILOSOPHY is broadly aligned.

Quick-reference glossary
Maximum exposure to loss
The maximum amount an entity could lose from its involvement with an unconsolidated structured entity, including from any financial support arrangements.
Summarised financial information
Key financial figures (assets, liabilities, revenue, profit/loss) disclosed for material subsidiaries with NCI, joint ventures, and associates.
