ASBJ Statement No. 3 (Abolished) — Partial Amendments to Accounting Standard for Retirement Benefits
Extended reference poster · Japan GAAP (J-GAAP) · ABOLISHED — folded into ASBJ Statement No. 26 · Retained for historical/comparative reference · JPY examples
ABOLISHED STATUS: ASBJ Statement No. 3 was a transitional partial amendment to Japan's retirement benefits accounting, issued as part of a staged reform process. It has since been ABOLISHED (廃止) and its substance fully absorbed into the comprehensive ASBJ Statement No. 26, Accounting Standard for Retirement Benefits — the current, authoritative J-GAAP retirement benefits standard. This poster is retained for historical reference and to explain the staged reform process that led to today's standard.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

A staged, incremental path toward today's comprehensive standard
Japan's retirement benefits accounting REFORM did NOT arrive as a single, comprehensive standard all at once — instead, it was introduced through a SERIES of PARTIAL amendments issued over time (ASBJ Statements No. 3, No. 14, and No. 19), each addressing a SPECIFIC aspect of retirement benefit accounting, BEFORE the ASBJ ultimately consolidated the reform into the SINGLE, comprehensive ASBJ Statement No. 26 — the standard now in force. Statement No. 3 represented an EARLY stage in this INCREMENTAL reform journey.
2

Scope — What This Amendment Historically Addressed

Companies sponsoring retirement benefit plans for employees
Like its successor standards, ASBJ Statement No. 3 HISTORICALLY applied to companies sponsoring RETIREMENT BENEFIT plans (both defined benefit pension arrangements and lump-sum retirement allowance plans, a distinctively COMMON Japanese practice) for their employees.
3

The Staged Reform Process — Statements No. 3, 14, and 19

Three successive partial amendments, each building on the last
StandardHistorical role
ASBJ Statement No. 3An EARLY partial amendment addressing specific retirement benefit measurement/disclosure matters — NOW ABOLISHED
ASBJ Statement No. 14A FURTHER partial amendment (Part 2) — ALSO now abolished
ASBJ Statement No. 19A THIRD partial amendment (Part 3) — this one has REMAINED referenced (not abolished in the same way), reflecting the staged nature of the reform
This staged, multi-part amendment approach is a genuinely DISTINCTIVE feature of J-GAAP standard-setting history — reflecting a preference for INCREMENTAL reform, allowing preparers TIME to adjust to changes before a fully comprehensive standard was finalised.
4

What Statement No. 3 Historically Changed

An early step toward more consistent retirement benefit accounting
As an EARLY-STAGE partial amendment, Statement No. 3 addressed SPECIFIC, NARROWER aspects of retirement benefit accounting current at the time — REFINING particular measurement or disclosure practices WITHOUT yet establishing the FULL, comprehensive framework that ASBJ Statement No. 26 would eventually provide. Its content has since been ENTIRELY absorbed, refined, and superseded by later stages of the reform.
5

Why It Was Abolished — Absorption Into ASBJ Statement No. 26

Consolidation into a single, comprehensive standard
Once the ASBJ completed its COMPREHENSIVE reform of retirement benefit accounting — culminating in ASBJ Statement No. 26, Accounting Standard for Retirement Benefits — the EARLIER, PIECEMEAL partial amendments (including Statement No. 3) became REDUNDANT, since their substance was FULLY incorporated (and, where appropriate, further developed) within the new comprehensive standard. The ASBJ therefore ABOLISHED (廃止) Statement No. 3, consistent with GOOD standard-setting practice of avoiding overlapping, superseded guidance remaining formally in force.
6

The Current Standard — ASBJ Statement No. 26

Today's authoritative retirement benefits framework
ASBJ Statement No. 26 is the CURRENT, comprehensive J-GAAP retirement benefits standard — addressing recognition and measurement of retirement benefit obligations (退職給付債務), plan assets, actuarial gains/losses, past service cost, and the presentation of net retirement benefit liabilities/assets on the Balance Sheet — broadly analogous in SCOPE to IAS 19 (Employee Benefits) internationally, though developed through Japan's OWN staged reform history rather than as a single comprehensive project from the outset.

Worked Example — the Historical Reform in Outline

Tracing a company through the staged reform
Example: A company's retirement benefit accounting across the reform period
A Japanese company sponsoring a lump-sum retirement allowance plan applied EARLIER retirement benefit guidance, then Statement No. 3's partial amendment, then Statements No. 14 and No. 19's further amendments, over a period of ongoing standard-setting reform.
TODAY, the SAME company simply applies ASBJ Statement No. 26 in its ENTIRETY — the comprehensive successor standard that has absorbed and superseded the ENTIRE staged reform history, including the NOW-ABOLISHED Statement No. 3 — with NO need to separately reference the earlier partial amendments.

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Then and Now

Convergence-driven reform, mirroring the broader ASBJ-IASB project
The STAGED retirement benefits reform (Statements No. 3, 14, 19, culminating in No. 26) took place LARGELY within the CONTEXT of the ASBJ's BROADER convergence project with the IASB, launched in 2005 — progressively bringing J-GAAP's retirement benefit accounting CLOSER to IAS 19's principles (e.g. recognising net retirement benefit obligations more fully on the Balance Sheet, refining actuarial gain/loss treatment) over SUCCESSIVE stages, rather than through a single, immediate wholesale adoption. This INCREMENTAL convergence PATTERN is itself a notable, recurring feature of how J-GAAP has approached international alignment across MANY topic areas, not just retirement benefits.
