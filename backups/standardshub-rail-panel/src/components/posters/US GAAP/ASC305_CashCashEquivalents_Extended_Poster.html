Conceptual Framework Linkage

The most liquid, least judgement-laden asset — until classification questions arise
Cash itself requires little measurement judgement, but drawing the LINE between "cash equivalents" and other short-term investments carries real consequences for balance sheet classification and cash flow statement presentation. ASC 305 supplies that boundary definition, working alongside ASC 210 (presentation) and ASC 230 (cash flow statement) to determine what counts as cash for reporting purposes.
2

Scope & Applicability

Applies to all entities
ASC 305 applies to all entities holding cash and short-term investments potentially qualifying as cash equivalents — a foundational, universally applicable Topic underlying both the Balance Sheet and the Statement of Cash Flows.
3

Definition of Cash Equivalents

Short-term, highly liquid, readily convertible
Cash equivalents are SHORT-TERM, HIGHLY LIQUID investments that are (a) READILY CONVERTIBLE to known amounts of cash, and (b) SO NEAR their maturity that they present INSIGNIFICANT risk of changes in value due to interest rate movements. Generally, only investments with ORIGINAL MATURITIES of THREE MONTHS OR LESS from the date of purchase qualify — the SAME three-month benchmark used under IAS 7.
4

Restricted Cash — Cross-Reference

Addressed primarily in ASC 230, but foundational here too
RESTRICTED cash — cash whose use is limited by contract or regulation (e.g. held as loan collateral, or set aside for a specific purpose) — is addressed in detail under ASC 230 (Statement of Cash Flows), particularly following ASU 2016-18's requirement to include restricted cash in the overall cash reconciliation. ASC 305 provides the underlying cash/cash-equivalent DEFINITION that restricted cash guidance builds upon.
5

Compensating Balance Arrangements

A distinctly named, specifically disclosed US GAAP concept
A COMPENSATING BALANCE is a minimum cash balance an entity agrees to maintain with a lending institution, often as an informal condition of a loan or credit arrangement, effectively restricting the entity's free use of that portion of its cash. ASC 305 specifically flags COMPENSATING BALANCE disclosure — the amount and general terms of such arrangements must be disclosed SEPARATELY from ordinary cash. This specifically NAMED concept and its dedicated disclosure requirement is more distinctly codified under US GAAP than under IAS 7, which addresses similar substance through its more general restricted-cash/undrawn-facility disclosure principles without a specifically named "compensating balance" category.
6

Overdrafts

Cross-reference to ASC 210 and ASC 230
Bank overdrafts are generally NOT netted against cash and cash equivalents under US GAAP — they are classified as a current LIABILITY (short-term borrowing), consistent with the treatment described in the ASC 210 (Balance Sheet) and ASC 230 (Statement of Cash Flows) posters, in contrast to IAS 7's narrower allowance for netting overdrafts that are repayable on demand and integral to cash management.
7

Worked Example — Classifying Short-Term Investments

Applying the three-month test
Example: Two money market instruments purchased on the same date
On 1 November, an entity purchases a Treasury bill maturing 15 January (2.5 months from purchase) for $2,000,000, and a separate commercial paper investment maturing 1 May (6 months from purchase) for $1,500,000.
The Treasury bill QUALIFIES as a cash equivalent (original maturity of 2.5 months, within the three-month threshold). The commercial paper does NOT qualify (original maturity of 6 months) — it is classified as a SHORT-TERM INVESTMENT instead, presented separately from cash and cash equivalents on the Balance Sheet.
8

Worked Example — Compensating Balance Disclosure

A loan arrangement requiring a minimum balance
Example: A $500,000 minimum balance requirement
An entity's $5,000,000 line of credit agreement requires it to maintain a minimum $500,000 compensating balance with the lending bank throughout the facility's term.
The entity discloses the $500,000 compensating balance requirement and its general terms SEPARATELY in the notes — while the $500,000 typically remains presented WITHIN cash on the Balance Sheet (since it is not formally legally restricted in the same way segregated restricted cash would be), the disclosure alerts users that this portion of reported cash is not, in practice, freely available for other uses.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where judgement matters most
The central judgement is SELECTING and CONSISTENTLY APPLYING a policy for which highly liquid investments are treated as cash equivalents — while the three-month benchmark is a common convention, ASC 305 leaves the specific policy choice (and any narrower threshold an entity may elect) to management, applied consistently period to period.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Cash equivalents
Short-term, highly liquid investments readily convertible to known cash amounts, with insignificant risk of value change, generally with original maturities of three months or less.
Restricted cash
Cash whose use is limited by contract or regulation, distinguished from a compensating balance by generally being a formal, legally enforceable restriction.
