Conceptual Framework Linkage

The headline change: nearly all leases now on the balance sheet
ASC 842 — the product of a JOINT FASB-IASB project — fundamentally changed lessee accounting: nearly ALL leases (with a narrow short-term exception) are now recognised on the LESSEE's balance sheet as a RIGHT-OF-USE (ROU) asset and a corresponding LEASE LIABILITY, eliminating the ASC 840 off-balance-sheet operating lease result that had long been criticised as obscuring genuine financial commitments.
Converged on recognition, diverged on subsequent expense pattern
ASC 842 and IFRS 16 are CONVERGED on the headline "put it on the balance sheet" principle — but IFRS 16 went further, ELIMINATING lessee lease classification entirely, while ASC 842 RETAINED a dual finance/operating classification model for lessees, producing a genuine, consequential divergence in subsequent EXPENSE RECOGNITION PATTERNS (Section 7) — arguably the most significant remaining US GAAP/IFRS split from the otherwise-joint lease project.
2

Scope & the Short-Term Lease Exception

A narrow carve-out preserving the old off-balance-sheet result
ASC 842 applies to leases of IDENTIFIED assets. A SHORT-TERM LEASE EXCEPTION exists: leases with a term of 12 MONTHS OR LESS (and no purchase option the lessee is reasonably certain to exercise) may be accounted for similarly to old ASC 840 operating leases — expensed on a straight-line basis, WITHOUT ROU asset/liability recognition — a genuine, narrow practical relief, broadly mirrored by IFRS 16's OWN short-term lease exception (also 12 months or less).
3

Lease Identification — Is There a Lease at All?

Control over the use of an identified asset
A contract IS (or CONTAINS) a lease if it conveys the RIGHT to CONTROL the USE of an IDENTIFIED asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration — "control" meaning the customer has the right to obtain substantially all of the economic benefits from the asset's use, AND the right to direct its use. This identification threshold is broadly CONVERGED with IFRS 16's own lease identification model — another product of the joint project.
4

Lessee Initial Recognition — ROU Asset & Lease Liability

Present value of lease payments, on both sides of the entry
At COMMENCEMENT, the lessee recognises a LEASE LIABILITY at the PRESENT VALUE of remaining lease payments (discounted using the rate implicit in the lease, or the lessee's INCREMENTAL BORROWING RATE if the implicit rate isn't readily determinable), and a corresponding RIGHT-OF-USE (ROU) ASSET — initially equal to the lease liability, adjusted for prepaid rent, lease incentives received, and initial direct costs. This initial measurement mechanic is CONVERGED with IFRS 16's own lessee initial recognition.
5

Lessee Classification — Finance vs Operating

Five criteria, echoing the old ASC 840 bright lines (now indicators, not rigid thresholds)
Lessees STILL classify each lease as FINANCE or OPERATING, based on five criteria closely echoing ASC 840's old capital lease tests: ownership transfer, bargain purchase option, lease term is a MAJOR PART of remaining economic life (guidance retains the 75% threshold as an example, not a rigid bright line), present value of payments represents SUBSTANTIALLY ALL of fair value (90% retained as an illustrative threshold), and the asset is so SPECIALISED it has no alternative use to the lessor at lease end. BOTH balance sheet recognition RESULTS look identical at commencement (ROU asset + lease liability either way) — the classification ONLY affects the SUBSEQUENT expense recognition pattern (Section 6).
6

Lessee Subsequent Measurement — Two Different Expense Patterns

Front-loaded finance vs straight-line single lease cost
ClassificationSubsequent expense pattern
Finance leaseROU asset amortised (typically straight-line) SEPARATELY from interest expense on the lease liability (effective interest method) — total expense is FRONT-LOADED (higher combined interest + amortisation early, declining over time), just like an old ASC 840 capital lease
Operating leaseA SINGLE LEASE COST is recognised on a STRAIGHT-LINE basis over the lease term (combining interest accretion and ROU amortisation into one even, level total expense) — a genuinely NEW mechanic, distinct from both the old ASC 840 operating lease AND the finance lease pattern
This dual-pattern outcome is exactly what IFRS 16 chose to ELIMINATE (Section 7) — under IFRS 16, EVERY lessee lease produces the FRONT-LOADED finance-lease-style pattern, with no operating lease "single lease cost" option at all.
7

THE KEY DIVERGENCE — IFRS 16's Single Lessee Model

No lessee classification distinction under IFRS 16 — everything is treated like a finance lease
IFRS 16 took a genuinely BOLDER structural step than ASC 842: it ELIMINATED lessee lease classification ENTIRELY. Under IFRS 16, EVERY lessee lease (beyond the short-term/low-value exceptions) is accounted for using a SINGLE model — ROU asset amortisation SEPARATE from interest expense on the lease liability, PRODUCING THE SAME FRONT-LOADED expense pattern for ALL leases, with NO "operating lease" straight-line single-cost alternative. ASC 842, by contrast, RETAINED the dual finance/operating classification for lessees — a genuine STRUCTURAL divergence: identical leases can produce a level, straight-line TOTAL expense under US GAAP (if classified operating) but ALWAYS a front-loaded expense pattern under IFRS, REGARDLESS of how that same lease would have been classified under the old dual-model thinking. IFRS 16 also includes a LOW-VALUE ASSET exception (for leases of assets below a low absolute value threshold) that ASC 842 does NOT have as a parallel elective scope exception.
8

Lessor Accounting

Sales-type, direct financing, and operating — largely carried forward from ASC 840
LESSOR accounting under ASC 842 largely RETAINS the three-category framework from ASC 840 — SALES-TYPE leases (dealer profit recognised at commencement), DIRECT FINANCING leases (pure financing, no dealer profit, recognised over the lease term), and OPERATING leases (lessor retains the asset on its own books, recognising rental income over time) — with updated classification criteria mirroring the lessee's five criteria.
9

Sale-Leaseback Transactions Under ASC 842

A genuine "sale" test, now aligned with ASC 606's control-transfer principle
Under ASC 842, whether a sale-leaseback transaction qualifies as a genuine SALE is assessed using ASC 606's CONTROL transfer criteria — if control genuinely transfers, the sale is recognised (gain/loss immediately, subject to adjustment for off-market terms), and the leaseback is accounted for under the normal lessee model; if control does NOT transfer, the transaction is accounted for as a FINANCING arrangement instead. IFRS 16 applies a broadly similar control-transfer test drawing on IFRS 15, producing a largely CONVERGED sale-leaseback assessment mechanism.
10

Practical Expedients & Transition

Package of practical expedients available at adoption
Entities adopting ASC 842 could elect a PACKAGE of practical expedients (applied consistently) allowing them to NOT reassess whether expired/existing contracts contain leases, NOT reassess lease classification for existing leases, and NOT reassess initial direct costs for existing leases — substantially easing the transition burden from ASC 840.

Worked Example — Initial ROU Asset & Lease Liability Measurement

Commencement recognition
Example: A 5-year office lease
A lessee enters a 5-year office lease with annual payments of $200,000 (paid at year-end), and an incremental borrowing rate of 6%. The lessee pays $15,000 of initial direct costs.
Present value of 5 annual $200,000 payments at 6% = approximately $842,600 (lease liability). ROU asset = $842,600 (lease liability) + $15,000 (initial direct costs) = $857,600. Both the ROU asset and lease liability are recognised on the balance sheet at commencement — regardless of whether this lease is subsequently classified as finance or operating.

Worked Example — Operating Lease Single Lease Cost vs IFRS 16's Front-Loaded Pattern

Same lease, genuinely different expense trajectories
Example: Comparing US GAAP operating lease vs IFRS 16 treatment for an identical lease
Using the same 5-year, $842,600 lease liability from the prior example, classified as an OPERATING lease under US GAAP (meeting none of the five finance lease criteria).
Under ASC 842 (operating lease): total lease cost of $200,000 per year is recognised on a STRAIGHT-LINE basis — a LEVEL $200,000 annual expense every year, for all 5 years.
Under IFRS 16: since NO operating lease alternative exists, the SAME lease produces interest expense (declining each year as the liability amortises, e.g. roughly $50,600 in year 1, declining thereafter) PLUS separate straight-line ROU amortisation of $842,600 ÷ 5 = $168,520 per year — a COMBINED expense that is HIGHEST in year 1 (interest + amortisation ≈ $219,100) and LOWEST in year 5, a genuinely FRONT-LOADED pattern, unlike the level $200,000 US GAAP result for the identical contract.

Quick-reference glossary preview
Right-of-use (ROU) asset
The lessee's asset representing its right to use an underlying asset over the lease term.
Single lease cost
The straight-line total expense recognised for an operating lease under ASC 842, combining interest accretion and ROU amortisation into one level amount — with no IFRS 16 equivalent.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Incremental borrowing rate
The rate a lessee would have to pay to borrow, on a collateralised basis, an amount equal to the lease payments, used when the rate implicit in the lease is not readily determinable.
Short-term lease exception
A scope exception for leases with a term of 12 months or less, allowing continued straight-line expensing without ROU asset/liability recognition.
