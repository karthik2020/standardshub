Objective, Scope & Out-of-Scope Interests (paras. 1–4)

Objective
Requires an entity to disclose information enabling users to evaluate (a) the NATURE OF, AND RISKS associated with, its interests in controlled entities, unconsolidated controlled entities, joint arrangements and associates, and unconsolidated structured entities; and (b) the EFFECTS of those interests on financial position, financial performance, and cash flows.
Applies to any entity with an interest in: controlled entities; joint arrangements; associates; or unconsolidated structured entities
What falls outside scope (para. 4)
Post-employment/long-term employee benefit plans (IPSAS 39) An entity's OWN separate financial statements (IPSAS 34) — with two carve-backs: structured-entity interests still apply the §9 requirements if separate statements are the entity's ONLY statements; investment entities measuring ALL controlled entities at fair value still apply the investment-entity disclosures Non-controlling participation in a joint arrangement — UNLESS it results in significant influence, or is itself a structured-entity interest Interests accounted for under IPSAS 41 — EXCEPT associates/joint ventures fair-valued under IPSAS 36, or unconsolidated structured entity interests
Held-for-sale/discontinued-operation classification (IPSAS 44) does NOT exempt an interest from this Standard's requirements, except in one narrow Application Guidance case (para. 3A).

Definitions — Interest, Structured Entity & Binding Arrangement (para. 7)

"Interest in another entity" — deliberately broad
Interest in another entity
Involvement by binding arrangement or otherwise that EXPOSES the entity to VARIABILITY OF BENEFITS from the other entity's performance — evidenced by equity/debt holdings, or OTHER forms like funding, liquidity support, credit enhancement, and guarantees. Includes whatever gives control, joint control, or significant influence.
ℹ️ A typical FUNDER/RECIPIENT or CUSTOMER/SUPPLIER relationship does NOT, by itself, create an "interest" under this Standard.
Structured entity — a control test that looks BEYOND votes and administration
A STRUCTURED ENTITY is one DESIGNED so that the normal drivers of control DON'T apply: where administrative arrangements/legislation would normally dominate, it's designed so binding arrangements instead direct the relevant activities; where voting rights would normally dominate, it's designed so voting rights are merely administrative and binding arrangements direct the relevant activities instead.
"Revenue from a structured entity" is DEFINED broadly — recurring/non-recurring fees, interest, dividends, remeasurement/derecognition gains or losses, and gains/losses from transferring assets/liabilities to the structured entity.

Significant Judgments, Assumptions & Investment Entity Status (paras. 9–16)

The overarching principle, and the judgment disclosures
If the specific disclosures in this Standard STILL don't meet the objective, additional information is required regardless (para. 10). An entity aggregates/disaggregates disclosures so useful information isn't OBSCURED — by either excess insignificant detail, or over-aggregation of dissimilar items (para. 11).
Methodology for concluding CONTROL exists (IPSAS 35 paras. 18, 20) Methodology for concluding JOINT CONTROL or SIGNIFICANT INFLUENCE exists How the TYPE of joint arrangement was determined, where structured through a separate vehicle
The six counter-intuitive examples (para. 14) & investment entity status
Controls an entity where the interest is NOT evidenced by equity/debt holdings Does NOT control an entity despite holding MORE than half its voting rights DOES control an entity despite holding LESS than half its voting rights Is an AGENT or a PRINCIPAL Lacks significant influence despite holding ≥20% voting rights — or HAS it despite holding <20%
Investment entities disclose the judgments behind that determination (unless meeting all three IPSAS 35 para. 61 characteristics) — and, on a STATUS CHANGE, the change itself, its reasons, and (for entities BECOMING investment entities) the fair value of newly-deconsolidated entities, any gain/loss, and its SOFPerformance line item (paras. 15–16).

Interests in Controlled Entities — Composition & Non-Controlling Interests (paras. 17–19)

The umbrella requirement (para. 17)
Users must be able to UNDERSTAND the economic entity's composition and non-controlling interests' stake in its activities/cash flows, AND EVALUATE restrictions, structured-entity risk, ownership-change consequences, and loss-of-control consequences. Where a controlled entity's reporting date differs from the group's, disclose that date and the reason (para. 18).
Per controlled entity WITH material non-controlling interests (para. 19)
Name, domicile, legal form, and operating jurisdiction Proportion of ownership interests AND voting rights held by non-controlling interests (if different) Surplus/deficit allocated to non-controlling interests during the period Accumulated non-controlling interests at period-end SUMMARIZED financial information about the controlled entity

Significant Restrictions & Risks From Consolidated Structured Entities (paras. 20–24)

Significant restrictions (para. 20)
Binding-arrangement restrictions on transferring cash/assets WITHIN the economic entity (statutory, contractual, regulatory) Guarantees/requirements restricting DIVIDENDS, distributions, loans, or advances to/from group members The nature/extent to which non-controlling interests' PROTECTIVE RIGHTS restrict asset access or liability settlement (e.g. a controlling entity obliged to settle a controlled entity's liabilities BEFORE its own) The CARRYING AMOUNTS of assets/liabilities subject to these restrictions
Structured entity risk & VOLUNTARY support provided
Terms of any binding arrangement that COULD require financial support to a consolidated structured entity, including loss-exposing events (liquidity arrangements, credit rating triggers) (para. 21) Support provided WITHOUT a binding obligation — type, amount, and REASONS (para. 22) Where such voluntary support RESULTED in the entity gaining control of a previously-unconsolidated structured entity → explain the factors behind that outcome (para. 23) Any CURRENT intentions to provide future support (para. 24)
6

Consequences of Ownership Changes & Loss of Control (paras. 25–26)

Ownership change WITHOUT losing control
Present a SCHEDULE showing the effect on net assets/equity attributable to the controlling entity's owners, for any ownership-interest change that does NOT result in losing control
Directly operationalizes the IPSAS 35 §11 mechanic (adjusting controlling/non-controlling carrying amounts) into a disclosed schedule (para. 25).
Losing control during the period
The gain or loss calculated under IPSAS 35 para. 52 The PORTION of that gain/loss attributable to remeasuring any RETAINED investment at fair value The SOFPerformance line item(s) where the gain/loss is recognized (para. 26)

Interests in Unconsolidated Controlled Entities — Investment Entities (paras. 27–34)

Per unconsolidated controlled entity (paras. 27–29)
DISCLOSE the fact that the IPSAS 35 fair value exception was applied Name, domicile, legal form, operating jurisdiction Proportion of ownership interest AND voting rights (if different)
Where an investment entity CONTROLS another investment entity, it provides the SAME disclosures for entities controlled by ITS OWN controlled investment entity — satisfiable by including that entity's own financial statements (para. 29).
Restrictions, support, and the non-investment-entity parent
Significant restrictions on the unconsolidated controlled entity's ability to transfer funds BACK to the investment entity (para. 30(a)) Current commitments/intentions to provide financial or other support (para. 30(b)) VOLUNTARY support actually provided, with type/amount/reasons (para. 31); structured-entity-specific binding arrangement terms (para. 32); explanation where voluntary support led to gaining control (para. 33)
⚠️ A controlling entity that is NOT ITSELF an investment entity, but controls one, discloses ALL the SAME §7 information in respect of its investment entity subsidiary's own unconsolidated controlled entities (para. 34).

Interests in Joint Arrangements & Associates (paras. 35–39)

Per MATERIAL joint arrangement/associate (para. 36)
Name, nature of relationship (activities, strategic significance), domicile, legal form, jurisdiction Proportion of ownership interest/participating share (and voting rights, if different) For joint ventures/associates specifically: equity method or fair value measurement; SUMMARIZED financial information; and quoted fair value of the investment, if a market price exists
For individually IMMATERIAL joint ventures/associates: AGGREGATE financial information — joint ventures and associates disclosed SEPARATELY from each other (para. 36(c)). Investment entities are exempt from the summarized-financial-information and aggregate disclosures (para. 37).
Restrictions, reporting-date mismatches, and unrecognized losses (para. 38)
Significant restrictions on funds transfer BACK to the entity (borrowing arrangements, regulatory requirements, investor binding arrangements) Reporting-date mismatch: the joint venture/associate's own period-end, and WHY a different date is used CUMULATIVE unrecognized share of losses, where the entity has stopped recognizing further losses under the IPSAS 36 deficit floor
Risk disclosures (para. 39): joint-venture COMMITMENTS shown separately from other commitments; CONTINGENT LIABILITIES relating to joint venture/associate interests (including jointly-incurred shares) shown separately, per IPSAS 19, unless loss is remote.

Interests in Structured Entities That Are Not Consolidated (paras. 40–48)

Nature of interests, and past involvement
Covers even PAST involvement (e.g. sponsorship) where the entity has NO current binding arrangement with the structured entity, if that past involvement still exposes it to risk (para. 41). Investment entities already providing §7 disclosures for a controlled-but-unconsolidated structured entity are exempt here (para. 42).
Qualitative AND quantitative information: nature, purpose, size, activities, and financing of the structured entity (para. 43) For SPONSORED structured entities where no current interest exists: how "sponsored" was determined; revenue earned from them during the period (by type); and the carrying amount of assets transferred to them during the period — in TABULAR format (paras. 44–45)
Nature of risks — a tabular risk summary (para. 46)
Carrying amounts of recognized assets/liabilities relating to the interests, and their SOFP line items MAXIMUM exposure to loss (with methodology) — or, if unquantifiable, that fact and why A COMPARISON of carrying amounts against maximum exposure to loss
Voluntary support provided during the period — WITHOUT a binding obligation — triggers the same type/amount/reasons disclosure as §5's consolidated-entity equivalent (para. 47).

Non-Quantifiable Ownership Interests (paras. 49–50)

A public-sector-specific catch-all — interests too poorly defined to measure
Some public sector ownership interests genuinely CAN'T be reliably measured (recall IPSAS 34/36's "poorly defined equity structure" carve-out from equity accounting) — but that doesn't exempt them from ALL disclosure. This Standard requires enough information for users to understand their NATURE and EXTENT.
Per MATERIAL non-quantifiable ownership interest: the NAME of the entity, and the NATURE of the ownership interest (para. 50)
This closes a gap left open by IPSAS 34/36/37, which apply only to QUANTIFIABLE ownership interests — ensuring genuinely unquantifiable public sector "investments" (like the hospital-funding example in IPSAS 36 §1) still get SOME disclosure, even without recognition or measurement.

Controlling Interests Acquired with the Intention of Disposal (paras. 51–57)

The "government rescue" scenario — a genuinely distinctive public sector disclosure
Applies where, at the point control AROSE, the entity (other than an investment entity) already INTENDED to dispose of the interest — and, at the reporting date, still has an ACTIVE intention to dispose (para. 51).
Failure intervention: a government may intervene to prevent a financial institution (or similar entity) from failing, obtaining control WITHOUT wanting to keep it — intending instead to sell or otherwise dispose, sometimes after a multi-year restructuring, potentially retaining residual assets/liabilities at the end (para. 53) Policy implementation: a government may direct an entity to acquire interests in OTHER entities purely to REDISTRIBUTE them, as a policy tool (para. 54)
⚠️ Even though disposal is intended, the entity is STILL CONSOLIDATED for every period control exists — which can have a MATERIAL impact on the consolidated statements. This disclosure package exists precisely because that consolidation might otherwise look permanent to a reader unaware of the disposal intention.
Required disclosures, at EVERY reporting date until disposal (or change of intention) (para. 55)
Name and a description of the controlled entity's KEY ACTIVITIES The RATIONALE for acquiring the controlling interest, and the factors establishing that control exists The IMPACT of consolidation on the group statements — effect on assets, liabilities, revenue, expenses, net assets/equity Current disposal STATUS — expected method and timing
In the period the entity ACTUALLY disposes (or CEASES to intend disposal), disclose that fact and the effect on the consolidated statements (para. 56). Cross-referencing to other disclosures that already satisfy these requirements is permitted (para. 57).
12

Current Value Measurement Disclosures (paras. 57A–57D)

The familiar fair value hierarchy package — for interests in other entities
Mirrors the fair value disclosure packages seen throughout this library (IPSAS 12, 16, 27, 31, 33) — applied here specifically to interests in other entities measured at fair value in the SOFP after initial recognition, including measurements based on IPSAS 46.
Per CLASS of fair-valued interest: the measurement at period-end (recurring or non-recurring), with reasons for non-recurring measurements The fair value HIERARCHY LEVEL (1, 2, or 3), in its entirety For unobservable-input (Level 3) measurements: the technique(s) and inputs used, with any technique CHANGE and its reason For Level 3 recurring measurements: the EFFECT on surplus/deficit or net assets/equity for the period
Where existing disclosures (this Standard plus others) don't fully meet the para. 57A objectives, additional information is required regardless (para. 57B).

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Comparison with IFRS 12

Comparison — IPSAS 38 vs IFRS 12
IPSAS 38 is drawn primarily from IFRS 12 (issued 2011, amendments to 31 December 2014), Disclosure of Interests in Other Entities.
TopicDifference
Terminology"Net assets/equity," "economic entity," "controlling entity," "controlled entity," "revenue" (IPSAS 38) vs "equity," "group," "parent," "subsidiary," "income" (IFRS 12)
Structured entity definitionIPSAS 38 EXPLICITLY acknowledges the differing ways control arises in the public sector (§2) — IFRS 12 has no equivalent public-sector framing
Non-investment-entity parent of an investment entityIPSAS 38 REQUIRES §7 disclosures; IFRS 12 has NO equivalent, because IFRS 10 simply requires such a parent to CONSOLIDATE the investment entity subsidiary instead
Non-quantifiable ownership interestsIPSAS 38 REQUIRES disclosure (§10); IFRS 12 has no equivalent
Controlling interests acquired with intention of disposalIPSAS 38 requires this DEDICATED disclosure package (§11) — genuinely unique to the public sector's "rescue and dispose" pattern; IFRS 12 has none. (IFRS 5 addresses held-for-sale assets generally, but not this specific scenario.)

Statement / Process Mapping

Disclosure decision map — which package applies to which interest?
1. Disclose the significant JUDGMENTS behind every control/joint-control/significant-influence/investment-entity determination (§3) 2. CONTROLLED entity, consolidated? → composition, non-controlling interests, restrictions, structured-entity risk, ownership-change and loss-of-control consequences (§4–§6) 3. CONTROLLED entity, UNCONSOLIDATED (investment entity exception)? → the §7 package instead 4. JOINT ARRANGEMENT or ASSOCIATE? → the §8 package — nature, extent, financial effects, restrictions, and risk 5. STRUCTURED ENTITY, not consolidated? → the §9 package — nature and risk, including past sponsorship exposure 6. NON-QUANTIFIABLE ownership interest? → the minimal §10 disclosure (name and nature only) 7. Controlling interest acquired WITH DISPOSAL INTENT, still held? → the dedicated §11 package, every period until disposal 8. Any interest measured at FAIR VALUE in the SOFP? → the §12 hierarchy disclosures on top of whichever package above applies

Quick-Reference Glossary

Interest in another entity
Involvement exposing an entity to variability of benefits from another entity's performance.
Structured entity
An entity designed so that voting rights or administrative arrangements are not the dominant factor in deciding control.
Non-quantifiable ownership interest
An ownership interest that cannot be reliably measured, and so falls outside IPSAS 34/36/37's scope.
Revenue from a structured entity
Fees, interest, dividends, remeasurement/derecognition gains or losses, and asset-transfer gains or losses from a structured entity.
