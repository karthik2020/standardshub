Conceptual Framework Linkage

Consolidation-specific mechanics for interim reporting purposes
ASBJ Statement No. 38 addresses SPECIFIC procedural questions that arise when PREPARING CONSOLIDATED interim (semi-annual) financial statements — going BEYOND the GENERAL interim reporting principles set out in ASBJ Statement No. 37 (see the companion poster) to address the ADDITIONAL complexity of combining a GROUP of entities' interim-period data.
2

Scope — Consolidation-Specific Interim Mechanics

Groups preparing consolidated interim financial statements
This Statement applies to PARENT companies preparing CONSOLIDATED interim financial statements, addressing PROCEDURAL questions SPECIFIC to combining subsidiaries' interim-period financial data WITHIN the group's own interim report.
3

Why Consolidated Interim Reporting Needs Its Own Guidance

Consolidation adds genuine procedural complexity beyond standalone interim reporting
PREPARING a SINGLE entity's OWN interim report is RELATIVELY straightforward — condense the statements, apply CONSISTENT policies, assess MATERIALITY for the shorter period. CONSOLIDATING a GROUP's interim figures adds GENUINE additional complexity: SUBSIDIARIES may have their OWN interim reporting cycles, intercompany ELIMINATIONS must still be performed at the INTERIM date, and NON-CONTROLLING INTERESTS must still be correctly ALLOCATED — all WITHIN the CONDENSED, time-pressured context of interim reporting. This Statement provides the SPECIFIC procedural guidance addressing THESE consolidation-specific questions.
4

Subsidiary Interim Data Alignment

Ensuring subsidiary data is genuinely comparable at the interim date
CONSISTENT with the GENERAL fiscal year-end alignment principle established under ASBJ Statement No. 22 (Consolidated Financial Statements — see the companion poster), this Statement addresses HOW subsidiaries' OWN interim-period data should be ALIGNED and INCORPORATED into the GROUP's consolidated interim figures, ensuring genuinely COMPARABLE, CONSISTENT information across the CONSOLIDATED group at the interim reporting date.
5

Coordination With ASBJ Statement No. 37

A companion piece within the broader reform package
This Statement was issued ON THE SAME DATE as ASBJ Statement No. 37 (renaming the general framework from "Semi-annual" to "Interim" Financial Reporting), No. 39 (consolidated cash flow statement amendments), and No. 40 (tax effect accounting amendments) — TOGETHER forming a genuinely COORDINATED reform package addressing DIFFERENT facets of the SAME underlying interim reporting transition.
6

Worked Example — Consolidating Subsidiary Interim Data

Aligning a subsidiary's interim figures with the parent's reporting date
Example: A domestic subsidiary's interim consolidation
A parent company preparing its consolidated interim report needs to incorporate a wholly-owned domestic subsidiary's own interim-period results, with intercompany sales during the period requiring elimination.
The parent INCORPORATES the subsidiary's interim data, ELIMINATING intercompany sales and any UNREALISED profit remaining in inventory at the interim date — CONSISTENT with the same GENERAL consolidation principles applied ANNUALLY (per ASBJ Statement No. 22), but APPLIED within the CONDENSED, interim reporting timeframe this Statement addresses.

Relationship to IAS/IFRS

A procedural refinement, not a substantive divergence point
IAS 34 addresses consolidated interim reporting through the SAME general principles applied to standalone interim reporting, WITHOUT a SEPARATE, dedicated standard for consolidation-specific interim mechanics — J-GAAP's CHOICE to address this through a DEDICATED companion Statement reflects its GENERAL preference for detailed, PRACTICAL procedural guidance, RATHER than a substantive divergence in the UNDERLYING consolidation principles themselves.
