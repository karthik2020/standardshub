Conceptual Framework Linkage

Matching costs to a system's genuine developmental phase
A NEWLY constructed cable television system typically operates BELOW its intended long-term capacity while its subscriber base is still GROWING – during this "PREMATURITY PERIOD," certain OPERATING costs may not yet be matched by proportionate revenue simply because the system hasn't reached its planned scale. ASC 922 addresses how to capitalise SUBSCRIBER installation (hookup) costs and, in defined circumstances, certain costs incurred during this developmental prematurity period.
2

Scope

Cable and satellite television system operators
ASC 922 applies to entities operating CABLE TELEVISION systems (and analogous subscriber-based video distribution systems), addressing costs of initial subscriber hookups and system construction/development.
3

Capitalizable Initial Subscriber Installation (Hookup) Costs

Direct costs of connecting a new subscriber
The DIRECT costs of INITIALLY connecting a subscriber to the cable system – labor and materials for the drop cable, converter box installation, and related direct costs – are CAPITALISED as part of the SUBSCRIBER installation asset, since they give rise to a lasting economic benefit (the ongoing connection enabling future subscription revenue), rather than being treated as period costs of a one-time transaction.
4

Hookup Revenue

A nonrefundable upfront fee, deferred under general ASC 606 principles
Any UPFRONT hookup/installation FEE charged to the new subscriber is evaluated under GENERAL ASC 606 principles – since the installation itself typically does NOT represent a distinct performance obligation providing standalone value apart from the ONGOING subscription service, such fees are generally DEFERRED and recognised as revenue over the expected SUBSCRIBER RELATIONSHIP period, rather than recognised immediately upon connection.
5

The Prematurity Period – Capitalizing Costs During System Development

A defined window for deferring certain operating costs during system buildout
During a system's DEFINED "PREMATURITY PERIOD" – the period during which the system is still being CONSTRUCTED and its subscriber base is still being BUILT toward planned, mature-market scale – certain SYSTEM-level costs (e.g. specific operating and depreciation charges attributable to the developing, not-yet-mature portion of the system) may be CAPITALISED rather than expensed as incurred, PROVIDED specific criteria establishing genuine "prematurity" are satisfied and the deferral period is appropriately DEFINED and LIMITED at the outset. This is a NARROW, specific exception to normal expense recognition, reflecting the unique construction/ramp-up economics of a capital-intensive subscriber network business.
6

Subsequent Operating Costs – Normal Expensing Once Mature

Standard expense recognition applies once the system matures
Once a system has passed its DEFINED prematurity period (or for systems that never qualified for prematurity treatment), ONGOING operating costs – programming costs, maintenance, customer service – are expensed as INCURRED under NORMAL GAAP principles, with NO further special deferral.
7

Worked Example – Initial Hookup Cost Capitalization

Capitalising a direct connection cost
Example: Connecting a new residential subscriber
A cable operator incurs $180 of direct labor and materials cost to install service for a new residential subscriber, and charges the subscriber a nonrefundable $50 installation fee.
The operator CAPITALISES the $180 direct installation cost as a subscriber installation asset, DEPRECIATED over its estimated useful life (i.e. the expected subscriber relationship period). The $50 installation FEE charged to the subscriber is DEFERRED and recognised as revenue over that SAME expected subscriber relationship period, rather than recognised immediately.
8

Worked Example – Prematurity Period Cost Deferral

Deferring costs during system buildout
Example: A newly constructed system ramping toward planned capacity
A newly built cable system is designed for 50,000 mature subscribers but has only 8,000 subscribers connected during its defined 18-month prematurity period, incurring certain system-level operating costs during this ramp-up window that meet the specific qualifying criteria for prematurity treatment.
During the DEFINED 18-month prematurity period, QUALIFYING system-level costs attributable to the not-yet-mature portion of the system may be CAPITALISED rather than expensed – deferred until the system reaches its planned operating scale, at which point normal expensing resumes for all ongoing operating costs.

No Dedicated IFRS Cable Standard – Discussion

General PP&E and revenue principles apply instead
IFRS has NO dedicated cable television industry standard. A cable operator reporting under IFRS would apply GENERAL PP&E capitalisation principles (IAS 16) to subscriber installation costs, and GENERAL IFRS 15 principles to hookup fee revenue deferral – reaching BROADLY similar practical outcomes to ASC 922's capitalisation and deferral treatment, but WITHOUT a codified, industry-specific "prematurity period" concept of the kind ASC 922 provides.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Prematurity period
A defined developmental window during which a newly constructed cable system is still building its subscriber base toward planned scale, permitting narrow cost deferral under specific criteria.
Hookup cost
The direct labor and materials cost of initially connecting a new subscriber to the cable system, capitalised and depreciated over the expected subscriber relationship.
