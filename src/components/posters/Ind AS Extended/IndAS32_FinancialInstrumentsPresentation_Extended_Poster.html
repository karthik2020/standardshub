Conceptual Framework Linkage

Identical substance-over-form philosophy to IAS 32
Ind AS 32 applies the IDENTICAL principle as IAS 32 — the classification of a financial instrument as a LIABILITY or EQUITY is based on its SUBSTANCE, not merely its legal form, turning on whether the issuer has a CONTRACTUAL OBLIGATION to deliver cash/another financial asset.
HOW IND AS 32 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Faithful representation — an instrument obligating the issuer to transfer economic resources is a liability REGARDLESS of its legal label (e.g. "preference share") — identical to IAS 32Widespread Indian application — Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares (CCPS) are the DOMINANT funding instrument in Indian startup/venture capital transactions, and redeemable preference shares are common in family business succession/estate planning structures, making Ind AS 32's classification test GENUINELY significant in Indian practice
Ind AS 32 introduced NO substantive change to the classification model relative to IAS 32 — its Indian relevance is most pronounced for the SCALE and FREQUENCY of CCPS/redeemable preference share structures.
The core classification test — identical to IAS 32
Contractual obligation to deliver cash/another financial asset = LIABILITY (not equity), regardless of legal form

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 32 covers
Ind AS 32 establishes principles for presenting financial instruments as liabilities or equity, and for offsetting financial assets/liabilities — IDENTICAL scope to IAS 32, working alongside Ind AS 109's recognition/measurement rules and Ind AS 107's disclosure requirements.
Key definitions you must know
Financial liability
IDENTICAL definition to IAS 32 — a contractual obligation to deliver cash/another financial asset, or a potentially unfavourable exchange of financial instruments.
CCPS
Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares — a preference share MANDATORILY converting into equity shares, the dominant Indian venture funding instrument.

The Substance-Over-Form Classification Test (with worked example)

Identical to IAS 32
Example 1: Redeemable preference shares — liability, not equity
An Indian family-owned company issues REDEEMABLE preference shares to a family member, with a CONTRACTUAL obligation to redeem (repay) the shares at a fixed date for a fixed cash amount.
Despite being legally labelled "preference SHARES" (typically associated with equity), this instrument is classified as a FINANCIAL LIABILITY, because the issuer has a contractual obligation to deliver CASH — an IDENTICAL substance-over-form analysis to how IAS 32 would classify the same instrument globally, illustrating why LEGAL form (share capital under company law) does NOT determine Ind AS 32 classification.

Compound Financial Instruments

Identical split-accounting approach to IAS 32
Instruments with BOTH a liability and equity component (e.g. convertible bonds) are SPLIT into their component parts at issuance — IDENTICAL to IAS 32, with the liability component measured first and the RESIDUAL allocated to equity.
5

Puttable Instruments Exception

Identical narrow exception to IAS 32
Certain puttable instruments meeting SPECIFIC criteria (e.g. entitling the holder to a pro-rata share of net assets on liquidation) may be classified as EQUITY despite the put feature — IDENTICAL narrow exception to IAS 32, relevant for certain Indian partnership/LLP-adjacent structures.
6

Treasury Shares

Identical to IAS 32
An entity's own equity instruments reacquired (treasury shares) are DEDUCTED from equity, with NO gain/loss recognised in P&L on purchase, sale, issue, or cancellation — IDENTICAL to IAS 32, relevant for Indian companies conducting share buybacks under the Companies Act, 2013.

CCPS & Redeemable Preference Shares — Indian Application Context (with worked example)

A genuinely important, practically prevalent Indian application area
CCPS are the DOMINANT financial instrument in Indian venture capital/private equity funding rounds, typically carrying preferential rights (liquidation preference, anti-dilution) alongside MANDATORY conversion to equity shares (often on a specified date or triggering event, such as an IPO). Because the CONVERSION is mandatory into a FIXED (or determinable) number of equity shares, CCPS are typically classified as EQUITY under Ind AS 32's fixed-for-fixed test — but any ATTACHED features (e.g. a redemption right if conversion conditions are not met, or a variable conversion ratio) require CAREFUL analysis, potentially triggering liability or compound instrument classification.
Example 2: CCPS with a fixed conversion ratio
An Indian startup issues CCPS to a venture capital investor, with MANDATORY conversion into a FIXED number of equity shares upon a qualified IPO or after 20 years, whichever is earlier, with NO redemption option for cash.
Since the CCPS convert into a FIXED number of equity shares with NO cash redemption obligation, they are classified as EQUITY under Ind AS 32's fixed-for-fixed test — an IDENTICAL classification outcome to how IAS 32 would treat a comparable mandatorily convertible instrument globally.

Offsetting Financial Assets and Liabilities

Identical to IAS 32
Offsetting is permitted ONLY when a CURRENTLY ENFORCEABLE legal right to set off exists AND the entity intends to settle net (or simultaneously) — IDENTICAL criteria to IAS 32.
