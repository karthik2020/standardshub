Conceptual Framework Linkage

A multi-payor system with genuinely uncertain realizable prices
Health care providers routinely bill patients and payors (Medicare, Medicaid, private insurers) at STANDARD gross charges that bear little resemblance to what will actually be COLLECTED – each payor negotiates or is statutorily assigned very DIFFERENT reimbursement rates. ASC 954 addresses how to recognise PATIENT SERVICE REVENUE at its genuinely EXPECTED, NET realisable amount, reflecting this multi-payor complexity, rather than at gross billed charges.
2

Scope

Hospitals, clinics, nursing facilities, and continuing care communities
ASC 954 applies to hospitals, physician practices, nursing homes, and continuing care retirement communities (CCRCs), among other health care providers.
3

Patient Service Revenue – Net of Contractual Allowances and Implicit Price Concessions

Recognised at the amount genuinely expected to be collected
Patient service REVENUE is recognised NET of CONTRACTUAL ALLOWANCES (the difference between gross billed charges and the CONTRACTUALLY agreed/statutorily mandated reimbursement rate for a specific payor, e.g. Medicare's fee schedule) and IMPLICIT PRICE CONCESSIONS (amounts the provider does not genuinely EXPECT to collect from certain SELF-PAY or underinsured patients, even absent a formal contractual rate) – consistent with ASC 606's TRANSACTION PRICE principle, which requires revenue to reflect the consideration an entity expects to be ENTITLED to, not gross billed charges.
4

Charity Care – Never Recognised as Revenue

No expectation of payment from the outset
CHARITY CARE – services provided to patients who meet the provider's criteria for financial assistance and for whom there was NEVER an expectation of collecting payment – is NOT recognised as revenue AT ALL, even at a reduced or net amount. This differs conceptually from an implicit price concession (where SOME collection was originally expected but ultimately not realised) – charity care involves NO expectation of payment from the very outset of providing the service.
5

The Shift From "Bad Debt Expense" to "Implicit Price Concessions"

A genuine presentational shift under ASC 606
PRIOR to ASC 606, much of what health care providers historically termed "BAD DEBT EXPENSE" (amounts billed to self-pay patients that ultimately proved uncollectible) was presented as a SEPARATE OPERATING EXPENSE below revenue. Under ASC 606, that SAME economic phenomenon is now typically analysed as an IMPLICIT PRICE CONCESSION – REDUCING patient service REVENUE directly (as part of the transaction price estimate) rather than being recognised as a separate bad debt expense line – a genuine, significant PRESENTATIONAL shift, even where the underlying economic collection experience is unchanged. GENUINE bad debt expense (a downstream failure to collect an amount that WAS genuinely expected to be collected at the time of service, e.g. from an insured patient who unexpectedly fails to pay their portion) can still exist as a separate expense in more limited circumstances, but the SCOPE of what's treated this way narrowed considerably under ASC 606.
6

Continuing Care Retirement Communities (CCRCs)

Advance fees deferred against a future service obligation
CCRCs commonly collect substantial ADVANCE (entrance) FEES from residents in exchange for a LIFETIME right to occupancy and future care services. These advance fees are DEFERRED and recognised as REVENUE over the resident's remaining life expectancy (or contract term), reflecting the FUTURE SERVICE obligation the CCRC has assumed – with a corresponding "OBLIGATION TO PROVIDE FUTURE SERVICES" liability recognised if the PRESENT VALUE of expected future costs of care exceeds the present value of expected future revenues (including remaining deferred advance fees and future recurring fees).

Worked Example – Patient Service Revenue Net of Price Concessions

Recognising the genuinely expected collection amount
Example: A hospital procedure billed across multiple payor types
A hospital bills $40,000 (gross charges) for a procedure. Based on the patient's insurance, the CONTRACTUALLY agreed reimbursement rate is $15,000 (a $25,000 contractual allowance), and based on historical collection patterns for the patient's remaining co-payment responsibility, the hospital estimates an ADDITIONAL $800 implicit price concession (out of a $1,200 patient co-payment portion) is unlikely to be collected.
Patient service REVENUE recognised = $15,000 (contractual rate) + $1,200 (co-payment) − $800 (implicit price concession) = $15,400 – reflecting the hospital's GENUINELY expected collection amount, NOT the $40,000 gross billed charge.
8

Worked Example – the Charity Care Exclusion

No revenue recognized at all
Example: A patient qualifying for the hospital's financial assistance policy
A patient with $22,000 of gross billed charges is determined, PRIOR to or at the time of service, to qualify for the hospital's charity care financial assistance policy, based on income and asset criteria.
Since the hospital NEVER expected to collect payment for this care, NO revenue is recognised for the $22,000 of charity care – this differs from an implicit price concession, where SOME collection was originally anticipated. The $22,000 is typically disclosed separately as the estimated COST (not the gross charge) of charity care provided during the period.

No Dedicated IFRS Health Care Standard – Discussion

General IFRS 15 variable consideration achieves a broadly similar net revenue outcome
IFRS has NO dedicated health care industry standard. A health care provider reporting under IFRS would recognise patient service revenue under GENERAL IFRS 15 principles – the SAME "transaction price" concept requiring revenue to reflect the consideration an entity EXPECTS to be entitled to (via variable consideration estimation) would produce a BROADLY similar NET revenue outcome to ASC 954's contractual allowance/implicit price concession approach, without a dedicated industry standard or the specific "charity care" terminology on the IFRS side.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Contractual allowance
The difference between gross billed charges and the contractually agreed or statutorily mandated reimbursement rate for a specific payor.
Implicit price concession
An amount a provider does not genuinely expect to collect from certain patients, reducing revenue directly rather than being recognised as a separate bad debt expense.
Charity care
Services provided to patients meeting financial assistance criteria, for which there was never an expectation of payment, never recognised as revenue.
