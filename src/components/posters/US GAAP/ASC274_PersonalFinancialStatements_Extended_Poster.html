Conceptual Framework Linkage

A genuinely different reporting objective
Almost every other Topic in the Codification serves the objective of general purpose financial reporting for BUSINESS entities to investors, lenders, and creditors. ASC 274 serves a narrower, different purpose entirely: presenting an INDIVIDUAL's (or married couple's) financial condition — typically for LENDING, ESTATE PLANNING, or similar personal financial purposes — where the most decision-useful measurement basis is CURRENT VALUE rather than historical cost, since users of personal financial statements care about a person's present net worth, not the historical cost of assets they happen to hold.
2

Scope — Individuals, Not Business Entities

A genuinely unusual scope within the Codification
ASC 274 applies when an INDIVIDUAL, or a group of related individuals (such as a married couple), prepares FORMAL personal financial statements — most commonly for a bank loan application, estate or gift tax planning, or a similar personal financial purpose. It is one of the very few Topics in the entire Codification that does NOT govern a business entity's reporting at all.
3

Estimated Current Value — the Core Measurement Departure

A wholesale departure from ordinary GAAP's mixed measurement model
Unlike ordinary business-entity GAAP, which uses a MIXED measurement model (historical cost for many assets, fair value for others, depending on the specific Topic), ASC 274 requires ESSENTIALLY ALL assets and liabilities to be presented at their ESTIMATED CURRENT VALUE as of the statement date — including personal residences, investments, business interests, and personal property — a fundamentally different, single-measurement-basis approach unlike any other Topic in the Codification.
4

Statement of Financial Condition

The primary (and often only required) personal financial statement
The primary statement required is the STATEMENT OF FINANCIAL CONDITION, presenting: assets at estimated current value, liabilities at estimated current amounts (generally the discounted amount of cash to be paid), a provision for ESTIMATED INCOME TAXES on the difference between the estimated current values of assets/liabilities and their tax bases (Section 5), and NET WORTH as the residual — a structure conceptually similar to a business Balance Sheet, but built entirely around current-value measurement.
5

Estimated Income Taxes on Unrealised Appreciation

A genuinely distinctive recognition requirement
Because assets are stated at CURRENT VALUE rather than tax basis, ASC 274 requires recognising a PROVISION for the ESTIMATED INCOME TAXES that would become payable if the assets were sold (and liabilities settled) at their current statement-date amounts — computed using applicable tax rates and laws, factoring in the individual's specific tax attributes. This is presented BETWEEN liabilities and net worth, as a distinct line item — a measurement concept with no direct parallel elsewhere in ordinary business GAAP.
6

Statement of Changes in Net Worth

An optional, but common, companion statement
A STATEMENT OF CHANGES IN NET WORTH — showing the major sources of INCREASE (e.g. income, increases in the estimated current value of assets) and DECREASE (e.g. living expenses, income taxes, decreases in estimated current values) in an individual's net worth during the period — is not strictly REQUIRED but is commonly presented alongside the Statement of Financial Condition to give a fuller picture of how net worth evolved.
7

Worked Example — Building a Statement of Financial Condition

A simplified personal statement
Example: Estimating net worth for a loan application
An individual's assets, at estimated current value: personal residence $1,200,000, investment portfolio $800,000, cash $150,000. Liabilities: mortgage $450,000. The combined tax basis of the residence and investments is $600,000 lower than their current value, and the applicable combined tax rate on that appreciation is estimated at 25%.
Total assets = $1,200,000 + $800,000 + $150,000 = $2,150,000. Estimated income tax provision on unrealised appreciation = $600,000 × 25% = $150,000. Net worth = $2,150,000 − $450,000 (mortgage) − $150,000 (estimated tax provision) = $1,550,000.

ASC 274 vs IFRS — No International Equivalent

A genuinely unique US GAAP concept
There is NO IFRS standard addressing personal (non-business) financial statements in any comparable way — IFRS Standards are, per the Preface to IFRS Standards, designed specifically for the general purpose financial statements of PROFIT-ORIENTED business entities, and the IASB has never developed guidance for an individual's personal net-worth reporting. Where an individual in an IFRS jurisdiction needs a formal statement of personal net worth (e.g. for a loan application), practice typically relies on informal, unstandardised current-value statements, or country-specific banking/lending industry conventions, rather than any codified international accounting standard.

Quick-reference glossary preview
Net worth (personal)
Total assets at estimated current value, less liabilities at estimated current amounts and the provision for estimated income taxes on unrealised appreciation — the personal-statement equivalent of business equity.
Estimated current value
The amount at which an asset or liability could be exchanged between a willing buyer and seller, the core measurement basis throughout ASC 274.
