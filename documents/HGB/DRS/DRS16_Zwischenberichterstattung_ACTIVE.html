1

Scope — Primarily a Capital-Market Concern

Filling a genuine gap HGB's own text leaves largely to market regulation
DRS 16 elaborates interim (quarterly/half-year) financial and management reporting for capital-market-oriented groups — a genuinely different cadence and audience than the annual Konzernabschluss/Konzernlagebericht this whole HGB poster series has documented in depth. Interim reporting obligations for listed companies are primarily anchored in securities law (WpHG) and stock exchange rules rather than HGB itself, with DRS 16 supplying the accounting-specific content recommendations that sit alongside those market-regulation requirements.
2

Core Content — a Condensed, Not Abbreviated, Set of Statements

What an interim report contains
A condensed Konzernbilanz and Konzern-GuV A condensed cash flow statement and equity statement Selected explanatory notes — not the full Konzernanhang, but enough to keep the condensed figures genuinely understandable An interim (Zwischen-)Lagebericht — narrower in scope than the full annual Konzernlagebericht established under DRS 20
"Condensed," not "loosely estimated"
DRS 16 is explicit that condensation means SUMMARISED presentation, not relaxed measurement rigour — the same recognition and measurement principles governing the annual accounts continue to apply; only the level of DETAIL and disclosure is genuinely reduced for the interim period.
3

Worked Example — a Genuine Interim Reporting Cycle

A listed group's half-year report
A capital-market-oriented group, required by securities regulation to publish half-year financial information, prepares its interim report six months into the fiscal year.
Under DRS 16, the group presents a condensed Konzernbilanz, Konzern-GuV, cash flow statement, and equity statement for the six-month period — applying the SAME measurement principles as the annual accounts, with selected notes explaining any significant events or changes since the last annual Konzernabschluss, plus an interim Lagebericht addressing material developments and updated risk factors, without needing to replicate DRS 20's own full annual disclosure depth.
4

Relationship to IFRS (IAS 34)

A close structural parallel, with a genuinely different institutional anchor
DRS 16's condensed-statement approach closely parallels IAS 34's own interim reporting model — both share the "same recognition/measurement, reduced disclosure" philosophy. The key structural difference: IAS 34 is a full IFRS standard with direct legal force wherever IFRS applies, while DRS 16 remains a RECOMMENDATION whose practical bite comes primarily through securities-market regulation requiring its use, rather than HGB itself mandating interim reporting for most companies.
