Conceptual Framework Linkage

"One-line consolidation" – reflecting influence without control
When an investor has SIGNIFICANT INFLUENCE over an investee – meaningful but not controlling power to affect its policies – neither full consolidation (appropriate for control) nor simple cost/fair-value measurement (appropriate for a passive investment) faithfully represents the relationship. ASC 323's equity method sits deliberately BETWEEN these two extremes, often described as "one-line consolidation": the investor's share of the investee's results flows through a SINGLE investment line and a single income statement line, rather than full line-by-line consolidation.
The direct counterpart to IAS 28
ASC 323 addresses SUBSTANTIALLY the same fact pattern IAS 28 governs, using a very similar significant-influence threshold and equity method mechanic – one of the more closely converged areas of investment accounting, with the FAIR VALUE OPTION (Section 6) standing out as the principal remaining structural divergence.
2

Scope – Significant Influence, Not Control

The threshold that triggers the equity method
ASC 323 applies when an investor has the ability to exercise SIGNIFICANT INFLUENCE over the operating and financial policies of an investee, but does NOT control it (control would instead trigger CONSOLIDATION under ASC 810) – including investments in common stock and in JOINT VENTURES where the investor shares control jointly with other parties rather than exercising sole control.

The 20–50% Rebuttable Presumption

A starting point, not an automatic conclusion
An investor holding, DIRECTLY or INDIRECTLY, 20% OR MORE of an investee's voting stock is PRESUMED to have significant influence, ABSENT evidence to the contrary – this presumption is REBUTTABLE in both directions: an investor holding LESS than 20% may still demonstrate significant influence through the qualitative indicators in Section 3, and an investor holding MORE than 20% (even up to just under 50%) may demonstrate it does NOT have significant influence despite the shareholding, if genuine evidence supports that conclusion. This is the identical 20% threshold and rebuttable-both-ways structure used under IAS 28.
5

Equity Method Mechanics

Initial cost, subsequently adjusted
The investment is initially recognised at COST, then SUBSEQUENTLY ADJUSTED each period to reflect the investor's PROPORTIONATE SHARE of the investee's net income or loss (increasing or decreasing the carrying amount, with the investor's share recognised as income/loss in its own income statement), and REDUCED by dividends received from the investee (treated as a partial return of the investment, not income) – mechanically identical to IAS 28's own equity method.
6

The Fair Value Option – a US-Specific Flexibility

An election with no broad IAS 28 equivalent
ASC 825 (Financial Instruments) permits an entity to make an IRREVOCABLE ELECTION to measure an otherwise equity-method-eligible investment at FAIR VALUE instead, with changes recognised through NET INCOME – the FAIR VALUE OPTION. IAS 28 does NOT offer an equivalent GENERAL fair value option for ordinary equity method investments; it permits a narrower scope exemption ONLY for investments held by venture capital organisations, mutual funds, and similar entities, which may elect FVTPL for associates/joint ventures under IFRS 9 – a materially narrower carve-out than ASC 825's broadly available fair value option.
7

Elimination of Unrealised Intercompany Profits

Consistent with IAS 28's own principle
The investor's share of INTERCOMPANY profits or losses on transactions with the equity-method investee is ELIMINATED (to the extent of the investor's ownership interest) until realised through a transaction with an outside party – the same substantive conclusion IAS 28 reaches (and the same principle formerly addressed by the now-withdrawn SIC-3 under IFRS, discussed earlier in this library).
8

Losses in Excess of the Investment

The equity method stops at zero, absent further obligations
If the investor's share of investee losses REDUCES the carrying amount of the investment to ZERO, the investor generally DISCONTINUES applying the equity method and does NOT recognise further losses – UNLESS the investor has GUARANTEED obligations of the investee, or is otherwise COMMITTED to provide further financial support, in which case additional losses continue to be recognised (as a liability, if appropriate) to the extent of that commitment. This is the same substantive conclusion IAS 28 reaches for losses exceeding the carrying amount of an equity-accounted interest.

Worked Example – Basic Equity Method Roll-Forward

A 30% ownership stake
Example: Investment roll-forward for the year
An entity purchases a 30% stake in an investee for $3,000,000 at the start of the year. During the year, the investee reports net income of $1,200,000 and pays dividends of $400,000.
Investor's share of net income = 30% × $1,200,000 = $360,000, recognised as INCOME in the investor's income statement, and ADDED to the investment carrying amount. Investor's share of dividends = 30% × $400,000 = $120,000, RECEIVED as cash and DEDUCTED from the investment carrying amount (not recognised as income). Closing carrying amount = $3,000,000 + $360,000 − $120,000 = $3,240,000.
10

Worked Example – Losses Exceeding Carrying Amount

The equity method suspends at zero
Example: Severe investee losses
An investor's equity-method investment carries a balance of $800,000. The investor's share of the investee's loss for the year is $1,100,000. The investor has no guarantee or further funding commitment to the investee.
The investor recognises its share of losses only up to $800,000, reducing the investment carrying amount to ZERO. The REMAINING $300,000 of the investor's share of losses is NOT recognised (since there is no guarantee or further commitment), and the investor SUSPENDS applying the equity method until the investee reports sufficient future income to offset the unrecognised losses.

ASC 323 vs IAS 28 – the Fair Value Option Divergence

Comparison – ASC 323 vs IAS 28
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 323)IFRS (IAS 28)
Significant influence threshold20–50% rebuttable presumptionSame 20% threshold, rebuttable both ways – fully converged
Core equity method mechanicsCost, adjusted for share of income/loss, less dividends receivedIdentical mechanics
General fair value optionBroadly AVAILABLE via ASC 825, for essentially any qualifying investmentNOT generally available – a much NARROWER carve-out limited to venture capital/mutual-fund-type entities
Losses exceeding carrying amountSuspended at zero, absent guarantee/commitmentSame conclusion – fully converged
The general fair value option's broad availability under US GAAP – but not under IFRS – is the standout structural difference; everywhere else, ASC 323 and IAS 28 are among the most closely aligned standard pairs across the two frameworks.

Quick-reference glossary preview
Significant influence
The power to participate in an investee's financial and operating policy decisions, without controlling those decisions – the threshold triggering equity method accounting.
One-line consolidation
A descriptive term for the equity method, reflecting how the investor's share of the investee's net assets and results appears as a single line on the investor's own financial statements.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Fair value option
An irrevocable election under ASC 825 to measure an otherwise equity-method-eligible investment at fair value through net income instead.
Joint venture
An entity owned and operated by a small group of businesses (venturers) as a separate and specific business project, sharing control jointly – within ASC 323's scope alongside ordinary equity method investees.
