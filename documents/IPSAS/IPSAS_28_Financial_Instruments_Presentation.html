Objective, Scope & Definitions (paras. 1–12)

Objective & scope
Establishes principles for presenting financial instruments as LIABILITIES or NET ASSETS/EQUITY, and for OFFSETTING financial assets and financial liabilities — from the ISSUER's perspective. Complements IPSAS 41 (recognition/measurement) and IPSAS 30 (disclosure).
Interests in controlled entities/associates/joint ventures (IPSAS 34/35/36) — except derivatives linked to them, and cases where those Standards themselves require IPSAS 41 Employers' rights/obligations under employee benefit plans (IPSAS 39) Insurance contract obligations (generally) — though embedded derivatives and financial guarantee contracts CAN fall within scope Share-based payment instruments/contracts — except treasury share provisions (§6)
Key definitions (para. 9)
Financial instrument
Any contract giving rise to BOTH a financial asset of one entity AND a financial liability or equity instrument of another.
Financial asset
Cash; an equity instrument of another entity; a contractual right to receive cash/another financial asset, or to exchange instruments on potentially FAVORABLE terms; or certain contracts settled in the entity's own equity instruments.
Financial liability
A contractual OBLIGATION to deliver cash/another financial asset, or to exchange instruments on potentially UNFAVORABLE terms; or certain contracts settled in the entity's own equity instruments.
Equity instrument
Any contract evidencing a RESIDUAL INTEREST in an entity's assets after deducting all of its liabilities.
Contracts on non-financial items — when they ARE financial instruments (paras. 4–6)
A contract to buy/sell a NON-FINANCIAL item falls WITHIN this Standard's scope if it can be settled NET in cash (or another financial instrument, or by exchanging instruments) — UNLESS entered into and held for the entity's own expected purchase/sale/usage requirements.
Either party's contract terms explicitly permit net settlement No explicit net-settlement term, but the entity has a PRACTICE of net-settling similar contracts The entity has a practice of taking delivery and reselling shortly after, to profit from short-term price fluctuations The non-financial item is READILY CONVERTIBLE to cash

Liabilities vs Net Assets/Equity — the Core Classification Test (paras. 13–14)

Substance over form, and the two-condition equity test
The issuer classifies a financial instrument (or its component parts) on initial recognition according to the SUBSTANCE of the contractual arrangement — not its legal form — applying the financial liability, financial asset, and equity instrument definitions.
An instrument is EQUITY if, and only if, BOTH conditions hold: (a) no contractual obligation to deliver cash/another financial asset or exchange on unfavorable terms; AND (b) if settled in own equity instruments, it's a fixed-for-fixed non-derivative or specific derivative arrangement
⚠️ A contractual obligation that WILL or MAY result in future receipt/delivery of the issuer's own equity instruments, but fails EITHER condition, is NOT an equity instrument — it's a financial liability (para. 14), subject to the puttable-instrument and liquidation-only exceptions in §3–§4.

Puttable Instruments — An Exception to the Liability Definition (paras. 15–16)

A narrow carve-out — ALL FIVE features required (para. 15)
Entitles the holder to a PRO RATA share of net assets on liquidation The instrument is in the class SUBORDINATE to all other classes — no priority on liquidation, and doesn't need conversion to reach that subordinate class ALL instruments in that subordinate class have IDENTICAL features (e.g. all puttable, same redemption pricing formula) Apart from the put obligation itself, NO other contractual obligation to deliver cash/another asset or settle unfavorably, and it's not a contract settled in the entity's own equity instruments Total expected cash flows over the instrument's life are based SUBSTANTIALLY on the entity's surplus/deficit, change in recognized net assets, or change in fair value of (recognized + unrecognized) net assets
Even meeting all five, the issuer must ALSO have NO OTHER instrument/contract with cash flows based substantially on the same measures, and with the effect of restricting or fixing the residual return to puttable holders (para. 16) — otherwise equity classification is barred.
4

Instruments Obligating Delivery of a Pro Rata Share Only on Liquidation, & Reclassification (paras. 17–19)

A second, related exception (paras. 17–18)
Some instruments obligate the issuer to deliver a pro rata net-asset share ONLY ON LIQUIDATION — because liquidation is either CERTAIN (a limited-life entity) or uncertain but at the HOLDER's option. As an exception to the liability definition, classified as equity if it meets essentially the SAME feature set as puttable instruments (§3) — pro rata entitlement, subordination, identical class features, no other unfavorable obligations, and cash flows based substantially on entity performance measures.
Reclassification (para. 19)
An instrument is reclassified FROM the date it starts (or stops) meeting all the relevant conditions — not retrospectively
E.g. if a puttable instrument stops meeting all five §3 conditions on a given date, it's reclassified from a financial liability to equity (or vice versa) from THAT date forward.
5

Compound Financial Instruments (paras. 33–37)

Split accounting for convertible-type instruments
A non-derivative financial instrument may contain BOTH a liability component AND a net assets/equity component — e.g. a bond convertible by the HOLDER into a fixed number of the entity's ordinary shares. Two components: a financial LIABILITY (the contractual obligation to deliver cash/another asset) and an EQUITY instrument (the holder's conversion option).
The entity presents the liability and net assets/equity components SEPARATELY on the statement of financial position from inception Classification is NOT revised for a change in the likelihood the conversion option will be exercised — even if exercise appears economically favorable at some point Transaction costs are ALLOCATED between the liability and equity components, in proportion to the allocation of proceeds
Economically, issuing a convertible bond is substantially the same as issuing a straight debt instrument WITH detachable share-purchase warrants — hence the split presentation (para. 34).
6

Treasury Shares (paras. 38–39)

Deducted from net assets/equity — no gain or loss, ever
Reacquired own equity instruments ("treasury shares") → DEDUCTED from net assets/equity; NO gain/loss recognized in surplus or deficit on purchase, sale, issue, or cancellation
May be acquired/held by the entity OR by other members of the economic entity. Consideration paid or received is recognized DIRECTLY in net assets/equity. Disclosed separately (SOFP or notes) per IPSAS 1; IPSAS 20 related-party disclosure applies if reacquired from related parties (para. 39).

Interest, Dividends or Similar Distributions, Losses, and Gains (paras. 40–46)

Classification follows the underlying component
Instrument componentWhere interest/dividends/gains/losses land
Financial LIABILITYRevenue or EXPENSE in surplus or deficit
EQUITY instrumentDistributions recognized DIRECTLY in net assets/equity
So: dividends on shares wholly classified as liabilities are expensed like bond interest; gains/losses on redeeming/refinancing financial LIABILITIES hit surplus/deficit, while redeeming/refinancing EQUITY instruments is a net assets/equity change. Changes in an equity instrument's FAIR VALUE are never recognized at all (para. 41).
Transaction costs & presentation
Transaction costs of an EQUITY transaction (registration fees, professional fees, printing, stamp duties) → deducted from net assets/equity, to the extent INCREMENTAL and directly attributable Costs of an ABANDONED equity transaction → expensed Compound-instrument transaction costs → allocated between the liability and equity components, proportionate to proceeds allocation (§5) Dividends classified as an expense → presented WITH interest on other liabilities, or as a separate SOFPerformance item
Income tax on distributions to equity holders and on equity-transaction costs follows the relevant income tax accounting standard (para. 40A) — not addressed within IPSAS 28 itself.

Offsetting a Financial Asset and a Financial Liability (paras. 47–55)

The two-part test — BOTH required (para. 47)
Currently has a LEGALLY ENFORCEABLE right to set off the recognized amounts INTENDS either to settle net, OR to realize the asset and settle the liability SIMULTANEOUSLY
A LEGAL right alone is NOT sufficient without the intention to actually exercise it or settle simultaneously — without that intention, future cash flow amount/timing is unaffected, so net presentation wouldn't better reflect the entity's position (para. 51). An intention to net-settle WITHOUT the legal right is equally insufficient (para. 52).
Circumstances where offsetting is usually INAPPROPRIATE (para. 54)
Several instruments used to emulate a single "synthetic instrument" Same primary risk exposure but DIFFERENT counterparties (e.g. a derivatives portfolio) Assets pledged as collateral for non-recourse liabilities Assets set aside in trust without creditor acceptance in settlement (e.g. a sinking fund) Obligations expected to be recovered from a third party via an insurance claim
A MASTER NETTING ARRANGEMENT creates a right of set-off typically enforceable only on a specified DEFAULT event — it does NOT, by itself, satisfy the para. 47 test unless BOTH conditions are independently met (para. 55). Where financial assets/liabilities under a master netting arrangement are NOT offset, IPSAS 30 requires disclosure of the arrangement's credit-risk effect.

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Comparison with IAS 32

Comparison — IPSAS 28 vs IAS 32
IPSAS 28 is drawn primarily from IAS 32 (2003), Financial Instruments: Presentation, including amendments up to 31 December 2008.
TopicDifference
Financial guarantee / insurance electionIAS 32 requires entities to have PREVIOUSLY ASSERTED financial guarantees are insurance contracts to elect that treatment; IPSAS 28 allows the same election WITHOUT requiring a prior explicit assertion
Terminology"Statement of financial performance," "net assets/equity" (IPSAS 28) vs "statement of comprehensive income (or separate income statement)," "equity" (IAS 32)
Revenue vs incomeIPSAS 28 does not distinguish "revenue" from "income"; IAS 32 uses "income" as the broader term
Substance-of-contract guidanceIPSAS 28 adds Application Guidance on identifying arrangements that are, in substance, contractual — no IAS 32 equivalent
Non-exchange revenue guidanceIPSAS 28 adds Application Guidance on when non-exchange-revenue assets/liabilities ARE financial instruments — a public-sector-specific issue
IFRIC 2 principlesIPSAS 28 embeds IFRIC 2 (Members' Shares in Co-operative Entities) principles AUTHORITATIVELY as an Appendix — under IFRS, IFRIC 2 is a separate document
Transitional provisionsIPSAS 28 has FIRST-TIME ADOPTION transitional provisions (for entities adopting accrual accounting or IPSAS 28 for the first time); IAS 32 has none

Statement / Process Mapping

Classification decision map
1. Does the instrument include a contractual obligation to deliver cash/another asset, or exchange on unfavorable terms? → FINANCIAL LIABILITY (unless a puttable/liquidation-only exception applies) (§2) 2. Is it puttable, meeting ALL FIVE §3 features (and no offsetting other-instrument test failure)? → classified as EQUITY, despite the put obligation (§3) 3. Does it obligate delivery of a pro rata net-asset share ONLY on liquidation, meeting the equivalent feature set? → EQUITY (§4) 4. Does the instrument contain BOTH liability and conversion-option components? → SPLIT into a liability component and an equity component (§5) 5. Has the entity reacquired its OWN equity instruments? → deduct from net assets/equity; no gain/loss ever (§6) 6. Do BOTH offsetting conditions hold — legal right AND intention to net-settle/settle simultaneously? → present NET on the SOFP (§8)

Quick-Reference Glossary

Financial instrument
Any contract giving rise to both a financial asset of one entity and a financial liability or equity instrument of another.
Equity instrument
Any contract evidencing a residual interest in an entity's assets after deducting all liabilities.
Puttable financial instrument
One including a contractual obligation for the issuer to repurchase or redeem it on exercise of the put.
Compound financial instrument
A non-derivative instrument containing both a liability component and an equity component.
Treasury shares
An entity's own equity instruments that it has reacquired.
Right of set-off
A debtor's legal right, by contract or otherwise, to settle or eliminate an amount due by applying an amount due from the same creditor.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position NA/E Net assets/equity (statement of changes) Notes Notes to the financial statements
