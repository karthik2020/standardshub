Conceptual Framework Linkage

One of the most significant structural divergences in this entire library
ASPE Section 3400 governs REVENUE recognition – GENUINELY distinctively, RETAINING the OLDER "risks AND rewards OF ownership" framework, RATHER than the NEWER, control-BASED five-STEP model VIRTUALLY every OTHER framework COVERED throughout THIS poster LIBRARY has ADOPTED (IFRS 15, ASC 606, and THE 2017-revised ASBE No. 14 – see the COMPANION posters). THIS stands ALONGSIDE Section 3065 (LEASES) as ONE of the MOST significant STRUCTURAL divergences FROM the GLOBAL convergence TREND this ENTIRE library has DOCUMENTED (Section 3).
2

Scope

Sale of goods, rendering of services, and use of enterprise resources by others
This Section applies to REVENUE arising FROM the SALE of GOODS, the RENDERING of SERVICES, and the USE by OTHERS of ENTERPRISE resources YIELDING interest, ROYALTIES, or DIVIDENDS.
3

THE CENTRAL DIVERGENCE – Risk-and-Rewards, Not the 5-Step Control Model

A fundamentally different conceptual foundation
RATHER than IDENTIFYING distinct PERFORMANCE obligations and RECOGNIZING revenue AS each is SATISFIED through the TRANSFER of CONTROL (the FIVE-step model UNDERPINNING IFRS 15, ASC 606, and THE modern ASBE No. 14), ASPE recognizes REVENUE based ON the TRANSFER of the SIGNIFICANT risks AND rewards of OWNERSHIP – a FRAMEWORK conceptually CLOSER to the PRE-2018 IAS 18 approach THAT IFRS 15 ITSELF superseded internationally. THIS is a GENUINELY fundamental, CONCEPTUALLY different STARTING point, RATHER than a MERELY mechanical VARIATION on the SAME underlying MODEL.

Revenue From the Sale of Goods – Recognition Criteria

Four cumulative conditions

Revenue From Services – Percentage of Completion or Completed Contract

A genuine policy choice between two older methods
FOR revenue FROM services (or LONG-term contracts), an ENTERPRISE elects EITHER the PERCENTAGE-of-completion METHOD (RECOGNIZING revenue PROGRESSIVELY as the SERVICE is PERFORMED, WHERE the OUTCOME can BE reliably ESTIMATED) OR the COMPLETED-contract method (DEFERRING all REVENUE recognition UNTIL the SERVICE is SUBSTANTIALLY complete) – BOTH genuinely OLDER methods, DISTINCT from the SINGLE, unified PERFORMANCE-obligation satisfaction APPROACH IFRS 15 and ASC 606 REQUIRE internationally.

Why ASPE Retained the Older Model

Weighing implementation cost against limited incremental benefit for private-enterprise users
THE global SHIFT to the FIVE-step, control-BASED revenue MODEL was LARGELY driven BY complex, MULTI-element arrangements INCREASINGLY common AMONG public, TECHNOLOGY-oriented, and SERVICE-heavy enterprises – WHERE identifying DISTINCT performance OBLIGATIONS and ALLOCATING a TRANSACTION price MEANINGFULLY improved COMPARABILITY and TRANSPARENCY for CAPITAL markets INVESTORS. FOR MANY private enterprises, WHOSE revenue ARRANGEMENTS are OFTEN comparatively SIMPLER, and WHOSE primary USERS have DIFFERENT informational NEEDS, ASPE'S standard-SETTERS concluded the SUBSTANTIAL implementation COST of adopting the NEWER model WOULD likely EXCEED its INCREMENTAL benefit – CONSISTENT with the SAME cost-BENEFIT reasoning UNDERLYING Section 3065'S retained LEASE model (see the COMPANION poster).

Worked Example – Recognizing Revenue on the Sale of Goods

Revenue recognized upon transfer of risks and rewards
Example: A wholesale product shipment
A distributor ships $85,000 of product to a customer under standard FOB shipping point terms, meaning risk of loss transfers to the buyer upon shipment. The customer has good credit, and collection is reasonably assured.
The COMPANY recognizes the FULL $85,000 of revenue UPON shipment – the SIGNIFICANT risks AND rewards of OWNERSHIP have TRANSFERRED, the COMPANY retains NO continuing MANAGERIAL involvement, revenue CAN be measured RELIABLY, and COLLECTION is REASONABLY assured.
9

Worked Example – Percentage of Completion for a Service Contract

Progressive revenue recognition based on completion progress
Example: A multi-year construction contract
A construction company has a $6,000,000 contract, electing the percentage-of-completion method. By year end, the company has incurred $2,400,000 of the estimated $4,000,000 total contract costs.
PERCENTAGE complete = $2,400,000 ÷ $4,000,000 = 60% – the COMPANY recognizes $6,000,000 × 60% = $3,600,000 of REVENUE for the PERIOD, PROGRESSIVELY reflecting the CONTRACT'S completion STATUS.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Risks and rewards of ownership
The transfer of the significant benefits and burdens of owning an asset, the central criterion for recognizing revenue on goods under ASPE.
Percentage-of-completion method
A method recognizing service or long-term contract revenue progressively based on the contract's measured stage of completion.
