HGB §§336–339 — Ergänzende Vorschriften für eingetragene Genossenschaften
Extended reference poster · Book 3, Section 3 (entire Section — no further Titel split) · A genuinely different legal form, with two distinctive structural features · German commercial law · EUR examples
A NEW LEGAL FORM, WITH TWO GENUINELY DISTINCTIVE FEATURES: cooperatives replace ordinary share capital with member-based "Geschäftsguthaben," and — most strikingly — are NOT audited by a freely-chosen Abschlussprüfer at all. Instead, mandatory membership in a Prüfungsverband (cooperative auditing association) performs the audit function entirely differently from everything the earlier Audit poster documented for corporations.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

The same overall philosophy, applied to a fundamentally member-based enterprise
A Genossenschaft (registered cooperative) is neither a Kapitalgesellschaft nor an ordinary partnership — it is a member-owned enterprise whose capital structure and governance genuinely differ from anything this series has covered so far. §§336–339 adapt the corporate accounting framework this entire poster series has documented to fit that different reality — mostly by CORRESPONDING APPLICATION of the general rules, but with two genuinely distinctive structural adaptations worth real attention.
HOW §§336–339 MAP TO GERMAN ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES
Substance over legal form, once again — a cooperative's genuinely different capital and membership structure demands genuinely different presentation, not a forced fit into the corporate Bilanz format Sector-appropriate audit institutions — the Prüfungsverband model reflects a completely different, historically-rooted approach to independent verification than the freely-contracted Abschlussprüfer model

Scope

Every eingetragene Genossenschaft, scaled by size
§§336–339 apply to every eingetragene Genossenschaft (registered cooperative) — with size-dependent relief scaling in a way that closely parallels, and in the smallest cases directly BORROWS, the §267/§267a size classifications established for corporations much earlier in this series.
3

§336(1) — the Expanded Jahresabschluss, and a Familiar 5-Month Deadline

The same three-component expansion, echoing the Konzernabschluss timeline
§336(1) requires the Vorstand (management board) of a Genossenschaft to EXPAND the Jahresabschluss (§242) with an Anhang forming a single unit with the Bilanz and GuV, and to prepare a Lagebericht — mirroring EXACTLY the same three-component expansion §264(1) required of Kapitalgesellschaften. The Jahresabschluss and Lagebericht must be prepared within the FIRST FIVE MONTHS of the following fiscal year — notably LONGER than the ordinary corporate three-month deadline, but genuinely matching the SAME five-month window §290(1) established for Konzernabschluss preparation many posters ago.

Worked Example — Ordinary vs Capital-Market-Oriented Cooperative Deadlines

The same capital-market-oriented compression seen throughout this series
Example 1: Two cooperatives with different deadlines
An ordinary agricultural cooperative and a capital-market-oriented cooperative (per §264d, and NOT exclusively issuing the debt-only securities §327a covers) both have a 31 December fiscal year-end.
The ordinary cooperative must prepare its Jahresabschluss and Lagebericht within FIVE months — by 31 May. The capital-market-oriented cooperative's deadline SHORTENS to FOUR months — by 30 April — directly paralleling the SAME capital-market-oriented compression pattern this series has now documented repeatedly for corporations (§264(1), §325(4)).
5

§336(2)–(3) — Corresponding Application, and Relief for Kleinstgenossenschaften

Borrowing the entire corporate framework by reference, once again
§336(2) applies the GENERAL corporate provisions this entire poster series has documented CORRESPONDINGLY to the cooperative's own Jahresabschluss and Lagebericht, unless this Section provides otherwise — the SAME "borrowing by reference" structural pattern already seen for §298(1)'s own treatment of group accounts. Cooperatives meeting the MICRO-corporation thresholds (§267a(1)) qualify as "Kleinstgenossenschaften" and may ALSO apply the SAME micro-company relief available to genuine Kleinstkapitalgesellschaften, per §337(4) and §338(4)'s own specific mechanics. §336(3) confirms §330's own regulatory-authorisation power (Formblätter, established in the earlier poster) applies here too.

§337(1) — THE Geschäftsguthaben Replacement of Gezeichnetes Kapital

Member-based capital, replacing the corporate share capital concept entirely
§337(1) provides that, INSTEAD OF gezeichnetes Kapital (subscribed/share capital — the corporate equity line established many posters ago in the Bilanz Titel), the AMOUNT of the members' Geschäftsguthaben (business credit balances) must be shown. This is a genuinely fundamental structural adaptation: a cooperative's equity is not a fixed pool of share capital, but the sum of individual member contribution accounts that themselves fluctuate as members join, leave, or vary their own participation — reflecting the cooperative's OWN distinctive, membership-based capital structure rather than the corporate shareholding model.
7

Worked Example — Presenting Geschäftsguthaben on the Balance Sheet

A genuinely different equity line, in the same structural position
Example 2: A cooperative's equity section
An agricultural cooperative has 240 members, each holding Geschäftsguthaben contributions that, in aggregate, total €1,800,000 at year-end.
Rather than a "Gezeichnetes Kapital" line showing a FIXED share capital figure (as a GmbH or AG would present under §272(1), established earlier in this series), the cooperative's Bilanz shows a "Geschäftsguthaben der Mitglieder" line of €1,800,000 IN THE SAME structural position — reflecting the genuinely different, membership-based nature of this equity component, even though it occupies an analogous role in the overall balance sheet structure.
8

§338(1) — Membership Movement and Haftsummen Disclosures

Genuinely distinctive disclosure categories, unique to the cooperative form
§338(1) requires the Anhang to ALSO disclose: the NUMBER of members who joined or left during the fiscal year, and the number belonging to the cooperative at year-end; the TOTAL amount by which members' Geschäftsguthaben AND their Haftsummen (liability sums — an additional, distinctively cooperative concept representing further liability some members may bear beyond their basic contribution) increased or decreased during the year; and the total Haftsummen amount all members together must answer for at year-end.
9

Worked Example — Disclosing Membership Changes and Haftsummen

Membership fluidity, made transparent
Example 3: A cooperative's Anhang membership disclosure
Over the fiscal year, a cooperative gains 18 new members and loses 12 existing members, ending the year with 240 total members. Aggregate Geschäftsguthaben increased by €140,000 over the year, and the cooperative's articles impose a Haftsumme (additional liability sum) equal to each member's own Geschäftsguthaben, totalling €1,800,000 across all members at year-end.
The Anhang discloses: 18 members joined, 12 left, 240 remain at year-end; Geschäftsguthaben increased by €140,000 during the year; and total Haftsummen of €1,800,000 all members together must answer for — giving readers genuine visibility into BOTH the cooperative's membership fluidity and the extent of additional liability its members collectively bear, neither of which has any direct equivalent in an ordinary corporate Anhang.
10

§338(2) — Prüfungsverband and Board Member Disclosures

Naming the cooperative's own auditing association
§338(2) requires the Anhang to ALSO state the name and address of the COMPETENT Prüfungsverband the cooperative belongs to (the genuinely distinctive audit body covered fully in Section 11), and to list all members of the Vorstand and Aufsichtsrat — including those who left during or after the year — with their family name and further identifying detail, paralleling similar governance-transparency disclosures established elsewhere in this series for corporations.
11

§339(1) — THE Prüfungsverband: a Genuinely Different Audit Model

Mandatory membership-based auditing, not a freely-contracted Abschlussprüfer
§339(1) requires the Vorstand, without undue delay after the Generalversammlung (general assembly — the cooperative's equivalent of a shareholders' meeting) but at the LATEST before the end of the twelfth month following the balance sheet date, to transmit the adopted Jahresabschluss, Lagebericht, the corresponding Bilanzeid-adjacent declarations under §264(2) sentence 3/§289(1) sentence 5 (established in earlier posters), and the Aufsichtsrat's own report, electronically to the Unternehmensregister. Where a Bestätigungsvermerk is REQUIRED under §58(2) of the Genossenschaftsgesetz (GenG — the Cooperatives Act) or Article 10(1) of the EU Audit Regulation, this is submitted alongside — but critically, this confirmation does NOT come from a freely-selected Abschlussprüfer under §§316–324a's own framework. Instead, EVERY registered cooperative must belong to a Prüfungsverband (cooperative auditing association), a MEMBERSHIP-BASED body that performs the mandatory audit function (Verbandsprüfung) as a condition of the cooperative's own continued registered status.
12

Worked Example — Corporate Audit vs Cooperative Verbandsprüfung, Side by Side

Two fundamentally different institutional models for the same underlying assurance objective
Example 4: How a GmbH and a cooperative each secure an audit
A medium-sized GmbH and a comparably-sized agricultural cooperative both require a statutory audit for the current fiscal year.
The GmbH's shareholders FREELY select and APPOINT an Abschlussprüfer under §318 (established in the earlier Audit poster) — any qualified, independent auditor or firm meeting §319's own independence requirements. The cooperative, by contrast, has NO such free choice — it MUST belong to a Prüfungsverband, and that specific association's own examiners perform the Verbandsprüfung as a condition of the cooperative's continued legal existence, REGARDLESS of whether the cooperative would prefer a different auditor — a genuinely different institutional model rooted in the cooperative movement's own historical governance traditions, not merely a variant of the ordinary corporate audit framework.
13

Worked Example — When the Prüfungsverband Refuses Confirmation

A parallel to the ordinary corporate refusal-of-opinion scenario
Example 5: A cooperative whose Verbandsprüfung uncovers serious issues
A cooperative's Prüfungsverband, conducting its mandatory Verbandsprüfung, identifies serious, pervasive irregularities in the Jahresabschluss and REFUSES to confirm it — broadly analogous to a corporate auditor's own "versagter" (adverse) opinion under §322, established earlier in this series.
§339(1) requires this refusal to be handled specifically within the disclosure filing itself — mirroring the SAME transparency principle §322/§328 established for corporate audit opinions, ensuring a cooperative cannot simply omit or obscure a refused confirmation any more than a corporation could quietly bury an adverse Bestätigungsvermerk.
14

The Credit Institution Carve-Out

Cooperative banks follow yet another, separate regime
§339 does NOT apply to credit institutions that are themselves organised as cooperatives (e.g. many German cooperative banks, Volksbanken and Raiffeisenbanken) — these instead fall under Book 3, Section 4's own separate, credit-institution-specific provisions, an area this poster series has flagged from the outset as falling outside its primary scope given its narrow, specialised audience.

Critical Judgments & Practical Difficulties

Where judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Correctly distinguishing Geschäftsguthaben movements attributable to genuine member turnover from those reflecting existing members simply varying their own contribution levels
PRACTICAL DIFFICULTIES
Coordinating the Prüfungsverband's own mandatory Verbandsprüfung timeline with the cooperative's broader disclosure deadline, given the fixed, membership-based nature of the audit relationship
16

Relationship to IFRS

A genuinely distinctive institutional feature with no direct IFRS parallel
IFRS itself has no cooperative-specific standard of this kind — IAS 32's own guidance on member shares in cooperatives addresses the CLASSIFICATION question (equity versus liability) for such instruments, but does not legislate an entire alternative AUDIT institutional model the way §339's Prüfungsverband system does. The mandatory-membership audit association is a genuinely distinctive feature of the German (and broader continental European) cooperative tradition, without a comparable structure anywhere in the IFRS framework.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Eingetragene Genossenschaft (eG)
Registered cooperative — a member-owned legal form distinct from both corporations and ordinary partnerships.
Geschäftsguthaben
Business credit balances — members' individual capital contribution accounts, replacing gezeichnetes Kapital on the balance sheet.
Haftsumme
Liability sum — an additional liability amount some members may bear beyond their basic Geschäftsguthaben contribution.
Generalversammlung
General assembly — the cooperative's equivalent of a corporate shareholders' meeting.
Prüfungsverband
Cooperative auditing association — the mandatory-membership body performing a cooperative's own audit function.
Verbandsprüfung
Association audit — the distinctive audit mechanism a Prüfungsverband performs, replacing the freely-contracted corporate Abschlussprüfer model.
