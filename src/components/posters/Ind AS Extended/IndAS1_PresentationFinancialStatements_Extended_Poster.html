Ind AS 1 – Presentation of Financial Statements
Extended reference poster · Converged with IAS 1 · Schedule III format mandate is the standout Indian divergence · Worked examples
🇮🇳 IND AS – CONVERGED WITH IAS 1, BUT WITH A GENUINE FORMAT-LEVEL DIVERGENCE – Ind AS 1's core presentation principles mirror IAS 1 closely, but Indian companies MUST also comply with Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013, which PRESCRIBES a specific balance sheet/P&L format that IAS 1 does not mandate globally. Ind AS 1 also REQUIRES a single combined statement of profit and loss (P&L + OCI), removing the two-statement option IAS 1 permits. Section 11 covers the full comparison.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Same fair presentation objective as IAS 1, different mandatory layout
Ind AS 1 shares IAS 1's core objective – ensuring financial statements FAIRLY present financial position, performance, and cash flows, and are COMPARABLE both across periods and across entities. Where Ind AS 1 genuinely diverges is that Indian company financial statements must ALSO comply with the PRESCRIBED format of Schedule III to the Companies Act, 2013, which IAS 1 does not impose on IFRS preparers globally.
HOW IND AS 1 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Comparability – Schedule III's prescribed format arguably ENHANCES comparability specifically WITHIN India, even though it narrows the flexibility IAS 1 otherwise allowsFaithful representation – the single combined statement of profit and loss (mandatory under Ind AS 1) is considered to present performance MORE COHESIVELY than a two-statement split
This is one of the FEW Ind AS standards with a genuine, structurally significant format-level divergence from its IFRS counterpart, rather than a purely regulatory-overlay difference.

Structure & Content – Balance Sheet (with worked example)

Schedule III's prescribed vertical format
Example 1: Balance sheet format constraint
An Indian manufacturing company wants to present its balance sheet in a horizontal (T-account) format, similar to a style permitted under IAS 1's flexible approach used by some global peers.
Schedule III MANDATES a VERTICAL format with specific line-item groupings (e.g. "Equity and Liabilities" then "Assets", each split between non-current and current) – the company CANNOT use a horizontal format, illustrating the genuine format constraint Ind AS 1 imposes beyond IAS 1's own more flexible presentation latitude.
5

Structure & Content – Statement of Profit and Loss

Mandatory single-statement approach – a genuine divergence from IAS 1
IAS 1 permits EITHER a single statement of profit or loss and OCI, OR two separate statements (a separate income statement followed by a separate statement of comprehensive income). Ind AS 1 does NOT offer this choice – it MANDATES the single combined Statement of Profit and Loss, incorporating both P&L and OCI sections, aligned with Schedule III's prescribed format.

Schedule III – The Mandatory Indian Format (with worked example)

The single most consequential Indian divergence point
Schedule III prescribes DETAILED line-item groupings, minimum disclosure line items, and rounding-off conventions (e.g. figures rounded to the nearest lakh/crore depending on turnover) for Indian company financial statements – a level of PRESCRIPTIVE detail that IAS 1 deliberately avoids globally, instead relying on principles-based minimum line item requirements.
Example 2: Rounding-off requirements
A large Indian listed company with turnover exceeding ₹100 crore must round off figures in its financial statements to the nearest LAKH, crore, or decimals thereof, per Schedule III's rounding-off table (based on turnover bands).
This SPECIFIC rounding-off mandate has NO direct equivalent in IAS 1, which leaves rounding/presentation currency conventions to entity/jurisdictional discretion – a clear illustration of Schedule III's prescriptive layer beyond Ind AS 1/IAS 1's own principles-based content.
