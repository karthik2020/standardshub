SIC-16 – Share Capital–Reacquired Own Equity Instruments (Treasury Shares)
Historical reference note · Issued 1998 · Withdrawn 2003
⚠ WITHDRAWN – Superseded by the 2003 revision of IAS 32 Financial Instruments: Presentation, effective for periods beginning 1 January 2005
What SIC-16 addressed
SIC-16 addressed accounting for an entity's OWN equity instruments that it REACQUIRES and holds – commonly known as "treasury shares" – confirming how such reacquired instruments should be presented and whether any gain or loss should be recognised on their purchase, sale, issue, or cancellation.
The conclusion it reached
Reacquired own equity instruments should be DEDUCTED FROM EQUITY, and NO GAIN OR LOSS should be recognised in profit or loss on the purchase, sale, issue, or cancellation of an entity's own equity instruments – consistent with the principle that an entity cannot recognise a profit or loss from transacting in its own equity, since equity instruments represent a residual interest, not an asset or liability of the entity itself.
Where this guidance lives today
Superseded byIAS 32 Financial Instruments: Presentation (revised 2003)
Why withdrawnThe treasury share treatment was incorporated directly into the main standard's text (IAS 32.33–34), making a standalone interpretation unnecessary
Current statusThe underlying principle remains good law today – it simply now sits in IAS 32 itself rather than in a separate SIC
