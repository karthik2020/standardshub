Objective, Scope & Definitions (paras. 1–7)

Objective & scope
Prescribes criteria for selecting and changing accounting policies, plus the accounting treatment and disclosure of (a) changes in accounting policies, (b) changes in accounting estimates, and (c) corrections of errors — to enhance relevance, faithful representation, and comparability over time and across entities.
Applied in selecting/applying accounting policies, and accounting for policy changes, estimate changes, and prior period error corrections
Tax effects of error corrections and retrospective adjustments are OUT of scope — not relevant for many public sector entities
Disclosure requirements for accounting policies generally (other than CHANGES) sit in IPSAS 1, not here. Paras. 5–6 [Deleted].
Key definitions
Accounting policies
The specific principles, bases, conventions, rules, and practices applied in preparing and presenting financial statements.
Change in accounting estimate
An adjustment to the carrying amount of an asset/liability, or periodic consumption of an asset, from assessing its present status and expected future benefits/obligations. Results from NEW information — not error correction.
Prior period errors
Omissions/misstatements from failing to use, or misusing, faithfully representative information that was available and could reasonably have been obtained when prior statements were authorized for issue.
Retrospective application / restatement
Applying a new policy, or correcting an error, as if it had always been applied / the error had never occurred.

Selection & Application of Accounting Policies (paras. 9–15)

Step 1 — an IPSAS specifically applies
Where an IPSAS specifically applies to a transaction, other event, or condition, the policy applied SHALL be determined by applying that Standard. Immaterial departures are never acceptable, even to achieve a particular presentation.
Guidance stated as an INTEGRAL PART of an IPSAS is mandatory; guidance that is not does not itself impose requirements.
Step 2 — no IPSAS specifically applies: the hierarchy (para. 14–15)
Management uses judgment to develop a policy that is relevant to accountability/decision-making needs, faithfully represents financial position/performance/cash flows, and meets understandability, timeliness, comparability, and verifiability.
SOURCES, IN ORDER (PARA. 14)
(a) Requirements in IPSAS dealing with similar/related issues (b) Definitions, recognition & measurement criteria in the Conceptual Framework
MAY ALSO CONSIDER (PARA. 15) — ONLY IF CONSISTENT WITH THE ABOVE
Most recent pronouncements of other standard-setters (e.g. IASB/IFRS, IFRIC/SIC) Accepted public or private sector practices
3

Consistency & Changes in Accounting Policies (paras. 16–23)

Consistency requirement (para. 16)
Policies are selected and applied CONSISTENTLY for similar transactions, events, and conditions — unless an IPSAS specifically requires/permits categorization for which different policies may apply, in which case a consistent policy per category is used.
When a policy change is permitted (para. 17)
Required by an IPSAS; OR Results in faithfully representative and MORE RELEVANT information about the entity's position, performance, or cash flows
NOT POLICY CHANGES (PARA. 21)
Applying a policy to transactions that differ in substance from previous ones Applying a NEW policy to transactions that didn't previously occur or were immaterial
⚠️ Initial application of a policy to revalue assets under IPSAS 31/45 is a policy change, but is dealt with as a REVALUATION under those Standards, not under IPSAS 3 (para. 22–23).
4

Applying Changes in Accounting Policy — Retrospective Application (paras. 24–32)

Which approach applies
Change from INITIAL APPLICATION of an IPSAS with transitional provisions → follow those specific provisions Change from initial application WITHOUT transitional provisions, or a VOLUNTARY change → apply RETROSPECTIVELY
Early application of a new Standard is NOT a voluntary policy change (para. 25).
Retrospective application mechanics (para. 27)
Adjust the opening balance of EACH affected net assets/equity component for the earliest period presented, and other comparative amounts, as if the new policy had always applied.
LIMITATIONS — WHEN IMPRACTICABLE (PARAS. 28–30)
Can't determine period-specific effects → apply prospectively from the earliest period practicable, adjusting that period's opening net assets/equity balance Can't determine the CUMULATIVE effect at all → apply the new policy PROSPECTIVELY from the earliest date practicable
5

Disclosure of Changes in Accounting Policy (paras. 33–36)

Initial application of an IPSAS (para. 33)
Title of the Standard Whether the change follows transitional provisions, and a description of them Nature of the change; transitional provisions that might affect future periods Amount of adjustment per line item, current period & each prior period presented (to the extent practicable) Amount relating to periods before those presented If impracticable for a particular period, the circumstances and how/from when the change was applied
Voluntary changes (para. 34) & impending standards (para. 35–36)
Same disclosure set as above, PLUS the reasons why the new policy provides faithfully representative and more relevant information.
NOT YET APPLIED A NEW IPSAS (ISSUED BUT NOT EFFECTIVE)
Disclose that fact, plus known/reasonably estimable information on the expected impact Consider disclosing: title, nature of the change, required application date, planned initial-application date, and expected impact (or a statement that it isn't known/estimable)
Financial statements of SUBSEQUENT periods need not repeat these disclosures.
6

Changes in Accounting Estimates (paras. 37–45)

Why estimates are needed & the policy/estimate boundary
TYPICAL ESTIMATES (PARA. 37)
Tax revenue due to government; bad debts on uncollected taxes Inventory obsolescence; fair value of financial assets/liabilities Useful lives / consumption pattern of depreciable assets; percentage completion; warranty obligations
⚠️ A change in MEASUREMENT BASIS is a change in accounting POLICY, not an estimate. Where the two are hard to distinguish, treat it as a change in ESTIMATE (para. 40).
Recognition — prospective only
Effect of an estimate change → recognized in surplus/deficit PROSPECTIVELY
Period of the change, if it affects that period only (e.g. a bad-debt estimate revision) The period of change AND future periods, if both are affected (e.g. a revised useful life affecting future depreciation) If it changes assets/liabilities or a net assets/equity item directly, adjust the CARRYING AMOUNT in the period of change (para. 42)
Disclosure: nature and amount of the change, current-period effect plus expected future-period effect (unless impracticable to estimate — then disclose that fact) (paras. 44–45).

Errors — Definition, Correction & Disclosure (paras. 46–54)

What counts as an error
Mathematical mistakes Mistakes in applying accounting policies Oversights or misinterpretations of facts Fraud
✕ Financial statements do NOT comply with IPSAS if they contain material errors, OR immaterial errors made INTENTIONALLY to achieve a particular presentation.
Correction mechanics (para. 47) & disclosure (para. 54)
Material prior period errors are corrected RETROSPECTIVELY in the first financial statements authorized for issue after discovery — restating prior-period comparatives, or (if the error predates the earliest period presented) restating opening balances of assets, liabilities, and net assets/equity.
DISCLOSE (PARA. 54)
Nature of the prior period error Amount of the correction per line item, for each prior period presented (to the extent practicable) Amount of the correction at the beginning of the earliest period presented If retrospective restatement is impracticable for a period, the circumstances and how/from when the error was corrected
Corrections are EXCLUDED from surplus/deficit for the period the error is discovered — they hit the comparatives instead. Distinguished from a change in estimate: e.g. resolving a contingency's actual outcome is not an error correction (para. 53).

Impracticability in Retrospective Application & Restatement (paras. 55–58)

The impracticability test — same standard applies to policy changes AND error corrections
A requirement is IMPRACTICABLE when the entity cannot apply it after making every reasonable effort. For retrospective application/restatement specifically, this arises when:
The effects are not determinable It requires assumptions about what management's INTENT would have been in that period It requires significant estimates, and it's impossible to objectively distinguish information that (i) evidences circumstances existing at the relevant date(s), from (ii) information that would have been available when those prior statements were authorized for issue
✕ No hindsight allowed: when applying a new policy or correcting an error for a prior period, an entity does NOT use hindsight — neither in assuming what management's intentions would have been, nor in estimating amounts. Example: correcting a prior-period misclassification of a government building does not get revised for a LATER management decision to use it differently; correcting a pollution-cleanup liability estimate disregards a large spill discovered in a LATER period.

Worked Examples (Implementation Guidance IG1–IG17)

Example 1 — retrospective restatement of a prior period error
CU6,500 of 20X1 tax revenue was omitted and wrongly recognized in 20X2
20X1 reported revenue CU67,000, surplus CU7,000, closing accumulated surplus CU27,000. 20X2 accounting records show revenue of CU60,000 (including the misplaced CU6,500) and expenses of CU86,500.
20X1 is RESTATED: revenue from taxation rises to CU40,500 (from CU34,000), restated surplus CU13,500. 20X2 revenue is corrected down to exclude the CU6,500 already recognized in 20X1, giving 20X2 surplus of CU11,000. Note discloses: increase in 20X1 revenue/surplus/debtors/net assets-equity of CU6,500 each, with NO effect on 20X2.
This is a correction of an ERROR (para. 47) — restated via comparatives, not a current-period revenue/expense line.
Example 2 — voluntary policy change with retrospective application
Change from capitalizing to expensing borrowing costs on a power station under construction
Previously capitalized: CU2,600 in 20X1, CU5,200 in periods before 20X1. Management judges expensing is more transparent and consistent with industry practice.
Opening 20X1 accumulated surplus reduced by CU5,200 (pre-20X1 adjustment). 20X1 restated surplus: CU18,000 − CU2,600 interest = CU15,400. 20X2 surplus (before interest CU30,000, less CU3,000 interest) = CU27,000. Statement of Changes in NA/E shows the CU5,200 policy-change adjustment as a distinct line, separate from the surplus for the year.
This is a VOLUNTARY policy change (para. 17(b)) applied retrospectively (para. 27) — disclosed per para. 34.
Example 3 — prospective application when retrospective application is impracticable
Adopting a fuller components approach and a current value model for PP&E
Existing asset records weren't detailed enough to apply a components approach fully in prior years. An engineering survey at the END of 20X1 provided current operational values, useful lives, and residual values for components going forward — but not enough to reliably reconstruct historical COST data for those components.
Management concludes it's impracticable to apply the change retrospectively, or prospectively from any earlier date. Applied PROSPECTIVELY from the start of 20X2: carrying amount of PP&E increases by CU6,000 at the start of the year, a revaluation reserve of CU6,000 is created, and depreciation expense increases by CU500 for 20X2. No effect on any prior period.

Statement / Process Mapping

Decision tree — is it a policy change, an estimate change, or an error?
1. Did the item arise from NEW information or new developments about the future? → CHANGE IN ESTIMATE (prospective) 2. Did the entity switch measurement basis, recognition, or treatment WITHIN or BETWEEN accounting bases? → CHANGE IN POLICY (retrospective, subject to impracticability) 3. Was information available and obtainable at the time, but omitted or misapplied? → PRIOR PERIOD ERROR (retrospective restatement, subject to impracticability) 4. Hard to tell whether it's a policy change or an estimate change? → treat as a CHANGE IN ESTIMATE (para. 40)
⚠️ All three categories are corrected/applied WITHOUT hindsight — the impracticability test in §8 governs how far back a policy change or error correction can practically reach.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Prospective application
Applying a new policy to transactions after the change date, or recognizing an estimate change in current/future periods.
Materiality
Assessed against a user with reasonable public-sector/accounting knowledge and a willingness to study information with reasonable diligence.
Impracticable
Cannot be applied after making every reasonable effort — includes cases requiring hindsight-free assumptions about management intent.
Retrospective restatement
Correcting recognition, measurement, and disclosure as if a prior period error had never occurred.
Change in accounting estimate
An adjustment from reassessing the present status and future benefits/obligations of assets/liabilities — arises from new information, not error.
Prior period errors
Omissions/misstatements from failing to use, or misusing, reliable information available at the time.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOFPerf Statement of financial performance NA/E Net assets/equity (statement of changes)
