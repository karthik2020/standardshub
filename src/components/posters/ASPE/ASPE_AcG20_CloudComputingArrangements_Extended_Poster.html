ASPE Accounting Guideline AcG-20 — Customer's Accounting for Cloud Computing Arrangements
Extended reference poster · Canadian GAAP (ASPE — Part II of the CPA Canada Handbook) · A genuinely recent guideline addressing the ubiquity of modern SaaS arrangements · CAD examples
A GENUINELY RECENT, TOPICAL GUIDELINE: AcG-20 addresses a modern accounting question that simply did not exist when most of ASPE's other Sections were first written — how a customer should account for fees paid under Software-as-a-Service and similar cloud computing arrangements. The core distinction closely tracks a similar issue US GAAP addressed through ASU 2018-15.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Distinguishing a purchased asset from a purchased service
AcG-20 addresses HOW a CUSTOMER (RATHER than the CLOUD service PROVIDER) accounts FOR fees PAID under a CLOUD computing ARRANGEMENT — MOST commonly, a SOFTWARE-as-a-Service (SaaS) ARRANGEMENT. THE CENTRAL question IS whether the ARRANGEMENT includes a SOFTWARE license THE customer itself CONTROLS (Section 3).
2

Scope — the Customer's Perspective

Fees paid by a business customer for cloud-based software access
This Guideline applies TO fees PAID by a CUSTOMER enterprise FOR access TO cloud-BASED software APPLICATIONS — ADDRESSING the CUSTOMER'S own ACCOUNTING, NOT the CLOUD provider'S revenue RECOGNITION (which WOULD instead be GOVERNED by Section 3400).
3

THE CENTRAL QUESTION — Does the Arrangement Include a Software License?

Can the customer take possession of the software?
A SOFTWARE license EXISTS where the CUSTOMER has the CONTRACTUAL right TO take POSSESSION of the SOFTWARE, WITHOUT significant PENALTY, and it IS feasible FOR the customer EITHER to RUN the software ON its OWN hardware OR to CONTRACT with ANOTHER party TO host it — WHERE these CONDITIONS are NOT met (the TYPICAL fact PATTERN for MOST modern SaaS ARRANGEMENTS, where the CUSTOMER only ACCESSES the vendor'S own hosted SOFTWARE over the CONTRACT term), NO software LICENSE exists.
4

Where a Software License Exists — Intangible Asset Accounting

Accounted for under Section 3064
WHERE a SOFTWARE license GENUINELY exists, the ARRANGEMENT is ACCOUNTED for AS the ACQUISITION of an INTANGIBLE asset UNDER Section 3064 (see the COMPANION poster) — WITH the LICENSE cost CAPITALIZED, and CERTAIN qualifying IMPLEMENTATION costs ALSO capitalized SIMILARLY to internally DEVELOPED software.
5

Where No Software License Exists — a Service Contract

Fees expensed as the service is received
WHERE the ARRANGEMENT does NOT include a SOFTWARE license (the TYPICAL, pure hosting-STYLE SaaS ARRANGEMENT), it is ACCOUNTED for AS a SERVICE contract — SUBSCRIPTION fees are EXPENSED as INCURRED (OR over the SERVICE period), RATHER than CAPITALIZED as an INTANGIBLE asset OR property, PLANT, and EQUIPMENT.
6

Implementation Costs

Certain costs may still qualify for separate capitalization
EVEN within a PURE service-CONTRACT-style arrangement, CERTAIN implementation ACTIVITIES (e.g. DATA conversion, CONFIGURATION, or CUSTOMIZATION work MEETING specific RECOGNITION criteria) MAY still QUALIFY for CAPITALIZATION as a SEPARATE asset, AMORTIZED over the TERM of the HOSTING arrangement — CONCEPTUALLY tracking a SIMILAR issue US GAAP ADDRESSED through ASU 2018-15. GIVEN the RELATIVELY technical, FACT-specific nature OF this DISTINCTION, it WARRANTS careful REVIEW against the CURRENT CPA Canada HANDBOOK text FOR any SPECIFIC arrangement.
7

Worked Example — a Licensed, On-Premise-Capable Arrangement

Capitalized as an intangible asset
Example: A perpetual license with self-hosting rights
A company pays $300,000 for a perpetual software license, with the contractual right to take possession of the software and run it on its own servers at any time, without penalty.
GIVEN the CUSTOMER genuinely CONTROLS a SOFTWARE license, the COMPANY capitalizes the $300,000 as an INTANGIBLE asset under Section 3064, AMORTIZED over ITS estimated USEFUL life.
8

Worked Example — a Pure Hosting/SaaS Arrangement

Expensed as a service, not capitalized
Example: A typical monthly SaaS subscription
The same company also pays $8,000 per month for a customer relationship management (CRM) SaaS subscription, accessed only through the vendor's own hosted servers, with no right to take possession of the underlying software.
GIVEN NO software LICENSE exists, the COMPANY expenses the $8,000 per month as a SERVICE cost, AS incurred — WITH NO intangible ASSET or PP&E RECOGNIZED for the SUBSCRIPTION fee ITSELF.

A Note on International Comparability

A genuinely topical issue addressed similarly across frameworks
THE core LICENSE-versus-SERVICE distinction UNDERLYING AcG-20 CLOSELY tracks a SIMILAR question ADDRESSED under US GAAP THROUGH ASU 2018-15, and IS conceptually CONSISTENT with how IFRS PREPARERS analyze SIMILAR arrangements UNDER IAS 38 (INTANGIBLE assets) and IFRS 15/IAS 37. GIVEN the GENUINELY recent, TOPICAL nature OF this GUIDANCE across MULTIPLE frameworks SIMULTANEOUSLY, it REFLECTS a SHARED, contemporary RESPONSE to the WIDESPREAD shift TOWARD cloud-based SOFTWARE delivery, RATHER than a LONGSTANDING point OF divergence.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Software license (in a cloud arrangement)
A contractual right for the customer to take possession of software without significant penalty, and to run it independently of the vendor's hosting.
Hosting arrangement / SaaS
A cloud computing arrangement providing access to vendor-hosted software without a distinct license, accounted for as a service contract.
