Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why a "first year" mechanical question needs an interpretation
IAS 29 requires financial statements of an entity whose functional currency is hyperinflationary to be stated in terms of the measuring unit current at the end of the reporting period. What IAS 29 does not spell out in detail is exactly HOW to apply that requirement in the specific period an economy is FIRST identified as hyperinflationary – including whether prior-period comparatives, already reported in nominal terms, must also be restated. IFRIC 7 answers that mechanical question.
Comparability under extreme measurement-unit change
Presenting a newly-restated current period alongside UN-restated comparatives would combine two genuinely different measuring units in the same set of financial statements, destroying comparability. IFRIC 7's requirement to restate comparatives too ensures the whole set of financial statements is expressed in a single, consistent measuring unit – current at the end of the reporting period.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRIC 7 covers
IFRIC 7 applies to an entity that PREPARES financial statements in the functional currency of a hyperinflationary economy, specifically addressing the reporting period in which the entity IDENTIFIES the existence of hyperinflation in that economy, where the economy was NOT hyperinflationary in the prior period.
TYPICAL FACT PATTERN
An economy's cumulative inflation rate crosses the approximate three-year 100% threshold used as a key indicator under IAS 29The entity applies IAS 29 for the first time in the current reporting periodComparative financial statements from the prior, non-hyperinflationary period were previously reported in nominal terms
Key definitions
Measuring unit current at the end of the reporting period
The general price index level at the reporting date, used as the common denominator to which all amounts – current period and comparatives – are restated.
General price index
A broad-based index of price levels used to restate historical or current cost amounts into the measuring unit current at the reporting date.
3

The First-Year Problem

What makes the first hyperinflationary period different
In every year AFTER the first, restatement is comparatively mechanical – the prior year's already-restated figures are simply rolled forward by the change in the price index during the current year. In the FIRST year hyperinflation is identified, however, the prior year's figures have NEVER been restated at all, since the economy was not hyperinflationary when they were originally reported. IFRIC 7 confirms this first-year restatement must still be carried out in full, for both current and comparative figures.
4

Restating Comparatives, Not Just the Current Period (with worked example)

The core requirement
Corresponding figures for the prior period(s), whether based on a historical cost approach or a current cost approach, are RESTATED by applying a general price index so that the comparative financial statements are presented in terms of the measuring unit current at the END of the reporting period – the SAME measuring unit as the current period figures.
Worked example – restating a comparative revenue figure
Example: First-year restatement of a comparative
An entity identifies hyperinflation for the first time in the current year. Prior-year revenue was reported as $8,000,000 in nominal terms. The general price index has risen from 140 at the end of the prior year to 210 at the end of the current year.
Restated comparative revenue = $8,000,000 × (210 / 140) = $12,000,000, expressed in the measuring unit current at the end of the CURRENT reporting period – not the unit current at the end of the prior period.
5

Deferred Tax on First Application

Restate first, then reassess deferred tax
Deferred tax items in the opening statement of financial position are first RESTATED by applying IAS 29, and deferred tax is then REASSESSED in accordance with IAS 12 after that restatement, by comparing the restated carrying amounts of assets and liabilities with their tax bases. Any resulting adjustment to deferred tax is recognised in profit or loss, unless it relates to items originally recognised outside profit or loss.
⚠️ A common error is reassessing deferred tax BEFORE restating the underlying carrying amounts – IFRIC 7 makes clear the restatement step comes first, since tax bases are typically NOT indexed for inflation and comparing an un-restated carrying amount to a nominal tax base would misstate the resulting temporary difference.
6

Corresponding Figures – Historical vs Current Cost

The restatement requirement applies either way
IAS 29 allows an entity's primary financial statements to be prepared on EITHER a historical cost basis or a current cost basis before restatement. IFRIC 7 confirms the requirement to restate comparatives into the current measuring unit applies regardless of which basis the entity's primary financial statements were originally prepared under – the restatement mechanics differ slightly by basis, but the underlying obligation to express everything in a single, current measuring unit does not.
7

Practical Restatement Mechanics

A structured sequence entities generally follow
StepAction
1Restate the opening statement of financial position (comparative period opening balances) using the general price index change from acquisition/transaction dates to the current reporting date
2Restate the closing comparative statement of financial position and the comparative profit or loss figures into the measuring unit current at the end of the CURRENT reporting period
3Reassess deferred tax on the restated balances per IAS 12
4Restate current period transactions and balances into the same current measuring unit, including recognising the net monetary gain or loss for the period
8

Ceasing to be Hyperinflationary

The mirror-image question, addressed elsewhere
IFRIC 7 addresses the START of hyperinflationary reporting. IAS 29 itself separately addresses the position when an economy CEASES to be hyperinflationary – the amounts expressed in the measuring unit current at the end of the immediately preceding period are treated as the basis for the carrying amounts in subsequent financial statements, without further restatement going forward.

IFRIC 7 vs US GAAP

Comparison – IFRIC 7 / IAS 29 vs US GAAP (ASC 830)
FeatureIFRS (IAS 29 / IFRIC 7)US GAAP (ASC 830)
Fundamental approachRESTATEMENT approach – all amounts restated into a current, inflation-adjusted measuring unit, functional currency unchangedREMEASUREMENT approach – for "highly inflationary" economies, the functional currency is deemed to be the REPORTING currency (e.g. US dollar) instead, and amounts are remeasured as if that were always the functional currency
ComparativesExplicitly restated on first application, per IFRIC 7Not restated in the same sense – remeasurement is applied prospectively from the date the economy becomes highly inflationary
Net monetary gain/loss conceptExplicitly recognised in profit or loss under IAS 29No direct equivalent concept – the remeasurement approach does not isolate a purchasing-power gain/loss in the same way
This is one of the more FUNDAMENTAL methodology differences between the two frameworks – restatement (IFRS) and remeasurement/functional-currency-change (US GAAP) are conceptually distinct responses to the same underlying problem of hyperinflation, not simply different numbers reached via a similar process.

Quick-reference glossary
Monetary items
Cash and items to be received or paid in fixed or determinable amounts of money – NOT restated under IAS 29, since they are already expressed in the current measuring unit at the reporting date.
Net monetary gain or loss
The gain or loss arising from holding a net monetary asset or liability position during a period of inflation, recognised in profit or loss under IAS 29.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Hyperinflationary economy
An economy exhibiting characteristics such as a cumulative inflation rate over three years approaching or exceeding 100%, among other qualitative indicators identified in IAS 29.
Restatement approach
The IAS 29 methodology of re-expressing all amounts in the financial statements in terms of the measuring unit current at the end of the reporting period, using a general price index, without changing the entity's functional currency.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOPL Statement of profit or loss OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
