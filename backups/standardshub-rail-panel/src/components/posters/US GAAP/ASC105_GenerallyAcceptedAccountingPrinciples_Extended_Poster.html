Purpose & Conceptual Role

The single source of truth for US GAAP
ASC 105 establishes the FASB Accounting Standards Codification (the "Codification" or "ASC") as the SINGLE SOURCE of authoritative U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) for nongovernmental entities, in addition to guidance issued by the SEC. Before the Codification, authoritative GAAP was scattered across thousands of separate pronouncements from multiple standard-setters, issued over more than 50 years — ASC 105 is the provision that collapsed all of that into one, consistently structured research system.
Codification did not create new GAAP
Critically, the Codification launch did NOT change GAAP — it reorganised EXISTING authoritative literature into a new structure. Any change in practice upon adoption resulted from the Codification's reorganisation and elimination of redundant/superseded guidance, not from any new technical requirement. This is analogous in spirit to how the IFRS Conceptual Framework and Preface are non-technical, orientation documents — except ASC 105, unlike those, IS itself part of the authoritative literature.
2

Scope — Nongovernmental Entities vs GASB

Who the Codification applies to
The Codification applies to NONGOVERNMENTAL entities — private companies, public companies, and not-for-profit organisations. This includes commercial, industrial, and financial entities in corporate or other organisational forms.
Governmental entities — a separate standard-setter entirely
STATE AND LOCAL GOVERNMENT entities in the US do NOT follow the FASB Codification — they instead follow standards issued by the GOVERNMENTAL ACCOUNTING STANDARDS BOARD (GASB), a separate, sister standard-setting body also operating under the Financial Accounting Foundation (FAF). GASB's own codification is structured and numbered independently, and the two frameworks (FASB GAAP and GASB GAAP) are NOT interchangeable.
3

Before the Codification — the Pre-2009 GAAP Hierarchy

The old four-level hierarchy under SAS 69
Before July 2009, determining "the" authoritative source on a given topic required navigating a FOUR-LEVEL hierarchy of pronouncements (established by AICPA Statement on Auditing Standards No. 69), often issued by different bodies with overlapping or even conflicting guidance.
CategorySources (highest authority first)
Category AFASB Statements of Financial Accounting Standards (SFAS), FASB Interpretations (FIN), FASB Statement 133 Implementation Issues, APB Opinions, AICPA Accounting Research Bulletins (ARB)
Category BFASB Technical Bulletins; AICPA Industry Audit and Accounting Guides and Statements of Position (SOP), if cleared by the FASB
Category CConsensus positions of the FASB Emerging Issues Task Force (EITF); AICPA Practice Bulletins, if cleared by the FASB
Category DAICPA Accounting Interpretations; FASB/AICPA "Q&A" guides; widely recognised, prevalent industry practice
⚠️ This structure made research genuinely difficult — the same topic could be addressed piecemeal across an SFAS, an EITF consensus, AND an AICPA SOP simultaneously, each carrying different authority. ASC 105 exists specifically to eliminate this fragmentation.
4

What the Codification Did

Flattening the hierarchy into a single authoritative level
Effective for financial statements covering interim and annual periods ending AFTER SEPTEMBER 15, 2009, the Codification became the SINGLE, exclusively authoritative source of nongovernmental U.S. GAAP — eliminating the four-category hierarchy in Section 3 entirely. All content within the Codification carries EQUAL authority (subject only to the SEC-content distinction in Section 8); there is no longer a "Category A vs Category D" ranking within GAAP itself. Superseded, duplicative, and internally inconsistent legacy guidance was eliminated in the process.

Codification Structure — Topics, Subtopics, Sections, Paragraphs

A consistent four-level citation format
CITATION FORMAT
ASC XXX-YY-ZZ-PP → Topic (XXX) – Subtopic (YY) – Section (ZZ) – Paragraph (PP)
LevelWhat it represents
TopicA collection of related guidance on a broad subject — e.g. Topic 842, Leases
SubtopicA subset of a Topic, generally distinguished by type or scope — e.g. within Leases, Subtopic 20 (Lessee) vs Subtopic 30 (Lessor)
SectionThe NATURE of the content — Sections are numbered consistently across every Subtopic (e.g. -05 Overview, -15 Scope, -25 Recognition, -30 Initial Measurement, -35 Subsequent Measurement, -45 Presentation, -50 Disclosure)
ParagraphThe individual numbered paragraph within a Section — the most granular citation level
The consistent Section numbering (e.g. -25 is always Recognition, -50 is always Disclosure, no matter which Topic) is one of the Codification's most distinctive design features — a researcher who knows this pattern can navigate ANY Topic predictably.

SEC Content Within the Codification

Included for convenience, not FASB-authoritative
The Codification also includes relevant SECURITIES AND EXCHANGE COMMISSION (SEC) guidance, organised using the SAME topical structure, in separate clearly-labelled sections. This SEC content — Regulation S-X, SEC Staff Accounting Bulletins (SAB), SEC Staff Observer comments, and similar material — is included purely as a CONVENIENCE for registrants and is NOT itself authoritative GAAP issued by the FASB; it is authoritative for SEC REGISTRANTS by virtue of separate SEC rulemaking authority, but does not apply to non-public entities.
9

Non-Authoritative Guidance — When the Codification Is Silent

A structured fallback, not a free-for-all
If the guidance for a transaction or event is NOT specified within a source of authoritative GAAP for that entity, an entity FIRST considers whether the guidance for SIMILAR transactions or events within the Codification can be applied by analogy. If accounting treatment is still not specified, an entity may consider OTHER accounting literature — but only after first considering the FASB CONCEPTS STATEMENTS (Section 10). Other non-authoritative sources an entity may consider include: practices widely recognised as GAAP for governmental entities or other non-GAAP jurisdictions, pronouncements of professional associations/regulatory agencies, AICPA Technical Practice Aids, and accounting textbooks/articles — applied based on the FACTS AND CIRCUMSTANCES, and consistently applied.
10

FASB Concepts Statements vs the Codification

The Concepts Statements are NOT authoritative GAAP
The FASB's Statements of Financial Accounting Concepts (the "CON" series, CON 1 through CON 8) form the FASB's own conceptual framework — parallel in role to the IASB's Conceptual Framework. Like the IASB's framework, the CON series is explicitly EXCLUDED from the Codification and is NOT itself a source of authoritative GAAP. It is consulted for its underlying reasoning and, per Section 9, is the FIRST non-authoritative source considered when the Codification is silent and no analogous guidance exists.
11

Amending the Codification — Accounting Standards Updates

ASUs, not new standalone standards
Since the Codification's launch, the FASB no longer issues new "Statements" or "Interpretations" as separate documents. Instead, all changes to U.S. GAAP are issued as ACCOUNTING STANDARDS UPDATES (ASUs), which amend the Codification text directly (deleting, replacing, or adding paragraphs within the relevant existing Topics/Subtopics) rather than existing as freestanding new pronouncements. An ASU is NOT itself authoritative until its provisions are incorporated into the Codification — the ASU is essentially a transmittal/cover document explaining the amendment and its background.

Worked Example — Researching an Issue in the Codification

Finding the right citation for a lease classification question
Example: A lessee wants to know how to classify a new equipment lease
A preparer needs to determine how to classify (and subsequently measure) a newly signed 5-year equipment lease under current US GAAP.
Research path: the preparer navigates to Topic 842 (Leases) — the CURRENT leases Topic (Topic 840 is the LEGACY, superseded leases Topic, retained in the Codification only for historical/comparative reference where 842 has not yet been adopted). Within Topic 842, Subtopic 20 (Lessee) applies, since the preparer's entity is the lessee. Classification guidance sits in the -25 (Recognition) Section, giving a full citation of roughly ASC 842-20-25 for the classification test itself, with subsequent measurement in the -30/-35 Sections and disclosure in the -50 Section of the same Subtopic — illustrating how the consistent Section-numbering convention (Section 6) lets a researcher predict where within any Topic to look.

Worked Example — Applying the Non-Authoritative Hierarchy

A genuinely novel transaction with no direct Codification guidance
Example: A transaction type not directly addressed anywhere in the Codification
An entity enters into a genuinely novel financing arrangement with characteristics not directly addressed by any existing Codification Topic, and no clearly analogous transaction exists elsewhere in the Codification either.
Per Section 9's hierarchy: the entity first confirms no ANALOGOUS Codification guidance applies. Finding none, it next considers the FASB CONCEPTS STATEMENTS (the CON series) for the underlying definitional and recognition principles relevant to the arrangement (e.g. does it meet the CON 6/CON 8 definition of a liability?). Only after considering the Concepts Statements would the entity look to OTHER non-authoritative sources — such as pronouncements of other standard-setters (potentially even IFRS Standards, considered as non-authoritative literature in this context), AICPA Technical Practice Aids, or accounting textbooks — selecting whichever source is most relevant to the entity's specific facts and circumstances, and applying it consistently.

ASC 105 vs IFRS Preface & Conceptual Framework

Comparison — ASC 105 vs the Preface to IFRS Standards / IASB Conceptual Framework
FeatureASC 105 (US GAAP)Preface / Conceptual Framework (IFRS)
Authoritative statusASC 105 IS itself authoritative GAAP — part of the CodificationNEITHER the Preface nor the Conceptual Framework is a Standard; both are explicitly non-authoritative and yield to any conflicting Standard
Structural philosophyONE integrated Codification; ~90 Topics under one numbering systemMANY separate numbered Standards (IAS/IFRS) plus separate Interpretations (IFRIC/SIC), not unified into one document
Conceptual framework equivalentFASB Concepts Statements (CON 1–8) — explicitly non-authoritative, used only per the Section 9 hierarchyIASB Conceptual Framework — also non-authoritative, but more heavily and directly referenced in day-to-day standard interpretation than the CON series tends to be in US practice
Governing/process document equivalentNo single "Preface" — governance and due process are described in FAF/FASB rules of procedure, separate from the Codification itselfPreface to IFRS Standards, a dedicated document describing IASB structure and due process
Hierarchy for unaddressed issuesExplicit Codification-analogy → Concepts Statements → other non-authoritative literature hierarchy (Section 9)IAS 8 provides an analogous hierarchy: apply requirements of Standards dealing with similar issues, then Conceptual Framework definitions/criteria, then (optionally) other standard-setters' pronouncements not in conflict with IFRS
Both frameworks converge on a broadly similar logical structure for handling gaps (look for analogous specific guidance first, then fall back to conceptual principles, then other literature) — but US GAAP's single-Codification structure makes ASC 105 a much more load-bearing, authoritative document than either of its two IFRS counterparts.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Accounting Standards Update (ASU)
The mechanism by which the FASB amends the Codification today — a transmittal document explaining and incorporating a change, rather than a freestanding new pronouncement.
EITF (Emerging Issues Task Force)
A FASB-affiliated group that historically reached "consensus" positions on emerging accounting issues, incorporated into the Codification as part of its source material.
GASB
Governmental Accounting Standards Board — the separate standard-setter for US state and local government entities, outside ASC 105's scope.
SEC Regulation S-X
The SEC's own rules governing the form and content of financial statements filed by registrants, reproduced for convenience (but not FASB-authored) within the Codification's SEC content.
Financial statement key (US GAAP native terminology)
BS Balance Sheet IS Income Statement OCI Other Comprehensive Income SE Statement of Shareholders' Equity SCF Statement of Cash Flows
ASC 105 is an organisational/foundational topic with no transaction-level recognition or measurement requirements of its own — it therefore has no line-item statement mapping in the way a transactional Topic (e.g. ASC 606, ASC 842) would.
