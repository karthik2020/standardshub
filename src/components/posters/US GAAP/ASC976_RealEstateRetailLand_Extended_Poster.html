Conceptual Framework Linkage

High cancellation risk historically drove specialised, now-superseded rules
RETAIL LAND SALES – selling individual lots (often via INSTALLMENT contracts) to retail/recreational buyers – historically experienced notably HIGH cancellation and default rates, prompting SPECIALISED, industry-specific revenue recognition criteria distinct from ordinary real estate sales. Under CURRENT guidance, this specialised legacy model has been LARGELY SUPERSEDED by ASC 606's general framework, with COLLECTIBILITY risk now addressed through ASC 606's own general contract-existence and variable consideration mechanisms.
2

Scope

Sellers of individual retail land lots
ASC 976 applies to entities selling individual parcels of land to retail buyers, typically for recreational or residential use, often financed through seller-provided installment contracts.
3

Historical Retail Land Sales Method – Largely Superseded

Bright-line down payment and collectibility thresholds, no longer the operative model
PRIOR to ASC 606, retail land sales were governed by a specific recognition method REQUIRING minimum DOWN PAYMENT thresholds (a specific percentage of the sales price), an assessment of the SELLER's remaining performance obligations, and specific COLLECTIBILITY criteria BEFORE full revenue recognition was permitted – reflecting genuine historical concern about high buyer default/cancellation rates in this specific market segment. This SPECIALISED, bright-line method has been LARGELY SUPERSEDED by ASC 606's general model (Section 4).
4

Current Treatment Under ASC 606

Control transfer and general collectibility assessment
Retail land sale revenue is now recognised under ASC 606's GENERAL model – evaluating WHEN control of the lot transfers to the buyer, and assessing COLLECTIBILITY as part of the general CONTRACT EXISTENCE criteria (a contract must be probable of COLLECTION for revenue recognition to begin) – WITHOUT the old bright-line down payment percentage thresholds. Given the historically HIGH default rates in this market, careful COLLECTIBILITY and VARIABLE CONSIDERATION analysis remains especially IMPORTANT in practice, even though the FORMAL bright-line test itself no longer applies.
5

Rescission Periods and Refund Obligations

A refund liability during any statutory or contractual rescission window
Many retail land sales are subject to a statutory or contractual RESCISSION period (a window during which the buyer may CANCEL the purchase and receive a refund) – during this period, the seller generally recognises a REFUND LIABILITY for amounts collected, consistent with ASC 606's general treatment of a right of return/rescission, rather than recognising full revenue immediately.
6

Worked Example – Retail Land Sale Revenue Recognition

Applying the general ASC 606 collectibility assessment
Example: An installment sale of a recreational lot
A developer sells a $60,000 recreational land lot via an installment contract, with a $6,000 down payment and the remainder financed over 10 years. Based on the buyer's credit profile and the developer's historical collection experience for similar buyers, collection of substantially all the consideration is deemed PROBABLE.
Since collection is deemed PROBABLE (satisfying ASC 606's contract existence criteria) and CONTROL of the lot transfers to the buyer at closing, the developer recognises the FULL $60,000 transaction price as revenue at that point (net of any statutory rescission-period refund liability) – WITHOUT needing to satisfy the OLD bright-line minimum down payment percentage test that would have previously applied.
