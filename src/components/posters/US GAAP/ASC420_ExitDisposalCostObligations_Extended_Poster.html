Conceptual Framework Linkage

Restructuring costs still need a genuine present obligation
A restructuring or exit plan, by itself, is simply a management INTENTION until specific triggering events occur that create a genuine present obligation to a third party. ASC 420 takes a firm position on WHEN that obligation genuinely arises – deliberately rejecting the idea that merely committing to a plan is itself enough, in favour of requiring each SPECIFIC cost item to independently meet the liability recognition criteria.
The most heavily tested recognition-timing divergence in this area
ASC 420's recognition philosophy stands in DIRECT, well-known contrast to IAS 37's CONSTRUCTIVE OBLIGATION approach for restructuring provisions – producing a genuinely different, heavily tested TIMING outcome for economically identical restructuring plans (Section 12).
2

Scope & Applicability

Costs associated with exit or disposal activities
ASC 420 applies to costs associated with EXIT or DISPOSAL activities, including ONE-TIME employee termination benefits, CONTRACT termination costs, and OTHER costs to consolidate facilities or relocate employees – covering the typical components of a corporate restructuring plan.
3

Recognition Timing – the Headline Divergence from IAS 37

A liability recognised when INCURRED, not when a plan is approved
A liability for a cost associated with an exit or disposal activity is recognised and measured at FAIR VALUE in the period in which the liability is INCURRED – meaning the SPECIFIC cost item itself meets the definition of a liability (generally, the event OBLIGATING the entity has occurred) – NOT merely when management COMMITS to, or approves, an exit or restructuring plan. Committing to a plan, by itself, does NOT create a liability under ASC 420.
4

Why ASC 420 Rejects the "Commitment to a Plan" Trigger

A deliberate policy choice, contrasted with IAS 37
ASC 420 was issued (as SFAS 146) specifically to REPLACE an earlier, more permissive US standard that had allowed liability recognition upon a mere commitment to an exit plan – the FASB concluded that a commitment ALONE does not create a present obligation to OTHERS, since management can always change its mind before specific binding actions are taken. IAS 37, by contrast, permits (and historically has emphasised) recognising a restructuring PROVISION once management has a sufficiently DETAILED FORMAL PLAN and has raised a VALID EXPECTATION in those affected (typically by announcing or beginning to implement the plan) – a CONSTRUCTIVE obligation arising from the announcement itself, without necessarily any individual cost item independently meeting ASC 420's stricter "incurred" test yet.
5

One-Time Termination Benefits

Timing depends on whether future service is required
If employees are REQUIRED to render service until termination beyond a "MINIMUM RETENTION PERIOD" to receive the one-time termination benefit, the liability is recognised RATABLY over the FUTURE SERVICE PERIOD. If NO significant future service is required, the liability is recognised at the COMMUNICATION DATE – the date employees are informed of the plan's specific terms (which employees are affected, benefit amounts, and timing).
6

Contract Termination Costs

Recognised upon termination or cessation of use
A liability for terminating a CONTRACT (other than a lease, now separately addressed under ASC 842) is recognised when the entity TERMINATES the contract in accordance with its terms. For a contract the entity will CONTINUE to use but that no longer provides economic benefit, the liability is recognised when the entity CEASES using the right conveyed by the contract – both measured at fair value at the relevant trigger date, not upon the broader restructuring plan's approval.
7

Other Associated Costs

Recognised in the period incurred
Other costs associated with an exit or disposal activity – such as costs to CONSOLIDATE or CLOSE facilities, or to RELOCATE employees – are recognised as a liability in the period the costs are INCURRED (not upon commitment to the overall plan), measured at fair value.

Worked Example – Contrasting Recognition Timing (US GAAP vs IFRS)

The same restructuring announcement, two different recognition dates
Example: A plant closure announcement
On 1 November, a company's board APPROVES a detailed plan to close a manufacturing plant, and PUBLICLY ANNOUNCES the closure the same day, including the specific plant, the number of affected employees, and the expected timeline. Actual employee terminations and facility exit costs will not be legally triggered/incurred until several months later, in the following fiscal year.
Under IAS 37: the PUBLIC ANNOUNCEMENT of a sufficiently detailed plan on 1 November creates a CONSTRUCTIVE obligation – a restructuring PROVISION is recognised IMMEDIATELY, in the CURRENT year, for the estimated exit costs.
Under ASC 420: NO liability is recognised on 1 November merely because the board approved and announced the plan – recognition is DEFERRED until each SPECIFIC cost item (e.g. employee termination benefits at the communication date, contract termination costs when contracts are actually terminated) independently meets its own incurred/recognition criteria, likely pushing MOST of the expense recognition into the FOLLOWING fiscal year instead.
9

Worked Example – One-Time Termination Benefit With a Future Service Requirement

Recognising a benefit ratably over a required retention period
Example: A 6-month retention requirement
On 1 March, a company communicates a restructuring plan to 50 affected employees, offering a total of $2,500,000 in termination benefits, CONDITIONAL on each employee continuing to work until their termination date on 1 September (a 6-month future service requirement, exceeding the "minimum retention period" threshold).
Because significant FUTURE SERVICE is required, the $2,500,000 liability is recognised RATABLY over the 6-month service period from 1 March to 1 September – approximately $416,667 per month – rather than the full amount being recognised immediately at the 1 March communication date.

ASC 420 vs IAS 37 – the Constructive Obligation Divergence

Comparison – ASC 420 vs IAS 37's restructuring provisions
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 420)IFRS (IAS 37)
Trigger for recognitionEach SPECIFIC cost item independently meets liability criteria ("incurred")A sufficiently detailed, ANNOUNCED plan creates a CONSTRUCTIVE obligation for the WHOLE restructuring
Board approval/commitment aloneNOT sufficient to recognise a liabilityCan be SUFFICIENT, combined with a public announcement raising valid expectations
Typical overall timing effectRecognition tends to be LATER, spread across multiple trigger eventsRecognition tends to be EARLIER, often as a single upfront provision upon announcement
One-time termination benefits with future serviceRatable recognition over the required service periodIAS 37/IAS 19 principles generally align on spreading benefits requiring future service, though the overall restructuring provision itself may still be recognised earlier
⚠️ This recognition-TIMING divergence is one of the most consequential and frequently tested differences in restructuring accounting – the SAME board-approved, publicly announced restructuring plan can result in a full upfront expense under IFRS in the year of announcement, while US GAAP defers most of that expense to later periods as individual cost items are actually incurred.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Minimum retention period
The threshold period of required future service beyond which one-time termination benefit recognition must be spread ratably rather than recognised at the communication date.
Constructive obligation (IFRS concept)
An obligation arising from an entity's own actions (such as announcing a detailed restructuring plan) that creates a valid expectation in others, sufficient to trigger provision recognition under IAS 37 – a concept with no equivalent recognition trigger under ASC 420.
