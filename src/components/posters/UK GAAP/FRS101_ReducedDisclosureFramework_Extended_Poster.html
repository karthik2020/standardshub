1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Full IFRS measurement, lighter disclosure – a genuine relief mechanism, not a separate GAAP
FRS 101 exists to solve a SPECIFIC, practical problem: within a group that prepares CONSOLIDATED accounts under IFRS, requiring EVERY individual subsidiary and the parent's own INDIVIDUAL accounts to carry the FULL IFRS disclosure burden is often DUPLICATIVE and disproportionate – since the group's own consolidated IFRS accounts already provide the primary, comprehensive disclosure. FRS 101 allows QUALIFYING entities to use IFRS RECOGNITION and MEASUREMENT (so the NUMBERS are identical to what full IFRS would produce) while EXEMPTING them from many IFRS DISCLOSURE requirements.
2

Scope – Qualifying Entities

Members of an IFRS group, preparing their own individual accounts
FRS 101 applies to the INDIVIDUAL (not consolidated) financial statements of a "QUALIFYING ENTITY" – broadly, a member of a GROUP where the ULTIMATE (or an intermediate) parent prepares PUBLICLY AVAILABLE consolidated financial statements which are INTENDED to give a true and fair view, using IFRS as adopted for use in the UK.
3

The Qualifying Conditions

Group membership, plus a restriction on certain financial institutions
To qualify, an entity must be a MEMBER of a group where a parent prepares PUBLICLY AVAILABLE consolidated financial statements under IFRS (as adopted for use in the UK) that give a true and fair view, and the entity itself is INCLUDED in that consolidation. Certain entities are EXCLUDED from qualifying regardless of group membership – notably entities with securities traded on a REGULATED market (which must use full IFRS themselves) and certain FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS required to prepare publicly available financial statements, given the heightened public interest in their FULL disclosure.
4

The Notice and No-Objection Requirement

Shareholders must be informed, and must not object
A qualifying entity must give WRITTEN NOTICE to its SHAREHOLDERS of its intention to apply the FRS 101 reduced disclosure framework, and shareholders holding a SPECIFIED minimum proportion of shares (or voting rights) must NOT have OBJECTED in writing within a set period – a genuine SHAREHOLDER PROTECTION mechanism, ensuring the reduced disclosure relief isn't imposed without at least an opportunity for affected owners to object.
5

6

What Is NOT Reduced – Recognition and Measurement Stay Fully IFRS

The underlying numbers are unaffected
Critically, FRS 101 does NOT change RECOGNITION or MEASUREMENT in ANY way – an entity applying FRS 101 measures its assets, liabilities, income, and expenses EXACTLY as it would under FULL IFRS. This is a genuinely important distinction from FRS 102 (which is based on the DIFFERENT, simplified IFRS for SMEs measurement model) – FRS 101 is purely a DISCLOSURE relief mechanism layered on top of otherwise UNCHANGED IFRS numbers.
7

Statement Presentation Under the Companies Act

IFRS measurement, Companies Act statutory formats
Despite using IFRS recognition and measurement, an entity applying FRS 101 must STILL comply with the UK/Irish Companies Act's STATUTORY FORMAT requirements for its primary statements – typically presented as a BALANCE SHEET and PROFIT AND LOSS ACCOUNT (rather than the IFRS-style "Statement of Financial Position" and "Statement of Comprehensive Income" titles, though either naming convention is generally understood to describe the same statements) – reflecting that FRS 101 operates WITHIN, not outside, UK/Irish company law's own presentational requirements.
8

Worked Example – Applying the Qualifying Conditions

Confirming eligibility before electing FRS 101
Example: A UK trading subsidiary within an IFRS group
A UK manufacturing subsidiary is wholly owned by a German parent listed on a regulated EU market, which prepares publicly available consolidated IFRS accounts including this subsidiary. The subsidiary is not a bank or insurer, and has issued the required shareholder notice with no objection received.
The subsidiary MEETS all FRS 101 qualifying conditions – it may prepare its INDIVIDUAL UK statutory accounts using FRS 101, applying IFRS recognition/measurement (matching the numbers used in the parent's consolidation) while benefiting from REDUCED disclosure requirements in its own standalone accounts.
9

Worked Example – a Disclosure Exemption in Practice

Omitting the cash flow statement
Example: Relying on the cash flow exemption
The same qualifying subsidiary would, under full IFRS (IAS 7), be required to present a statement of cash flows.
Applying FRS 101's cash flow exemption (subject to the specified conditions being met, including that the parent's own PUBLICLY AVAILABLE consolidated financial statements include a consolidated cash flow statement), the subsidiary may OMIT its own individual cash flow statement – while its Balance Sheet and Profit and Loss Account continue to be measured EXACTLY as full IFRS would require.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Qualifying entity
A member of a group whose parent prepares publicly available consolidated IFRS accounts, eligible to apply FRS 101.
Disclosure exemption
A specific relief from an otherwise-mandatory IFRS disclosure requirement, available to qualifying entities under FRS 101, with no effect on recognition or measurement.
