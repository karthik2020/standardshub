Conceptual Framework Linkage

A currency changeover is not itself an accounting event
When a country's currency is replaced by a new common currency at an irrevocably FIXED conversion rate, no genuine economic gain or loss arises purely from that fixing — no value has actually changed hands. SIC-7 exists to confirm that IAS 21's ordinary translation mechanics should simply continue to apply through the changeover, rather than treating the fixing event itself as something requiring special one-off recognition.
Continuity of existing accounting relationships
Contracts, hedge relationships, and translation policies established under the legacy currency remain economically unchanged by a currency redenomination at a fixed rate — SIC-7's insistence on continuing to apply IAS 21 unchanged preserves that continuity and avoids artificially disrupting existing accounting treatments purely because of a currency-naming event.
2

Scope & Applicability

What SIC-7 covers
SIC-7 addresses how IAS 21 should be applied when a country's national currency is replaced by a new common currency at a fixed conversion rate — most notably the euro, adopted by multiple European countries through a defined transition period before physical notes and coins were introduced.
TYPICAL CONTEXT
An entity with foreign operations or transactions denominated in a legacy national currency undergoing a fixed-rate currency changeoverA transition period during which both the legacy currency and the new common currency remain in some form of concurrent use
Key definitions
Transition period
The period between the date a currency's conversion rate to the new common currency is irrevocably fixed and the date the new currency's physical notes and coins actually become legal tender.
Fixed conversion rate
The exchange rate between a legacy national currency and the new common currency, set irrevocably and without further fluctuation from the fixing date onward.
3

The Transition Period Problem

A currency risk that has technically disappeared, but not formally ended
During the transition period, the legacy currency and the new common currency exist in a fixed 1:1 relationship (via the conversion rate) — meaning there is, in economic substance, NO remaining exchange rate risk between them. Yet formally, the legacy currency remains in use for many purposes until the transition period ends. SIC-7 clarifies how to treat translation, hedge accounting, and monetary item measurement during this in-between period.
4

The Core Requirement — Continue Applying IAS 21

No special treatment for the changeover itself
The general principles of measuring foreign currency transactions and translating the financial statements of foreign operations using the CLOSING RATE method continue to be applied during the transition period, exactly as they would in the absence of the currency changeover — using the newly FIXED conversion rate as the relevant exchange rate where the legacy currency is one of the currencies involved.
5

No Gain or Loss on the Changeover

The fixing event itself is a non-event for P&L purposes
Because the conversion rate is fixed and does not fluctuate from the fixing date, translating balances at that fixed rate does NOT, by itself, give rise to any exchange difference or gain/loss purely as a consequence of the fixing — cumulative translation differences that existed BEFORE the fixing date remain in equity (the translation reserve) exactly as they were, and are not artificially crystallised or reset by the currency changeover.
6

Contracts, Hedges, and Fixed Rates

Existing arrangements are not disrupted
SIC-7 confirms that the introduction of a common currency at a fixed rate does not, by itself, require the termination or renegotiation of existing hedge accounting relationships, nor does it constitute a "change in circumstances" requiring re-designation. Terms of existing contracts denominated in the legacy currency remain valid, translated at the fixed conversion rate where relevant for reporting purposes.

Worked Example — Translating a Legacy-Currency Subsidiary

Consolidating a subsidiary during the transition period
Example: Closing rate translation using the fixed conversion rate
A parent group's presentation currency is USD. A foreign subsidiary's functional currency, historically a national legacy currency, has been fixed at a conversion rate of 1.95583 legacy-currency units to 1 unit of a new regional common currency, which in turn trades at a USD spot rate of $1.10 at the reporting date. The subsidiary's net assets total 50,000,000 legacy-currency units.
The subsidiary's net assets are first translated at the FIXED conversion rate to the common currency: 50,000,000 ÷ 1.95583 ≈ 25,565,000 common-currency units, then translated to USD at the period-end spot rate: 25,565,000 × $1.10 ≈ $28,121,500. Ordinary IAS 21 closing-rate consolidation mechanics apply throughout — no special "changeover" adjustment is made.
8

Why This Interpretation Still Matters as a Template

A reusable model for any future fixed-rate currency changeover
Although the specific euro transition period concluded decades ago, SIC-7 remains an ACTIVE, non-withdrawn interpretation, since its reasoning applies equally to ANY future situation where a country's currency is replaced by another at an irrevocably fixed rate (for example, dollarisation or a new monetary union). It functions as a standing template for how IAS 21 should be applied whenever such an event occurs again.

Disclosure Requirements

What SIC-7 requires
SIC-7 does not add a distinct disclosure checklist of its own — the effect is reflected through IAS 21's general foreign currency accounting policy disclosuresNature of any currency changeover affecting the entity's functional or presentation currencies, where material to understanding the financial statements

SIC-7 vs US GAAP

Comparison — SIC-7 vs US GAAP (ASC 830)
FeatureIFRS (SIC-7)US GAAP (ASC 830)
Dedicated interpretation for fixed-rate currency changeoversYes — SIC-7 specifically, and remains standing guidance for any future such eventNo direct codified counterpart; addressed under general ASC 830 translation principles applied to the specific facts
Treatment of the fixing event itselfExplicitly no gain/loss purely from the fixing — general IAS 21 mechanics continue unchangedUS practice reached a broadly similar conclusion for the euro transition, applying ordinary ASC 830 mechanics without a special one-off event
Because the euro transition was such a widely observed, globally coordinated event, US practice and IFRS practice converged on essentially the same substantive outcome — the main difference is that IFRS codified it as a standing interpretation, while US GAAP relied on general principles applied consistently at the time.

Quick-reference glossary
Closing rate method
The IAS 21 translation approach whereby assets and liabilities of a foreign operation are translated at the exchange rate ruling at the reporting date.
Legal tender
Currency that must be accepted for payment of debts by law — physical notes and coins of the new common currency become legal tender only at the END of the transition period.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Currency changeover
The process by which a country's national currency is replaced by a new common currency, typically involving a fixed conversion rate and a transition period before physical replacement of notes and coins.
Translation reserve
The cumulative OCI balance representing exchange differences on translating foreign operations, unaffected in a step-change sense by a fixed-rate currency changeover under SIC-7.

SOFP Statement of financial position SOPL Statement of profit or loss OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
