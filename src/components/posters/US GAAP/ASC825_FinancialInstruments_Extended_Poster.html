Conceptual Framework Linkage

A broad, general election to simplify mixed-measurement complexity
Different financial instruments are often measured on DIFFERENT bases across the Codification – amortised cost for some, fair value for others – creating potential "accounting mismatches" and operational complexity when related instruments are measured inconsistently. ASC 825 provides the general FAIR VALUE OPTION: a broadly available ELECTION to measure many financial instruments (and certain other specified items) at FAIR VALUE, with changes through EARNINGS, INSTRUMENT BY INSTRUMENT.
2

Scope – the Fair Value Option's Reach

Broadly available, with specific scope exclusions
The fair value option is available for MOST recognised financial assets and financial liabilities, with SPECIFIC scope EXCLUSIONS – including investments in CONSOLIDATED subsidiaries, pension/OPEB plan obligations governed by ASC 715, most LEASE-related assets/liabilities, and demand deposit liabilities of financial institutions.
3

Fair Value Option Mechanics

Irrevocable, instrument-by-instrument, generally at initial recognition
Once elected for a specific eligible instrument, the fair value option is IRREVOCABLE – the election is made on an INSTRUMENT-BY-INSTRUMENT basis (not for an entire class of instruments), generally at INITIAL RECOGNITION (or at certain specified remeasurement event dates thereafter, such as a business combination or a significant modification). This is a genuinely BROAD election, extending well beyond the narrower situations discussed elsewhere in this library (e.g. equity method investments under ASC 323, or hybrid instruments with embedded derivatives under ASC 815) – the fair value option can be applied to ordinary loans, receivables, and payables too.
4

The Key Divergence – Unconditional Election vs IFRS 9's Accounting Mismatch Condition

A meaningful eligibility gate that only IFRS 9 imposes
US GAAP's fair value option is available essentially UNCONDITIONALLY for eligible instruments – an entity need NOT demonstrate any particular justification (such as reducing a measurement mismatch) to make the election; it is simply available as a general policy choice. IFRS 9's OWN fair value option is genuinely NARROWER and CONDITIONAL: it may be elected ONLY IF doing so ELIMINATES OR SIGNIFICANTLY REDUCES an "accounting mismatch" that would otherwise arise from measuring related assets/liabilities on different bases, OR for a group of financial instruments that is managed and evaluated on a FAIR VALUE basis in accordance with a documented risk management or investment strategy. This is a genuine, meaningful ELIGIBILITY CONDITION IFRS 9 imposes that ASC 825's fair value option simply does not require – a real, worth-noting divergence in how freely each framework allows this election.
5

Concentrations of Credit Risk Disclosure

A broader disclosure requirement, independent of measurement basis
ASC 825 also requires disclosure of SIGNIFICANT CONCENTRATIONS of CREDIT RISK arising from financial instruments – for example, a large receivable balance concentrated with a small number of customers, or cash deposits concentrated with a single financial institution – a disclosure requirement that applies BROADLY, regardless of whether the instruments in question are measured at fair value or amortised cost.
6

Fair Value Disclosures for Instruments Not Measured at Fair Value

Note disclosure even for amortised-cost instruments
ASC 825 requires disclosing the FAIR VALUE (in the notes) of financial instruments even when they are carried at AMORTISED COST on the balance sheet – for example, debt, held-to-maturity securities, or loans – giving readers visibility into fair value even where it isn't the primary measurement basis. IFRS 7 imposes a broadly SIMILAR fair value disclosure requirement for financial instruments not measured at fair value on the balance sheet, making this a reasonably CONVERGED disclosure principle in substance.

Worked Example – a Fair Value Option Election

Electing fair value for an ordinary loan receivable
Example: A bank electing the fair value option for a loan portfolio
A bank originates a $50,000,000 portfolio of fixed-rate commercial loans, which it intends to actively manage on a fair value basis alongside interest rate derivatives used to hedge the portfolio's economics, without formally designating the derivatives as ASC 815 hedges.
The bank ELECTS the fair value option for the loan portfolio at ORIGINATION – an IRREVOCABLE, instrument-by-instrument election (here applied loan-by-loan or to the portfolio as elected). The loans are subsequently measured at FAIR VALUE each period, with changes recognised DIRECTLY in EARNINGS – achieving an accounting result economically similar to a formal hedge relationship, but WITHOUT needing to satisfy ASC 815's hedge documentation and effectiveness requirements.
8

Worked Example – Contrasting Eligibility Conditions

The same fact pattern, a genuinely different eligibility test
Example: A fixed-rate bond payable, no offsetting instrument
An entity has a single fixed-rate bond payable outstanding, with no related derivative or offsetting instrument that would create any accounting mismatch if the bond remained at amortised cost.
Under US GAAP: the entity may STILL elect the fair value option for this bond, even in the ABSENCE of any accounting mismatch – the election is available essentially UNCONDITIONALLY.
Under IFRS 9: the entity likely CANNOT elect the fair value option here, since there is NO accounting mismatch being eliminated or reduced, and the bond isn't part of a fair-value-managed group – IFRS 9's eligibility CONDITION is simply NOT met, even though the underlying instrument itself would otherwise be eligible in substance.

ASC 825 vs IFRS 9 – Comprehensive Comparison

Comparison – ASC 825 vs IFRS 9's fair value option
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 825)IFRS (IFRS 9)
Eligibility conditionNONE – essentially unconditional for eligible instrumentsREQUIRED – must reduce an accounting mismatch, or relate to a fair-value-managed group
Election basisInstrument-by-instrumentInstrument-by-instrument (or designated groups for the mismatch exception)
IrrevocabilityIrrevocable once electedIrrevocable once elected – converged
Fair value disclosure for non-FV-measured instrumentsRequiredRequired under IFRS 7 – broadly converged
The core divergence is genuinely about FLEXIBILITY – US GAAP's fair value option is a broad, largely unconditional policy tool, while IFRS 9's version is deliberately narrower, reserved for specific mismatch-reduction or fair-value-management scenarios.

Fair value option
An irrevocable, instrument-by-instrument election to measure an eligible financial instrument at fair value through earnings.
Accounting mismatch
An inconsistency in measurement or recognition arising from measuring related assets/liabilities on different bases – the IFRS 9-specific gate for its own fair value option.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Concentration of credit risk
An exposure to loss from financial instruments concentrated with a limited number of counterparties, industries, or regions, requiring specific disclosure.
Instrument-by-instrument election
The requirement that the fair value option be applied separately to each eligible instrument, rather than to an entire class or category.
