Objective, Scope & the "Operation" Test (paras. 1–4)

Objective
Improves the relevance, faithful representation, and comparability of information about a public sector combination — establishing how a reporting entity CLASSIFIES a combination (amalgamation vs. acquisition), and how it recognizes/measures the resulting assets, liabilities, non-controlling interests, net assets/equity, and goodwill or gain.
Applies to any transaction/event meeting the "public sector combination" definition — the bringing together of separate operations into ONE public sector entity
The gateway test — is it an "operation"?
An operation = an INTEGRATED SET of activities and related assets/liabilities, capable of being conducted/managed to achieve an entity's objectives by providing goods/services
A joint arrangement's OWN formation, in its own statements — excluded Acquiring/receiving an asset (or asset group) that does NOT constitute an operation — identify and recognize the individual assets instead; NO goodwill arises Assuming a liability (or liability group) that does NOT constitute an operation — recognize the individual liabilities instead An investment entity's acquisition of a controlled-entity investment required to be fair-valued (IPSAS 35)

Definitions — Amalgamation, Acquisition & Related Terms (para. 5)

Amalgamation vocabulary
Amalgamation
A combination where NO party gains control (a); OR one party gains control but the combination has the ECONOMIC SUBSTANCE of an amalgamation (b).
Resulting entity
The entity resulting from two or more operations combining in an amalgamation.
Combining operation
An operation that combines with one or more others to form the resulting entity.
Acquisition vocabulary
Acquisition
A combination where one party gains control of one or more operations, and there's evidence it is NOT an amalgamation.
Acquirer / acquired operation
The entity gaining control / the operation being controlled.
Goodwill
An asset representing future economic benefits from acquired assets NOT individually identified and separately recognized.
Shared terms
Identifiable asset — separable (sellable/transferable/licensable/rentable/exchangeable alone or together with a related item), OR arises from binding arrangements, regardless of transferability Public sector combination under common control — ALL entities/operations involved are ultimately controlled by the SAME entity, both before AND after the combination Contingent consideration — an obligation to transfer additional assets/equity if future conditions are met, OR a right to the return of consideration already transferred

Identifying & Classifying a Public Sector Combination (paras. 6–9)

The two-part classification logic
NO party gains control of any operation → automatically an AMALGAMATION
ONE party gains control → an ACQUISITION, UNLESS the combination has the ECONOMIC SUBSTANCE of an amalgamation
Classification is judged against whether the resulting accounting treatment gives users information meeting financial reporting's OBJECTIVES and satisfying the QUALITATIVE CHARACTERISTICS (para. 9). Individually or combined, the §4 indicators USUALLY resolve this conclusively — a combination need NOT satisfy both indicator sets to qualify as an amalgamation (para. 9).
4

Indicators That a Combination Is an Amalgamation (paras. 10–14)

Consideration-related indicators (para. 12)
Consideration is paid for reasons OTHER than compensating those entitled to the transferred operation's net assets Consideration is NOT paid to those entitled to the net assets Consideration is NOT paid because NO ONE has an entitlement to the transferred entity's net assets
Decision-making-process indicators (para. 13)
The combination is IMPOSED by a third party — no party to the combination is involved in the decision The combination requires approval by each party's CITIZENS through referenda The combination occurs UNDER COMMON CONTROL
In EXCEPTIONAL cases where both indicator sets are inconclusive, the entity instead considers which classification and treatment would BEST meet financial reporting's objectives and BEST satisfy the qualitative characteristics (relevance, faithful representation, understandability, timeliness, comparability, verifiability) (para. 14).
5

Accounting for Amalgamations — the Modified Pooling of Interests Method (paras. 15–20)

Four steps (para. 16)
Identify the RESULTING entity Determine the AMALGAMATION DATE Recognize/measure identifiable assets received, liabilities assumed, and non-controlling interests Recognize/measure net assets/equity components and other adjustments
Identifying the resulting entity & the amalgamation date
The resulting entity is whichever entity OBTAINS CONTROL of the combining operations (paras. 17–18). The amalgamation date is when that control transfers — often when assets/liabilities are formally DELEGATED to the resulting entity, though legislation or written agreement may specify a different date. All pertinent facts/circumstances are considered (paras. 19–20).
6

Amalgamations — Recognition, Measurement & Exceptions (paras. 21–35)

Recognition & measurement — carrying amounts, not fair value
Measure identifiable assets/liabilities at their COMBINING-OPERATION CARRYING AMOUNTS at the amalgamation date — adjusted only to conform to the resulting entity's OWN accounting policies
This is THE defining contrast with acquisitions (§11) — amalgamations POOL carrying amounts; acquisitions REMEASURE to fair value. Intra-combining-operation transactions are ELIMINATED (para. 22). Assets/liabilities must meet the CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK definitions at the amalgamation date — future exit/termination costs the entity merely EXPECTS (but isn't obliged) to incur are NOT liabilities here (para. 23).
Classification consistency, and the licenses exception
Assets/liabilities keep the SAME classifications/designations the combining operations previously used (e.g. fair-value vs. amortized-cost financial instrument classification) — the resulting entity does NOT adopt different ones on initial recognition, even where another IPSAS would otherwise permit it (paras. 24–25) EXCEPTION: a license/similar right previously granted BETWEEN combining operations, recognized as an intangible by the recipient, is recognized by the resulting entity too — NOT eliminated as an intra-group transaction (para. 32)
Non-controlling interests are measured at THEIR carrying amounts in the combining operation, adjusted for their proportionate share of any conforming-policy adjustments (para. 29).
7

Amalgamations — the Net Assets/Equity Mechanism (paras. 37–39)

No goodwill, no gain — just a balancing entry within net assets/equity
Recognize WITHIN net assets/equity, amounts EQUAL AND OPPOSITE to: the combining operations' asset carrying amounts, liability carrying amounts, and non-controlling interest carrying amounts
PLUS corresponding adjustments for: intra-combining-operation elimination (§6); accounting-policy conformity adjustments; and any recognition/measurement EXCEPTIONS applied. This is the pooling method's core mechanic — since assets/liabilities transfer at carrying amount rather than fair value, there's no "purchase price" to reconcile into goodwill; instead the WHOLE transaction nets out through net assets/equity, which MAY be presented as either a single opening balance, OR disaggregated into components (paras. 37–39).
8

Amalgamations — Measurement Period, Related Costs & Tax Forgiveness (paras. 40–49)

The measurement period — up to one year to finalize
Provisional amounts may be adjusted for UP TO ONE YEAR from the amalgamation date, as new information emerges — adjustments are made AS IF the accounting had been finalized at the amalgamation date, including REVISING comparative information and any depreciation/amortization already recognized (paras. 41, 43). AFTER the measurement period closes, only IPSAS 3 ERROR corrections can revise the accounting (para. 44).
Amalgamation-related costs, and tax forgiveness
Amalgamation-related costs (advisory, legal, accounting, valuation fees, general administrative costs, debt/equity registration/issuance costs) are EXPENSED as incurred, by both the resulting entity and combining operations (para. 45) Where a tax authority FORGIVES tax as a consequence of the amalgamation, the resulting entity accounts for it PROSPECTIVELY, per the applicable income tax standard (para. 49)

Amalgamations — Statement Formats & Disclosures (paras. 50–56)

Required statement set — a genuinely new resulting entity (para. 50)
Opening statement of financial position AS OF the amalgamation date Statement of financial position at the reporting date Statement of financial performance, amalgamation date → reporting date Statement of changes in net assets/equity, same period Cash flow statement, same period Budget-vs-actual comparison, IF the entity publishes an approved budget Notes — significant accounting policies and other explanatory notes
Where the resulting entity is NOT a new entity (i.e., one combining operation is treated as continuing), disclosure requirements shift instead to §51 — amounts recognized by class, conforming adjustments, and elimination adjustments.
Per-amalgamation disclosures (para. 54)
Name/description of each combining operation, the amalgamation date, and the PRIMARY REASONS (including legal basis) Amounts recognized by major asset/liability class, and the adjustments made (elimination + conformity) An ANALYSIS of net assets/equity, including any separately presented components and significant adjustments (e.g. revaluation surpluses)
An entity MAY (not required to) present PRIOR-PERIOD financial statements for each combining operation — but if it does, that information is disclosed on the SAME basis as originally prepared, NOT restated, with the basis disclosed (para. 52, 54(g)).

Accounting for Acquisitions — the Acquisition Method (paras. 58–63)

Four steps — the fair-value mirror of §5
Identify the ACQUIRER Determine the ACQUISITION DATE Recognize/measure identifiable assets acquired, liabilities assumed, and non-controlling interests Recognize/measure GOODWILL, or a gain/loss
Identifying the acquirer & the acquisition date
The acquirer is already identified during the §3/§4 CLASSIFICATION step — the party that gains control (para. 61). The acquisition date is USUALLY the closing date (legal consideration transfer / asset-liability assumption) — but a written agreement may set an EARLIER or LATER date; all facts/circumstances are considered (paras. 62–63).
11

Acquisitions — Recognition & Measurement Principles (paras. 64–74)

Recognition — separate from goodwill, and part of the exchange
Recognized SEPARATELY from goodwill, as of the acquisition date. Must meet Conceptual Framework asset/liability definitions AT that date (expected-but-not-obliged future exit/termination costs are excluded, as in §6) (para. 65) — AND be part of what was actually EXCHANGED in the acquisition transaction, not a separate transaction accounted for on its own terms (para. 66). This recognition can bring PREVIOUSLY UNRECOGNIZED items onto the books — e.g. an internally-developed patent the acquired operation never capitalized (para. 67).
Measurement — acquisition-date FAIR VALUE (the core contrast with §6)
Identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed → measured at ACQUISITION-DATE FAIR VALUE (IPSAS 46)
Present-ownership non-controlling interests entitled to a proportionate liquidation share of net assets are measured EITHER at fair value, OR at their proportionate share of the acquired operation's recognized identifiable net assets — an ELECTION (para. 73). Classifications/designations (financial instrument categories, hedge designations, embedded derivatives) are made based on conditions AS THEY EXIST at the acquisition date — NOT carried over from the acquired operation, unlike the amalgamation approach in §6 (paras. 69–70). Two narrow exceptions: lease classification (IPSAS 43) and insurance contract classification are carried over from inception instead (para. 71).
12

Acquisitions — Exceptions to the Recognition/Measurement Principles (paras. 75–84)

A limited, specified list
Paragraphs 76–84 list specific items recognized/measured DIFFERENTLY from the general principles — e.g. CONTINGENT LIABILITIES are recognized as of the acquisition date if they're a PRESENT obligation from past events AND fair value can be measured reliably, EVEN IF an outflow isn't probable — a genuine departure from IPSAS 19's ordinary recognition threshold, which would otherwise leave many contingent liabilities off the books entirely (paras. 76–77). Other exception items (not itemized here) follow the same pattern: applying additional or different conditions than the general recognition/measurement principles.

Goodwill, Bargain Purchases & Non-Exchange Acquisitions (paras. 85–93)

The goodwill formula — and a public-sector cap
Goodwill = [consideration transferred + non-controlling interest + fair value of any previously-held interest] − [net identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed]
⚠️ Public-sector-specific cap (para. 86): goodwill is recognized ONLY to the extent the acquisition generates CASH INFLOWS, or reduces the acquirer's NET CASH OUTFLOWS. Any further excess is recognized as a LOSS in surplus or deficit instead — reflecting that many public sector acquisitions aren't commercially return-generating at all.
Bargain purchases & the genuinely public-sector non-exchange scenario
Bargain purchase — net identifiable assets EXCEED the consideration/NCI/previously-held-interest aggregate → after reassessing all measurements, recognize the gain in SURPLUS OR DEFICIT, attributed to the acquirer (paras. 88, 90) Non-exchange acquisitions WITH some consideration — compensated seizures, or a donor transfer for nominal consideration → treated as bargain purchases (paras. 91–92) Non-exchange acquisitions with NO consideration — uncompensated seizures (forced nationalizations), or donor transfers/bequests for NO consideration at all (para. 93)
Where NO consideration is transferred at all, the acquirer substitutes the acquisition-date fair value of ITS OWN interest in the acquired operation for the "consideration transferred" figure in the goodwill formula (para. 87).

Non-Controlling Interests, Measurement Period & Acquisitions Achieved in Stages (paras. 73, 99–105)

Measurement period — an acquisition-side mirror of §8
Up to ONE YEAR from the acquisition date to finalize provisional amounts for: identifiable assets/liabilities/NCI; consideration transferred; any previously-held equity interest; and the resulting goodwill/gain/loss (para. 104). Ends as soon as the acquirer gets the sought information, or learns no more is obtainable — whichever is EARLIER, but never beyond the one-year cap (para. 103).
Step acquisitions — remeasure the old stake to fair value
Entity A holds a 35% non-controlling interest in Entity B; on 31 Dec 20X1 it buys an additional 40%, gaining control
Entity A REMEASURES its PREVIOUSLY HELD 35% interest to acquisition-date fair value, recognizing any resulting gain/loss in surplus or deficit (or net assets/equity, if that's where prior changes in the interest's value were recognized — recycled on the SAME basis as a direct disposal would require) (para. 100).
The SAME mechanic applies where a joint OPERATION party (IPSAS 37) gains control of that operation — its ENTIRE previously-held interest is remeasured (para. 100A).

Acquisitions — Disclosure Requirements (paras. 119–125)

Per acquisition — the core package (para. 120)
Name/description of the acquired operation, acquisition date, and % of voting/equivalent interests acquired Primary reasons for the acquisition, and how control was obtained (incl. legal basis) QUALITATIVE description of what makes up recognized goodwill (expected synergies, non-separable intangibles, etc.) Acquisition-date fair value of TOTAL consideration, broken out by major class (cash, other assets, liabilities incurred, equity interests) Contingent consideration/indemnification assets: amount recognized, arrangement description, and an OUTCOME RANGE (or why one can't be estimated) Acquired receivables: fair value, gross contractual amount, and best estimate of uncollectible cash flows — by major receivable class
Further required items
Amounts recognized by MAJOR asset/liability class at the acquisition date Recognized contingent liabilities — the IPSAS 19 para. 98 disclosure package, or (if fair value can't be reliably measured) para. 100's package plus WHY reliable measurement wasn't possible Total goodwill expected to be TAX-DEDUCTIBLE Separately-recognized transactions (per para. 109) — description, accounting treatment, amounts, and the line item(s) affected
Where a MATERIAL acquisition occurs AFTER period-end but before statements are authorized for issue, the SAME disclosure package is required for that subsequent-event acquisition too (para. 119(b)).

Public Sector Combinations Under Common Control (paras. AG37–AG39)

The classic case — a government merges two ministries
Common-control combinations are often INSTIGATED by, and on behalf of, the controlling entity, which sets the terms — e.g. a government deciding to merge two ministries for administrative/political reasons. Where the controlling entity makes the ultimate decision AND sets the terms, this is evidence the combination is economically an AMALGAMATION (para. AG37).
Voluntary combinations still need controlling-entity involvement
Even where two commonly-controlled operations agree to combine VOLUNTARILY, that decision is usually subject to the controlling entity's approval (explicit or implicit) — which STILL points to amalgamation classification (para. AG38). Only where there's genuinely NO evidence the controlling entity instigated, set terms for, or approved the combination does this indicator fail to apply — in which case the entity falls back on the OTHER §4 indicators (para. AG39).

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Comparison with IFRS 3

Comparison — IPSAS 40 vs IFRS 3 (acquisitions only)
The ACQUISITION accounting requirements in IPSAS 40 are drawn primarily from IFRS 3 (issued 2004, amendments to 31 December 2015), Business Combinations. The AMALGAMATION model, however, has NO IFRS equivalent whatsoever — IFRS abandoned pooling-of-interests accounting decades ago, while the IPSASB reintroduced a MODIFIED version specifically because so many public sector combinations (ministry mergers, forced local-government amalgamations) genuinely lack an "acquirer" in any meaningful economic sense.
TopicDifference
Determining the acquirerIFRS 3 has dedicated guidance; IPSAS 40 folds this into the broader amalgamation-vs-acquisition CLASSIFICATION step (§3–§4) instead
Public-sector-specific transactionsIPSAS 40 adds guidance IFRS 3 lacks entirely — tax forgiveness (§8), non-exchange acquisitions via seizure/donation (§13), and the goodwill cash-generation cap (§13)
Terminology"Public sector combination," "operation," "acquired operation" (IPSAS 40) vs "business combination," "business," "acquiree" (IFRS 3)

Statement / Process Mapping

The master decision map
1. Do the assets/liabilities involved constitute an "OPERATION"? If no → account for individual assets/liabilities under the ordinary IPSAS; no goodwill arises (§1) 2. Does ANY party gain control? NO → AMALGAMATION automatically. YES → apply the §4 indicators to test economic substance (§3, §4) 3. AMALGAMATION path → modified pooling of interests: carrying amounts, net-assets/equity plug, up-to-1-year measurement period, full new-entity statement set (§5–§9) 4. ACQUISITION path → acquisition method: fair values, goodwill/bargain-purchase formula, up-to-1-year measurement period, extensive disclosure package (§10–§15) 5. Is this a step acquisition, or a joint-operation party gaining control? → remeasure the previously-held interest to fair value first (§14) 6. Is this a common-control combination? → strong indicator toward amalgamation classification (§16)

Quick-Reference Glossary

Operation
An integrated set of activities and related assets/liabilities capable of being conducted and managed to achieve objectives by providing goods/services.
Modified pooling of interests method
The accounting method for amalgamations — assets and liabilities recognized at carrying amounts, not fair value.
Acquisition method
The accounting method for acquisitions — assets and liabilities recognized at acquisition-date fair value.
Bargain purchase
An acquisition where the net identifiable assets acquired exceed the consideration transferred, resulting in a gain.
Measurement period
The period, up to one year, during which provisional amounts recognized for a combination may be adjusted.
Acquisition achieved in stages
An acquisition where the acquirer already held an equity interest in the acquired operation before gaining control.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOFPerf Statement of financial performance NA/E Net assets/equity (statement of changes)
