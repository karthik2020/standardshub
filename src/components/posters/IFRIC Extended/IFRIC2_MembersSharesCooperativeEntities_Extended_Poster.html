Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why a puttable instrument needs its own interpretation
IAS 32's liability/equity distinction turns on whether an entity has an unconditional right to avoid delivering cash. Co-operative members' shares are often redeemable ON DEMAND by the member – a feature that, applied literally, would push most co-operative capital into liability classification. IFRIC 2 works through the practical consequences of that logic for genuine mutual/co-operative structures.
Substance over the "share" label
IFRIC 2 reinforces that the LEGAL FORM of an instrument as a "share" does not override IAS 32's substance-based classification test. A redeemable-on-demand members' share behaves economically like a liability unless a genuine, unconditional restriction on redemption exists – the interpretation is really an application of IAS 32's core principle to a specific, common fact pattern.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRIC 2 covers
IFRIC 2 applies to financial instruments within the scope of IAS 32, including members' shares in co-operative entities, that give the holder the right to request redemption for cash or another financial asset – or that are automatically redeemed on withdrawal of membership.
TYPICAL ENTITIES IN SCOPE
Co-operative banks and credit unionsAgricultural and consumer co-operativesMutual insurance and building society-type entities with similar redeemable member capital
Key definitions
Members' shares
Instruments that establish membership in a co-operative entity, typically representing an interest in members' equity and sometimes redeemable on demand.
Redeemable on demand
A feature that entitles the holder to require the entity to redeem the instrument, at or before a specified time, for an amount of cash or another financial asset.
3

The Default Classification

The starting point: liability, unless proven otherwise
Members' shares that are redeemable on demand are, by default, FINANCIAL LIABILITIES – the entity has a contractual obligation to deliver cash or another financial asset that it cannot avoid. IFRIC 2 then sets out the two narrow circumstances in which such shares can instead be classified, wholly or partly, as EQUITY.
✕ The mere fact that redemption is subject to co-operative bylaws, is at management's discretion in practice, or has historically never been exercised does NOT by itself change the default liability classification – only an unconditional, enforceable restriction does.
4

Unconditional Right to Refuse Redemption

Path 1 to equity classification
If the entity has an UNCONDITIONAL right to refuse redemption of members' shares, those shares are equity. The right must be genuinely unconditional – a right that is conditional on, for example, maintaining minimum liquidity or regulatory capital is NOT unconditional for this purpose, since it can lapse in exactly the circumstances redemption would matter most.
Worked example – a governing charter with discretion
Example: Board discretion clause
A co-operative's charter states the board "may, at its sole and unconditional discretion, decline any redemption request, without condition or limitation."
This is a genuine unconditional right to refuse. The members' shares are classified as EQUITY in their entirety, notwithstanding the on-demand redemption feature.
5

Prohibitions Imposed by Law, Regulation, or Charter

Path 2 to equity classification
Local law, regulation, or the entity's own governing charter can impose a PROHIBITION on redemption – either an unconditional prohibition, or one based on liquidity or similar criteria (e.g. a minimum regulatory capital ratio). To the extent such a prohibition exists, the shares subject to it are equity.
TWO FORMS OF PROHIBITION RECOGNISED
Unconditional prohibition – e.g. redemption is simply not permitted under the applicable lawConditional prohibition based on liquidity or a specified financial criterion – e.g. redemption is prohibited if it would breach a statutory minimum capital requirement
Worked example – liquidity-based statutory prohibition
Example: Regulatory minimum capital floor
Banking regulation prohibits a co-operative bank from redeeming members' shares if doing so would reduce regulatory capital below a mandated minimum. The co-operative currently holds capital of $12.0 million against a $10.0 million minimum.
Only $2.0 million of members' shares (the amount by which redemption would NOT breach the floor) may be classified as a financial LIABILITY; the remaining shares up to the $10.0 million floor are effectively subject to the prohibition and classified as EQUITY.
6

Partial Equity / Partial Liability Splits

Reassessed at every reporting date
Because a liquidity-based prohibition (Section 5) depends on a figure that moves – such as available regulatory capital or total members' shares outstanding – the SPLIT between the equity and liability portions is reassessed at each reporting date. A change in the amount subject to the prohibition results in a transfer between the liability and equity components, without gain or loss recognition on the transfer itself.

8

Members' Shares Above a Fixed Amount

Where the charter caps redemption in monetary terms
Some governing charters prohibit redemption of members' shares beyond a specified NUMBER of shares or fixed MONETARY amount. The same logic as Section 5 applies: shares subject to the fixed prohibition are equity, and shares that could still be redeemed (i.e. below the cap) are liabilities – even where individual members hold amounts below that cap, requiring the entity to track the AGGREGATE prohibition across all members' shares outstanding.

IFRIC 2 vs US GAAP

Comparison – IFRIC 2 vs ASC 480 (Distinguishing Liabilities from Equity)
FeatureIFRIC 2 (IFRS)US GAAP (ASC 480)
Mandatorily/on-demand redeemable sharesLiability by default; equity carve-out available via unconditional refusal right or statutory prohibitionGenerally classified as a liability (or mezzanine/temporary equity in some cases), with a narrower and differently structured set of exceptions
Co-operative-specific guidanceDedicated interpretation (IFRIC 2) addressing the fact pattern directlyNo equivalent dedicated interpretation – co-operatives apply general ASC 480 principles
Reassessment of the equity/liability splitRequired at each reporting date where criteria-basedGenerally reassessed only when triggering events occur, with different remeasurement mechanics
The absence of a direct US GAAP counterpart to IFRIC 2 makes co-operative capital structuring one of the more genuinely divergent areas between the two frameworks, despite both starting from a similar "substance over form" liability/equity philosophy.

Quick-reference glossary
Puttable instrument
A financial instrument that gives the holder the right to put the instrument back to the issuer for cash or another financial asset – the general category members' shares fall into.
Mutual entity
An entity, such as a co-operative, owned by its members/customers rather than by external investors, typically financed substantially through redeemable member capital.
14

Quick-Reference Glossary

Unconditional right to refuse
A contractual or charter-based right of the entity to decline a redemption request that is not subject to any condition, event, or criterion that could cause the right to lapse.
Liquidity-based prohibition
A restriction on redemption tied to a measurable financial criterion, such as a minimum capital or liquidity ratio, which limits the amount an entity may classify as a liability to only the "headroom" above that threshold.
