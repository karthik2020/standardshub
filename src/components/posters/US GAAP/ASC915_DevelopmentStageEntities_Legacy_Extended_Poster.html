ASC 915 — Development Stage Entities (Legacy)
Extended reference poster · US GAAP · Reporting requirements ELIMINATED by ASU 2014-10 · Retained for historical/comparative reference · USD examples
SUPERSEDED STATUS: ASU 2014-10 ("Development Stage Entities: Elimination of Certain Financial Reporting Requirements") removed the special inception-to-date reporting requirements described below, effective for annual periods beginning after December 15, 2014 (early application permitted). Development stage entities now simply apply NORMAL GAAP, supplemented by the general going concern disclosure framework in ASC 205-40. This poster is retained for historical reference and legacy-filing comparability.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Extra transparency for pre-revenue entities — since eliminated as unnecessary
A "DEVELOPMENT STAGE ENTITY" was one devoting substantially all its efforts to establishing a NEW business, with either NO significant revenue yet, or revenue insufficient to be considered an established operating business. ASC 915 historically required such entities to provide SUPPLEMENTAL cumulative "INCEPTION-TO-DATE" financial information, on the theory that investors in pre-revenue companies would benefit from seeing the FULL cumulative history of the enterprise, not just the current period. The FASB later concluded this supplemental reporting provided LIMITED incremental value relative to its preparation cost, and ELIMINATED the requirement via ASU 2014-10 (Section 3).
2

Scope — What Qualified as a Development Stage Entity

Planned principal operations not yet commenced, or generating insignificant revenue
An entity qualified as a DEVELOPMENT STAGE ENTITY if either: its PLANNED PRINCIPAL operations had NOT yet commenced, OR its planned principal operations HAD commenced but there was NO significant revenue from them — common examples included pre-revenue biotech/pharmaceutical companies, exploration-stage mining companies, and early-stage technology startups.
3

The Elimination — ASU 2014-10

A deliberate simplification, removing a reporting requirement seen as low-value
ASU 2014-10 eliminated the requirement to (a) present INCEPTION-TO-DATE cumulative financial statement columns, (b) LABEL the entity as a "development stage entity" in the financial statements, and (c) disclose a description of the entity's development stage ACTIVITIES — effective for annual reporting periods beginning after DECEMBER 15, 2014, with EARLY application PERMITTED. The FASB's stated rationale was that this supplemental information had become of QUESTIONABLE incremental USEFULNESS to investors relative to its PREPARATION COST — part of a broader FASB SIMPLIFICATION initiative during this period.
4

What Used to Be Required — Inception-to-Date Cumulative Reporting

An extra cumulative column, on top of normal current/prior period columns
Historically, a development stage entity's INCOME STATEMENT, STATEMENT OF CASH FLOWS, and STATEMENT OF STOCKHOLDERS' EQUITY each required an ADDITIONAL cumulative column presenting amounts from the entity's INCEPTION through the current balance sheet date — layered on top of (not replacing) the normal current-period and prior-period comparative columns otherwise required.
5

What Used to Be Required — Additional Disclosures

Identification and description requirements
Development stage entities were historically also required to DESCRIBE the nature of their development stage activities, and to identify themselves AS a "development stage entity" prominently within the financial statements — signalling to readers that normal historical trend analysis (e.g. year-over-year revenue growth) would be of limited use given the entity's pre-operational status.
6

Current Treatment — Normal GAAP Plus ASC 205-40 Going Concern

No special reporting category anymore — general principles now apply
Following ASU 2014-10, a development-stage (pre-revenue) entity now simply prepares its financial statements under NORMAL GAAP, exactly like any other reporting entity — with NO special inception-to-date cumulative columns and NO special "development stage entity" labelling. Where genuine doubt exists about the entity's ability to CONTINUE as a going concern (a common circumstance for cash-burning, pre-revenue entities), the GENERAL going concern evaluation and disclosure framework in ASC 205-40 applies instead — a framework applicable to ALL entities, not one specific to development-stage companies.
7

Worked Example — Historical Inception-to-Date Presentation

How the old cumulative column used to look
Example: A pre-revenue biotech company's historical income statement (pre-2014)
A biotech company incorporated in 2011 had cumulative research and development expenses since inception of $12,000,000 through the current year-end, with $3,000,000 incurred in the CURRENT year alone.
Under the OLD ASC 915 requirements, the income statement would present THREE columns: the CURRENT year ($3,000,000 R&D expense), the PRIOR year (comparative), and a CUMULATIVE "INCEPTION (2011) THROUGH [current year-end]" column showing the full $12,000,000. Under CURRENT guidance (post-ASU 2014-10), only the normal current and prior year columns would be presented — the $12,000,000 cumulative column is simply NO LONGER required.

What applies today instead
Normal GAAP financial statement presentation, with no special development-stage columns or labelsASC 205-40 going concern disclosures, where substantial doubt exists about the entity's ability to continue operating

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where judgement matters most (historically and today)
HISTORICAL JUDGEMENTS
Whether an entity's revenue was "significant" enough to no longer qualify as development stage
CURRENT JUDGEMENTS
Whether substantial doubt exists about the entity's ability to continue as a going concern under ASC 205-40
10

No Direct IFRS Equivalent Existed, Even Historically

A US GAAP-specific reporting category from the start
IFRS never had a DISTINCT "development stage entity" reporting category, even BEFORE ASU 2014-10's elimination — pre-revenue entities reporting under IFRS have always simply applied GENERAL IFRS principles, with no separate inception-to-date cumulative reporting requirement of the kind ASC 915 historically imposed. This makes ASC 915 a case where a genuinely US GAAP-SPECIFIC requirement was later eliminated, converging the two frameworks' treatment of pre-revenue entities even FURTHER — both now rely on general principles plus going concern disclosure, without any special development-stage reporting category on either side.

Inception-to-date column
