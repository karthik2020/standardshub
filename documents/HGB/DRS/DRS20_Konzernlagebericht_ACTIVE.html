Scope — Elaborating §315 HGB in Full

The single most comprehensive DRS in the library
DRS 20 applies to every group required to prepare a Konzernlagebericht under §315 HGB (established in detail in the earlier HGB poster series), elaborating essentially the ENTIRE statutory content requirement — business review, position, forecast, risk, opportunity, internal control disclosure, takeover-relevant information, and (via reference) the non-financial statement content this whole HGB poster series documented across several posters.
2

History — a Genuinely Deliberate Consolidation Project

A response to a documented quality problem
Prior to DRS 20, several separate, narrower standards (DRS 5, 5-10, 5-20, 15, 15a) governed different pieces of Lagebericht content in a fragmented way. DRSC's own review of practice (2000s-era academic and practitioner analysis) found this fragmentation was genuinely undermining the QUALITY and COMPARABILITY of Lageberichte in practice. DRS 20 was the deliberate response: adopted 14 September 2012, announced 4 December 2012, consolidating all of this content into one unified standard.

Worked Example — the Prognosebericht's Own Quantification Standard

How specific must a forecast actually be?
A group's Konzernlagebericht states that it expects revenue to grow "moderately" in the coming year, without any further quantification, citing general economic uncertainty.
DRS 20 requires forecast statements to be QUANTIFIED wherever reasonably possible — either as a point estimate, a range, or at minimum a qualified directional statement (e.g., "increase of 3-5%") — with genuinely vague terms like "moderate growth" alone generally falling short of the standard's own expectations, UNLESS the group can genuinely justify why more specific quantification isn't reasonably achievable, given the demanding "dauernde Erfüllbarkeit"-adjacent rigor this whole series has documented as characteristic of German prudence-driven disclosure.
5

The Ongoing CSRD/ESRS Interaction Question

A live, currently unresolved question this poster series has flagged before
Recall from the earlier HGB Konzernlagebericht poster: Germany has still NOT transposed the EU's CSRD into national law as of this poster series' own research. DRS 20's own Anwendungshinweis 5 (AH 5, most recently amended March 2026) addresses a genuinely practical question this creates: for groups VOLUNTARILY adopting the ESRS framework pending eventual CSRD transposition, how do DRS 20's own established Grundsätze ordnungsmäßiger Lageberichterstattung (Tz. 12-35) interact with the ESRS's own, quite different reporting architecture? This remains a genuinely live, actively-updated area — AH 5 has itself needed repeated extension and revision as the underlying CSRD/ESRS legislative landscape kept shifting.

Relationship to IFRS

Still the same fundamental divergence flagged throughout this series
As this series has noted repeatedly, IFRS itself has no mandatory, statutorily-anchored forward-looking narrative requirement equivalent to the Konzernlagebericht — the closest comparator (the IFRS Practice Statement on Management Commentary) is explicitly NON-MANDATORY guidance, not a binding standard. DRS 20 remains one of the most distinctively HGB-native components of German group reporting, with no direct IFRS parallel of comparable legal force.
