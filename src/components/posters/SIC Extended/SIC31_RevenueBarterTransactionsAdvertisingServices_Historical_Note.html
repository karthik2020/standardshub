SIC-31 – Revenue–Barter Transactions Involving Advertising Services
Historical reference note · Issued 2001 · Withdrawn 2018
⚠ WITHDRAWN – Superseded by IFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers, effective for periods beginning 1 January 2018
What SIC-31 addressed
SIC-31 addressed whether and how an entity providing ADVERTISING services in exchange for RECEIVING advertising services from its customer as counter-consideration (a "barter" transaction, common between media/advertising businesses) should recognise REVENUE from that exchange.
The conclusion it reached
Revenue from such a barter transaction generally could NOT be measured reliably by reference to the fair value of the advertising services RECEIVED (since these are typically too similar and non-monetary to value independently). It COULD, however, be measured reliably by reference to the fair value of the advertising services PROVIDED, established using evidence from comparable NON-BARTER transactions that were similar, occurred frequently, represented a predominant share of transactions/value, involved cash or other reliably measurable consideration, and did not involve the same counterparty.
Where this guidance lives today
Superseded byIFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers (2014)
Why withdrawnIFRS 15's general principles for measuring NON-CASH consideration (at fair value, or indirectly by reference to the standalone selling price of the goods/services promised, if fair value cannot be reasonably estimated) absorbed and superseded SIC-31's narrower, advertising-specific barter guidance
Current statusBarter transactions involving advertising (or any other goods/services) are now analysed under IFRS 15's general non-cash consideration measurement framework, not a standalone SIC
