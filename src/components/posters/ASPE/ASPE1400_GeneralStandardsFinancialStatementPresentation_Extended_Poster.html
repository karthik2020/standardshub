Conceptual Framework Linkage

The overarching presentation principles beneath every ASPE statement
ASPE Section 1400 SETS OUT the GENERAL standards GOVERNING how a COMPLETE set of FINANCIAL statements is PRESENTED — FAIR presentation, CONSISTENCY, aggregation, OFFSETTING, comparative INFORMATION, and CLEAR identification of the STATEMENTS themselves. THIS Section WORKS alongside the MORE detailed, statement-SPECIFIC Sections (1510, 1520, 1521, 1540) to FORM the COMPLETE presentation FRAMEWORK.
2

Scope — the General Presentation Umbrella

Applying to every complete set of ASPE financial statements
This Section applies to the PRESENTATION of a COMPLETE set of GENERAL-purpose FINANCIAL statements PREPARED in ACCORDANCE with ASPE — SETTING the GENERAL, OVERARCHING rules that APPLY REGARDLESS of the SPECIFIC statement BEING prepared.
3

Fair Presentation

Reasonably reflecting the effects of transactions
FINANCIAL statements must FAIRLY present an ENTERPRISE'S financial POSITION, results OF operations, and CASH flows — APPLYING ASPE APPROPRIATELY, WITH additional DISCLOSURE where NECESSARY, is PRESUMED to RESULT in FAIR presentation IN all but EXCEPTIONAL circumstances.
4

Consistency of Presentation

The same presentation and classification from period to period
The PRESENTATION and CLASSIFICATION of items WITHIN the financial STATEMENTS is RETAINED from ONE period to the NEXT, UNLESS a SIGNIFICANT change in the NATURE of the enterprise'S operations, or a REVIEW of its FINANCIAL statement presentation, INDICATES that a DIFFERENT presentation or CLASSIFICATION would be MORE appropriate, OR a CHANGE is REQUIRED by another ASPE Section.
5

Aggregation and Materiality

Grouping items of a similar nature, presenting material items separately
EACH material CLASS of SIMILAR items is PRESENTED separately WITHIN the financial STATEMENTS — items of a DISSIMILAR nature or FUNCTION are PRESENTED separately UNLESS they are IMMATERIAL, CONSISTENT with the GENERAL materiality CONCEPT introduced in SECTION 1000 (see the COMPANION poster).
6

Offsetting Restrictions

Assets and liabilities, income and expenses, presented separately
ASSETS and LIABILITIES, and INCOME and EXPENSES, are NOT offset AGAINST each OTHER UNLESS SPECIFICALLY permitted or REQUIRED by ANOTHER ASPE Section (SUCH as SECTION 3856'S OWN specific OFFSETTING criteria for FINANCIAL instruments).
7

Comparative Information

Prior period figures presented for comparison
COMPARATIVE information for the PRECEDING period is DISCLOSED for ALL amounts REPORTED in the CURRENT period'S financial STATEMENTS, UNLESS a SPECIFIC ASPE Section permits OR requires OTHERWISE — HELPING users ASSESS trends and CHANGES over TIME.

The Immateriality Override — a Practical Compliance Relief

Compliance with a specific requirement is unnecessary if the effect is immaterial
An ENTERPRISE need NOT comply with a PARTICULAR presentation REQUIREMENT if the EFFECT of doing SO is IMMATERIAL — a GENUINELY practical, EXPLICIT relief REFLECTING ASPE'S broader COST-benefit orientation TOWARD private-ENTERPRISE financial reporting, RATHER than REQUIRING mechanical COMPLIANCE with EVERY presentation rule REGARDLESS of ITS practical SIGNIFICANCE.
10

Going Concern — Cross-Referenced From Section 1000

The default basis of preparation, applied consistently
FINANCIAL statements are PREPARED on a GOING concern basis UNLESS management EITHER intends to LIQUIDATE the ENTERPRISE or to CEASE trading, or HAS no REALISTIC alternative BUT to do SO — THIS Section CROSS-references the SAME going CONCERN assumption INTRODUCED in Section 1000 (see the COMPANION poster), applying IT specifically to the PRESENTATION of the FINANCIAL statements themselves.
11

Worked Example — Applying the Immateriality Override

Skipping a technically required but practically pointless disclosure
Example: A genuinely trivial classification distinction
A small private enterprise has $800 of prepaid expenses that, strictly applying a specific presentation requirement, would need to be separately classified from a related $2,000 balance — an immaterial amount relative to the company's overall $15,000,000 balance sheet.
GIVEN the AMOUNT'S genuine IMMATERIALITY relative to the COMPANY'S overall FINANCIAL statements, the COMPANY need NOT separately CLASSIFY the $800 — the IMMATERIALITY override PERMITS this PRACTICAL simplification.
12

Worked Example — Identifying the Financial Statements

The basic labeling every statement requires
Example: A properly identified Balance Sheet header
A private manufacturing company prepares its annual Balance Sheet.
The BALANCE Sheet CLEARLY states: the COMPANY'S full LEGAL name, that the STATEMENT presents the INDIVIDUAL enterprise (NOT a group), the BALANCE sheet DATE, the PRESENTATION currency (CAD), and the LEVEL of ROUNDING applied (e.g. "IN thousands of DOLLARS," if APPLICABLE) — SATISFYING the IDENTIFICATION requirements of THIS Section.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Fair presentation
The requirement that financial statements reasonably reflect an enterprise's financial position, results of operations, and cash flows.
Immateriality override
A practical relief permitting an enterprise to depart from a specific presentation requirement where the effect of compliance would be immaterial.
Financial statement key (ASPE terminology)
BS Balance Sheet IS Income Statement SRE Statement of Retained Earnings / Statement of Changes in Equity SCF Cash Flow Statement
