Conceptual Framework Linkage

Accounting for genuinely uncertain future events
ASPE Section 3290 governs the RECOGNITION and DISCLOSURE of CONTINGENCIES — EXISTING conditions OR situations INVOLVING uncertainty AS to a POSSIBLE gain OR loss, TO be resolved BY a FUTURE event. GENUINELY distinctively, ASPE retains "CONTINGENCY" terminology THROUGHOUT (Section 8), and USES a THREE-category likelihood FRAMEWORK — LIKELY, unlikely, AND not determinable — RATHER than IAS 37'S own PROBABLE/possible/remote STRUCTURE (Section 3).
2

Scope

Uncertain future events, both losses and gains
This Section applies TO contingent LOSSES and CONTINGENT gains — EXCLUDING asset RETIREMENT obligations (SECTION 3110), WHICH involve a KNOWN legal OBLIGATION rather THAN a genuinely UNCERTAIN future EVENT.

CategoryDescription
LikelyTHE future EVENT confirming the LOSS or GAIN is LIKELY to OCCUR
UnlikelyTHE future EVENT is UNLIKELY to OCCUR
Not determinableTHE likelihood CANNOT reasonably BE determined GIVEN the AVAILABLE information
4

Contingent Losses — Accrual Criteria

Likely, and reasonably estimable
A CONTINGENT loss IS accrued (RECOGNIZED as a LIABILITY and EXPENSE) WHERE it IS BOTH LIKELY that a FUTURE event WILL confirm THE loss, AND the AMOUNT can BE reasonably ESTIMATED — BOTH conditions MUST be MET together FOR accrual to BE appropriate.
5

Contingent Losses — Likely but Not Reasonably Estimable

Disclosed, not accrued
WHERE a LOSS is LIKELY but CANNOT be REASONABLY estimated, IT is DISCLOSED (DESCRIBING the NATURE of the CONTINGENCY and, WHERE possible, a RANGE of POTENTIAL loss), BUT NOT accrued as a LIABILITY.
6

Contingent Losses — Unlikely or Not Determinable

Generally no disclosure required, with limited exceptions
WHERE a LOSS is UNLIKELY, NO disclosure IS generally REQUIRED (THOUGH an ENTERPRISE may VOLUNTARILY disclose IT). WHERE likelihood IS not DETERMINABLE, DISCLOSURE is GENERALLY required, GIVEN the GENUINE uncertainty INVOLVED could STILL be MATERIAL to a READER'S understanding.
7

Contingent Gains — Never Accrued

A conservative, asymmetric treatment
CONTINGENT gains are NEVER accrued PRIOR to REALIZATION, REGARDLESS of HOW likely THEY may APPEAR — accruing an UNREALIZED gain COULD result IN recognizing INCOME that MAY never ACTUALLY be REALIZED, a RESULT the CONSERVATISM principle (RETAINED explicitly WITHIN Section 1000'S own QUALITATIVE characteristics — see the COMPANION poster) SPECIFICALLY guards AGAINST. A CONTINGENT gain IS disclosed only WHERE it IS likely TO be realized, AVOIDING misleading IMPLICATIONS of confidence.
8

Terminology — "Contingency," Not "Provision"

A single, unified framework rather than a split provisions/contingencies structure
INTERNATIONALLY, IAS 37 explicitly DISTINGUISHES between "PROVISIONS" (liabilities OF uncertain TIMING or AMOUNT that ARE nonetheless RECOGNIZED, GIVEN a PROBABLE outflow) and "CONTINGENCIES" (matters TOO uncertain TO recognize, ONLY disclosed). ASPE, BY contrast, RETAINS a SINGLE, unified "CONTINGENCY" framework THROUGHOUT — WHAT IFRS WOULD call a "PROVISION" is SIMPLY an ACCRUED contingent LOSS under ASPE'S OWN terminology, RATHER than a SEPARATELY labeled CONCEPT. THIS is a GENUINELY notable TERMINOLOGICAL divergence, EVEN where the UNDERLYING accrual OUTCOMES are LARGELY similar IN substance.
9

Worked Example — Accruing a Likely, Estimable Loss Contingency

Both criteria genuinely met
Example: A pending lawsuit with a probable, estimable outcome
A company is a defendant in a lawsuit. Legal counsel advises that an unfavorable outcome is likely, and estimates the probable loss at $350,000, based on comparable settled cases.
GIVEN the LOSS is BOTH LIKELY and REASONABLY estimable, the COMPANY accrues a $350,000 liability AND corresponding EXPENSE — CONSISTENT with the ACCRUAL criteria UNDER Section 3290.
10

Worked Example — Disclosing an Unlikely Contingent Gain

No accrual for gains, regardless of likelihood
Example: A pending favorable litigation outcome
A company is the plaintiff in a lawsuit against a supplier, with legal counsel advising that a favorable outcome, resulting in a $500,000 award, is likely.
DESPITE the FAVORABLE outcome BEING LIKELY, the COMPANY does NOT accrue the $500,000 GAIN — it MAY disclose the CONTINGENCY, GIVEN it IS likely TO be REALIZED, but RECOGNIZES no AMOUNT within the FINANCIAL statements UNTIL the GAIN is ACTUALLY realized.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Contingent loss
A possible loss dependent on a future event, accrued if likely and reasonably estimable, otherwise disclosed or omitted depending on likelihood.
Contingent gain
A possible gain dependent on a future event, never accrued prior to realization, disclosed only if likely.
