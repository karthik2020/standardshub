Conceptual Framework Linkage

Capitalising the cost of financing a qualifying asset
ASBE No. 17 governs the RECOGNITION and MEASUREMENT of BORROWING COSTS – interest and OTHER costs an ENTERPRISE incurs in CONNECTION with the BORROWING of FUNDS – REQUIRING CAPITALISATION of BORROWING costs DIRECTLY attributable to the ACQUISITION, construction, or PRODUCTION of a QUALIFYING asset, CONSISTENT with IAS 23's OWN current (post-2007) approach internationally.
2

Scope – What Counts as a Borrowing Cost

Interest, discount/premium amortisation, ancillary costs, and certain exchange differences
BORROWING costs WITHIN this Standard's SCOPE include INTEREST expense, AMORTISATION of DISCOUNTS or PREMIUMS on BORROWINGS, ANCILLARY costs INCURRED in CONNECTION with ARRANGING borrowings, and EXCHANGE differences on FOREIGN currency borrowings TO THE EXTENT they REPRESENT an ADJUSTMENT to INTEREST costs.
3

Qualifying Assets

Assets requiring a substantial period to prepare for use or sale
A "QUALIFYING ASSET" is an ASSET that NECESSARILY requires a SUBSTANTIAL period of TIME to get READY for its INTENDED use or SALE – TYPICALLY fixed ASSETS under construction, INVESTMENT property under CONSTRUCTION, or LARGE inventories REQUIRING an EXTENDED production PROCESS.
4

Mandatory Capitalisation – No Expensing Option

Capitalisation is required, not elective
BORROWING costs DIRECTLY attributable to a QUALIFYING asset MUST be CAPITALISED as PART of the asset's COST – THERE is NO option to SIMPLY expense SUCH costs as INCURRED, CONSISTENT with IAS 23's OWN elimination of its FORMER expensing option (WHICH was REMOVED in a 2007 revision INTERNATIONALLY, ALIGNING the two frameworks).
5

Conditions for Commencing Capitalisation

All three conditions must be met simultaneously
Asset expenditures have been incurredBorrowing costs have been incurredActivities necessary to prepare the asset for its intended use or sale are in progress
6

Specific Borrowings – Actual Interest, Less Investment Income

Netting off any temporary investment returns
WHERE FUNDS are BORROWED SPECIFICALLY for the PURPOSE of OBTAINING a QUALIFYING asset, the AMOUNT of BORROWING costs ELIGIBLE for CAPITALISATION is the ACTUAL borrowing COSTS incurred on THAT specific BORROWING DURING the period, LESS any INVESTMENT income EARNED on the TEMPORARY investment of ANY unused BORROWED funds.
7

General Borrowings – the Weighted Average Capitalisation Rate

Applying a blended rate to the excess expenditure
WHERE an ENTERPRISE uses GENERAL (RATHER than SPECIFIC) borrowings to FUND a QUALIFYING asset, the AMOUNT to CAPITALISE is DETERMINED by APPLYING a WEIGHTED AVERAGE capitalisation RATE (based on the ENTERPRISE'S general BORROWINGS outstanding DURING the period) to the PORTION of asset EXPENDITURES that EXCEEDS any SPECIFIC borrowings DIRECTLY related to THAT asset.
8

Suspension and Cessation of Capitalisation

Pausing during extended interruptions, stopping once the asset is ready
CAPITALISATION is SUSPENDED DURING extended PERIODS in WHICH active DEVELOPMENT of the QUALIFYING asset is INTERRUPTED, and CEASES ENTIRELY once the ASSET is SUBSTANTIALLY ready for its INTENDED use or SALE – AT which POINT any FURTHER borrowing costs are SIMPLY expensed as INCURRED.
9

Worked Example – Capitalising Interest on a Specific Borrowing

Netting off temporary investment income
Example: A dedicated construction loan
A company borrows RMB 100,000,000 specifically to fund the construction of a new factory, incurring RMB 6,000,000 of interest during the year. Unused funds were temporarily invested, earning RMB 400,000 of investment income.
The company CAPITALISES RMB 6,000,000 − RMB 400,000 = RMB 5,600,000 as PART of the factory's COST, with the REMAINDER of any OTHER unrelated interest expensed AS incurred.
10

Worked Example – Applying the Weighted Average Rate to General Borrowings

Capitalising a blended rate on the excess expenditure
Example: Funding a qualifying asset from a general borrowing pool
A company spends RMB 50,000,000 on a qualifying asset during the year, with no specific borrowing attached. The company's weighted average capitalisation rate on its general borrowings is 5%.
The company CAPITALISES RMB 50,000,000 × 5% = RMB 2,500,000 of borrowing COSTS as PART of the asset's COST, DETERMINED using the WEIGHTED AVERAGE rate ACROSS its GENERAL borrowing POOL.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Qualifying asset
An asset that necessarily requires a substantial period of time to get ready for its intended use or sale.
Weighted average capitalisation rate
A blended interest rate, derived from an enterprise's general borrowings, applied to the portion of qualifying asset expenditure not funded by specific borrowings.
