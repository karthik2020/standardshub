1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

A condensed snapshot, not a miniature annual report
An INTERIM financial report – covering a period SHORTER than a full financial year (typically half-yearly) – serves a genuinely DIFFERENT purpose than the annual accounts: providing an UPDATE on significant events and changes since the last annual report, rather than reproducing a full, standalone set of financial statements. FRS 104 sets out the MINIMUM content and principles for such interim reports, for entities WITHIN the FRS 102/103 suite.
2

Scope

Entities preparing interim reports under the FRS 102/103 framework
FRS 104 applies to entities that PREPARE an interim financial report and that otherwise apply FRS 102 (or FRS 103, for insurers) as their annual reporting framework – it does NOT itself REQUIRE any entity to prepare interim reports; that requirement, where it exists, comes from OTHER sources (e.g. a listing rule, loan covenant, or voluntary choice).
3

Minimum Content of an Interim Report

A condensed set of statements, plus selected explanatory notes
A COMPLIANT interim report includes, at minimum: a CONDENSED Balance Sheet (SOFP); a condensed Profit and Loss Account (or statement of comprehensive income); a condensed statement of changes in equity; a condensed statement of cash flows; and SELECTED explanatory notes – considerably ABBREVIATED compared to a full annual set of financial statements, focusing on what has CHANGED since the last annual report.
4

Same Accounting Policies as the Annual Financial Statements

Consistency between interim and annual reporting
An entity applies the SAME accounting POLICIES in its interim report as it applies (or will apply) in its ANNUAL financial statements – EXCEPT for policy changes made AFTER the date of the most recent annual financial statements, which would ALSO be reflected in the NEXT annual statements. This ensures genuine CONSISTENCY and COMPARABILITY between interim and annual reporting.
5

Materiality in the Interim Context

Materiality assessed relative to the interim period itself
MATERIALITY for interim reporting purposes is assessed in relation to the INTERIM period's OWN financial data – NOT by simply extrapolating the entity's ANNUAL materiality threshold – reflecting that an item significant enough to warrant separate disclosure within a SHORTER interim period might not be considered material when judged solely against ANNUAL figures, and vice versa.
6

Worked Example – Interim Materiality Assessment

A different materiality lens for a shorter period
Example: An unusual half-year expense
A company incurs a £250,000 one-off restructuring charge during its first half-year, against half-year profit before tax of £2,000,000 (compared to a much larger £15,000,000 ANNUAL profit before tax in the prior year).
Although £250,000 might NOT be considered material relative to the FULL-YEAR £15,000,000 figure, it represents a MEANINGFUL 12.5% of the HALF-YEAR £2,000,000 profit – assessed against the INTERIM period's OWN scale, the item warrants SEPARATE disclosure in the interim report, even though it might not have triggered separate disclosure in the ANNUAL accounts.
7

Worked Example – Minimum Interim Content

A condensed, not comprehensive, set of statements
Example: A half-yearly report's minimum components
A company preparing its half-yearly report under FRS 104.
The report includes a CONDENSED Balance Sheet as at the half-year end, a condensed Profit and Loss Account for the half-year (and the CORRESPONDING half-year of the prior year), a condensed statement of changes in equity, a condensed statement of cash flows, and SELECTED notes – considerably SHORTER than the full annual accounts, but SUFFICIENT to update readers on significant developments since the last annual report.
8

Disclosure Requirements

What FRS 104 requires
A statement that the same accounting policies as the annual accounts have been applied (or details of any changes)Explanatory notes on significant events and transactions since the last annual reportSeasonal or cyclical factors affecting interim results, where relevant
9

Critical Judgments

Where judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Materiality assessment relative to the interim period, distinct from the annual materiality threshold
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Interim-period estimates (e.g. tax charge, inventory provisions) may be less precise than year-end equivalents, given time and information constraints
