Ind AS 19 – Employee Benefits
Extended reference poster · Converged with IAS 19 · Gratuity Act, EPFO interest-guarantee, and Indian actuarial context · Worked examples
🇮🇳 IND AS – CONVERGED WITH IAS 19, WITH A GENUINE PLAN-CLASSIFICATION NUANCE – Ind AS 19's defined benefit measurement model (projected unit credit method, OCI remeasurements) mirrors IAS 19 closely. The distinctive Indian consideration is that EPFO-administered Provident Fund contributions, which carry a GOVERNMENT INTEREST RATE GUARANTEE, may require DEFINED BENEFIT treatment rather than the simpler defined contribution treatment – a genuinely important classification nuance for Indian employers. Conceptual Framework Linkage

Identical actuarial measurement philosophy to IAS 19
Ind AS 19 applies the IDENTICAL principle as IAS 19 – employee benefit obligations are recognised as the SERVICE is rendered, using the PROJECTED UNIT CREDIT METHOD for defined benefit plans, with remeasurements recognised in OCI (not recycled to P&L).
HOW IND AS 19 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Faithful representation – recognising the true cost of employee service as earned – identical to IAS 19EPFO interest guarantee classification – where an Indian Provident Fund arrangement (e.g. company-managed PF trusts under EPFO oversight) carries a STATUTORY GUARANTEE of a minimum interest rate, and the fund's actual returns are insufficient to meet that guarantee, the EMPLOYER bears the shortfall risk – meaning the arrangement may need to be classified as a DEFINED BENEFIT plan (not defined contribution) under Ind AS 19's own definitional test, IDENTICAL in principle to how IAS 19 would classify a comparable guaranteed-return arrangement anywhere globally
This is NOT a divergence in Ind AS 19's own classification PRINCIPLES from IAS 19 – it is a genuinely Indian APPLICATION consideration, since EPFO's guaranteed-return PF structure is a specifically Indian institutional feature.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 19 covers
Ind AS 19 applies to all employee benefits except those within Ind AS 102's scope (share-based payment) – IDENTICAL scope to IAS 19.
Genuinely significant for Indian employers given the Gratuity Act's mandatory nature and PF's widespread applicability.
Key definitions you must know
Gratuity
A statutory defined benefit under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, mandatory for most Indian employers with 10+ employees.
EPFO
Employees' Provident Fund Organisation – India's statutory body administering employee provident fund schemes, some carrying an interest rate guarantee.

Short-Term vs Long-Term Employee Benefits

Identical to IAS 19
Short-term benefits (settled within 12 months) are recognised undiscounted; other long-term benefits use actuarial measurement WITHOUT the OCI remeasurement mechanism (all remeasurements go through P&L) – IDENTICAL to IAS 19.
4

Defined Contribution Plans

Identical to IAS 19
Contributions are expensed as payable, with NO further obligation once paid – IDENTICAL to IAS 19, applicable to standard (non-guaranteed) Indian PF/pension contribution arrangements.
5

Defined Benefit Plans – Projected Unit Credit Method (with worked example)

Identical actuarial methodology to IAS 19
Example 1: Gratuity obligation measurement
An Indian company has a gratuity obligation under the Payment of Gratuity Act, requiring an independent actuary to determine the present value of the defined benefit obligation using the projected unit credit method, discounted at the yield on Indian government bonds of matching duration.
The projected unit credit method attributes benefit to periods of service, discounted using a HIGH-QUALITY CORPORATE BOND yield (or, in the ABSENCE of a deep corporate bond market as in India, GOVERNMENT bond yields) – an IDENTICAL discount rate selection principle to IAS 19's own fallback rule for markets without a deep corporate bond market.
6

Remeasurements – OCI Treatment

Identical to IAS 19
Actuarial gains/losses and return on plan assets (excluding amounts in net interest) are recognised in OCI, NEVER recycled to P&L – IDENTICAL to IAS 19's revised (post-2011 amendment) treatment.
7

Gratuity Act & EPFO Interest Guarantee – Indian Plan Classification (with worked example)

The single most important Indian application nuance
Many Indian employers maintain their OWN Provident Fund trusts (rather than depositing directly with the government EPFO scheme), which are required to provide a return AT LEAST equal to the EPFO-notified interest rate. If the trust's own investment returns fall SHORT of this guaranteed rate, the EMPLOYER is obligated to make up the shortfall – meaning the arrangement carries INVESTMENT and ACTUARIAL RISK for the employer, the defining characteristic of a DEFINED BENEFIT plan under Ind AS 19's own classification test (IDENTICAL to IAS 19's own test).
Example 2: Company-managed PF trust with a shortfall guarantee
An Indian company operates its OWN PF trust, guaranteeing the EPFO-notified rate of 8.25% to employees. In a particular year, the trust's actual investment returns are only 7.10%, requiring the company to fund the 1.15% shortfall from its own resources.
Because the EMPLOYER bears this INVESTMENT RISK, the arrangement is classified as a DEFINED BENEFIT plan requiring actuarial valuation under Ind AS 19's projected unit credit method – NOT simple defined contribution expensing – an application of Ind AS 19's classification test IDENTICAL to IAS 19, but arising specifically from India's EPFO guarantee structure.
