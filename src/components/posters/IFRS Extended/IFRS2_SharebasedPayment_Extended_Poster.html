Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why share options are a genuine expense
The Conceptual Framework requires an expense to be recognised whenever an entity CONSUMES an economic resource in exchange for goods or services received – receiving employee SERVICE in exchange for share options is no different from paying cash wages, even though no cash changes hands. IFRS 2 exists specifically to stop share-based remuneration being treated as a "free" cost that bypasses the SOPL.
HOW IFRS 2 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Expense recognition – services received in exchange for equity instruments are recognised as an expense as those services are CONSUMED, matching employee service to the vesting period Faithful representation – omitting share-based payment expense from the SOPL (as was historically common under some old GAAPs) would materially understate an entity's true remuneration cost Substance over form – the SAME underlying instrument (a share option) can create either an EQUITY increase or a LIABILITY, depending on whether the entity or the counterparty controls the settlement method
IFRS 2 was historically controversial precisely because it forced companies to recognise a genuine P&L expense for something many previously treated as having "no cost" – a direct triumph of the Framework's expense-recognition principle over a long-standing accounting convention.
Measurement principles applied
IFRS 2's measurement basis differs fundamentally depending on classification – a direct consequence of whether the ultimate settlement obligation is fixed (equity) or variable (liability).
ClassificationMeasurement basis
Equity-settledFair value AT GRANT DATE, fixed and never remeasured thereafter
Cash-settledFair value REMEASURED at each reporting date until settlement
ℹ️ Qualitative characteristics in action: fixing equity-settled fair value at grant date reflects faithful representation of the ORIGINAL bargain struck with employees – remeasuring it later (as if it were a liability) would introduce volatility unrelated to the entity's actual obligation, since equity holders bear share price risk directly, not the entity.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRS 2 covers
IFRS 2 applies to all share-based payment transactions, whether the entity can identify specifically some or all of the goods/services received – including transactions with EMPLOYEES and with OTHER PARTIES (e.g. suppliers, consultants) settled in cash, other assets, or equity instruments.
THREE CATEGORIES
Equity-settled – the entity receives goods/services and settles by issuing its own equity instruments Cash-settled – the entity incurs a liability for amounts based on the price (or value) of its equity instruments Transactions with a choice of settlement – either the entity or the counterparty can choose whether settlement is in cash or equity instruments
IFRS 2 explicitly EXCLUDES share-based payments to acquire goods as part of a business combination consideration under IFRS 3 (specific IFRS 3 rules apply instead), and transactions within the scope of IAS 41 or IAS 2 for the contribution of assets in specific circumstances.
Key definitions you must know
Grant date
The date at which the entity and the counterparty AGREE to a share-based payment arrangement, having a shared understanding of the terms and conditions – for arrangements subject to approval, this is the date approval is obtained.
Vesting conditions
Conditions that determine whether the entity receives the services entitling the counterparty to receive cash, other assets, or equity instruments – comprising SERVICE conditions and PERFORMANCE conditions (market and non-market).
Vesting period
The period during which all specified vesting conditions of a share-based payment arrangement are to be satisfied.
Grant date fair value
The fair value of an equity instrument at the grant date, determined using an option pricing model where market prices are not directly available – used and FIXED for equity-settled transactions.

Equity-Settled – Recognition & Grant Date Fair Value (with worked example)

The core recognition mechanics
Total expense = Number of instruments expected to vest × Grant date fair value per instrument, recognised on a STRAIGHT-LINE basis over the vesting period, with a corresponding CREDIT to equity
The entity recognises goods/services received (and the corresponding increase in equity) DIRECTLY as an expense (for employee/service arrangements), unless the goods/services qualify for recognition as an asset under another Standard.
Worked example – basic 3-year equity-settled option grant
Example 1: Share options with a 3-year service condition
On 1 January 20X1, an entity grants 100 share options each to 50 employees, conditional on 3 years' continued service. Grant date fair value: $12 per option. At the end of Year 1, the entity estimates 5 employees will leave before vesting (45 expected to vest); by the end of Year 2, this estimate is revised to 6 leavers (44 expected to vest); by the end of Year 3, 43 employees actually remain and receive their options.
Year 1: cumulative expense = 45 × 100 × $12 × (1/3) = $18,000
Year 2: cumulative expense = 44 × 100 × $12 × (2/3) = $35,200; Year 2 charge = 35,200 − 18,000 = $17,200
Year 3: cumulative expense = 43 × 100 × $12 × (3/3) = $51,600; Year 3 charge = 51,600 − 35,200 = $16,400
The estimate of the NUMBER of instruments expected to vest is REVISED each period based on the best available information – but the GRANT DATE FAIR VALUE PER OPTION ($12) is NEVER revised, even if the entity's share price moves dramatically.

Vesting Conditions – Market vs Non-Market (with worked example)

The critical distinction – WHERE the condition is reflected
Condition typeTreatment
Service conditionAffects the NUMBER of instruments expected to vest, re-estimated each period (Example 1 above)
Non-market performance condition(e.g. a profit target) ALSO affects the NUMBER expected to vest, re-estimated each period, just like a service condition
Market condition(e.g. a specific SHARE PRICE target, or total shareholder return relative to a peer index) is built INTO the GRANT DATE FAIR VALUE calculation itself, using an option pricing model – NEVER re-estimated, and expense is recognised REGARDLESS of whether the market condition is ultimately achieved
Example 2: Market condition (share price target) – expense recognised even if the target is missed
An entity grants options vesting only if the share price reaches $25 within 3 years (a market condition). The grant date fair value, already reflecting the probability of achieving this target via an option pricing model, is $8 per option. By the end of Year 3, the share price target was NOT achieved, and the options lapse unexercised.
The FULL expense based on the original $8 grant date fair value is STILL recognised over the vesting period, EVEN THOUGH the options never actually vested – because a market condition is treated as having ALREADY been factored into fair value at grant date, its failure does NOT reverse previously recognised expense.
✕ This is one of the most frequently tested and counter-intuitive rules in IFRS 2: failure to satisfy a MARKET condition does NOT result in reversal of expense already recognised, unlike a service or non-market performance condition, which DOES adjust the number expected to vest (and can reduce cumulative expense to nil if nobody ultimately vests).

Modifications, Cancellations & Forfeitures

Modifications – a floor, never a reduction
If a modification INCREASES the fair value of the instruments granted (e.g. reducing the exercise price), the entity includes the INCREMENTAL fair value granted in the measurement of the amount recognised for services received. If a modification DECREASES total fair value, the entity CONTINUES to recognise the services received based on the ORIGINAL grant date fair value, as if the modification had not occurred – a decrease in fair value is simply IGNORED for expense purposes.
Cancellations & forfeitures
Forfeiture (failure to meet a NON-market vesting condition, e.g. an employee leaves) → treated as if the grant had NEVER been made; cumulative expense already recognised is REVERSED Cancellation by the entity (e.g. buying out unvested options) → treated as an ACCELERATION of vesting; any amount NOT yet recognised is recognised IMMEDIATELY
Cancellation is treated very differently from forfeiture: cancellation ACCELERATES the remaining expense into the current period, while forfeiture (for failing a non-market/service condition) REVERSES the expense already booked.

Cash-Settled Share-based Payment (with worked example)

Share appreciation rights – remeasured every period
For cash-settled transactions, the entity measures the goods/services acquired, and the LIABILITY incurred, at the FAIR VALUE of the liability. Until the liability is settled, the entity REMEASURES the fair value of the liability at EACH reporting date and at the date of settlement, with any changes in fair value recognised in profit or loss for the period.
Example 3: Share appreciation rights (SARs) over 3 years
An entity grants 200 cash-settled SARs to each of 100 employees, vesting after 3 years of service, entitling each employee to a cash payment based on the share price increase since grant. Fair value of each SAR: Year 1 end $5, Year 2 end $7, Year 3 end (vesting date) $9. 90 employees remain employed through vesting.
Year 1: 90 × 200 × $5 × (1/3) = $30,000 (liability and expense)
Year 2: cumulative = 90 × 200 × $7 × (2/3) = $84,000; Year 2 charge = 84,000 − 30,000 = $54,000
Year 3: cumulative = 90 × 200 × $9 × (3/3) = $162,000; Year 3 charge = 162,000 − 84,000 = $78,000

Transactions with a Choice of Settlement

Counterparty has the choice
Where the COUNTERPARTY has the right to choose whether a share-based payment transaction is settled in cash or equity instruments, the entity has granted a COMPOUND financial instrument, split into a DEBT component (the counterparty's right to demand cash) and an EQUITY component – measured similarly to IAS 32's compound instrument split.
Entity has the choice
Where the ENTITY has the choice of settlement, the entity determines whether it has a PRESENT OBLIGATION to settle in cash (in which case cash-settled accounting applies), or otherwise accounts for the transaction as EQUITY-SETTLED – the classification hinges on whether a genuine cash obligation exists in substance.

Employee Share Purchase Plans & Group Share-based Payment Transactions

Discounted employee share purchase plans
Where employees can purchase shares at a DISCOUNT to market price, the discount itself is generally accounted for as a share-based payment expense, UNLESS the plan qualifies for a specific practical exemption for plans with no meaningful discount and no vesting-type conditions attached.
Group share-based payment arrangements
Where a PARENT grants rights to its own equity instruments to employees of a SUBSIDIARY, the subsidiary recognises the SERVICES received as an expense (with a corresponding increase in EQUITY, treated as a capital contribution from the parent), and the parent recognises the increase in equity in its OWN or consolidated financial statements, as appropriate.

Disclosure Requirements

Required disclosures
NATURE & EXTENT DISCLOSURES
A description of each type of share-based payment arrangement, including general terms and conditions (vesting requirements, maximum term, method of settlement) Number and weighted average exercise prices of share options – outstanding at the start/end of the period, granted, forfeited, exercised, and expired during the period Weighted average share price at the date of exercise for options exercised during the period
MEASUREMENT & IMPACT DISCLOSURES
How the fair value of goods/services received, or the fair value of equity instruments granted, was determined (inputs to the option pricing model used) The total expense recognised in the period for share-based payment transactions For cash-settled arrangements: total carrying amount of the liability at the end of the period, and the intrinsic value of vested liabilities

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Classifying a condition as market vs non-market (a critical distinction determining whether it's built into fair value or re-estimated) Distinguishing forfeiture from cancellation, given their opposite accounting effects Determining whether the entity has a present obligation to settle in cash in a choice-of-settlement arrangement
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Fair value of options/other equity instruments at grant date, using an appropriate option pricing model (e.g. Black-Scholes, binomial) and its inputs (volatility, expected life, risk-free rate) Estimated number of instruments expected to vest, revised each period for service and non-market performance conditions Fair value of cash-settled liabilities at each subsequent reporting date
⚠️ Option pricing model INPUTS (particularly expected volatility and expected life) can be significantly judgemental, especially for newly listed or unlisted entities without a long trading history – small changes in these assumptions can materially affect the total remuneration expense recognised.

IFRS vs US GAAP – Key Differences

Comparison – IFRS 2 vs US GAAP (ASC 718)
FeatureIFRS (IFRS 2)US GAAP
Core grant-date fair value approachFair value at grant, fixed for equity-settledVery similar approach – broadly converged following a joint standard-setting effort
Forfeiture estimatesEstimate forfeitures and revise each period (as described in Section 3)Also permits either estimating forfeitures OR accounting for them as they actually occur, a POLICY CHOICE not available under IFRS 2
Market condition failureExpense recognised regardless of whether the market condition is achieved (never reversed)Similarly, expense is not reversed for failure to achieve a market condition – largely converged
Modification decreasing fair valueOriginal grant date fair value continues to be used; decrease ignoredBroadly similar treatment, converged in substance
IFRS 2 and ASC 718 are among the more converged pairs of standards, following a dedicated joint project – the main practical difference is the forfeiture ESTIMATION POLICY CHOICE available under US GAAP (estimate vs actual) that IFRS 2 does not offer, since IFRS mandates the estimation approach.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Market condition
A condition related to the market price of the entity's equity instruments (e.g. achieving a specified share price) – built into grant date fair value, never re-estimated.
Non-market performance condition
A condition related to something OTHER than a market price (e.g. a profit or sales target) – affects the NUMBER of instruments expected to vest, re-estimated each period.
Intrinsic value
The difference between the fair value of shares a counterparty has the right to subscribe for (or receive) and the price it must pay for those shares.
Reload feature
A feature providing for an automatic grant of additional share options whenever the option holder exercises previously granted options using shares (rather than cash) to satisfy the exercise price.
Share appreciation right (SAR)
A cash-settled instrument entitling the holder to a cash payment based on the increase in the entity's share price over a specified period.
Expected term / expected life
The expected period an option/SAR is expected to be outstanding before exercise, a key input to the option pricing model used to determine fair value.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position (balance sheet) SOPL Statement of profit or loss (income statement) OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
