SIC-12 – Consolidation–Special Purpose Entities
Historical reference note · Issued 1998 · Withdrawn 2013
⚠ WITHDRAWN – Superseded by IFRS 10 Consolidated Financial Statements, effective for periods beginning 1 January 2013
What SIC-12 addressed
SIC-12 addressed when a sponsor should CONSOLIDATE a "special purpose entity" (SPE) – an entity created to accomplish a narrow, well-defined objective – even where the sponsor held NO majority of voting rights or legal ownership. It set out INDICATORS of control looking through legal form to economic substance: whether the SPE's activities were conducted on the sponsor's behalf per its specific business needs, whether the sponsor had decision-making power to obtain the majority of benefits, and whether the sponsor retained the majority of residual or ownership risks.
Its influence and eventual replacement
SIC-12 was highly influential in pushing consolidation analysis beyond simple voting-rights tests toward genuine SUBSTANCE-based control indicators, particularly following the accounting scandals of the early 2000s involving off-balance-sheet SPE structures. Its indicator-based approach was eventually absorbed into – and superseded by – a single, comprehensive control model.
Where this guidance lives today
Superseded byIFRS 10 Consolidated Financial Statements (2011)
Why withdrawnIFRS 10 introduced a single, unified control model – power over the investee, exposure to variable returns, and the link between the two – that applies to ALL types of investees, absorbing SIC-12's SPE-specific indicators into a broader framework
Current statusThe underlying substance-over-form philosophy remains central to IFRS 10, but SIC-12's specific SPE indicator checklist is no longer separately applied
