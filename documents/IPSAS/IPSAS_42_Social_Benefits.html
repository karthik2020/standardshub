Objective, Scope & the Social Benefit Definition (paras. 1–5, 4A)

Objective
Improves the relevance, faithful representation, and comparability of information about social benefits — helping users assess the NATURE of benefits provided, the KEY FEATURES of the schemes, and the IMPACT on financial performance, position, and cash flows.
Financial instruments (IPSAS 41/29) Employee benefits (IPSAS 39) Insurance contracts (the relevant international/national insurance Standard) Collective and individual SERVICES (IPSAS 19 sense) — these are NOT social benefits at all (para. 4A)
The three-part definition (para. 5)
Social benefits = cash transfers to specific individuals/households meeting ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA, to mitigate SOCIAL RISKS, addressing the needs of society as a whole
Social risks
Events/circumstances relating to individual/household CHARACTERISTICS (age, health, poverty, employment status) that may adversely affect welfare — imposing additional resource demands, or reducing income.

The General Approach — Recognizing a Liability & Expense (paras. 6–11)

The two-part recognition test (para. 6)
A PRESENT OBLIGATION for an outflow of resources, resulting from a PAST EVENT The obligation can be MEASURED in a way achieving the Conceptual Framework's qualitative characteristics, given its constraints
An obligation settleable WITHOUT any resource outflow is not a liability at all (para. 7). Measurement uncertainty does NOT itself block recognition — unless it's so severe that relevance and faithful representation genuinely can't be achieved; where recognition proceeds despite uncertainty, that uncertainty is factored INTO the measurement (para. 8).
The past event — a genuinely distinctive trigger
The past event = satisfaction by EACH beneficiary of ALL eligibility criteria for a specific social benefit PAYMENT
Each social benefit payment's eligibility satisfaction is its OWN separate past event (para. 9) — a liability isn't recognized for a whole future benefit stream merely because someone qualifies for the scheme generally; it accrues PAYMENT by PAYMENT as eligibility is (re-)established. The expense is recognized at the SAME point as the liability (para. 10). A payment made BEFORE the next payment's eligibility criteria are satisfied is recognized as a PREPAYMENT ASSET instead of an expense — unless it becomes irrecoverable (para. 11).
3

The General Approach — Measurement (paras. 12–21)

Initial measurement — cost of fulfillment
Measure the liability at the BEST ESTIMATE of the social benefit payments the entity will make to fulfill the present obligation
Directly applies IPSAS 46's cost-of-fulfillment basis — the same measurement concept underlying IPSAS 19 provisions (para. 12). The estimate accounts for the possible effect of SUBSEQUENT EVENTS on those payments (para. 13). If NOT expected to settle within 12 months of period-end, the liability is DISCOUNTED at a rate reflecting the time value of money, matched in currency and term (paras. 14, 19).
Subsequent measurement — settle, unwind, re-estimate
Liability REDUCED as payments are made; any difference between payment cost and carrying amount → surplus or deficit, in the period of settlement (para. 16) DISCOUNTED liabilities: increased each period for INTEREST EXPENSE as the discount unwinds (para. 17) REVIEWED at every reporting date, adjusted to the CURRENT best estimate (para. 18)
The expense is initially measured at the SAME amount as the liability; a prepayment/expense from an early payment is measured at the CASH transferred (para. 21).

General Approach Disclosures (paras. 22–25)

Per major social benefit scheme (para. 22–23)
The NATURE of benefits provided (retirement, unemployment, child benefits, etc.) KEY FEATURES — legislative framework, main eligibility criteria, and how to obtain further information How the scheme is FUNDED — budget appropriation, transfer from another public entity, or otherwise; if funded by social CONTRIBUTIONS, a cross-reference (or availability statement) for that contribution/dedicated-asset information Key DEMOGRAPHIC, ECONOMIC, and other external factors driving expenditure levels — may be aggregated across similarly-affected schemes
Financial figures & amendments
TOTAL expenditure on social benefits recognized in the SOFPerformance, analyzed BY SCHEME A description of SIGNIFICANT AMENDMENTS during the period — changes to benefit levels, or to eligibility criteria/covered population — with the expected EFFECT described
IPSAS 1's materiality and aggregation guidance (paras. 45–47) governs how these disclosures are organized (para. 24). If a scheme qualifies for the insurance approach, a STATEMENT to that effect is also required (para. 25) — even if the entity elects NOT to use it.

The Insurance Approach — an Optional Alternative (paras. 26–28)

Two required criteria — BOTH, together (para. 28)
The scheme is intended to be FULLY FUNDED from contributions Evidence the entity MANAGES the scheme the way an insurance issuer would — including regularly assessing its financial performance and position
Where BOTH criteria are met, an entity is PERMITTED, but not REQUIRED, to recognize/measure the scheme's assets/liabilities/revenue/expenses BY ANALOGY to the relevant international/national insurance-contracts standard (IFRS 17, or a national standard adopting substantially the same principles) (para. 26).
Why optional, not mandatory — a genuine cost-benefit tradeoff
The IPSASB concluded the insurance approach likely gives users the MOST USEFUL information for qualifying schemes — surfacing whether contributions are sufficient to meet expected liabilities, and whether persistent surpluses might be cross-subsidizing other spending. But it's also more COSTLY and COMPLEX to implement (actuarial-style cash flow projections over the scheme's whole duration, vs. the general approach's simpler eligibility-triggered liability). The Board settled on OPTIONAL specifically for cost/benefit reasons, while flagging this could be revisited as practical experience accumulates.
Electing NOT to use the insurance approach for a qualifying scheme simply means applying the general approach (§2–§4) instead (para. 27).
6

Insurance Approach Disclosures & Long-Term Sustainability Reporting (paras. 29–32)

Insurance approach disclosures — layered on top
The BASIS for determining the insurance approach is appropriate Everything the underlying insurance-contracts standard itself requires PLUS the same scheme-characteristics and significant-amendment disclosures as the general approach (§4) — nature, key features, and amendment descriptions (para. 30–31)
Long-term fiscal sustainability — encouraged, not required
Entities with social benefits are ENCOURAGED (not required) to prepare general purpose financial reports on the long-term sustainability of the entity's finances — with RPG 1, Reporting on the Long-Term Sustainability of an Entity's Finances, providing guidance for doing so (para. 32). This sits alongside, rather than replacing, the mandatory disclosures above — a genuinely useful complement given how social benefit obligations often extend far beyond any single reporting period.
7

Transitional Provisions & Effective Date (paras. 33–35)

Two transition paths, matching the two accounting approaches
General approach — full RETROSPECTIVE application under IPSAS 3 (para. 33) Insurance approach — the RELEVANT INSURANCE STANDARD's OWN transitional provisions apply instead (para. 34) — a deliberate choice to keep the two approaches' transition mechanics consistent with their respective measurement frameworks
Effective date: periods beginning on/after 1 January 2023 — earlier application encouraged, with disclosure if elected (para. 35)

Relationship to Statistical & Insurance Frameworks — No IAS/IFRS Equivalent

Comparison — IPSAS 42 vs Government Finance Statistics (GFS)
IPSAS 42 has NO IFRS Standard equivalent — social benefits at this scale are a distinctly public-sector phenomenon. Instead, the Handbook's comparison section benchmarks it against GOVERNMENT FINANCE STATISTICS (GFS) reporting guidelines, the same statistical framework underlying IPSAS 22 and IPSAS 24.
TopicComparison
Social risk conceptIPSAS 42 uses a SIMILAR concept to GFS — "social risk" is a defined term underpinning the social benefit definition
Scope of "social benefits"IPSAS 42 is NARROWER — limited to CASH transfers (and cash equivalents); GFS also covers IN-KIND benefits (e.g. health services)
Liability recognitionIPSAS 42 recognizes a liability for payments up to the NEXT eligibility point; GFS GENERALLY does not recognize such a liability, except for FUNDED social insurance schemes
Insurance approachIPSAS 42 permits it as an OPTION; GFS has no equivalent
DisclosureIPSAS 42 includes disclosure requirements NOT present in GFS
Where the insurance approach IS elected for a qualifying scheme, IPSAS 42's byproduct is close alignment with IFRS 17 mechanics by analogy — but this is a DELIBERATE OPTIONAL cross-reference to another framework, not a "drawn from" relationship the way most IPSAS relate to their IFRS counterparts.

Statement / Process Mapping

Decision map
1. Is this a CASH transfer to eligible individuals/households, mitigating social risk, addressing society's needs? If it's a SERVICE (collective or individual, IPSAS 19 sense) → not a social benefit at all (§1) 2. Does the scheme meet BOTH insurance-approach criteria — fully contribution-funded, AND managed like an insurance issuer? (§5) 3a. NO, or elects not to use it → GENERAL APPROACH: recognize a liability/expense when EACH beneficiary satisfies ALL eligibility criteria for a specific payment (§2) 3b. YES, and elects to use it → INSURANCE APPROACH: apply the relevant insurance-contracts standard by analogy (§5) 4. General approach: measure at best estimate of cost of fulfillment, discount if settlement is beyond 12 months, review every period (§3) 5. Apply the disclosure package matching whichever approach was used, plus the mandatory cross-disclosure of insurance-approach ELIGIBILITY even where not elected (§4, §6)

Quick-Reference Glossary

Social benefits
Cash transfers to specific individuals/households meeting eligibility criteria, mitigating social risks and addressing society's needs.
Social risks
Events or circumstances related to individual/household characteristics that may adversely affect welfare.
General approach
The default social benefit accounting model — a liability recognized at eligibility satisfaction, measured at cost of fulfillment.
Insurance approach
An optional model applying insurance-contract accounting by analogy, for fully contribution-funded schemes managed like insurance.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOFPerf Statement of financial performance Notes Notes to the financial statements
