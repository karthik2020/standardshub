Ind AS 24 – Related Party Disclosures
Extended reference poster · Converged with IAS 24 · KMP definition aligned with Companies Act, 2013 · Worked examples
🇮🇳 IND AS – CONVERGED WITH IAS 24, WITH A GENUINE KMP DEFINITIONAL ALIGNMENT – Ind AS 24's related party identification principles mirror IAS 24 closely. The distinctive Indian feature is that "Key Managerial Personnel" is interpreted CONSISTENTLY with the Companies Act, 2013's own STATUTORY definition (Section 2(51)) – a more PRESCRIPTIVE list of specific roles than IAS 24's own broader, principles-based "key management personnel" concept. Conceptual Framework Linkage

Identical transparency philosophy to IAS 24
Ind AS 24 applies the IDENTICAL principle as IAS 24 – financial statements must disclose the POSSIBILITY that an entity's financial position/performance may have been affected by the EXISTENCE of related parties and by transactions with them, even in the ABSENCE of actual transactions.
HOW IND AS 24 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Transparency – related party relationships can affect an entity's profit/loss and financial position even without any actual transactions – identical to IAS 24KMP alignment with Companies Act – Indian practice interprets "key management personnel" CONSISTENTLY with the Companies Act, 2013's Section 2(51) statutory definition, which SPECIFICALLY enumerates roles (Managing Director, Whole-time Director, CFO, Company Secretary, CEO) – a more PRESCRIPTIVE identification than IAS 24's own broader "authority and responsibility for planning, directing, controlling" test
This is NOT a divergence in Ind AS 24's own underlying PRINCIPLE from IAS 24 – it is a genuinely Indian INTERPRETIVE alignment, anchoring the broader IAS 24 concept to specific statutory roles under company law.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 24 covers
Ind AS 24 applies to identifying related party relationships/transactions, outstanding balances, and circumstances requiring disclosure – IDENTICAL scope to IAS 24.
Key definitions you must know
Related party
IDENTICAL definition to IAS 24 – a person/entity related to the reporting entity via control, joint control, significant influence, or KMP status.
KMP (Companies Act sense)
Specific statutory roles enumerated under Section 2(51) of the Companies Act, 2013.

Key Management Personnel (with worked example)

Broader IAS 24 principle, applied via Companies Act specifics in India
Example 1: Identifying KMP for an Indian listed company
An Indian listed company has a Managing Director, a CFO, a Company Secretary, and a Non-Executive Independent Director who does not participate in day-to-day management decisions.
The Managing Director, CFO, and Company Secretary are KMP under BOTH the Companies Act, 2013's statutory definition AND IAS 24's broader "authority/responsibility" test. The Non-Executive Independent Director is ALSO typically included as KMP under the Companies Act's definition (which specifically includes directors), even though their day-to-day operational involvement might be more limited than IAS 24's own principles-based test alone might suggest – illustrating the Indian statutory alignment's slightly BROADER practical net for board members.

Related Party Transactions

Identical to IAS 24
A transfer of resources, services, or obligations between a reporting entity and a related party, REGARDLESS of whether a price is charged – IDENTICAL definition to IAS 24.
6

Government-Related Entities Exemption

Identical to IAS 24
Reduced disclosure requirements for transactions with OTHER government-related entities (where the reporting entity is itself government-related) – IDENTICAL exemption to IAS 24, RELEVANT for India's extensive public sector undertaking (PSU) landscape.

Companies Act KMP Alignment – The Indian Divergence Point

A genuinely important interpretive alignment
Section 2(51) of the Companies Act, 2013 provides a SPECIFIC, enumerated list of roles constituting KMP (CEO, Managing Director, Whole-time Director, CFO, Company Secretary, and any officer notified by the Central Government) – a more PRESCRIPTIVE, statute-anchored identification than IAS 24's own broader "authority and responsibility for planning, directing, controlling" functional test. In PRACTICE, Indian preparers typically ensure their Ind AS 24 KMP population is CONSISTENT with (and generally at least as broad as) this Companies Act list, given the overlapping compliance context (e.g. Schedule V managerial remuneration disclosures, Section 188 related party transaction approval requirements).
8

Pricing of Related Party Transactions

Identical to IAS 24
Ind AS 24 does NOT require related party transactions to be at arm's length, nor does it prescribe pricing methodologies – IDENTICAL to IAS 24, though Indian companies separately navigate transfer pricing requirements under the Income Tax Act for TAX purposes, a distinct regulatory layer from Ind AS 24 itself.
