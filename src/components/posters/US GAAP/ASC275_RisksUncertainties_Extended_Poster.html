Conceptual Framework Linkage

Warning users about vulnerability the numbers alone don't show
Financial statements measured under GAAP can look robust while concealing significant near-term VULNERABILITY — heavy reliance on a single customer, a specialised estimate that could swing materially, or an unusual concentration of risk. ASC 275 requires entities to surface this kind of vulnerability EXPLICITLY, supporting the faithful representation and relevance of the overall financial report beyond what the recognised numbers alone convey.
A general, entity-wide Topic — not tied to financial instruments
Unlike much of IFRS's comparable risk disclosure content (concentrated within IFRS 7 for financial instruments specifically), ASC 275 is a GENERAL, entity-wide disclosure requirement applicable to ANY significant risk or uncertainty an entity faces — financial instrument-related or not — making it broader in scope than any single comparable IFRS disclosure standard.
2

Scope & Applicability

Applies to all entities
ASC 275 applies to the financial statements of ALL entities preparing US GAAP financial statements — the four disclosure areas in Section 3 are broadly universal requirements, not limited to public companies or any particular industry.

4

Nature of Operations

A brief, descriptive baseline disclosure
Financial statements should include a brief description of the entity's PRINCIPAL business activities and the industry(ies) in which it primarily operates, along with the relative importance of each activity if operating in more than one line of business — providing users the basic context needed to interpret every other disclosure that follows.
5

Use of Estimates in Preparing Financial Statements

A near-universal, boilerplate-style disclosure
A general statement is required that preparation of financial statements in conformity with US GAAP requires management to make ESTIMATES AND ASSUMPTIONS that affect reported amounts, and that ACTUAL RESULTS could differ from those estimates — a standardised disclosure appearing in the accounting policies note of virtually every set of US GAAP financial statements.
6

Certain Significant Estimates — the "Reasonably Possible" Threshold

A specific, defined disclosure trigger
Disclosure of a SPECIFIC significant estimate is required when it is AT LEAST REASONABLY POSSIBLE that the estimate will CHANGE in the NEAR TERM (generally understood as within one year of the financial statement date) by a MATERIAL amount. "Reasonably possible" is a defined probability threshold — MORE than remote but LESS than probable — the same three-tier probability language (remote / reasonably possible / probable) used throughout US GAAP's contingency guidance (ASC 450).
7

Vulnerability Due to Concentrations

A three-part test
CriterionRequirement
(a)The concentration EXISTS at the date of the financial statements
(b)The concentration makes the entity VULNERABLE to the risk of a NEAR-TERM SEVERE IMPACT
(c)It is AT LEAST REASONABLY POSSIBLE that the events causing the severe impact will occur in the near term
Common concentration categories include: a single customer or small group of customers, a single supplier, a specific product/service line, a particular market or geographic area, and a specific labour source (e.g. a workforce heavily unionised or dependent on a specific expiring agreement).

Worked Example — Significant Estimate Disclosure

A litigation-related estimate
Example: Warranty reserve subject to a pending design review
An entity has recognised a $4,000,000 warranty reserve based on historical claims experience. A newly identified design issue is under investigation, and management believes it is REASONABLY POSSIBLE the reserve estimate could increase materially within the next year once the investigation concludes.
This triggers a Section 6 disclosure: the entity discloses the NATURE of the warranty reserve estimate and that it is reasonably possible the estimate will change materially in the near term, WITHOUT necessarily needing to disclose a specific range or amount if one cannot yet be reasonably estimated — the disclosure's purpose is to WARN users of the volatility risk, not to pre-emptively quantify an outcome that remains genuinely uncertain.
9

Worked Example — Concentration Disclosure

A single-customer concentration
Example: Revenue concentrated in one customer
An entity derives 45% of its annual revenue from a single customer under a contract renewable annually, with no long-term commitment from the customer to continue purchasing.
This concentration EXISTS at the reporting date, plausibly makes the entity VULNERABLE to a severe impact (loss of nearly half its revenue), and it is at least REASONABLY POSSIBLE the customer relationship could end within the next year given the annual renewal structure — ALL THREE criteria are met, triggering disclosure of the concentration, its nature, and the percentage of revenue involved.

ASC 275 vs IFRS — Spread Across Multiple Standards

Comparison — ASC 275 vs the IFRS equivalents
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 275)IFRS equivalent(s)
Dedicated general-purpose TopicYes — one unified Topic covering all four disclosure areasNO single equivalent standard — content is SPREAD across IAS 1 (judgements and estimation uncertainty disclosures) and IFRS 7 (concentrations of risk, but limited to financial instruments)
Significant estimate disclosure trigger"Reasonably possible" change within the "near term" (~1 year)IAS 1 requires disclosure of sources of estimation uncertainty with a SIGNIFICANT RISK of a material adjustment within the "next financial year" — a very similarly worded, closely converged threshold
Concentration disclosure scopeGeneral — ANY type of concentration (customer, supplier, product, geography, labour)IFRS 7's concentration disclosure is limited to CREDIT RISK concentrations related to financial instruments — narrower in scope than ASC 275's general concentration requirement
The "significant estimates" disclosure trigger is one of the more closely converged specific thresholds between the frameworks, even though the OVERALL Topic structure differs — US GAAP consolidates it into one Topic, IFRS spreads the same substantive content across two separate standards.

Reasonably possible
A defined probability threshold — more than remote but less than probable — used throughout ASC 275 and ASC 450.
Near term
Generally understood as a period of approximately one year from the financial statement date, the time horizon used in ASC 275's disclosure triggers.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Concentration
A lack of diversification in an aspect of an entity's operations, such as reliance on a single customer, supplier, product, geographic market, or labour source.
Severe impact
A significant, financially disruptive negative effect on the entity — a higher bar than a merely material or adverse effect, the threshold ASC 275 uses for concentration vulnerability.
