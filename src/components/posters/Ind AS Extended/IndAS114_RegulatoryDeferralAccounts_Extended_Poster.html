Ind AS 114 – Regulatory Deferral Accounts
Extended reference poster · Converged with IFRS 14 · An optional standard of LIMITED practical applicability in India · Worked examples

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Identical interim-standard philosophy to IFRS 14
Ind AS 114 applies the IDENTICAL rationale as IFRS 14 – as an INTERIM, transitional measure, it permits FIRST-TIME ADOPTERS to continue recognising "regulatory deferral account balances" under their PREVIOUS GAAP accounting policies, pending a comprehensive rate-regulated activities project.
HOW IND AS 114 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Comparability trade-off – this is an ACKNOWLEDGED, deliberate exception to full Ind AS/IFRS comparability, isolated via separate line items – identical rationale to IFRS 14Genuinely limited Indian relevance – Indian GAAP (the pre-Ind AS framework) generally did NOT recognise "regulatory deferral account" balances as distinct assets/liabilities in the manner IFRS 14 contemplates (e.g. US rate-regulated utility accounting under US GAAP ASC 980) – meaning FEW Indian first-time adopters have had a PREVIOUS GAAP balance to carry forward under this optional standard
This is one of the LEAST practically applied Ind AS standards in India – its inclusion in the Ind AS suite provides CONVERGENCE completeness with IFRS 14, rather than reflecting active, widespread Indian application.
Optional, first-time-adopter-only scope – identical to IFRS 14
Applicable ONLY IF: (a) first-time Ind AS adopter, AND (b) recognised regulatory deferral account balances under previous GAAP
⚠️ An entity NOT already recognising such balances under previous GAAP CANNOT create them upon Ind AS adoption – IDENTICAL restriction to IFRS 14.
2

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 114 covers
Ind AS 114 applies ONLY to first-time Ind AS adopters that recognised regulatory deferral account balances under previous GAAP – IDENTICAL scope restriction to IFRS 14.
Given prior Indian GAAP's general non-recognition of such balances, this standard has seen VERY LIMITED actual application among Indian first-time adopters.
Key definitions you must know
Regulatory deferral account balance
IDENTICAL definition to IFRS 14 – an amount of expense/income that would NOT be recognised as an asset/liability under other Ind AS, but qualifies for deferral because of rate regulation.
CERC / SERC
Central/State Electricity Regulatory Commission – India's tariff-setting regulators for the power sector, operating SEPARATELY from Ind AS accounting requirements.
3

Eligibility – First-Time Adopters Only

Identical eligibility gate to IFRS 14
Only entities ALREADY recognising such balances under previous GAAP, transitioning to Ind AS for the first time, may elect to continue this policy – IDENTICAL to IFRS 14, with NO broader applicability to established Ind AS preparers.
4

Recognition of Regulatory Deferral Account Balances

Identical continuation-of-policy approach to IFRS 14
An eligible entity continues its PREVIOUS GAAP accounting policies for recognition, measurement, impairment, and derecognition of regulatory deferral account balances – IDENTICAL to IFRS 14's own "grandfather" approach.
5

Presentation

Identical isolation requirement to IFRS 14
Regulatory deferral account balances are presented as SEPARATE line items in the balance sheet, and net movements as separate line items in profit or loss/OCI – IDENTICAL to IFRS 14, ensuring these amounts do NOT contaminate comparability of the "regular" Ind AS-compliant figures.

Why This Standard Is Rarely Applied in India (with worked example)

The key India-specific practical observation
Example 1: An Indian electricity distribution company's transition to Ind AS
An Indian state electricity distribution company transitions to Ind AS for the first time. Under its PREVIOUS Indian GAAP financial statements, it followed the Companies Act/ICAI accounting standards, which did NOT recognise a "regulatory asset" for under-recovered tariff costs as a distinct balance sheet item – tariff true-ups were instead reflected through CERC/SERC-approved tariff orders affecting FUTURE billing, without a corresponding Indian GAAP balance sheet recognition mechanism.
Because the company had NO previous GAAP regulatory deferral account balance to carry forward, Ind AS 114's optional election is simply NOT AVAILABLE to it – illustrating why this standard, despite being fully converged with IFRS 14 in its TEXT, has seen minimal actual USE among Indian first-time adopters.
7

CERC/SERC Tariff Regulation Context

A separate regulatory framework, not an Ind AS 114 substitute
India's electricity sector tariff mechanism (multi-year tariff orders, true-up adjustments for cost under/over-recovery) operates as its OWN distinct regulatory process under CERC/SERC, largely INDEPENDENT of whether an entity applies Ind AS 114 – a genuinely important Indian-specific observation about why the two frameworks do not automatically align.

Impairment

Identical to IFRS 14
Regulatory deferral account debit balances are assessed for impairment using the entity's previous GAAP policy – IDENTICAL to IFRS 14.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether the entity qualifies as an eligible first-time adopter with a genuine previous GAAP balance
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES

Quick-reference glossary
Rate regulation
A framework where a regulator (e.g. CERC/SERC) establishes prices that customers are charged, potentially creating regulatory deferral account balances under previous GAAP frameworks that recognise them.
True-up
India's electricity tariff mechanism for reconciling estimated vs actual costs, operating through the CERC/SERC process rather than through an Ind AS 114 balance.
