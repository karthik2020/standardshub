Conceptual Framework Linkage

Stock options as genuine compensation expense, fair-valued at grant
ASBJ Statement No. 8 governs STOCK OPTIONS (ストックオプション) and other SHARE-BASED PAYMENT arrangements granted to directors, employees, and others for SERVICES rendered — establishing that such grants represent genuine COMPENSATION EXPENSE, measured at FAIR VALUE on the GRANT date and recognised over the VESTING period, rather than going UNRECOGNISED simply because no cash changes hands.
2

Scope

Companies granting stock options and similar equity-settled awards
This Statement applies to companies granting STOCK OPTIONS (typically structured as "subscription rights to shares," 新株予約権, under the Companies Act) or other SHARE-BASED payment arrangements to directors, employees, or other parties in exchange for services.
3

Recognition — Fair Value at Grant Date, Expensed Over the Vesting Period

A fixed, grant-date fair value spread across the service period
The FAIR VALUE of a stock option grant is determined AT GRANT DATE (typically using an OPTION-PRICING model, such as Black-Scholes), and the RESULTING total compensation expense is recognised in the Statement of Income OVER the VESTING period — the period during which the RECIPIENT must provide services before the option becomes exercisable — reflecting that the option is compensation for services rendered OVER that entire period, not a single point-in-time event.
4

The Credit Entry — Subscription Rights to Shares (新株予約権)

Building up within its own dedicated Net Assets component
As stock option EXPENSE is recognised, the OFFSETTING credit builds up within "SUBSCRIPTION RIGHTS TO SHARES" (新株予約権) — the DISTINCT Net Assets component established by ASBJ Statement No. 5 — rather than DIRECTLY within Shareholders' Equity, since the recipient has NOT yet become an actual shareholder until the option is EXERCISED.
5

Exercise of Stock Options — Transfer to Shareholders' Equity

Reclassified into Common Stock and Capital Surplus upon exercise
When a stock option is EXERCISED, the ACCUMULATED balance in Subscription Rights to Shares (relating to that option), TOGETHER with the exercise price PAID by the option holder, is TRANSFERRED into Common Stock and/or Capital Surplus — reflecting that the holder has NOW genuinely become a SHAREHOLDER, moving the balance INTO the ordinary Shareholders' Equity subsection.
6

THE KEY DIVERGENCE — Gain Recognition on Expiration of Unexercised Options

A notable, well-known departure from IFRS 2
If a stock option EXPIRES WITHOUT being exercised, J-GAAP requires the ACCUMULATED "Subscription Rights to Shares" balance relating to that option to be TRANSFERRED to the Statement of Income as a GAIN ON EXPIRATION OF STOCK OPTIONS (新株予約権戻入益) — typically presented as an EXTRAORDINARY or NON-OPERATING gain. This is a genuinely DISTINCTIVE, WELL-KNOWN divergence from IFRS 2, which explicitly PROHIBITS any REVERSAL of previously recognised share-based payment EXPENSE upon an equity-settled award's forfeiture or lapse due to failure to EXERCISE (as opposed to failure to VEST) — under IFRS 2, the amount SIMPLY remains WITHIN equity (often reclassified to retained earnings), WITHOUT ever flowing back through profit or loss. J-GAAP's approach of recognising a P&L GAIN upon expiration is therefore a MEANINGFUL, well-recognised point of departure.
7

Forfeiture and Vesting Conditions

Adjusting for expected and actual forfeitures during the vesting period
Where an employee LEAVES before an award VESTS (failing to satisfy a SERVICE condition), previously recognised compensation expense relating to that FORFEITED award is generally REVERSED — this is DISTINCT from the "expiration after vesting" scenario in Section 6, since a FORFEITURE before vesting means the award was NEVER genuinely earned, whereas EXPIRATION after vesting means the SERVICES were fully rendered but the holder simply chose NOT to exercise.
8

Worked Example — Expensing a Stock Option Grant Over the Vesting Period

Spreading grant-date fair value across the service period
Example: A three-year vesting stock option grant
A company grants stock options to employees with a total grant-date fair value of ¥90,000,000, vesting evenly over a 3-year service period.
The company recognises COMPENSATION EXPENSE of ¥90,000,000 ÷ 3 = ¥30,000,000 per year, with a CORRESPONDING credit to Subscription Rights to Shares WITHIN Net Assets each year — building up to the full ¥90,000,000 by the END of the 3-year vesting period.
9

Worked Example — Gain on Expiration of Unexercised Options

A distinctively J-GAAP profit-and-loss consequence
Example: Options lapsing after the exercise window closes
Continuing the prior example, the fully-vested options (¥90,000,000 accumulated in Subscription Rights to Shares) are NEVER exercised, and EXPIRE at the end of their exercise window (e.g. because the share price never exceeded the exercise price).
The company recognises a ¥90,000,000 GAIN ON EXPIRATION OF STOCK OPTIONS in the Statement of Income — a DISTINCTIVELY J-GAAP outcome; under IFRS 2, the SAME ¥90,000,000 would simply REMAIN within equity (typically reclassified to retained earnings), with NO P&L gain ever recognised.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Stock option (ストックオプション)
A right granted to a director, employee, or other party to acquire company shares at a specified price, in exchange for services.
Gain on Expiration of Stock Options (新株予約権戻入益)
The J-GAAP-specific profit-and-loss gain recognised when vested stock options expire unexercised, a treatment IFRS 2 does not permit.
Vesting period
The period during which a recipient must provide services before a share-based award becomes exercisable.
