Conceptual Framework Linkage

The minority shareholders' stake within a consolidated group
ASPE Section 1602 governs the RECOGNITION, MEASUREMENT, and PRESENTATION of NON-CONTROLLING interests (NCI) – the PORTION of a SUBSIDIARY'S net ASSETS and net INCOME NOT attributable, DIRECTLY or INDIRECTLY, to the PARENT – WITHIN consolidated financial STATEMENTS prepared UNDER Section 1601. NCI is PRESENTED within EQUITY, CONSISTENT with the "ENTITY theory" approach IFRS 10 ALSO applies internationally (Section 3).
2

Scope – Applies Only Where Consolidation Is Elected

A companion to Section 1601's consolidation mechanics
This Section applies ONLY where an ENTERPRISE has ELECTED, UNDER Section 1591, TO consolidate ITS subsidiaries – an ENTERPRISE electing THE cost or EQUITY method has NO non-CONTROLLING interest to PRESENT, SINCE it does NOT combine THE subsidiary'S own assets AND liabilities line-BY-line at ALL.
3

Presentation Within Equity

Separate from the parent's own equity, but still within equity
NON-CONTROLLING interests are PRESENTED WITHIN the CONSOLIDATED Balance SHEET'S owners' EQUITY section, SEPARATELY from the PARENT'S own EQUITY – GENUINELY consistent WITH the MODERN, entity-THEORY approach IFRS 10 ALSO applies internationally (AND which we SAW earlier under ASBE No. 33 – see the COMPANION Chinese GAAP poster), RATHER than an OLDER "PARENT company" approach that WOULD present NCI as a SEPARATE, non-equity item.
4

Two Components of Non-Controlling Interests in Net Assets

Initial measurement plus subsequent share of changes
ComponentDescription
Initial NCI measurementTHE amount OF non-CONTROLLING interests AT the DATE of the ORIGINAL combination, CALCULATED in ACCORDANCE with SECTION 1582
Subsequent share of changesTHE non-CONTROLLING interests' SHARE of CHANGES in EQUITY since THE date of THE combination
5

Attributing Net Income to Both Parent and NCI

A proportionate split each period
CONSOLIDATED net INCOME for the PERIOD is ATTRIBUTED between the PARENT'S own OWNERS and NON-CONTROLLING interests – REFLECTING EACH party'S RESPECTIVE ownership STAKE in the SUBSIDIARY'S own PROFIT or LOSS for the PERIOD.
6

Net Income Attributed to NCI Even in a Deficit Position

Losses allocated in full, regardless of resulting deficit
NET income (or LOSS) is ATTRIBUTED to the PARENT and NON-CONTROLLING interests EVEN if it RESULTS in the NON-CONTROLLING interests HAVING a DEFICIT balance – a GENUINELY notable POINT (and ONE that historically DIFFERED under OLDER, "PARENT company" approaches to NCI, WHICH sometimes CAPPED losses ATTRIBUTED to a MINORITY interest at ZERO). ASPE'S current APPROACH follows the MODERN, entity-THEORY treatment, CONSISTENT with IFRS 10 internationally.
7

Changes in Ownership Interest Not Resulting in Loss of Control

Treated as equity transactions
WHERE a PARENT'S ownership INTEREST in a SUBSIDIARY changes WITHOUT a LOSS of control (e.g. PURCHASING an ADDITIONAL 10% stake FROM a NON-controlling shareholder), the TRANSACTION is ACCOUNTED for as an EQUITY transaction – ADJUSTING the CARRYING amounts OF the PARENT'S and NON-CONTROLLING interests' RESPECTIVE equity, WITHOUT recognizing a GAIN or LOSS in NET income.
8

Reciprocal Shareholdings Within the Group

A narrow, technical allocation rule
WHERE a DIFFERENCE arises FROM the ELIMINATION of RECIPROCAL shareholdings AMONG companies WITHIN the CONSOLIDATED group, it IS allocated TO the PARENT and NON-CONTROLLING interests ON the BASIS of THEIR proportionate SHAREHOLDINGS – a NARROW, technical RULE addressing a RELATIVELY uncommon but GENUINELY complex group-STRUCTURE scenario.
9

Worked Example – Measuring NCI at the Combination Date

The starting point for tracking NCI going forward
Example: An 75%-owned subsidiary acquisition
A parent acquires 75% of a subsidiary. At the acquisition date, the subsidiary's identifiable net assets have a fair value of $4,000,000, and the parent elects to measure NCI at its proportionate share of net identifiable assets.
NCI at the COMBINATION date = $4,000,000 × 25% = $1,000,000, presented WITHIN consolidated EQUITY, separately FROM the PARENT'S own OWNERSHIP interest.
10

Worked Example – Attributing a Loss to NCI, Creating a Deficit

A negative NCI balance, fully consistent with ASPE's approach
Example: A loss-making subsidiary
Continuing the prior example, the subsidiary incurs a $4,800,000 net loss during its first year under the parent's ownership, with the NCI's opening balance at $1,000,000.
The NCI'S SHARE of the LOSS = $4,800,000 × 25% = $1,200,000, REDUCING the NCI BALANCE from $1,000,000 to NEGATIVE $200,000 – a DEFICIT that IS fully RECOGNIZED and PRESENTED, CONSISTENT with ASPE'S entity-THEORY approach, RATHER than CAPPING the LOSS attribution AT zero.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Non-controlling interest (NCI)
The portion of a subsidiary's net assets and net income not attributable, directly or indirectly, to the parent, presented within consolidated equity.
Equity transaction
A change in a parent's ownership interest in a subsidiary that does not result in a loss of control, accounted for as an adjustment within equity rather than through net income.
