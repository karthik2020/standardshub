Conceptual Framework Linkage

Identical cash flow classification philosophy to IAS 7
Ind AS 7 applies the IDENTICAL principle as IAS 7 – cash flows are classified into OPERATING, INVESTING, and FINANCING activities, providing users information to evaluate changes in net assets, financial structure, and the ability to affect amounts/timing of cash flows.
HOW IND AS 7 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Relevance – cash flow information complements accrual-based P&L/balance sheet data – identical to IAS 7Comparability – the three-category classification enables consistent comparison across entities – identical to IAS 7
Ind AS 7 introduced NO substantive change to the classification model relative to IAS 7 – its Indian relevance is most pronounced for the RBI's own cash flow-adjacent regulatory reporting for banks/NBFCs, layered ALONGSIDE Ind AS 7's own requirements.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 7 covers
Ind AS 7 requires ALL entities to present a statement of cash flows as an integral part of financial statements – IDENTICAL scope to IAS 7.
Key definitions you must know
Cash equivalents
IDENTICAL definition to IAS 7 – short-term, highly liquid investments readily convertible to known amounts of cash, subject to insignificant risk of value change.
Working capital cycle
The operating cash conversion cycle, particularly relevant for Indian manufacturing/trading companies' operating cash flow analysis.

The Three Classifications (with worked example)

Identical classification approach to IAS 7
Example 1: Classifying an Indian company's cash flows
An Indian manufacturing company pays ₹50,00,000 for a new factory machine, receives ₹2,00,00,000 from customer collections, and repays ₹75,00,000 of term loan principal to an Indian bank.
Machine purchase: INVESTING outflow
Customer collections: OPERATING inflow
Loan principal repayment: FINANCING outflow
An IDENTICAL classification methodology to how IAS 7 would categorise the same cash flows for a comparable global manufacturer.
4

Direct vs Indirect Method

Identical choice to IAS 7
Operating cash flows may be presented using the DIRECT method (major classes of gross cash receipts/payments) or the INDIRECT method (adjusting profit/loss for non-cash items and working capital changes) – IDENTICAL choice to IAS 7, with the indirect method being the PREVALENT choice among Indian preparers, consistent with global practice.
5

Interest and Dividends

Identical flexible classification to IAS 7
Interest and dividends received/paid may be classified as operating, investing, or financing, provided CONSISTENT classification is applied period to period – IDENTICAL to IAS 7, with the SAME common practice of classifying interest/dividends paid as financing and interest/dividends received as investing for non-financial entities.
6

Reconciliation of Financing Liabilities

Identical to IAS 7
A reconciliation between opening and closing balances of liabilities arising from FINANCING activities (including both cash and non-cash changes, e.g. foreign exchange, fair value changes) is required – IDENTICAL to IAS 7's own amendment requirement.
7

Indian Working Capital & NBFC Context

A practically prominent Indian application area
Indian companies often face LONGER working capital cycles (extended receivable/payable terms common in Indian B2B trade), making operating cash flow analysis under Ind AS 7 particularly significant for lenders and rating agencies (e.g. CRISIL, ICRA) assessing credit risk. For Indian NBFCs specifically, RBI's regulatory reporting also draws on cash flow information, operating ALONGSIDE (not replacing) Ind AS 7's own statement.
8

Foreign Currency Cash Flows

Identical to IAS 7
Foreign currency cash flows are translated using the exchange rate at the DATE of the cash flow (or a suitable weighted average rate) – IDENTICAL to IAS 7, with unrealised exchange differences on cash/cash equivalents presented separately, reconciling opening/closing cash balances.
