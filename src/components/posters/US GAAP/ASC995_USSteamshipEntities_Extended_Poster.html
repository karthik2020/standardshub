Conceptual Framework Linkage

A narrow accounting consequence of a specific US maritime tax policy
ASC 995 is one of the NARROWEST, most niche Topics in the entire Codification — it addresses the accounting consequence of a SPECIFIC US federal maritime policy tool: the "CAPITAL CONSTRUCTION FUND" (CCF), a tax-advantaged savings mechanism Congress created to encourage US-flag vessel operators to set aside funds for building, acquiring, or reconstructing vessels. ASC 995's substantive content is essentially a SINGLE, specific DEFERRED TAX exception tied to this program.
2

Scope

US-flag steamship and vessel operating entities
ASC 995 applies to US steamship (vessel operating) entities that participate in the Capital Construction Fund program under the Merchant Marine Act.
3

The Capital Construction Fund (CCF) — Merchant Marine Act Section 607

A statutory savings program encouraging US vessel construction
Under SECTION 607 of the Merchant Marine Act, qualifying US-flag vessel operators may DEPOSIT eligible earnings into a "CAPITAL CONSTRUCTION FUND" (CCF) — a special reserve intended to accumulate resources for the future CONSTRUCTION, ACQUISITION, or RECONSTRUCTION of vessels — a statutory policy tool designed to encourage continued investment in the US-flag merchant marine fleet.
4

Deferred Tax Exception for CCF Deposits

A specific exception to normal deferred tax recognition
Amounts DEPOSITED into a CCF, and used for QUALIFYING vessel construction/reconstruction purposes, receive favourable TAX deferral treatment under the Merchant Marine Act itself — and ASC 995 confirms that a DEFERRED TAX LIABILITY need NOT be recognised for this SPECIFIC, statutorily-sanctioned deferral, provided the CCF deposits and their USE continue to meet the qualifying statutory conditions — a narrow, SPECIFIC exception layered on top of ASC 740's GENERAL deferred tax recognition principles (see the companion ASC 740 poster), tailored to this ONE specific maritime tax policy mechanism.
5

Worked Example — CCF Deposit and Deferred Tax Treatment

A narrow, specific deferred tax exception in action
Example: A shipping company's CCF deposit
A US-flag shipping company deposits $10,000,000 of qualifying earnings into its Capital Construction Fund, intending to use the funds toward constructing a new vessel within the required statutory timeframe.
Under NORMAL ASC 740 principles, a temporary difference of this kind might otherwise require deferred tax liability recognition. However, under ASC 995's SPECIFIC CCF exception, NO deferred tax liability is recognised for this $10,000,000 deposit, PROVIDED it continues to meet the Merchant Marine Act's qualifying conditions for use toward approved vessel construction purposes.

No Direct IFRS Equivalent — Discussion

A genuinely US-specific maritime policy construct
IFRS has NO equivalent standard or exception paralleling ASC 995 — the CAPITAL CONSTRUCTION FUND is a construct SPECIFIC to US federal maritime policy under the Merchant Marine Act, without a directly analogous mechanism in most other jurisdictions' tax or accounting frameworks. An IFRS reporter would simply apply GENERAL IAS 12 deferred tax principles, WITHOUT any comparable specific exception for a vessel construction savings program. This makes ASC 995 one of the NARROWEST, most genuinely US-specific Topics in the entire Codification, similar in character to ASC 808 (Collaborative Arrangements), ASC 852 (Reorganizations), and ASC 912 (Contractors—Federal Government).
