Ind AS 20 — Accounting for Government Grants and Disclosure of Government Assistance
Extended reference poster · Converged with IAS 20 · A genuine presentation divergence — the deduction method is PROHIBITED · Worked examples
🇮🇳 IND AS — CONVERGED WITH IAS 20, WITH A GENUINE PRESENTATION DIVERGENCE — IAS 20 permits grants related to assets to be presented EITHER as deferred income OR as a deduction from the asset's carrying amount. Ind AS 20 REMOVES the deduction option — grants related to assets MUST be presented as DEFERRED INCOME only. Section 11 covers the full comparison.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Same income-matching philosophy as IAS 20, narrower presentation choice
Ind AS 20 shares IAS 20's core principle — government grants are recognised in P&L on a SYSTEMATIC basis over the periods in which the entity recognises related costs, matching grants to the expenses/assets they are intended to compensate. Where Ind AS 20 genuinely diverges is PRESENTATION: it REMOVES IAS 20's option to deduct asset-related grants from the asset's carrying amount, mandating DEFERRED INCOME presentation only.
HOW IND AS 20 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Comparability — mandating a SINGLE presentation approach (deferred income) is considered to enhance comparability WITHIN India, even though it narrows IAS 20's own flexibilityTransparency — presenting the GROSS asset cost (rather than a net-of-grant amount) is considered by Indian standard-setters to provide clearer information about the asset's full cost
This is one of the FEW Ind AS standards with a genuine, explicit presentation-level divergence from its IFRS counterpart, comparable in character to Ind AS 1's format divergence.
Grant recognition — identical timing principle to IAS 20
Grants recognised in P&L systematically over the periods matching the related costs they compensate
ℹ️ IDENTICAL recognition TIMING principle to IAS 20 — the divergence is confined to PRESENTATION of asset-related grants.
2

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 20 covers
Ind AS 20 applies to accounting for and disclosure of government grants, and disclosure of other forms of government assistance — IDENTICAL scope to IAS 20, EXCLUDING government participation in ownership (covered by Ind AS 109/32) and grants covered by Ind AS 41's fair value model.
Key definitions you must know
Government grants
IDENTICAL definition to IAS 20 — assistance in the form of transfers of resources in return for past/future compliance with conditions.
Deferred income presentation
Presenting an asset-related grant as a SEPARATE liability line item, released to P&L over the asset's useful life — the ONLY presentation permitted under Ind AS 20.
3

Recognition Criteria

Identical to IAS 20
Grants are recognised only when there is REASONABLE ASSURANCE the entity will COMPLY with attached conditions and the grant will be RECEIVED — IDENTICAL recognition criteria to IAS 20.
4

Grants Related to Income

Identical to IAS 20
Grants related to income are presented EITHER as a separate credit in P&L, OR deducted from the related expense — IDENTICAL choice preserved under Ind AS 20 (this choice is NOT removed — only the asset-related grant deduction option is removed).
5

Grants Related to Assets — Presentation (with worked example)

The core divergence point, illustrated
Example 1: Deferred income vs deduction method comparison
An Indian manufacturing company purchases machinery for ₹1,00,00,000 and receives a government capital subsidy of ₹20,00,000 under a state industrial promotion scheme, with the machinery having a 10-year useful life.
Under IAS 20's DEDUCTION method (NOT available under Ind AS 20): the asset would be recognised at ₹80,00,000 net, with ₹8,00,000 annual depreciation.
Under Ind AS 20's MANDATORY DEFERRED INCOME method: the asset is recognised at the FULL ₹1,00,00,000, with ₹10,00,000 annual depreciation, while the ₹20,00,000 grant is recognised as DEFERRED INCOME (a liability), released to P&L as ₹2,00,000 income per year over 10 years.
The NET P&L IMPACT is IDENTICAL either way (₹8,00,000 net annual charge), but the GROSS PRESENTATION of the asset and the grant differs materially — illustrating Ind AS 20's mandatory presentation approach.
6

Non-Monetary Grants

Identical to IAS 20
Non-monetary grants (e.g. land, or other resources transferred at below-market terms) are usually recorded at FAIR VALUE — IDENTICAL to IAS 20, common for Indian state industrial land allotment schemes.
7

The Mandatory Deferred Income Presentation — Indian Divergence

A genuinely important, well-documented Ind AS 20 divergence
Unlike IAS 20, which offers preparers a CHOICE between deferred income presentation and netting the grant against the asset's carrying amount, Ind AS 20 removed this choice ENTIRELY for asset-related grants — the DEFERRED INCOME approach is the ONLY permitted presentation. This means Indian companies applying Ind AS 20 will ALWAYS show the GROSS cost of the subsidised asset on the balance sheet, with the grant appearing as a SEPARATE deferred income liability, unlike some IFRS preparers globally who might net the two together.
8

Repayment of Government Grants

Identical to IAS 20
A grant becoming repayable is accounted for as a CHANGE IN ESTIMATE (per Ind AS 8) — first against any remaining deferred income balance, with any excess recognised immediately in P&L — IDENTICAL to IAS 20.
