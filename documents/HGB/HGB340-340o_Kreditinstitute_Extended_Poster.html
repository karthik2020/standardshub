Conceptual Framework Linkage

Where the general corporate framework meets a genuinely different balance sheet reality
Everything this series has documented about Bilanz/GuV structure, measurement, and disclosure assumes an ordinary trading or manufacturing enterprise. A bank's own balance sheet — dominated by financial instruments, deposits, interbank lending, and trading positions — simply doesn't fit that mould. §§340–340o exist precisely because §330's own Formblatt-delegation power (established many posters ago) recognised this: specialised industries need genuinely tailored presentation, not a forced application of the general format.

Scope — Four Distinct Institution Types

Genuinely broader than "banks" alone
§340(1), (4), (4a), and (5) confirm this Subsection applies to FOUR distinct institution types: Kreditinstitute (credit institutions, per the Kreditwesengesetz's own definition), Finanzdienstleistungsinstitute (financial services institutions), Wertpapierinstitute (investment firms), and, per the Subsection's own current title, Zahlungsinstitute and E-Geld-Institute (payment institutions and e-money institutions) — a genuinely broader population than "banks" in the everyday sense, reflecting the modern financial-services landscape's own increasing diversity beyond traditional deposit-taking banks.

§340c — the Handelsbestand Netting Mechanism

A single net figure for trading activity, rather than gross income and expense lines
§340c(1) requires income or expense from the Handelsbestand (trading book) to be shown as a SINGLE NET DIFFERENCE figure — combining all income and expenses from financial-instrument and precious-metal trading transactions, together with related write-up income and write-down expense, and provisions for threatened losses on such transactions. §340c(2) permits a SIMILAR netting of write-downs and write-ups on Beteiligungen, shares in affiliated undertakings, and securities treated as fixed assets. This netting approach is a genuinely distinctive departure from the general prohibition on offsetting income/expense line items this series established for ordinary corporations.

Worked Example — Netting Trading Book Income and Expense

One net line, rather than a lengthy grossed-up presentation
Example 1: A bank's trading activity for the year
A bank's trading book generates €12,400,000 of gains on financial instrument trading, €9,100,000 of losses on other trading positions, €800,000 of write-up income, and €600,000 of write-down expense, plus a €200,000 provision for a threatened trading loss.
Under §340c(1), the GuV shows a SINGLE net trading-book result: €12,400,000 − €9,100,000 + €800,000 − €600,000 − €200,000 = €3,300,000 — one net figure, rather than five separate gross line items an ordinary corporate GuV structure would otherwise require under the general offsetting prohibition established much earlier in this series.

§340d — Fristengliederung (Maturity Banding), Anhang-Only

A structured maturity breakdown, confined to the notes rather than the face of the Bilanz
§340d requires receivables and liabilities to be broken down by REMAINING TERM (Restlaufzeit) — a structured maturity-banding disclosure introduced by the EU's own Bank Accounts Directive, following the same broader pattern as the general Bilanzrichtlinie and Konzernbilanzrichtlinie. Critically, these figures are disclosed EXCLUSIVELY in the Anhang, not on the face of the Bilanz itself — giving readers visibility into a bank's own asset/liability maturity profile (a genuinely central concern for assessing liquidity risk) without cluttering the primary balance sheet presentation.
6

Worked Example — Maturity Band Disclosure

Giving readers a genuine liquidity-risk picture, in the notes
Example 2: Disclosing a bank's own loan maturity profile
A bank's loan portfolio includes €40,000,000 maturing within 3 months, €65,000,000 maturing between 3 months and 1 year, and €120,000,000 maturing beyond 1 year.
The Bilanz itself shows only the AGGREGATE €225,000,000 receivables figure — the FULL maturity breakdown appears exclusively in the Anhang under §340d, giving readers genuine insight into the bank's own liquidity structure without expanding the primary balance sheet presentation itself.

§340e — Dual Measurement, Echoing §253 With Banking-Specific Detail

The same Anlage/Umlaufvermögen dual approach this series established early on, applied to banking assets
§340e(1) requires credit institutions to measure Beteiligungen (including shares in affiliated undertakings), business licences, industrial property rights, and similar assets according to their DEDICATION (Zweckbestimmung) — following either the fixed-asset measurement principles of §253(3), established in the very early Measurement poster, OR the current-asset principles of §253(4), depending on whether the asset is genuinely intended for permanent use in the business. This is conceptually the SAME dual-classification logic §247's own Anlage/Umlaufvermögen distinction established at the very start of this series, now applied with banking-specific detail to the kinds of assets a credit institution's balance sheet actually holds.
8

§340f–340g — the Distinctive Prudential Reserve for General Banking Risks

A genuinely bank-specific, quantified prudential mechanism with no ordinary-corporate equivalent
§340f permits credit institutions to make PRECAUTIONARY value adjustments (Vorsorge) on certain receivables and securities beyond what strict measurement rules alone would require, reflecting the PARTICULAR risks inherent in banking business — a genuinely distinctive, banking-specific prudence mechanism. §340g then requires a special reserve — the "Fonds für allgemeine Bankrisiken" (fund for general banking risks) — to be allocated EACH YEAR an amount of AT LEAST 10% of the net income from the Handelsbestand (the SAME trading-book result established in §340c above), shown as its OWN separate Bilanz item. This reserve may only be released under specific conditions, and any release must be disclosed and explained in the Anhang.

Worked Example — the Mandatory 10% Trading-Income Allocation

A quantified, mandatory reserve — not merely a discretionary buffer
Example 3: Calculating the mandatory Fonds für allgemeine Bankrisiken allocation
Continuing Example 1: the bank's net Handelsbestand result for the year was €3,300,000.
Under §340g(4), the bank must allocate AT LEAST 10% of this net trading result — €330,000 — to the "Fonds für allgemeine Bankrisiken" special reserve, shown as its OWN distinct Bilanz item, EVERY year, regardless of the bank's own overall profitability — a mandatory, quantified prudential mechanism with genuinely no direct parallel anywhere else in this poster series' documentation of ordinary corporate accounting.
10

§340i — Credit-Institution-Specific Konzernabschluss Adaptations

The same consolidation framework, with banking-specific measurement carried through
§340i requires credit institutions to prepare a Konzernabschluss and Konzernlagebericht largely per the general §§290 et seq. framework this series has documented extensively — but with the SAME banking-specific measurement and presentation adaptations (trading book netting, maturity banding, the general banking risk reserve) carried through at the consolidated level too, ensuring the group accounts reflect the same specialised banking reality as the individual accounts.
11

§340k — Prüfungsvorschriften: Where Bank and Cooperative Audit Models Meet

A genuinely interesting overlap with the previous poster's own cooperative-audit model
§340k(2) provides a distinctive rule: where the credit institution is ITSELF organised as a Genossenschaft (cooperative) or a rechtsfähiger wirtschaftlicher Verein (registered economic association), the audit is performed — DEPARTING from the ordinary §319(1) sentence 1 corporate rule established in the earlier Audit poster — by the SAME Prüfungsverband (cooperative auditing association) the institution belongs to as a member, established in the previous poster's own documentation of cooperative accounting. This is CONDITIONAL, however: more than HALF of the Prüfungsverband's own managing board members must themselves be Wirtschaftsprüfer (chartered accountants) — or, where the Prüfungsverband has only two board members, at least ONE of them must be.

Worked Example — a Cooperative Bank's Audit

Two specialised frameworks converging on the same institution
Example 4: A Volksbank's own audit arrangement
A Volksbank — a credit institution organised as a registered cooperative, per the earlier Cooperatives poster — belongs to a regional Prüfungsverband whose own managing board has four members, three of whom are qualified Wirtschaftsprüfer.
Because MORE than half (three of four) of the Prüfungsverband's own board members are chartered accountants, the Volksbank's statutory audit is performed by ITS OWN Prüfungsverband under §340k(2) — genuinely combining the cooperative-specific Verbandsprüfung model established in the previous poster with the credit-institution-specific accounting framework this poster documents, rather than requiring the bank to separately engage an ordinary, freely-chosen Abschlussprüfer under §318.
13

§340l — Offenlegungsvorschriften, Including Cross-Border EU/EEA Disclosure

A genuinely wider disclosure footprint than an ordinary domestic corporation faces
§340l(1) requires credit institutions to disclose their Jahresabschluss, Lagebericht, Konzernabschluss, and Konzernlagebericht per the general §325 framework established earlier in this series. UNIQUELY, however, credit institutions that are NOT themselves branches must ALSO disclose these SAME documents in EVERY OTHER EU/EEA member state where they have established a branch — with that FOREIGN disclosure governed by the LAW of the relevant host state, not by HGB itself — a genuinely wider cross-border disclosure footprint than an ordinary domestic corporation, reflecting banks' own characteristically cross-border branch networks.

§340m–340n — Extending Criminal Provisions to Non-Corporate-Form Institutions

The same extension logic as §335b, now for a genuinely different population
Confirmed content extends the §§331–333 criminal provisions established many posters ago ALSO to credit institutions, financial services institutions, investment firms, and other §340-covered institutions that are NOT organised in the legal form of a Kapitalgesellschaft — directly paralleling the SAME "nulla poena sine lege certa" logic that required §335b's own separate extension to certain partnerships in the previous cluster of posters. §331 itself is further extended to cover breaches of duty by [specific further categories the source material did not fully detail] — reflecting the SAME care this series has consistently observed when extending criminal liability to genuinely new populations.

Relationship to IFRS (IFRS 9)

A major divergence point — expected-credit-loss impairment versus HGB's own dual measurement approach
This Subsection represents one of the MOST significant IFRS divergence areas this poster series has touched: IFRS 9's own EXPECTED CREDIT LOSS impairment model for financial instruments — requiring forward-looking loss estimation from initial recognition — differs substantially from HGB's own §340e dual-measurement, incurred-loss-oriented approach, itself rooted in the SAME §253(3)/(4) fixed/current-asset distinction established very early in this series. The §340f/§340g prudential reserve mechanism, similarly, has NO direct IFRS equivalent — IFRS's own approach relies on its expected-credit-loss provisioning model rather than a separate, quantified general-risk reserve sitting alongside ordinary loss provisions.
