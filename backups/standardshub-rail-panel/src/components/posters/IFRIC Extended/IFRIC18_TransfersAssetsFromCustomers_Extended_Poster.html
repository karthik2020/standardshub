Conceptual Framework Linkage (Historical)

A multi-element deliverable analysis, later generalised into IFRS 15
IFRIC 18 required entities that RECEIVED an asset (or cash to construct one) from a customer — typically in exchange for connecting the customer to a network or providing ongoing access — to (1) recognise the asset if it met the IAS 16 definition, (2) IDENTIFY the separate deliverables owed to the customer (e.g. connection service, ongoing access), and (3) recognise revenue for EACH deliverable as it was satisfied.
WHY THIS BECAME PART OF IFRS 15'S GENERAL MODEL
Multi-element deliverable identification — IFRIC 18's approach to separating connection services from ongoing access rights DIRECTLY foreshadowed IFRS 15's own "distinct performance obligation" identificationNon-cash consideration recognition — IFRIC 18's treatment of a TRANSFERRED ASSET as consideration for future services anticipated IFRS 15's own broader "non-cash consideration" measurement principles
Alongside IFRIC 13, IFRIC 18 is regarded as one of the interpretations whose multi-element logic MOST DIRECTLY shaped IFRS 15's eventual comprehensive revenue model.
The three-step historical analysis IFRIC 18 required
StepAction
1Determine if the transferred item meets the IAS 16 definition of an asset
2Identify the separate deliverables (e.g. connection, ongoing access)
3Recognise revenue for each deliverable as it is satisfied

Scope & Applicability (Historical)

What IFRIC 18 covered
IFRIC 18 applied to agreements where entities received PPE (or cash to construct one) from customers, requiring the entity to use that item to CONNECT the customer to a network or provide ongoing ACCESS — historically central to utility (electricity, water, gas, telecom) sector revenue recognition.
Key definitions (historical)
Transfer of assets from customers
A customer providing an item of PPE (or cash for its construction) as part of obtaining connection/ongoing access to a network.
Connection service
The service of physically connecting the customer to a network, historically ONE of the separate deliverables identified under IFRIC 18.
3

Does the Transferred Item Meet the Definition of an Asset? (Historical)

The gateway question
If the transferred item met the IAS 16 asset definition (probable future economic benefits, reliable cost measurement), it was recognised at FAIR VALUE, with a CORRESPONDING credit representing consideration for identified deliverables — a recognition mechanism CONCEPTUALLY related to IFRS 15's own non-cash consideration measurement.
4

Identifying the Separate Deliverables (Historical, with worked example)

Historical illustration
Example 1: A utility connection arrangement
A power distribution utility received a transformer (fair value $150,000) from a large industrial customer, in exchange for connecting the customer to the grid and supplying ongoing electricity access for a MINIMUM 10-year period.
Under IFRIC 18, the utility recognised the transformer as PPE at $150,000, IDENTIFYING two separate deliverables: the CONNECTION service (revenue recognised immediately upon connection) and ONGOING ACCESS (revenue recognised over the 10-year period) — an allocation methodology that IFRS 15's current five-step model would reach a SIMILAR outcome for, reframed as identifying distinct performance obligations and allocating the transaction price (here, the transformer's fair value) between them.
5

Recognising Revenue for Connection Services (Historical)

Typically a point-in-time deliverable
Where connection was identified as a SEPARATE deliverable satisfied at a single point in time, revenue was recognised IMMEDIATELY upon connection — a treatment PRESERVED conceptually within IFRS 15's own point-in-time performance obligation satisfaction principles.
6

Recognising Revenue for Ongoing Access (Historical)

Recognised over the service period
Where ONGOING ACCESS was identified as a separate deliverable, revenue was recognised over the PERIOD during which the entity was obligated to provide that access (often the useful life of the transferred asset, or a specified contractual period) — a treatment PRESERVED conceptually within IFRS 15's own over-time performance obligation satisfaction principles.
7

How This Guidance Became Part of IFRS 15

A generalisation into the comprehensive revenue model
When the IASB developed IFRS 15 as a single, comprehensive revenue standard, IFRIC 18's multi-element deliverable identification and non-cash consideration measurement approach was RECOGNISED as directly consistent with (and GENERALISABLE into) the standard's own five-step model. IFRIC 18 was formally WITHDRAWN upon IFRS 15 becoming effective, with utility connection/asset transfer arrangements now analysed under IFRS 15's own general performance obligation and non-cash consideration framework, rather than a dedicated interpretation.
8

Common Application Sectors

Where this analysis remains genuinely relevant
Power distribution, gas distribution networks, water utilities, and telecommunications infrastructure providers FREQUENTLY receive customer contributions (equipment or cash) toward connection infrastructure, making the analytical approach IFRIC 18 pioneered GENUINELY relevant today — now analysed under IFRS 15's own performance obligation identification and non-cash consideration measurement principles.
