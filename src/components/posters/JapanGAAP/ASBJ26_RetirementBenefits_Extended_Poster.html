Conceptual Framework Linkage

The comprehensive destination of a decades-long staged reform
ASBJ Statement No. 26 is the CURRENT, COMPREHENSIVE J-GAAP standard for retirement benefit accounting – the CULMINATION of the staged reform process traced through Statements No. 3, No. 14 (both now ABOLISHED), and No. 19 (still integrated, see companion posters). It governs the RECOGNITION and MEASUREMENT of an entity's retirement benefit OBLIGATION, PLAN ASSETS, and the resulting NET liability (or asset) recognised on the Balance Sheet.
2

Scope – Including Japan's Distinctive Lump-Sum Retirement Allowances

Both pension arrangements and Japan's traditional lump-sum system
This Statement applies to companies sponsoring RETIREMENT BENEFIT plans – INCLUDING both Western-style DEFINED BENEFIT PENSION arrangements AND Japan's DISTINCTIVELY COMMON "LUMP-SUM RETIREMENT ALLOWANCE" (退職一時金) system – a SINGLE lump-sum payment at retirement, typically based on FINAL salary and YEARS of service – which is accounted for under the SAME defined benefit framework as a formal pension plan, requiring FULL actuarial valuation.
3

The Retirement Benefit Obligation

The actuarial present value of benefits attributable to service to date
The RETIREMENT BENEFIT OBLIGATION (退職給付債務) is the ACTUARIAL PRESENT VALUE of retirement benefits ATTRIBUTABLE to employee SERVICE rendered UP TO the reporting date – reflecting assumptions about FUTURE salary increases, employee TURNOVER, mortality, and the DISCOUNT rate (Section 6) – a forward-looking, ACTUARIALLY determined figure, NOT simply the amount that would be payable if all employees resigned TODAY.
4

Plan Assets and the Net Recognised Liability/Asset

Netting the obligation against any funded plan assets
Where an entity FUNDS its retirement benefit obligation through a SEPARATE pension trust or fund, PLAN ASSETS are measured at FAIR VALUE. The Balance Sheet recognises a SINGLE NET figure: the retirement benefit OBLIGATION LESS plan assets – a NET LIABILITY where the obligation EXCEEDS plan assets (the typical case), or a NET ASSET in the LESS common case where plan assets EXCEED the obligation.
5

Components of Retirement Benefit Cost

Service cost, interest cost, and expected return on plan assets
ComponentDescription
Service costThe increase in the obligation attributable to employee service rendered DURING the current period
Interest costThe increase in the obligation reflecting the passage of time (unwinding the discount)
Expected return on plan assetsThe anticipated investment return on funded plan assets, offsetting the above costs
6

Discount Rate Determination

High-quality corporate bond yields matching the obligation's duration
The DISCOUNT RATE used to determine the present value of the retirement benefit obligation is based on the YIELD of HIGH-QUALITY corporate bonds (or, in some cases, government bonds), with a DURATION broadly MATCHING the estimated TIMING of future benefit payments – reflecting the TIME VALUE of money appropriate to the obligation's OWN expected payment profile.
7

The Consolidated/Individual Divergence – Recap From Statement No. 19

A live, current feature of today's standard
As established through Statement No. 19 (see the companion poster), ACTUARIAL gains/losses and PRIOR SERVICE COST are recognised DIFFERENTLY depending on WHICH set of financial statements is being prepared: IMMEDIATE recognition within Net Assets (later recycled) for CONSOLIDATED purposes, but GRADUAL amortisation through profit or loss for INDIVIDUAL (non-consolidated) purposes – a distinctive, STILL-CURRENT feature of this comprehensive standard.
8

The Simplified Method for Smaller Enterprises

A formula-based alternative to full actuarial valuation
For SMALLER enterprises (generally those below a specified EMPLOYEE count threshold), this Statement permits a "SIMPLIFIED METHOD" – estimating the retirement benefit obligation using a FORMULA-BASED approach (e.g. the amount that would be PAYABLE if all eligible employees VOLUNTARILY resigned at the reporting date, adjusted by a standard FACTOR) RATHER than requiring a FULL, individualised actuarial valuation – a genuine, SIZE-BASED practical expedient recognising that full actuarial valuations can be DISPROPORTIONATELY costly for smaller employers.
9

Worked Example – Calculating the Net Recognised Liability

Netting the obligation against plan assets
Example: A funded pension arrangement
A company's retirement benefit obligation is ¥3,200,000,000. Plan assets held in a separate pension trust have a fair value of ¥2,500,000,000.
The company recognises a NET RETIREMENT BENEFIT LIABILITY of ¥3,200,000,000 − ¥2,500,000,000 = ¥700,000,000 on the Balance Sheet – a SINGLE net figure, rather than presenting the obligation and plan assets as separate gross line items.
10

Worked Example – the Simplified Method in Practice

A formula-based estimate for a small employer
Example: A small company's lump-sum retirement allowance liability
A small company with 15 employees estimates that, if all eligible employees voluntarily resigned today, it would owe ¥120,000,000 in lump-sum retirement allowances. It applies a standard adjustment factor of 0.9 under the simplified method.
Estimated retirement benefit obligation = ¥120,000,000 × 0.9 = ¥108,000,000 – a REASONABLE APPROXIMATION obtained WITHOUT the cost of a FULL, individualised actuarial valuation, appropriate given the company's SMALL size.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Lump-sum retirement allowance (退職一時金)
A single lump-sum payment made at employee retirement, typically based on final salary and years of service – a distinctively common Japanese retirement benefit arrangement.
Simplified method
A formula-based estimation approach for the retirement benefit obligation, available to smaller enterprises in place of a full actuarial valuation.
