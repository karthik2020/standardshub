Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why acquisitions are measured at fair value, not historical cost
The Conceptual Framework's asset/liability definitions require FAITHFUL REPRESENTATION of what an entity actually controls after a business combination. IFRS 3's acquisition method achieves this by recognising the acquiree's identifiable assets and liabilities at their FAIR VALUE at the acquisition date – a genuinely NEW measurement basis for the combined entity, distinct from the acquiree's own historical carrying amounts.
HOW IFRS 3 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Fresh-start measurement – the ACQUIRER'S financial statements reflect what it PAID and ACQUIRED at fair value, not the acquiree's own historical cost basis, since a genuinely new economic transaction has occurred Goodwill as a residual – goodwill represents future economic benefits from assets NOT individually identifiable/separately recognisable (e.g. synergies, workforce), captured as the RESIDUAL after all identifiable items are fair-valued Control as the recognition trigger – the entire acquisition method is triggered by the ACQUIRER obtaining CONTROL, directly invoking the Framework's control-based definition of a reporting entity's boundary
IFRS 3's acquisition method is one of the clearest applications of the Framework's "faithful representation over historical continuity" principle – a business combination is treated as a genuinely NEW transaction, not merely a combination of two sets of pre-existing book values.
Measurement principles applied by IFRS 3
IFRS 3 applies fair value measurement almost universally at the acquisition date – one of the most comprehensive applications of fair value anywhere in IFRS.
Goodwill = Consideration transferred + Fair value of any NCI + Fair value of previously held equity interest (if any) − Fair value of net identifiable assets acquired
ℹ️ Qualitative characteristics in action: measuring virtually EVERYTHING at fair value on the SAME date reflects comparability – a consistent measurement basis across the entire transaction, rather than a patchwork of historical costs from different original acquisition dates.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRS 3 covers
IFRS 3 applies to a transaction or other event in which an acquirer obtains CONTROL of one or more BUSINESSES – the definition of "business" itself is critical, since IFRS 3 applies ONLY to acquisitions meeting this threshold.
SCOPE EXCLUSIONS
Formation of a JOINT ARRANGEMENT (IFRS 11) in the financial statements of the joint arrangement itself Acquisition of an asset or group of assets that does NOT constitute a business (an ordinary ASSET acquisition, accounted for by allocating cost to individual assets/liabilities based on relative fair values – NO goodwill arises) Combinations of entities under COMMON CONTROL (e.g. group reorganisations) – outside IFRS 3's scope, though the IASB has published separate guidance
⚠️ Whether a transaction is a "business" or merely an "asset acquisition" is one of the most consequential judgements in the whole standard – a business combination applies the fair value acquisition method and can generate goodwill; an asset acquisition allocates cost proportionately and NEVER generates goodwill.
Key definitions you must know
Business combination
A transaction or other event in which an acquirer obtains control of one or more businesses.
Business
An integrated set of activities and assets that is capable of being conducted and managed to provide a return to investors/other owners, members, or participants – assessed via the "outputs" test and "substantive process" screening test.
Acquisition date
The date the acquirer obtains CONTROL of the acquiree – normally the CLOSING date, though it can be earlier or later if control is obtained on a different date by agreement.
Non-controlling interest (NCI)
The equity in a subsidiary NOT attributable, directly or indirectly, to a parent.

Identifying the Acquirer & Acquisition Date

Determining WHO is the acquirer, and WHEN
For EVERY business combination, one of the combining entities must be identified as the ACQUIRER – using IFRS 10's control guidance, and, if unclear, additional indicators (relative size, which entity issues equity, which party initiated the combination, which entity's former management dominates the combined entity).
REVERSE ACQUISITIONS
In a "reverse acquisition," the LEGAL acquirer (e.g. the entity issuing shares) may be identified as the ACCOUNTING ACQUIREE, and vice versa, if the SUBSTANCE of the transaction indicates the legally acquired entity's former owners/management actually control the COMBINED entity – a classic example is a private company "acquiring" a smaller public shell company to obtain a stock exchange listing.
4

The Acquisition Method – Four Steps

The mandatory sequence
Step 1 – Identify the ACQUIRER Step 2 – Determine the ACQUISITION DATE Step 3 – Recognise and measure the IDENTIFIABLE assets acquired, liabilities assumed, and any NCI in the acquiree Step 4 – Recognise and measure GOODWILL or a gain from a BARGAIN PURCHASE
Every business combination is accounted for by applying this SAME four-step sequence – a genuinely consistent, universal method REGARDLESS of the specific structure or industry involved.
5

Recognising Identifiable Assets & Liabilities (with worked example)

Recognition and measurement principles
Identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed are measured at their ACQUISITION-DATE FAIR VALUES
To qualify for RECOGNITION, an item must meet the Framework's asset/liability definitions at the acquisition date – the acquirer's INTENTIONS for future use are NOT relevant to recognition, only whether a genuine present asset/liability exists.
⚠️ A CONTINGENT LIABILITY of the acquiree is RECOGNISED at fair value at the acquisition date, EVEN IF it would not otherwise meet IAS 37's normal "probable" recognition threshold – a specific IFRS 3 override, since the acquirer has effectively PAID for it as part of the purchase price.
Worked example – intangibles not on the acquiree's own books
Example 1: Customer relationships recognised only on acquisition
A target company has never recognised its customer relationships as an intangible asset (since internally generated customer relationships are prohibited from recognition under IAS 38). On acquisition, the acquirer's valuation specialists determine these customer relationships have an acquisition-date fair value of $2,400,000.
The $2,400,000 customer relationship intangible IS recognised SEPARATELY in the consolidated accounts at fair value, even though it NEVER appeared on the acquiree's own standalone balance sheet – this is a classic example of IFRS 3 recognising items that IAS 38's internal-generation prohibition would otherwise exclude.

Consideration Transferred (with worked example)

Fair value of everything given up – including contingent consideration
Consideration transferred is measured at FAIR VALUE, calculated as the sum of the acquisition-date fair values of assets transferred, liabilities incurred, and equity instruments issued by the acquirer – PLUS the fair value of any CONTINGENT CONSIDERATION.
Example 2: Consideration with cash, shares, and contingent consideration
An acquirer pays $8,000,000 cash, issues 500,000 of its own shares (fair value $6 each), and agrees to pay an additional $1,500,000 if the acquiree achieves a specific profit target within 2 years (fair value of this contingent consideration at acquisition date, discounted for probability and time value: $900,000).
Total consideration transferred = 8,000,000 (cash) + 3,000,000 (500,000 × $6 shares) + 900,000 (contingent consideration) = $11,900,000
⚠️ Contingent consideration classified as a LIABILITY is REMEASURED each period through PROFIT OR LOSS (per IFRS 9), NOT as a subsequent adjustment to goodwill – only measurement PERIOD adjustments (Section 9) affect goodwill; changes after the measurement period ends go through SOPL.

Goodwill & Bargain Purchase Gains (with worked example)

The full goodwill formula, applied
Example 3: Calculating goodwill
Continuing Example 2: total consideration transferred was $11,900,000, for 80% of the acquiree. The acquirer elects to measure NCI at its PROPORTIONATE SHARE of net identifiable assets (Section 8). The fair value of net identifiable assets acquired is $12,000,000.
NCI at proportionate share = 12,000,000 × 20% = $2,400,000
Goodwill = 11,900,000 (consideration) + 2,400,000 (NCI) − 12,000,000 (net identifiable assets) = $2,300,000
✕ A "bargain purchase" (negative goodwill) arises RARELY, when the fair value of net identifiable assets EXCEEDS the total of consideration transferred plus NCI plus any previously held interest. Before recognising a bargain purchase GAIN in profit or loss, the acquirer MUST REASSESS whether it has correctly identified ALL assets acquired/liabilities assumed and correctly MEASURED all amounts – only after this reassessment confirms the excess is genuine is the gain recognised, since bargain purchases are inherently unusual and often signal a measurement error rather than a true economic gain.

Non-Controlling Interests – Two Measurement Options (with worked example)

The full goodwill vs partial goodwill method – a genuine choice
For EACH business combination, the acquirer elects to measure any NCI in the acquiree EITHER: (a) at FAIR VALUE ("full goodwill" method – this INCLUDES NCI's share of goodwill); OR (b) at NCI's PROPORTIONATE SHARE of the acquiree's identifiable net assets ("partial goodwill" method – goodwill relates ONLY to the parent's controlling interest).
Example 4: Comparing the two NCI measurement methods
Same facts as Example 3, but suppose the FAIR VALUE of the 20% NCI (based on quoted market price or valuation techniques) is instead determined to be $2,700,000, rather than the $2,400,000 proportionate share.
Full goodwill method (NCI at fair value): Goodwill = 11,900,000 + 2,700,000 − 12,000,000 = $2,600,000 (HIGHER – includes NCI's implied share of goodwill)
Partial goodwill method (NCI at proportionate share, from Example 3): Goodwill = $2,300,000 (LOWER – excludes NCI's share of goodwill)
The election is made SEPARATELY for EACH business combination, but MUST be applied CONSISTENTLY within that single transaction.
9

Measurement Period Adjustments

The 12-month window for retrospective adjustment
If the initial accounting for a business combination is INCOMPLETE by the end of the reporting period in which the combination occurs, the acquirer reports PROVISIONAL amounts. During the "MEASUREMENT PERIOD" (a period NOT EXCEEDING ONE YEAR from the acquisition date), the acquirer RETROSPECTIVELY adjusts the provisional amounts to reflect new information about facts/circumstances that EXISTED AT the acquisition date, had they been known at that date – with a CORRESPONDING adjustment to GOODWILL.
⚠️ Adjustments MADE AFTER the measurement period ends, or resulting from EVENTS occurring AFTER the acquisition date (rather than clarifying acquisition-date facts), are accounted for as CORRECTIONS OF ERRORS (IAS 8) or as ORDINARY period changes in estimate – NOT as adjustments to goodwill.

Disclosure Requirements

Required disclosures
FOR EACH MATERIAL BUSINESS COMBINATION
Name and description of the acquiree, acquisition date, percentage of voting equity acquired, and primary reasons for the combination Fair value of total consideration transferred, and the fair value of each major class of consideration Amounts recognised for each major class of assets acquired and liabilities assumed Amount of goodwill (or bargain purchase gain), and a QUALITATIVE description of the factors making up goodwill
FURTHER DISCLOSURES
NCI measurement basis chosen for each combination, and the amount recognised Revenue and profit/loss of the acquiree since the acquisition date included in the group's results (and, if practicable, what group revenue/profit would have been had the acquisition occurred at the start of the period) Details of measurement period adjustments made during the reporting period

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether an acquisition constitutes a "business" (goodwill possible) or merely an asset acquisition (no goodwill) Identifying the accounting acquirer, especially in reverse acquisitions and mergers of equals Which NCI measurement method to elect for each combination
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Fair value of identifiable intangible assets not previously recognised by the acquiree (e.g. customer relationships, brands, technology) Fair value of contingent consideration, and its probability-weighted future payment Fair value of NCI, where the fair value method is elected
⚠️ Purchase price allocation (PPA) – the process of identifying and fair-valuing every acquired asset and assumed liability – is one of the most JUDGEMENT- and specialist-VALUATION-intensive exercises in all of IFRS, frequently requiring external valuation experts for intangibles and contingent liabilities.

IFRS vs US GAAP – Key Differences

Comparison – IFRS 3 vs US GAAP (ASC 805)
FeatureIFRS (IFRS 3)US GAAP
NCI measurementCHOICE: fair value OR proportionate share of net identifiable assetsMANDATORY fair value only – no proportionate share option available
Bargain purchase gainRecognised in profit or loss after reassessmentAlso recognised in earnings, broadly converged in substance
Measurement periodUp to 1 year from acquisition dateAlso up to 1 year, largely converged
Acquisition-related costs (legal, advisory fees)EXPENSED as incurredAlso EXPENSED as incurred, converged
"Business" definitionRefined by a 2018 amendment adding a "concentration test" screening stepSimilar refinement made via ASU 2017-01, broadly converged following coordinated efforts
IFRS 3 and ASC 805 are substantially converged following coordinated joint standard-setting – the CLEAREST remaining difference is the NCI measurement choice, which IFRS uniquely offers but US GAAP does not.

Quick-reference glossary
Contingent consideration
An obligation of the acquirer to transfer additional assets/equity interests to the former owners of an acquiree, if specified future events occur or conditions are met.
Measurement period
The period after the acquisition date, not exceeding one year, during which the acquirer may retrospectively adjust provisional amounts recognised for a business combination.
Bargain purchase
A business combination where the fair value of net identifiable assets acquired exceeds the sum of consideration transferred, NCI, and any previously held equity interest – recognised as a gain, after mandatory reassessment.

SOFP Statement of financial position (balance sheet) SOPL Statement of profit or loss (income statement) OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
