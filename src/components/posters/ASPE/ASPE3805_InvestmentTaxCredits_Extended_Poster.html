Conceptual Framework Linkage

A distinctly Canadian tax-system mechanism, given dedicated treatment
ASPE Section 3805 governs the RECOGNITION, MEASUREMENT, and PRESENTATION of INVESTMENT tax CREDITS (ITCs) – REDUCTIONS in INCOME taxes OTHERWISE payable, EARNED by INCURRING specified TYPES of expenditures UNDER Canadian FEDERAL and PROVINCIAL tax LEGISLATION. GENUINELY distinctively, THIS is a DEDICATED, standalone SECTION addressing a TAX mechanism SPECIFIC to the CANADIAN tax SYSTEM, MOST commonly ASSOCIATED with the SR&ED PROGRAM (Section 4).
2

Scope

Tax credits tied to specified qualifying expenditures
This Section applies to INVESTMENT tax CREDITS – GOVERNED separately FROM the BROADER government ASSISTANCE framework IN Section 3800 (see the COMPANION poster), GIVEN their SPECIFIC, direct INTERACTION with the INCOME tax system.
3

What Is an Investment Tax Credit

A reduction in taxes otherwise payable, earned through qualifying expenditures
AN investment TAX credit is a REDUCTION in INCOME taxes OTHERWISE payable, EARNED by an ENTERPRISE incurring SPECIFIED types OF expenditures (e.g. QUALIFYING research AND development COSTS, or CERTAIN capital INVESTMENTS in SPECIFIC regions OR industries) UNDER federal OR provincial TAX legislation – a TAX-system incentive MECHANISM, RATHER than a DIRECT cash GRANT.
4

The SR&ED Program – the Most Common Source of ITCs

Canada's Scientific Research and Experimental Development incentive
THE Scientific RESEARCH and EXPERIMENTAL Development (SR&ED) PROGRAM is the MOST common SOURCE of INVESTMENT tax CREDITS for CANADIAN private ENTERPRISES – PROVIDING tax CREDITS for QUALIFYING research AND development EXPENDITURES, a GENUINELY significant INCENTIVE for TECHNOLOGY, manufacturing, AND other INNOVATION-driven private ENTERPRISES across CANADA.
5

Recognition Criteria – Reasonable Assurance

The same reasonable assurance threshold as government assistance generally
AN investment TAX credit is RECOGNIZED when THERE is reasonable ASSURANCE that the ENTERPRISE has MET, or WILL meet, the REQUIREMENTS to BE entitled TO the credit – MIRRORING the SAME "reasonable ASSURANCE" recognition THRESHOLD applied MORE broadly under SECTION 3800.
6

Presentation for ITCs Related to Depreciable Assets

The same cost-reduction / deferred-credit choice as Section 3800
WHERE an ITC RELATES to the ACQUISITION of a DEPRECIABLE asset, it MAY be DEDUCTED from the COST of the ASSET (REDUCING future DEPRECIATION), OR recorded AS a DEFERRED credit AMORTIZED to INCOME over the ASSET'S useful LIFE – the SAME genuine PRESENTATION choice AVAILABLE under Section 3800'S own GOVERNMENT assistance FRAMEWORK.
7

Presentation for ITCs Related to Current Expenditures

Reducing the related expense, or presented as income
WHERE an ITC RELATES to CURRENT expenditures (MOST commonly, SR&ED-qualifying RESEARCH and DEVELOPMENT costs), it IS generally RECOGNIZED as a REDUCTION of the RELATED expense – THOUGH presenting IT as a SEPARATE item OF income IS also GENERALLY acceptable.
8

Relationship to Section 3800's Government Assistance Framework

A specialized companion, not a wholly separate model
CONCEPTUALLY, ITCs SHARE the SAME underlying RECOGNITION philosophy AS the BROADER government ASSISTANCE framework IN Section 3800 – BOTH require REASONABLE assurance BEFORE recognition, and BOTH offer SIMILAR presentation CHOICES for ASSET-related versus EXPENSE-related amounts. Section 3805 EXISTS as its OWN dedicated SECTION primarily BECAUSE investment TAX credits INTERACT directly WITH the enterprise'S own INCOME tax CALCULATION (SECTION 3465 – see the COMPANION poster), WARRANTING specific, TAX-focused guidance BEYOND Section 3800'S more GENERAL treatment.
9

Worked Example – an SR&ED Credit on Current Expenditures

Reducing R&D expense directly
Example: A technology company's SR&ED claim
A private technology company incurs $800,000 of qualifying SR&ED research and development expenditures during the year, earning a $280,000 investment tax credit, with reasonable assurance of entitlement.
The COMPANY recognizes the $800,000 of R&D EXPENSE, REDUCED by the $280,000 ITC, FOR a NET R&D expense OF $520,000 – REFLECTING the GENUINELY significant COST offset the SR&ED program PROVIDES.
10

Worked Example – an ITC Reducing the Cost of Capital Equipment

Lower future depreciation, mirroring Section 3800's own mechanics
Example: A regional capital investment credit
A manufacturing company purchases $2,000,000 of qualifying equipment in a designated region, earning a $150,000 investment tax credit toward the purchase. The equipment has a 10-year useful life, and the company elects the cost reduction approach.
The COMPANY records the EQUIPMENT at $2,000,000 − $150,000 = $1,850,000, DEPRECIATING it AT $1,850,000 ÷ 10 = $185,000 per year – RATHER than the $200,000 per YEAR it WOULD otherwise HAVE recognized.

A Note on International Comparability

A genuinely Canada-specific mechanism, without a direct standalone counterpart
UNLIKE MOST other ASPE Sections COVERED throughout THIS poster LIBRARY, Section 3805 DOES not HAVE a DIRECTLY comparable STANDALONE counterpart WITHIN IFRS or MOST other FRAMEWORKS this LIBRARY has COVERED – OTHER jurisdictions' TAX credit REGIMES (e.g. RESEARCH and DEVELOPMENT tax INCENTIVES elsewhere) ARE typically ADDRESSED through THEIR own frameworks' GENERAL government ASSISTANCE or INCOME tax STANDARDS, RATHER than a DEDICATED, standalone SECTION. Section 3805'S existence AS its OWN Section REFLECTS the SPECIFIC, WIDESPREAD significance of THE SR&ED program TO Canadian PRIVATE enterprise PRACTICE.
GIVEN this Section'S GENUINELY jurisdiction-SPECIFIC nature, a FORMAL side-by-SIDE comparison table (AS used ELSEWHERE in this LIBRARY) is LESS meaningful HERE than FOR most OTHER ASPE Sections.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Investment tax credit (ITC)
A reduction in income taxes otherwise payable, earned by incurring specified qualifying expenditures under Canadian federal or provincial tax legislation.
SR&ED (Scientific Research and Experimental Development)
Canada's primary tax incentive program for qualifying research and development expenditures, the most common source of investment tax credits for private enterprises.
