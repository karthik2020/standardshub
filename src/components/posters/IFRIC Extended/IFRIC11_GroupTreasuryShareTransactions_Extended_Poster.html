IFRIC 11 – IFRS 2: Group and Treasury Share Transactions (WITHDRAWN)
Extended reference poster · Withdrawn 2010 · Absorbed into IFRS 2 itself via 2009 amendments · Retained for historical/comparative reference
🚫 WITHDRAWN INTERPRETATION – ABSORBED INTO IFRS 2 – IFRIC 11 clarified share-based payment accounting for TWO scenarios: (a) an entity granting rights to its OWN equity instruments where it may need to purchase them from the market (treasury shares), and (b) a PARENT (or another group entity) granting rights to its equity instruments to employees of a SUBSIDIARY. Both scenarios' guidance was WITHDRAWN and folded directly into IFRS 2's own group share-based payment requirements. This poster is retained for historical/comparative reference.

Conceptual Framework Linkage (Historical)

A group-structuring clarification, later absorbed into the standard itself
IFRIC 11 established that EQUITY-SETTLED accounting under IFRS 2 applied even where the entity granting the rights had NO obligation to settle in cash – for example, a SUBSIDIARY whose employees receive rights to the PARENT's shares was still required to account for this as an EQUITY-SETTLED transaction (with a corresponding capital contribution from the parent), rather than treating it as cash-settled simply because the subsidiary itself had no shares to issue.
WHY THIS ABSORPTION HAPPENED
Group structuring clarity – WITHOUT this clarification, entities within complex group structures might have INCONSISTENTLY classified share-based payments as equity- or cash-settled depending on which group entity legally held the sharesConceptual continuity – this group/treasury share logic was considered CORE enough to IFRS 2's own group transaction scope that the IASB folded it directly into the standard via 2009 amendments
IFRIC 11 is a genuinely INSTRUCTIVE example, alongside IFRIC 4, 8, and 9, of an interpretation whose SUBSTANCE was absorbed almost entirely into its successor standard's own text.

Scope & Applicability (Historical)

What IFRIC 11 covered
IFRIC 11 applied to share-based payment arrangements INVOLVING an entity's own equity instruments (potentially settled via market purchase) and arrangements where a PARENT or fellow subsidiary granted rights to employees of ANOTHER group entity.
Key definitions (historical)
Treasury shares
An entity's own previously issued shares, reacquired and potentially used to settle share-based payment obligations.
Group share-based payment transaction
An arrangement where one group entity grants rights to equity instruments to employees of ANOTHER group entity.
3

Part A – Treasury Share Transactions (Historical)

Equity-settled classification confirmed, regardless of settlement mechanism
IFRIC 11 confirmed that an arrangement where an entity granted rights to its OWN equity instruments was classified as EQUITY-SETTLED even if the entity chose (or was required) to purchase shares from the MARKET to satisfy the obligation, rather than issuing NEW shares – the settlement MECHANISM did not change the fundamental classification.
4

Part B – Group Share-Based Payment Transactions (Historical, with worked example)

Historical illustration
Example 1: Parent-granted options to subsidiary employees
A listed parent company granted share options over its OWN shares to employees of a wholly-owned SUBSIDIARY, with the subsidiary having NO obligation to settle the awards and no shares of its own involved.
Under IFRIC 11, the SUBSIDIARY recognised the share-based payment expense as EQUITY-SETTLED (with a CORRESPONDING increase in equity, representing a deemed capital contribution from the parent), even though the subsidiary itself had no obligation to deliver shares – an analysis that would reach the SAME conclusion under IFRS 2's current absorbed group transaction guidance today.
5

Which Entity Recognises the Expense (Historical)

The subsidiary recognises the expense; the parent recognises an investment increase
The SUBSIDIARY receiving employee services recognised the share-based payment EXPENSE in its own (standalone) financial statements, with the PARENT recognising a CORRESPONDING increase in its investment in the subsidiary – a two-sided treatment PRESERVED in IFRS 2's current group transaction guidance.
6

Cash-Settled vs Equity-Settled Classification in the Subsidiary (Historical)

Identical to the current approach
The SUBSIDIARY's own classification (equity-settled vs cash-settled in ITS standalone financial statements) depended on WHETHER it had an obligation to settle in cash to its own employees – IDENTICAL classification logic PRESERVED in IFRS 2's current group transaction framework.
7

How This Guidance Became Part of IFRS 2

A genuinely instructive absorption case
The IASB concluded IFRIC 11's group/treasury share transaction logic was FUNDAMENTAL enough to IFRS 2's overall scope that it should be embedded DIRECTLY within the standard. Via 2009 amendments to IFRS 2 (effective for periods beginning on/after 1 January 2010), IFRIC 11's substance was FOLDED INTO IFRS 2 itself, and IFRIC 11 was FORMALLY WITHDRAWN.
8

Common Historical Application Areas

Where this analysis was most frequently applied
Multinational groups whose employees at various subsidiaries participate in a GLOBAL parent's equity incentive plan are a common application context – the subsidiary must recognise its OWN share-based payment expense even though shares are ultimately issued by the foreign parent, an application governed today by IFRS 2's own absorbed group transaction guidance.
