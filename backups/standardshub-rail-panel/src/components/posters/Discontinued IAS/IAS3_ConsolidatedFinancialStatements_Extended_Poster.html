IAS 3 — Consolidated Financial Statements
Extended reference poster · Historical/withdrawn standard · Origin of group accounting principles · Mapping to IAS 27/28 and IFRS 10/11/12
⚠️ WITHDRAWN STANDARD — IAS 3 (issued 1976) was the IASC's original consolidation standard. It was effectively SUPERSEDED in 1989 by the SPLIT into IAS 27 Consolidated and Separate Financial Statements and IAS 28 Investments in Associates, which were themselves later superseded by IFRS 10, IFRS 11, IFRS 12, and the revised IAS 28 (2011-2013). IAS 3 is retained here purely for historical context — for current group accounting, always apply IFRS 10/11/12 and IAS 27/28.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

The first attempt to define the "group" reporting entity
Even in 1976, the core Framework question was the same as today: what does a PARENT genuinely CONTROL, and should the group's financial statements reflect that combined economic entity rather than each legal entity separately? IAS 3 was the IASC's FIRST attempt to codify this — establishing that a parent with a MAJORITY of voting power (or otherwise exercising control) should consolidate its subsidiaries' financial statements LINE BY LINE.
HOW IAS 3 MAPPED TO THE FRAMEWORK
Substance over form — even in its earliest form, the standard recognised that a group of LEGALLY separate entities under common control represented a single ECONOMIC entity for reporting purposes Faithful representation — full consolidation (rather than reporting only the parent's own legal-entity accounts) was seen as necessary to fairly present the group's combined resources and obligations An early, voting-rights-centric control test — IAS 3's control definition relied heavily on legal/voting majority, a LIMITATION that decades of later standards (culminating in IFRS 10's power/returns/linkage model) progressively refined
IAS 3's basic consolidation MECHANICS — combining assets, liabilities, income, expenses line by line, eliminating intragroup transactions — have remained essentially UNCHANGED in substance through every subsequent iteration (IAS 27, then IFRS 10) even as the CONTROL definition itself has been repeatedly refined.
The evolution of the "group" concept over time
EraGoverning standard
1976–1989IAS 3 — the original, single consolidation standard
1989–2013IAS 27 (consolidation) + IAS 28 (associates) + IAS 31 (joint ventures)
2013–presentIFRS 10 (control) + IFRS 11 (joint arrangements) + IFRS 12 (disclosure) + IAS 28 (revised, associates & JVs)
ℹ️ This progression reflects the Framework's ongoing refinement toward substance over form — each iteration closed loopholes the PREVIOUS control definition allowed.
2

Scope & Applicability (Historical)

What IAS 3 covered
IAS 3 applied to the preparation and presentation of consolidated financial statements for a GROUP of companies under the control of a parent.
Its scope architecture — parent, subsidiary, group — established terminology STILL used today, even though the detailed control test has been repeatedly refined.
Key definitions you must know (historical)
Subsidiary (original)
A company controlled by another company (the parent), largely through ownership of MORE THAN HALF the voting power.
Minority interest (original term)
The portion of a subsidiary's net assets/profit attributable to shareholders OTHER than the parent — the direct ancestor of today's "non-controlling interest" (NCI) terminology.
3

The Original Control Test

A voting-majority-centric definition
IAS 3's control test was largely based on holding MORE THAN 50% of voting power, or otherwise having the POWER to govern financial and operating policies so as to obtain benefits — a relatively SIMPLE test compared to IFRS 10's later, far more elaborate power/returns/linkage framework (which explicitly addresses de facto control, structured entities, and principal/agent scenarios that IAS 3 never contemplated).
The GAP between IAS 3's simple voting test and IFRS 10's sophisticated model reflects nearly 40 years of standard-setting responding to increasingly complex corporate structures — including the special purpose/structured entities that proliferated in the decades AFTER IAS 3's issuance.
4

Consolidation Procedures (Historical)

The mechanics that survive to this day
Combine LIKE items of assets, liabilities, income, and expenses line by line Eliminate the parent's investment against its share of subsidiary equity Eliminate INTRAGROUP balances and transactions in full
These THREE mechanical steps are essentially IDENTICAL to IFRS 10's own consolidation procedures today — the enduring PROCEDURAL core of consolidation accounting, unaffected by decades of control-definition refinement.
5

Minority Interest (Original Terminology)

From "minority interest" to "non-controlling interest"
IAS 3 used the term "MINORITY INTEREST" for the portion of a subsidiary not owned by the parent — this terminology PERSISTED for decades until IFRS 3 (2008 revision) and IAS 27 (2008 revision) formally replaced it with "NON-CONTROLLING INTEREST" (NCI), a deliberate shift acknowledging that a "minority" stake is not always small, and framing the concept in terms of CONTROL rather than mere ownership proportion.
6

Exclusion from Consolidation (Historical)

Early, now-abolished exclusion grounds
IAS 3 (and its immediate successor IAS 27) originally permitted EXCLUDING a subsidiary from consolidation if control was intended to be TEMPORARY, or if the subsidiary operated under SEVERE LONG-TERM restrictions impairing its ability to transfer funds to the parent. These exclusion grounds were LATER REMOVED — modern IFRS 10 contains NO such exclusions; ALL controlled subsidiaries must be consolidated (subject only to the narrow investment entities exception).
⚠️ This is a genuinely IMPORTANT historical difference — the "temporary control" and "severe restrictions" exclusions no longer exist under current IFRS, a significant NARROWING of permissible non-consolidation grounds over time.
7

Equity Method for Associates (Historical Origin)

The seed of what became IAS 28
Even before IAS 28 existed as a separate standard, early consolidation guidance recognised that investments where the investor had SIGNIFICANT INFLUENCE (but not control) warranted a DIFFERENT treatment than either full consolidation or simple cost/fair value measurement — the basic equity method concept (recognising a share of the investee's profit, adjusting the investment's carrying amount) traces its roots to this same era, later formalised and refined in IAS 28.
8

Why IAS 3 Was Split

Growing complexity demanded specialisation
By the late 1980s, the IASC recognised that consolidation (control), associates (significant influence), and JOINT VENTURES (joint control) each raised sufficiently DISTINCT issues to warrant SEPARATE, more detailed standards — leading to IAS 3's replacement by IAS 27 (consolidation) and IAS 28 (associates), with joint ventures addressed by the later IAS 31. This pattern of increasing SPECIALISATION repeated again in 2011 with the IFRS 10/11/12 package.

Disclosure Requirements (Historical)

Required disclosures under IAS 3
A list of significant subsidiaries, including name, country of incorporation, and proportion of ownership The reason(s) for excluding any subsidiary from consolidation (temporary control, severe restrictions — grounds later abolished)

Critical Judgments & Estimates (Historical)

Where management judgement mattered most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether voting majority genuinely conferred practical control Whether a subsidiary's control was genuinely "temporary" (a now-abolished exclusion ground)
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Fair value of consideration and net assets at acquisition (predating IFRS 3's later, far more detailed acquisition method)
⚠️ IAS 3's relatively simple voting-majority test would be considered SIGNIFICANTLY under-developed by today's standards — it had no answer for de facto control, structured entities, or principal/agent scenarios that IFRS 10 now directly addresses.

Comparison — IAS 3 (1976) vs IFRS 10/11/12 (current)
FeatureIAS 3 (withdrawn)Current (IFRS 10/11/12)
Control testLargely voting-majority basedPower + variable returns + linkage, covering de facto control and structured entities
Non-consolidation groundsTemporary control, severe long-term restrictionsABOLISHED — no such exclusions exist (only the narrow investment entities exception)
Joint venturesNot separately addressedDedicated IFRS 11, with joint operation/joint venture split
DisclosureBasic subsidiary listingExtensive IFRS 12 disclosures, including judgements and structured entity exposure
The DIRECTION of travel across nearly 50 years has been consistently toward more SUBSTANCE-based, judgement-requiring control tests, and toward NARROWING (not widening) permissible grounds for non-consolidation.
