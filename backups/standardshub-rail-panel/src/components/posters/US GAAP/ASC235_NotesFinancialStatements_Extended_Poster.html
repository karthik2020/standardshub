Conceptual Framework Linkage

Understanding the numbers requires understanding the choices behind them
Two entities with identical underlying economics can report different figures purely because of different, equally acceptable accounting policy choices (e.g. FIFO vs weighted-average inventory costing). ASC 235 requires disclosure of the SIGNIFICANT ACCOUNTING POLICIES an entity has selected, so users can properly interpret and compare the financial statements — a disclosure requirement broadly analogous to the accounting policy disclosure requirements found in IAS 1, though housed as its own dedicated Topic in the Codification.
2

Scope & Applicability

Applies to all entities
ASC 235 applies to all entities preparing US GAAP financial statements — the requirement to disclose significant accounting policies is essentially universal, regardless of entity size or public/private status.
3

The Significant Accounting Policies Note

A dedicated, typically first, note
Financial statements should include a disclosure of all SIGNIFICANT accounting policies as an INTEGRAL PART of the statements — in practice, almost universally presented as the FIRST note (commonly titled "Summary of Significant Accounting Policies"), covering the specific principles and methods used in preparing the statements, including the criteria and methods applied to select from among ACCEPTABLE ALTERNATIVES, and to apply GAAP to the entity's own particular circumstances.
4

What Counts as a "Significant" Policy

Judgement-driven, not a mandated checklist
Disclosure should be given particular prominence for policies involving a selection from among ACCEPTABLE ALTERNATIVES, policies UNIQUE to the entity's specific industry, and policies involving UNUSUALLY significant application of GAAP to the entity's particular facts. Routine, non-controversial policies with no meaningful choice involved warrant less prominent (or no) disclosure — a genuinely judgement-driven threshold rather than a fixed checklist, echoing the same materiality-based philosophy the IFRS Practice Statement on Making Materiality Judgements applies to IFRS accounting policy disclosures.
5

Location & Order of Notes

Convention, not a strict codified sequence
While ASC 235 does not mandate an exact ordering of subsequent notes, US practice conventionally places the Significant Accounting Policies note FIRST, followed by notes elaborating on specific balance sheet and income statement line items, then broader disclosures (commitments, contingencies, subsequent events, segment information) — a convention broadly similar to how IFRS preparers typically structure their own notes, though neither framework STRICTLY mandates a specific note sequence.
6

Worked Example — Drafting a Policy Disclosure

Choosing what warrants prominent disclosure
Example: Inventory costing method disclosure
A manufacturing entity uses the FIFO method for costing inventory — one of several acceptable methods under US GAAP (FIFO, weighted-average, or LIFO for entities that elect it).
Because inventory costing involves a genuine SELECTION FROM ACCEPTABLE ALTERNATIVES with a material effect on reported cost of goods sold and inventory value, this policy WARRANTS prominent disclosure in the Significant Accounting Policies note — e.g. "Inventory is stated at the lower of cost, determined using the first-in, first-out (FIFO) method, or net realisable value." A routine matter with no real choice (e.g. that cash includes demand deposits) would not need the same prominence.

8

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where judgement matters most
The central judgement under ASC 235 is determining WHICH policies are significant enough to warrant disclosure — too little detail leaves users unable to understand the basis for reported figures, while excessive boilerplate detail on routine, non-judgemental policies dilutes the note's usefulness, echoing the "disclosure overload" concern that motivated the IFRS materiality guidance discussed elsewhere in this library.

ASC 235 vs IAS 1's Accounting Policy Disclosures

Comparison — ASC 235 vs IAS 1
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 235)IFRS (IAS 1)
Dedicated TopicYes — its own standalone Topic in the CodificationNo separate standard; accounting policy disclosure requirements sit within IAS 1 itself
Materiality-based threshold"Significant" policies, with prominence for genuine alternatives/judgement2021 IAS 1 amendments similarly shifted emphasis toward MATERIAL, entity-specific policies over generic boilerplate — a closely converged philosophy
Typical placementConventionally the first noteConventionally the first or second note, similarly by market convention rather than strict mandate
This is one of the more philosophically converged disclosure areas — both frameworks have moved, over time, toward emphasising genuinely decision-useful, entity-specific policy disclosure over routine boilerplate.

Quick-reference glossary preview
Accepted alternatives
Situations where more than one acceptable accounting method exists under GAAP for the same type of transaction — the primary trigger for prominent policy disclosure under ASC 235.
Integral part of the financial statements
The characterisation ASC 235 gives to the accounting policies note — it is not optional supplementary material, but a required component of a complete set of financial statements.
