Conceptual Framework Linkage

Presenting a group as a single economic entity
ASBJ Statement No. 22 establishes the FRAMEWORK for CONSOLIDATED financial statements – presenting a PARENT company and its SUBSIDIARIES as a SINGLE economic reporting entity, rather than as a collection of legally SEPARATE companies. It defines WHEN one entity must consolidate ANOTHER (the concept of CONTROL), and sets out the MECHANICS of combining, eliminating, and presenting the group's financial position and performance as a WHOLE.
2

Scope

Parent companies preparing group financial statements
This Statement applies to ANY entity that is a PARENT – an entity CONTROLLING one or more OTHER entities (subsidiaries) – requiring the preparation of CONSOLIDATED financial statements presenting the GROUP as a single reporting entity.
3

Defining Control – the Basis for Consolidation

Majority voting rights, or de facto control indicators
CONTROL is generally PRESUMED where an entity holds a MAJORITY of another entity's VOTING rights. Where voting rights ALONE don't clearly establish control, J-GAAP ALSO considers "DE FACTO" control indicators – such as holding a SIGNIFICANT (though not majority) voting interest COMBINED WITH other factors demonstrating EFFECTIVE control (e.g. the ability to APPOINT a majority of the board, or CONTROLLING financial and operating POLICIES through contractual or other arrangements) – broadly PARALLELING IFRS 10's OWN control model internationally.
4

Uniform Accounting Policies Within the Group

Consistency across all consolidated entities
SUBSIDIARIES' financial statements must be prepared using UNIFORM accounting policies CONSISTENT with the parent's OWN policies for the purposes of consolidation – where a subsidiary applies a DIFFERENT policy in its OWN standalone reporting, adjustments must be made BEFORE consolidation, ensuring the CONSOLIDATED figures reflect a genuinely CONSISTENT basis of measurement throughout the group.
5

Elimination of Intercompany Transactions and Balances

Removing transactions that don't represent transactions with genuine outside parties
ALL intercompany transactions and BALANCES – sales, purchases, loans, dividends – between GROUP members are ELIMINATED on consolidation, ALONG WITH any UNREALISED profit remaining in inventory or other assets still HELD WITHIN the group – reflecting that, from the CONSOLIDATED group's perspective, these are NOT genuine transactions with OUTSIDE, unrelated parties.
6

Non-Controlling Interests Presentation

Presented as a distinct component within Net Assets
The portion of a SUBSIDIARY's net assets and profit/loss attributable to shareholders OTHER than the parent – NON-CONTROLLING INTERESTS (非支配株主持分) – is presented as a SEPARATE, distinct component WITHIN Net Assets (see the companion ASBJ Statement No. 5 poster), and non-controlling interests' SHARE of profit/loss is presented SEPARATELY within the consolidated Statement of Income, distinguishing it from profit attributable to the PARENT's OWN shareholders (see the companion ASBJ Statement No. 2, EPS, poster).
7

Fiscal Year-End Alignment – a Practical J-GAAP-Specific Rule

Adjustments required where a subsidiary's year end differs materially
A DISTINCTIVE, PRACTICAL J-GAAP rule: where a SUBSIDIARY's fiscal year-end DIFFERS from the parent's by MORE than THREE MONTHS, the subsidiary must PREPARE financial statements as of a date CLOSER to the parent's own fiscal year-end (or the parent must otherwise make appropriate ADJUSTMENTS) for CONSOLIDATION purposes – ensuring the CONSOLIDATED figures reflect a genuinely COMPARABLE reporting period across the GROUP, rather than allowing a MATERIAL timing mismatch to persist unaddressed.
8

Worked Example – Consolidating a Subsidiary at Acquisition

Combining the subsidiary's assets/liabilities and recognising non-controlling interests
Example: An 80%-owned subsidiary
A parent acquires an 80% controlling interest in a subsidiary with identifiable net assets of ¥500,000,000 at fair value.
The parent CONSOLIDATES 100% of the subsidiary's assets and liabilities LINE-BY-LINE, and recognises NON-CONTROLLING INTERESTS of 20% × ¥500,000,000 = ¥100,000,000 WITHIN Net Assets – reflecting the PORTION of the subsidiary's net assets attributable to the OUTSIDE 20% shareholders, with GOODWILL calculated on the parent's OWN 80% acquired interest only (per the companion ASBJ Statement No. 21 poster's partial goodwill method).
9

Worked Example – Fiscal Year-End Misalignment

Adjusting for a subsidiary's differing year end
Example: A subsidiary with a 6-month different fiscal year end
A parent's fiscal year ends March 31. A foreign subsidiary's fiscal year ends September 30 – a 6-month difference, EXCEEDING the 3-month threshold.
The subsidiary must PREPARE additional financial statements as of a date CLOSER to March 31 (or the parent must make appropriate ADJUSTMENTS for significant intervening transactions) – the subsidiary CANNOT simply be consolidated using its September 30 figures UNADJUSTED, given the DIFFERENCE exceeds the 3-month threshold.

Relationship to IAS/IFRS

Comparison – J-GAAP vs IFRS 10
FeatureJ-GAAP (ASBJ Statement No. 22)IFRS (IFRS 10)
Control definitionMajority voting rights, plus de facto control indicatorsPower, exposure to variable returns, and the ability to affect those returns – converged in overall substance
Fiscal year-end alignmentSpecific 3-month threshold ruleGeneral requirement for consistent reporting dates, without the same specific numeric threshold
The OVERALL control-based consolidation MODEL is broadly CONVERGED with IFRS 10, reflecting SUBSTANTIAL mutual development during the 2000s convergence era – the MOST distinctive J-GAAP-specific feature is the SPECIFIC, numerically-defined fiscal year-end alignment rule.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Control
The basis for consolidation, generally presumed at majority voting rights but also assessed through de facto control indicators.
De facto control
Effective control over an entity established through indicators other than majority voting rights, such as board appointment power or contractual arrangements.
