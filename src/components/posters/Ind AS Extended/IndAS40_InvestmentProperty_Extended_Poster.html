Ind AS 40 — Investment Property
Extended reference poster · Converged with IAS 40 · Mandatory cost model — a major, well-known Indian divergence · Worked examples
🇮🇳 IND AS — CONVERGED WITH IAS 40, WITH A MAJOR MEASUREMENT DIVERGENCE — IAS 40 permits a CHOICE between the cost model and the FAIR VALUE model for subsequent measurement of investment property. Ind AS 40 REMOVES this choice entirely — the COST MODEL is MANDATORY, with fair value change gains/losses NEVER flowing through P&L. Fair value must still be DISCLOSED in the notes, but cannot be used for balance sheet measurement. Section 11 covers the full comparison.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Same rental/appreciation definition as IAS 40, narrower Indian measurement choice
Ind AS 40 shares IAS 40's core DEFINITION — investment property is land/buildings held to earn RENTAL income or for CAPITAL APPRECIATION, rather than for use in production/supply or sale in the ordinary course of business. Where Ind AS 40 genuinely diverges is SUBSEQUENT MEASUREMENT: IAS 40 offers a CHOICE between the cost model and the fair value model (with fair value changes through P&L); Ind AS 40 REMOVES this choice, mandating the COST MODEL only.
HOW IND AS 40 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Reliability over relevance trade-off — Indian standard-setters favoured the RELIABILITY of a cost-based measurement (avoiding potentially volatile or difficult-to-verify property valuations flowing through P&L) over the added relevance a fair value model might offerConsistency with Ind AS 16 — mandating the cost model for investment property aligns its measurement approach more closely with Ind AS 16's OWN predominant Indian practice (see Ind AS 16 poster) for owner-occupied property
This is one of the MOST significant, well-documented Ind AS divergences from its IFRS counterpart — comparable in weight to Ind AS 20's grant presentation divergence and Ind AS 1's format divergence.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 40 covers
Ind AS 40 applies to recognition, measurement, and disclosure of investment property — IDENTICAL scope to IAS 40 in terms of WHAT qualifies, but with the MANDATORY cost model overlay on subsequent measurement.
Key definitions you must know
Investment property
IDENTICAL definition to IAS 40 — property held to earn rentals or for capital appreciation, or both.
Owner-occupied property
Property held for use in production/supply, or administrative purposes — SCOPED OUT of Ind AS 40, governed by Ind AS 16 instead, identical distinction to IAS 40.
3

Identifying Investment Property

Identical to IAS 40
The KEY distinguishing factor is whether cash flows are LARGELY INDEPENDENT of other entity assets (investment property) versus generated jointly with other assets used in operations — IDENTICAL classification test to IAS 40.
4

Initial Measurement — Cost

Identical to IAS 40
Purchase price plus directly attributable expenditure (legal fees, property transfer taxes, other transaction costs) — IDENTICAL initial measurement to IAS 40.
5

Subsequent Measurement — The Mandatory Cost Model (with worked example)

The core divergence, illustrated
Example 1: Cost model vs fair value model comparison
An Indian real estate company holds a commercial building as investment property, purchased for ₹50,00,00,000 (with a 30-year useful life, no residual value), currently having a fair value of ₹75,00,00,000 after 5 years.
Under IAS 40's FAIR VALUE model (NOT available under Ind AS 40): the property would be carried at ₹75,00,00,000, with a ₹25,00,00,000 cumulative fair value GAIN recognised through P&L over the holding period.
Under Ind AS 40's MANDATORY COST MODEL: the property is carried at ₹50,00,00,000 less 5 years' accumulated depreciation (₹50,00,00,000 ÷ 30 × 5 = ₹8,33,33,333), i.e. approximately ₹41,66,66,667 — with NO fair value gain recognised in P&L, though the ₹75,00,00,000 fair value MUST still be DISCLOSED in the notes.
This illustrates a MATERIAL difference in both balance sheet carrying amount and P&L impact between the two frameworks.
6

Mandatory Fair Value Disclosure

A partial preservation of IAS 40's fair value relevance
Even though the fair value MODEL is prohibited for measurement, Ind AS 40 STILL requires disclosure of investment property's FAIR VALUE in the notes — preserving SOME of the relevance IAS 40's fair value option would otherwise provide, without the P&L volatility of full fair value measurement.
7

Why Ind AS 40 Prohibits the Fair Value Model — Indian Rationale

The standard-setting rationale behind this divergence
India's standard-setters (the ICAI/ASB, feeding into the MCA's Ind AS notification process) determined that RELIABLE, VERIFIABLE fair valuations for real estate are NOT consistently available across India's diverse property markets (particularly outside major metros), and that introducing FAIR VALUE VOLATILITY into P&L for investment property could distort reported profits in ways not reflective of genuine operating performance — a POLICY judgement resulting in the mandatory cost model, distinct from IAS 40's own global flexibility.
8

Transfers To/From Investment Property

Identical to IAS 40 (as applied under the cost model)
Transfers to/from investment property occur when there is a CHANGE IN USE — since Ind AS 40 uses ONLY the cost model, transfers are recorded at the asset's EXISTING carrying amount (no fair value remeasurement gain/loss arises on transfer, unlike under IAS 40's fair value model option).
