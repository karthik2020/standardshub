Conceptual Framework Linkage

The single richest concentration of German accounting philosophy in positive law
If §§246–251 decided WHAT belongs on the balance sheet, §§252–256a decide the AMOUNT it is carried at — and in doing so, codifies the six foundational measurement principles that underpin every other HGB provision. §252(1) alone is arguably the single most important paragraph in all of German commercial accounting law: it is where the Vorsichtsprinzip, Realisationsprinzip, and Imparitätsprinzip are given DIRECT statutory force, rather than existing merely as judicially-developed GoB.
HOW §§252–256A MAP TO GERMAN ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES
Vorsichtsprinzip (prudence) — §252(1) no.4 names it directly as a general principle, then SPLITS it into two named sub-principles Realisationsprinzip — profits recognised ONLY when realised as of the balance sheet date Imparitätsprinzip — ALL foreseeable risks and losses must be recognised, even those only becoming known between the balance sheet date and the preparation date Bewertungsstetigkeit (consistency) — measurement methods should generally be retained period to period

Scope

Every recognised item, once §§246–251 has determined it belongs on the balance sheet
§§252–256a govern the MEASUREMENT of every asset, liability, and RAP item recognition has already been established for under the preceding Titel — they answer "how much," having already assumed the prior Titel's "whether" question is settled.

§252(1) — the Six General Measurement Principles

Six numbered principles, each doing distinct legal work
No.Principle
1Bilanzidentität — opening balances must match the prior year's closing balances exactly
2Going concern (Fortführungsprinzip) — assumed unless facts or circumstances indicate otherwise
3Einzelbewertung — assets and liabilities valued INDIVIDUALLY, not on an aggregated/portfolio basis (Section 6)
4Vorsicht (prudence) — split into Realisations- and Imparitätsprinzip (Section 4)
5Periodenabgrenzung — expenses and income matched to their economic period, independent of cash timing
6Bewertungsstetigkeit — measurement methods generally retained period to period
4

Realisationsprinzip and Imparitätsprinzip — Prudence Made Asymmetric

Realisationsprinzip: profits recognised only when realised
Unrealised GAINS are never recognised — a rise in a held asset's market value above its cost, however well-evidenced, produces NO income until an actual realisation event (typically a sale) occurs. This is considerably stricter than IFRS's own treatment of many assets measured at fair value through profit or loss.
Imparitätsprinzip: losses recognised the moment they are foreseeable
Foreseeable LOSSES, by contrast, must be recognised immediately and in full — including those only becoming known between the balance sheet date and the date the accounts are actually prepared (a "Wertaufhellung" / value-illuminating subsequent event, similar in spirit to IAS 10's adjusting events, but embedded directly as a NAMED general principle rather than a separate standard).
⚠️ Together, these two principles create a genuinely ASYMMETRIC treatment: gains wait for realisation, losses do not wait at all — the deliberate, structural source of virtually every "prudent," conservative outcome this entire poster series has documented.

Worked Example — Applying the Imparitätsprinzip

A loss known only after the balance sheet date, but before the accounts are signed
Example 1: A major customer's insolvency, discovered during account preparation
A company's balance sheet date is 31 December. On 20 January — while the annual accounts are still being prepared — the company learns that its largest customer, whose €300,000 receivable was outstanding and appeared fully collectible AT 31 December, filed for insolvency on 15 January, with recovery now expected to be negligible.
Although the insolvency filing occurred AFTER the balance sheet date, the underlying financial distress was already DEVELOPING as of 31 December, and the information became known before the accounts were finalised — under the Imparitätsprinzip, the company writes the receivable DOWN to its now-expected negligible recoverable amount WITHIN the 31 December accounts themselves, rather than waiting to recognise the loss in the following year.
6

Einzelbewertung — Individual Valuation

Each item stands alone — netting gains against losses across a portfolio is not permitted
§252(1) no.3 requires each asset and liability to be valued individually and separately as of the balance sheet date — an enterprise cannot offset an unrealised loss on one security against an unrealised gain on another within the SAME portfolio, even if both are economically related. Only specific, EXPRESSLY permitted exceptions (e.g. the §240(4) group valuation method, or the §254 Bewertungseinheit hedge mechanism discussed in Section 14) allow items to be considered together rather than strictly individually.
7

§252(2) — Departure Only in Justified Exceptional Cases

A high bar for departing from any of the six principles
§252(2) permits departure from the principles in §252(1) ONLY in justified EXCEPTIONAL cases (begründeten Ausnahmefällen) — a deliberately narrow escape valve, requiring genuine justification rather than mere managerial preference, and any such departure must be disclosed and explained in the Anhang.
8

§253(1) — Initial Measurement at Cost or Settlement Amount

Assets at cost; liabilities at the settlement amount; provisions per prudent commercial judgement
§253(1) sentence 1 requires assets to be measured, at most, at their Anschaffungs- oder Herstellungskosten (acquisition or production cost — defined in full in §255, Sections 16–17). Liabilities are measured at their Erfüllungsbetrag (settlement amount) — the amount actually required to discharge them. Provisions are measured at the amount necessary according to prudent commercial judgement (vernünftiger kaufmännischer Beurteilung), taking future price and cost increases into account where relevant, and pension-type obligations specifically using recognised actuarial methods.
9

§253(2) — Discounting Long-Term Provisions (with worked example)

A market-based average rate, published monthly by the Bundesbank
§253(2) requires provisions with a REMAINING TERM of MORE than one year to be discounted, using the average market interest rate of the corresponding maturity over the preceding SEVEN years — as calculated and published monthly by the Deutsche Bundesbank. Pension and comparable long-term employee benefit provisions specifically use a longer, TEN-year average (a distinction introduced by a 2016 amendment, responding to distortions from a prolonged period of historically low interest rates).
Example 2: Discounting a long-term restoration provision
A company recognises a €500,000 undiscounted restoration provision for a site it must return to its original condition in 12 years. The relevant Bundesbank-published 7-year average market rate for a 12-year maturity is 2.4%.
The provision is DISCOUNTED to present value using the 2.4% Bundesbank rate over the 12-year term — producing a materially LOWER initial carrying amount than the undiscounted €500,000, with the discount then UNWOUND (recognised as an interest-like expense) over the remaining term as the provision approaches its full settlement amount.
10

§253(3) — Fixed Assets: a Modified Lower-of-Cost Test

Depreciable assets: systematic depreciation; non-depreciable: write-down only for PERMANENT declines
§253(3) sentence 1 requires fixed assets with a LIMITED useful life to be systematically depreciated (planmäßige Abschreibung) over that life. For fixed assets NOT subject to systematic depreciation (e.g. land, or long-lived assets without a determinable useful life), a write-down is required ONLY where a diminution in value is expected to be PERMANENT (dauernde Wertminderung) — a genuinely important qualifier: a merely TEMPORARY dip in value does NOT require any write-down at all for fixed assets.
11

§253(4) — Current Assets: the STRICT Niederstwertprinzip

No "permanent" qualifier here — every decline, however temporary, forces a write-down
§253(4) applies the STRICT lower-of-cost-and-market principle (strenges Niederstwertprinzip) to current assets, WITHOUT the "permanent" qualifier §253(3) grants to fixed assets — ANY decline below cost, even one management genuinely expects to reverse shortly, MUST be written down immediately. This deliberate asymmetry between fixed and current assets is one of HGB's most distinctive measurement features.
12

Worked Example — Fixed vs Current Asset Write-Down, Side by Side

Identical price decline, two completely different accounting outcomes
Example 3: A commodity price dip affecting two different balance sheet categories
A company holds (a) a long-term strategic equity stake in a supplier, classified as Anlagevermögen, and (b) inventory of a raw material, classified as Umlaufvermögen. Both experience an identical, temporary 15% market price decline at year-end, which management — with good supporting evidence — genuinely expects to fully reverse within the next few months.
The STRATEGIC EQUITY STAKE (fixed asset) requires NO write-down — the decline is judged temporary, and §253(3)'s "permanent" qualifier is not met. The RAW MATERIAL INVENTORY (current asset), however, MUST be written down to the lower market value IMMEDIATELY under §253(4)'s strict test — REGARDLESS of the identical temporary nature of the decline, since no "permanent" qualifier exists for current assets at all.
13

§253(5) — the Wertaufholungsgebot (Mandatory Write-Up), and Goodwill's Exception

A REQUIRED reversal, once the reason for a prior write-down disappears — except for goodwill
§253(5) sentence 1 REQUIRES a write-up (Wertaufholung) where the reasons for a PRIOR write-down no longer exist — this is not a mere permission but a genuine MANDATE, structurally similar in spirit to IAS 36's own reversal requirement (for assets other than goodwill). §253(5) sentence 2, however, carves out a SPECIFIC exception: goodwill may NEVER be written back up, once amortised or impaired down — consistent with §246(1) sentence 4's own treatment of goodwill as a systematically amortising asset rather than one subject to ordinary impairment-reversal logic.
✅ The general write-up MANDATE (not merely a permission) is itself a point worth flagging carefully — many jurisdictions permit reversal without REQUIRING it; HGB requires it, reflecting the underlying view that once its cause disappears, an artificially depressed carrying value would itself violate the completeness and fair-measurement objectives running through this whole Titel.
14

§254 — Bewertungseinheiten (Hedge Accounting)

Combining a hedged item and hedging instrument into a single valuation unit
§254 — introduced by the 2009 BilMoG reform — permits an asset, liability, pending transaction, or highly probable forecast transaction to be combined with a financial instrument hedging a COMPARABLE risk into a single Bewertungseinheit (valuation unit). To the EXTENT the offsetting risks genuinely compensate one another, the general Einzelbewertung and Imparitätsprinzip rules are SUSPENDED for that combined unit — meaning an unrealised loss on one side need not be recognised where it is genuinely offset by a compensating gain on the hedging instrument, avoiding the artificially volatile result strict individual valuation would otherwise produce.
15

Worked Example — Forming a Bewertungseinheit

Suspending Einzelbewertung where a genuine, offsetting hedge exists
Example 4: Hedging a foreign currency payable with a forward contract
A company has a US-Dollar-denominated payable, and enters into a forward foreign exchange contract specifically to hedge the currency risk on that same payable. By year-end, the payable's EUR-equivalent value has increased (an unrealised loss on the payable), fully offset by a corresponding gain on the forward contract.
Rather than applying Einzelbewertung separately (which would recognise the payable's LOSS immediately under the Imparitätsprinzip while potentially deferring the OFFSETTING gain on the forward contract under the Realisationsprinzip), the company forms a Bewertungseinheit under §254 — recognising NEITHER the loss NOR the gain separately, to the extent they genuinely offset, since the COMBINED position has not actually changed the company's net economic exposure.
16

§255(1) — Anschaffungskosten (Acquisition Cost)

Purchase price, plus incidental costs, less reductions
§255(1) defines Anschaffungskosten as the purchase price PLUS incidental acquisition costs (Anschaffungsnebenkosten — e.g. delivery, installation, legal fees directly attributable) PLUS costs incurred to bring the asset into a usable condition, LESS any price reductions such as discounts and rebates — broadly similar in substance to how IAS 16 and IAS 2 define cost internationally.
17

§255(2) — Herstellungskosten: the Mandatory Overhead Floor

Material and production overheads: a MANDATORY minimum since BilMoG 2009
§255(2) requires Herstellungskosten (production cost) to include, as an absolute MINIMUM: materials consumed, direct labour, special direct production costs (Sondereinzelkosten der Fertigung), AND — critically, since the 2009 BilMoG reform — "angemessene Teile" (appropriate/reasonable portions) of NECESSARY material and production OVERHEADS, plus production-related depreciation. This overhead inclusion was OPTIONAL before BilMoG; it is now a MANDATORY floor. General administrative costs, voluntary social benefit expenses, company pension costs, and interest on capital used to finance production REMAIN elective (§255(3), Section 19) — they MAY be included, but need not be.
⚠️ Practitioners still working from pre-2009 intuitions sometimes assume production overheads remain optional under HGB — they do not. This is a genuinely important, commonly-tested point.
18

Worked Example — Building Up Herstellungskosten

Assembling the mandatory floor, then layering in the elective components
Example 5: Costing a manufactured unit
A manufacturer produces a machine at the following cost: €40,000 direct materials, €25,000 direct labour, €8,000 special direct production costs, €12,000 reasonably attributable production overheads, and €3,000 of administrative overhead the company elects to include as well.
MANDATORY floor = €40,000 + €25,000 + €8,000 + €12,000 = €85,000 — this amount MUST be capitalised regardless of any election. Adding the ELECTED €3,000 of administrative overhead brings total Herstellungskosten to €88,000 — the company COULD have stopped at €85,000, but having elected to include administrative overhead, must apply that election CONSISTENTLY going forward per §252(1) no.6's Bewertungsstetigkeit principle.
19

§255(3) — Borrowing Costs: an ELECTION, Not a Mandate

A genuine, significant divergence from IAS 23's mandatory capitalisation regime
§255(3) permits interest on borrowed capital used to finance the PRODUCTION of an asset to be included in Herstellungskosten, to the extent it relates to the production period — but this remains an ELECTION, never a requirement. This stands in DIRECT, genuine contrast to IAS 23 (Borrowing Costs), which since 2009 MANDATES capitalisation of borrowing costs directly attributable to the acquisition, construction, or production of a qualifying asset, with NO election available internationally.

§255(2a) — Development Costs of Self-Created Intangibles

A specific test for what counts as "development," feeding directly into §248(2)'s election
§255(2a) defines development costs (Entwicklungskosten) for purposes of the §248(2) capitalisation election introduced earlier in this poster series — distinguishing development from RESEARCH costs (Forschungskosten), which remain permanently excluded from capitalisation. The underlying research/development distinction shares a broadly similar conceptual shape with IAS 38's own research/development split, though the specific German statutory test and its accompanying commentary have their own distinct doctrinal history.
21

§256 — THE LIFO SURVIVAL — Simplified Measurement Methods

LIFO remains fully legal under HGB, where IAS 2 and ASPE both abolished it
§256 permits FIFO, LIFO, or a similar consumption-sequence method (Verbrauchsfolgeverfahren) for measuring similar items of raw materials, supplies, and goods, PROVIDED the chosen method corresponds to GoB. Critically, and GENUINELY distinctively, LIFO (Lifo-Verfahren) remains a FULLY LEGAL option under HGB — standing in direct contrast to IAS 2, which abolished LIFO entirely, and to ASPE Section 3031 (see the earlier companion poster in this library's ASPE series), which likewise does not permit it.
🚫 In PRACTICE, German companies rarely elect LIFO today, largely because German TAX law's own treatment of inventory valuation methods creates disincentives that make LIFO less attractive despite its continued COMMERCIAL-law legality — but the legal availability itself remains a genuine, standing divergence from both IFRS and ASPE.
22

Worked Example — Comparing FIFO and LIFO Outcomes

The same inventory, two legally available methods, two different results
Example 6: Rising input costs under FIFO vs LIFO
A company purchases 1,000 units at €10 each early in the year, then 1,000 more units at €14 each later in the year, as raw material costs rose. By year-end, 1,200 units have been consumed, leaving 800 units in closing inventory.
Under FIFO, closing inventory (the LAST units purchased) = 800 × €14 = €11,200. Under LIFO (still legally available under §256, though rarely elected in practice), closing inventory would instead consist of the EARLIEST units still assumed on hand = 800 × €10 = €8,000 — a MATERIALLY lower closing inventory value, and correspondingly higher cost of goods sold, PURELY as a result of the accounting method chosen, with NO equivalent choice available under IAS 2 or ASPE.
23

§256a — Currency Translation, and the Short-Term Realisation Override

Short-term FX items translate at the closing rate, EVEN if this produces an unrealised gain
§256a requires foreign-currency assets and liabilities to be translated at the spot mid-rate (Devisenkassamittelkurs) on the balance sheet date. For receivables and payables with a REMAINING TERM of one year or less, this closing-rate translation applies WITHOUT being overridden by the general Realisations-/Imparitätsprinzip — meaning a SHORT-term foreign currency item is translated at the current rate even where doing so would recognise an UNREALISED GAIN, a deliberate, narrow carve-out from HGB's usual asymmetric prudence for genuinely short-dated positions. For LONGER-term items (over one year remaining), the general prudence principles continue to apply in FULL — unrealised gains on such items generally remain excluded, while unrealised losses are still recognised.
24

Worked Example — Short-Term vs Long-Term FX Translation

Identical currency movement, different outcomes depending on remaining term
Example 7: Two USD receivables with different remaining terms
A company holds two USD receivables, both originated at the same historical EUR-equivalent value, and both benefiting from an identical, favourable USD appreciation by year-end. Receivable A has a 4-month remaining term; Receivable B has a 3-year remaining term.
Receivable A (SHORT-term, ≤1 year) is translated at the year-end SPOT rate, recognising the FULL unrealised gain, per §256a's short-term override. Receivable B (LONG-term, >1 year) remains subject to the GENERAL Realisationsprinzip — its unrealised gain is generally NOT recognised, DESPITE both receivables experiencing the identical favourable currency movement, purely because of their different remaining terms.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Realisationsprinzip
The principle that profits are recognised only when realised as of the balance sheet date, never on unrealised gains.
Imparitätsprinzip
The asymmetric principle requiring all foreseeable losses to be recognised immediately, even before they are realised.
Niederstwertprinzip
The lower-of-cost-and-market principle, applied strictly (regardless of permanence) to current assets under §253(4).
Wertaufholungsgebot
The mandatory requirement to reverse a prior write-down once its underlying cause no longer exists, except for goodwill.
Bewertungseinheit
A valuation unit combining a hedged item and hedging instrument, suspending individual valuation to the extent risks offset.
Herstellungskosten
Production cost, with materials, labour, and production overheads forming a mandatory capitalisation floor since BilMoG 2009.
