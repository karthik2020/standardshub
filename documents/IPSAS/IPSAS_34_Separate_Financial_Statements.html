Objective, Scope & Definitions (paras. 1–8)

Objective & scope
Prescribes accounting and disclosure requirements for investments in CONTROLLED ENTITIES, JOINT VENTURES, and ASSOCIATES when an entity prepares SEPARATE financial statements — i.e., the CONTROLLING entity's OWN (non-consolidated) statements.
This Standard does NOT mandate which entities must produce separate financial statements — it simply governs HOW, when an entity elects or is required by regulation to present them (para. 3).
The core definitions (para. 6)
Consolidated financial statements
Statements of an ECONOMIC ENTITY, presenting the controlling entity's and controlled entities' assets/liabilities/net assets/revenue/expenses/cash flows as ONE entity.
Separate financial statements
Statements where the entity ELECTS to account for its controlled-entity/joint-venture/associate investments EITHER at COST, per IPSAS 41, OR using the EQUITY METHOD (IPSAS 36).
⚠️ Financial statements of an entity with NO controlled entity, associate, or joint venture interest are NOT "separate financial statements" in this Standard's sense — they're simply that entity's ordinary statements (para. 8).
2

When Separate Financial Statements Are the ONLY Financial Statements (paras. 7, 9–10)

Ordinarily presented ALONGSIDE consolidated statements
Separate financial statements are normally presented IN ADDITION TO consolidated statements — or in addition to an investor's own statements where it has NO controlled entities but DOES hold equity-method associate/joint-venture interests (para. 7).
The two exceptions — separate statements stand ALONE
An entity EXEMPTED from consolidation (IPSAS 35, para. 5) or from applying the equity method (IPSAS 36, para. 23) MAY present separate financial statements as its ONLY statements (para. 9) An INVESTMENT ENTITY required, throughout the current AND all comparative periods, to measure ALL controlled-entity investments at fair value through surplus or deficit (IPSAS 35 para. 56) presents separate statements as its ONLY statements (para. 10)
3

Preparation of Separate Financial Statements — the Three Measurement Choices (paras. 11–13)

One policy per investment CATEGORY, applied consistently
For EACH category of investment (controlled entities, joint ventures, associates) → account at COST, per IPSAS 41 (fair value), OR using the EQUITY METHOD (IPSAS 36)
The SAME accounting is applied to each category — no mixing within a category (para. 12). Investments held for SALE or distribution are reclassified under IPSAS 44 if accounted for at cost or equity method — but IPSAS 41-measured investments are UNCHANGED by that classification, since they're already at fair value (para. 12). If an entity elects FAIR VALUE for associates/joint ventures under IPSAS 36 para. 24, it must use the SAME treatment in its separate statements (para. 13).
4

Investment Entities & Changes in Investment Entity Status (paras. 14–15)

Mandatory fair value flows through to separate statements too
Where a controlling entity is REQUIRED (IPSAS 35 para. 56) to measure a controlled entity at fair value through surplus or deficit, it applies the SAME treatment in its own separate statements (para. 14). A controlling entity that is NOT ITSELF an investment entity measures its investment in a controlled INVESTMENT entity per the ordinary §3 choices instead.
Status changes — treated as a deemed disposal/acquisition
CEASES to be an investment entity → the change date becomes a DEEMED ACQUISITION date; the controlled entity's fair value AT that date becomes the deemed transferred consideration for §3 accounting going forward (para. 15(a)) BECOMES an investment entity → switches to fair value through surplus or deficit; the difference between the PREVIOUS carrying amount and fair value at the change date is a gain/loss in surplus or deficit, and any CUMULATIVE net-assets/equity amount is treated as if the entity had been DISPOSED of at that date (para. 15(b))
5

Dividends or Similar Distributions & Group Reorganizations (paras. 16–18)

Dividend recognition timing
Dividends recognized when the entity's RIGHT TO RECEIVE is established
Cost or IPSAS 41 measurement basis → dividend goes to SURPLUS OR DEFICIT Equity method → dividend REDUCES the investment's carrying amount instead (para. 16)
Group reorganizations — "new top company" transactions
Where a NEW controlling entity is inserted above the original one via an equity-instrument exchange (or equivalent mechanism), and the underlying economic entity's assets/liabilities and owners' relative interests are UNCHANGED before and after — the new controlling entity measures its investment cost at the CARRYING AMOUNT of its share of the original controlling entity's net assets/equity, as shown in the ORIGINAL entity's own separate statements at the reorganization date (paras. 17–18). This prevents an artificial step-up in value from a purely structural reorganization.

Disclosure Requirements (paras. 19–23)

Where separate statements substitute for consolidated ones (para. 20)
That the statements ARE separate financial statements, and that the CONSOLIDATION exemption was used The NAME of the entity whose IPSAS-compliant consolidated statements ARE publicly available, and WHERE to obtain them A list of significant investments — names, jurisdictions (if different), ownership proportions and how determined The METHOD used to account for each
An INVESTMENT ENTITY presenting separate statements as its only statements discloses that fact, PLUS the IPSAS 38 investment-entity disclosures (para. 21). A non-investment-entity controlling entity applying IPSAS 35 para. 58 to an investment-entity subsidiary discloses its accounting policy CHOICE, plus IPSAS 38 disclosures (para. 22).
Where separate statements accompany consolidated ones (para. 23)
IDENTIFY the consolidated (or IPSAS 36/37-based) statements to which the separate statements relate The fact that they ARE separate statements, and WHY they're prepared (if not legally mandated) The same significant-investment LIST and accounting METHOD disclosures as para. 20

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Comparison with IAS 27

Comparison — IPSAS 34 vs IAS 27 (Amended 2011)
IPSAS 34 is drawn primarily from IAS 27 (Amended in 2011), Separate Financial Statements, with amendments to 31 December 2014. Where IAS 27 references IFRS 9, IPSAS 34 substitutes the relevant IPSAS financial instruments Standard instead.
TopicDifference
Terminology"Net assets/equity," "economic entity," "controlling entity," "controlled entity," "revenue" (IPSAS 34) vs "equity," "group," "parent," "subsidiary," "income" (IAS 27)
Controlling entity holding a controlled INVESTMENT entityIPSAS 34 has SPECIFIC requirements (§4) for a non-investment-entity controlling entity holding an investment-entity subsidiary; IAS 27 has none — it simply requires consolidation in that case

Quick-Reference Glossary

Separate financial statements
Statements where investments in controlled entities, joint ventures, and associates are accounted for at cost, IPSAS 41 fair value, or the equity method.
Consolidated financial statements
Statements presenting an economic entity's controlling and controlled entities as a single entity.
Investment entity
An entity required to measure its investments in controlled entities at fair value through surplus or deficit, under conditions set in IPSAS 35.
Equity method
A method where an investment is initially recognized at cost and adjusted for the investor's share of the investee's post-acquisition surplus/deficit.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOFPerf Statement of financial performance Notes Notes to the financial statements
