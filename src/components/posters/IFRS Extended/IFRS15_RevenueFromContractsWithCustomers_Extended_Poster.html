Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why revenue is recognised as CONTROL transfers
The Conceptual Framework recognises income when an entity's assets increase or liabilities decrease, resulting in an increase in equity (other than from owner contributions). IFRS 15 operationalises this for revenue specifically: revenue is recognised as the entity SATISFIES a performance obligation by TRANSFERRING CONTROL of a good or service to the customer — replacing the old "risks and rewards" test with a single, principles-based CONTROL-based model.
HOW IFRS 15 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Faithful representation — revenue reflects the CONSIDERATION the entity expects to be ENTITLED TO in exchange for goods/services, not merely cash received Comparability — a SINGLE five-step model replaced numerous industry- and transaction-specific revenue standards (IAS 18, IAS 11, and various interpretations), improving consistency across industries Relevance — recognising variable consideration only to the extent HIGHLY PROBABLE of no significant reversal (the "constraint," Section 4) balances relevance against reliability
IFRS 15 was developed JOINTLY with the US FASB, replacing DOZENS of previously disparate revenue recognition standards and interpretations with one converged, principles-based model.
The five-step model, at a glance
1. Identify the contract → 2. Identify performance obligations → 3. Determine the transaction price → 4. Allocate the transaction price → 5. Recognise revenue as obligations are satisfied
ℹ️ Every revenue transaction, in every industry, is analysed through this SAME five-step sequence — a direct embodiment of the Framework's comparability objective.

Scope & Applicability

What IFRS 15 covers
IFRS 15 applies to ALL contracts with customers, EXCEPT: lease contracts (IFRS 16); insurance contracts (IFRS 17); financial instruments (IFRS 9); and certain non-monetary exchanges between entities in the SAME line of business to facilitate sales to customers/potential customers.
A "customer" is a party that has CONTRACTED with an entity to obtain goods/services that are an OUTPUT of the entity's ordinary activities, in exchange for consideration — collaborative arrangements with a partner sharing risks/rewards (rather than purchasing an output) generally fall OUTSIDE IFRS 15's scope.
Key definitions you must know
Performance obligation
A promise in a contract to transfer to the customer a good/service (or bundle) that is DISTINCT.
Transaction price
The amount of consideration an entity expects to be ENTITLED TO in exchange for transferring promised goods/services, excluding amounts collected on behalf of third parties.
Stand-alone selling price
The price at which an entity would sell a promised good/service SEPARATELY to a customer — the basis for allocating the transaction price.

Step 1 & 2 — Identify the Contract and Performance Obligations (with worked example)

The contract criteria, and the "distinct" test
A contract exists only if: the parties have APPROVED the contract; each party's rights regarding goods/services are IDENTIFIABLE; PAYMENT terms are identifiable; the contract has COMMERCIAL SUBSTANCE; and COLLECTION of consideration is PROBABLE. A good/service is DISTINCT if the customer can benefit from it either on its own or with other readily available resources, AND it is separately identifiable from other promises in the contract.
Example 1: Identifying distinct performance obligations
A software company sells a perpetual software licence bundled with 2 years of technical support and unspecified future updates. The licence is fully functional on its own; the support and updates are routinely sold separately by the vendor and other providers.
This contract likely contains THREE distinct performance obligations: (1) the software licence, (2) technical support, and (3) unspecified future updates — each is capable of being distinct AND separately identifiable, since the customer can benefit from each on its own or with readily available resources, and they are not highly interdependent or integrated.

Step 3 — Determine the Transaction Price (with worked example)

Variable consideration and the constraint
Variable consideration (discounts, rebates, refunds, credits, performance bonuses/penalties) is estimated using EITHER the EXPECTED VALUE method (probability-weighted) or the MOST LIKELY AMOUNT method (whichever better predicts the entitled consideration) — but INCLUDED in the transaction price ONLY to the extent HIGHLY PROBABLE that a SIGNIFICANT REVERSAL will NOT occur when the uncertainty resolves (the "constraint").
Example 2: Applying the variable consideration constraint
A construction company enters a contract with a $10,000,000 base price plus a $2,000,000 bonus if completed 30 days early. Based on historical performance on similar projects, management estimates an 80% probability of achieving the bonus, but the project has genuine, significant uncertainty (weather-dependent activities not yet begun).
Even though the EXPECTED VALUE would suggest including 2,000,000 × 80% = $1,600,000, the constraint requires the entity to assess whether it is HIGHLY PROBABLE no SIGNIFICANT reversal will occur — given the genuine remaining uncertainty, the entity may conclude ONLY the $10,000,000 base price should be included at this early stage, with the bonus recognised LATER as uncertainty resolves.
⚠️ The constraint is a DELIBERATE reliability safeguard — without it, entities could recognise highly speculative variable consideration prematurely, overstating revenue before genuine certainty exists.

Step 4 — Allocate the Transaction Price (with worked example)

Relative stand-alone selling price basis
The transaction price is allocated to EACH performance obligation in proportion to its RELATIVE STAND-ALONE SELLING PRICE — estimated (if not directly observable) using an adjusted market assessment, expected cost plus margin, or residual approach.
Example 3: Allocating a bundled transaction price
Continuing Example 1: total contract price is $12,000. Stand-alone selling prices: licence $9,000, support $2,000, updates $1,500 (total stand-alone: $12,500).
Licence = 12,000 × (9,000/12,500) = $8,640
Support = 12,000 × (2,000/12,500) = $1,920
Updates = 12,000 × (1,500/12,500) = $1,440
Total allocated = $12,000 (the overall $500 bundle discount is spread PROPORTIONATELY across all three obligations).

Step 5 — Recognise Revenue (with worked example)

Point in time vs over time
Revenue is recognised OVER TIME if ONE of: the customer simultaneously receives/consumes benefits as the entity performs; the entity's performance creates/enhances an asset the customer CONTROLS as it is created; or the asset has NO ALTERNATIVE USE to the entity AND the entity has an ENFORCEABLE RIGHT to payment for performance completed to date. Otherwise, revenue is recognised at a POINT IN TIME — when control transfers.
Example 4: Over-time recognition using percentage of completion
A construction company builds a custom factory (no alternative use, enforceable right to payment for work done) with a total contract price of $12,000,000 and estimated total costs of $9,000,000. At year end, costs incurred to date are $3,600,000.
Percentage complete = 3,600,000 / 9,000,000 = 40%
Revenue recognised = 12,000,000 × 40% = $4,800,000, recognised OVER TIME, based on progress toward completion (an input method, using costs incurred).

Contract Costs

Capitalising incremental costs to obtain and fulfil contracts
INCREMENTAL costs of OBTAINING a contract (e.g. sales commissions) are capitalised as an asset IF the entity expects to RECOVER them — with a practical expedient to expense them immediately if the amortisation period would be ONE YEAR or less. Costs to FULFIL a contract not covered by another Standard are capitalised if they relate DIRECTLY to a contract, generate/enhance resources used to satisfy future performance obligations, and are expected to be RECOVERED.
8

Principal vs Agent

Gross vs net revenue presentation
An entity determines whether it is a PRINCIPAL (controls the good/service BEFORE transfer to the customer — recognises revenue GROSS) or an AGENT (arranges for another party to provide the good/service — recognises revenue NET, being its commission/fee only) — this assessment focuses on whether the entity CONTROLS the specified good/service before it is transferred.

Disclosure Requirements

Required disclosures
DISAGGREGATION & CONTRACT BALANCES
Revenue recognised, disaggregated into categories depicting how economic factors affect the nature/amount/timing/uncertainty of revenue and cash flows Opening/closing balances of contract assets, contract liabilities, and receivables from contracts with customers
PERFORMANCE OBLIGATIONS & JUDGEMENTS
Information about remaining performance obligations, including the aggregate transaction price allocated to them and when revenue is expected to be recognised Significant judgements affecting the amount/timing of revenue recognition (e.g. determining timing of satisfaction, transaction price, allocation)

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Identifying distinct performance obligations within a bundled contract Determining whether revenue is recognised over time or at a point in time Principal vs agent classification
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Variable consideration estimates, and application of the constraint Stand-alone selling prices where not directly observable Percentage of completion measures for over-time recognition
⚠️ IFRS 15 is one of the MOST judgement-intensive standards for many industries (software, construction, telecoms) given the sheer variety of contract structures it must accommodate through a single principles-based model.

An entity's right to consideration in exchange for goods/services transferred, conditional on something other than the passage of time.

The requirement to include variable consideration only to the extent highly probable a significant reversal will not occur.
