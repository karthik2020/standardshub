1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Measuring exchanges that do not involve cash
ASPE Section 3831 governs the MEASUREMENT of NON-monetary transactions – EXCHANGES of ASSETS, liabilities, OR services THAT do NOT primarily INVOLVE monetary CONSIDERATION (e.g. BARTER arrangements, or EXCHANGES of ONE productive ASSET for ANOTHER). THE GENERAL rule is FAIR value MEASUREMENT, SUBJECT to SPECIFIC exceptions DEFAULTING instead to CARRYING amount (Sections 4–7).
2

Scope

Exchanges of non-monetary assets, liabilities, or services
This Section applies to TRANSACTIONS in WHICH an ENTERPRISE exchanges NON-monetary assets, LIABILITIES, or SERVICES for OTHER non-MONETARY assets, LIABILITIES, or SERVICES, WITH little OR no MONETARY consideration INVOLVED.
3

The General Rule – Measurement at Fair Value

Recognizing a gain or loss on exchange
AS a GENERAL rule, a NON-monetary transaction is MEASURED at the FAIR value OF the asset(s) GIVEN up, OR the asset(s) RECEIVED, WHICHEVER is MORE reliably MEASURABLE – RECOGNIZING a GAIN or LOSS for the DIFFERENCE between the FAIR value and the CARRYING amount of the ASSET given UP.
4

Exception One – Lack of Commercial Substance

No meaningful change in future cash flows
WHERE a TRANSACTION lacks COMMERCIAL substance (Section 8), it IS measured AT the CARRYING amount OF the asset GIVEN up, RATHER than FAIR value – NO gain OR loss is RECOGNIZED, GIVEN the ENTERPRISE'S economic POSITION has NOT genuinely CHANGED as a RESULT of the EXCHANGE.
5

Exception Two – Fair Value Not Reliably Measurable

Carrying amount used where no reliable fair value exists
WHERE the FAIR value of NEITHER the asset GIVEN up NOR the ASSET received CAN be reliably MEASURED, the TRANSACTION is MEASURED at CARRYING amount INSTEAD – GIVEN there IS no RELIABLE basis for RECOGNIZING a GAIN or LOSS.
6

Exception Three – Exchanges to Facilitate Sales to Third Parties

Inventory swaps between competitors, filling regional gaps
WHERE an ENTERPRISE exchanges a PRODUCT or PROPERTY held FOR sale in the ORDINARY course OF business FOR a SIMILAR product OR property TO be sold WITHIN the SAME line OF business – SOLELY to FACILITATE sales TO customers OTHER than the PARTIES to the EXCHANGE (a COMMON practice AMONG competitors FILLING regional SUPPLY gaps) – the TRANSACTION is MEASURED at CARRYING amount, NOT fair VALUE, SINCE no GENUINE earnings PROCESS has BEEN completed.
7

Exception Four – Non-Reciprocal Transfers to Owners

Spin-offs and similar restructuring transactions
A NON-monetary, non-RECIPROCAL transfer TO owners (e.g. a SPIN-off, or OTHER form of CORPORATE restructuring OR liquidation) is MEASURED at CARRYING amount – REFLECTING that SUCH transactions ARE fundamentally EQUITY distributions, RATHER than ARM'S-length exchanges GIVING rise TO a genuine GAIN or LOSS.
8

The Commercial Substance Test

Does the transaction genuinely change future cash flows?
A TRANSACTION has COMMERCIAL substance WHERE the ENTERPRISE'S future CASH flows are EXPECTED to CHANGE significantly AS a RESULT of the EXCHANGE – EITHER in TERMS of RISK, timing, OR amount – CONCEPTUALLY similar TO the COMMERCIAL substance test IAS 16 applies SPECIFICALLY to EXCHANGES of PROPERTY, plant, AND equipment (see the COMPANION Section 13 comparison), THOUGH ASPE'S own Section 3831 APPLIES this test MORE broadly, to ALL non-monetary TRANSACTIONS rather THAN PP&E exchanges alone.
9

Worked Example – an Exchange With Commercial Substance

A genuine change in future cash flows, recognized at fair value
Example: Trading specialized equipment for a delivery vehicle fleet
A manufacturing company exchanges specialized production equipment (carrying amount $400,000, fair value $550,000) for a fleet of delivery vehicles (fair value $550,000), fundamentally changing its future operating cash flow profile from production to distribution activities.
GIVEN the TRANSACTION genuinely CHANGES the COMPANY'S future CASH flows, it HAS commercial SUBSTANCE – the COMPANY recognizes the VEHICLE fleet AT its $550,000 FAIR value, RECOGNIZING a $550,000 − $400,000 = $150,000 gain ON the exchange.
10

Worked Example – an Exchange Lacking Commercial Substance

No meaningful change in economic position, no gain recognized
Example: Swapping nearly identical delivery trucks
A logistics company exchanges one delivery truck (carrying amount $45,000, fair value $52,000) with another logistics company for an essentially identical truck (fair value $52,000), used in the exact same capacity, with no meaningful change to expected future cash flows.
GIVEN the TRANSACTION lacks GENUINE commercial SUBSTANCE, the COMPANY records the NEW truck at the CARRYING amount OF the OLD truck ($45,000) – NO gain is RECOGNIZED, DESPITE the $52,000 fair VALUE of BOTH trucks.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Commercial substance
A characteristic of a transaction where the enterprise's future cash flows are expected to change significantly as a result of the exchange.
Non-monetary transaction
An exchange of assets, liabilities, or services involving little or no monetary consideration.
