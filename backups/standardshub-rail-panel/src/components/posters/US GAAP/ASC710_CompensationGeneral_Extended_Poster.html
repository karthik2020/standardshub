1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Employee costs earned but not yet paid
Employees often EARN compensation — vacation time, deferred bonuses — before they actually RECEIVE it, creating a genuine present obligation the employer must recognise as services are rendered, not merely when cash changes hands. ASC 710 is a general "catch-all" compensation Topic addressing matters not more specifically covered elsewhere (retirement benefits sit in ASC 715, nonretirement postemployment benefits in ASC 712, and stock compensation in ASC 718).
2

Scope & Applicability

Compensated absences and individual deferred compensation
ASC 710 applies to COMPENSATED ABSENCES (vacation, sick pay, and similar paid time off) and to individual DEFERRED COMPENSATION arrangements (contractual arrangements with specific employees, typically executives, distinct from broad-based retirement plans governed by ASC 715).
3

Compensated Absences — the Four-Part Accrual Test

All four conditions must be met
CriterionRequirement
(a)The obligation is attributable to employee SERVICES already rendered
(b)The obligation relates to rights that VEST or ACCUMULATE
(c)Payment of the compensation is PROBABLE
(d)The amount can be REASONABLY ESTIMATED
This four-part accrual test closely mirrors IAS 19's own treatment of short-term compensated absences — accruing the undiscounted expected cost of accumulating paid absences as employees render the service that entitles them to future compensated time off, a broadly converged approach.
4

Deferred Compensation Arrangements (710-20)

Individual contracts, distinct from broad-based plans
ASC 710-20 addresses DEFERRED compensation contracts with SPECIFIC INDIVIDUALS (typically key executives) — such as arrangements to pay a specified amount at retirement or another future date — distinct from the broad-based, actuarially-determined retirement plans governed by ASC 715. The obligation is generally accrued systematically over the employee's required service period.
5

Rabbi Trusts

A distinctly US arrangement structure
A "RABBI TRUST" is a trust an employer establishes to set aside assets FUNDING an individual deferred compensation arrangement — but critically, the assets REMAIN subject to the claims of the employer's GENERAL CREDITORS in the event of insolvency (unlike assets held in a genuinely protected, bankruptcy-remote qualified retirement plan trust). This specific structure — named after the original private letter ruling request that established the concept — is a distinctly American legal/tax planning device.
6

Why the Employer Consolidates the Rabbi Trust

No genuine risk transfer to a third party
Because rabbi trust assets remain exposed to the employer's OWN general creditors, the trust does NOT achieve a genuine legal risk transfer — the employer effectively still CONTROLS and bears the risk of these assets. As a result, the employer CONSOLIDATES the rabbi trust's assets (rather than treating them as a plan asset offsetting the compensation liability), and continues to recognise its FULL deferred compensation liability GROSS, without netting against the trust assets.

Worked Example — Compensated Absences Accrual

Accruing vested vacation pay
Example: Year-end vacation accrual
At year end, employees have accumulated (vested) but unused vacation time equivalent to $340,000 in wages, with all four accrual criteria met (probable payment, reasonably estimable, vested rights, attributable to past service).
Debit Compensation expense $340,000 / Credit Accrued compensated absences (liability) $340,000 — recognised in the CURRENT period, matching the expense to the period the employees actually earned the vacation entitlement.
8

Worked Example — Rabbi Trust Consolidation

Gross presentation, no netting
Example: Funding a deferred compensation liability
An employer establishes a rabbi trust and contributes $2,000,000 of marketable securities to fund a $2,000,000 deferred compensation obligation to a group of executives.
The employer CONSOLIDATES the $2,000,000 of trust assets on its own Balance Sheet (typically as investments), and CONTINUES to recognise the FULL $2,000,000 deferred compensation LIABILITY separately — the two amounts are presented GROSS, side by side, rather than netted against each other, since the trust assets remain exposed to the employer's own creditors and don't achieve genuine settlement of the obligation.

ASC 710 vs IAS 19 — Comprehensive Comparison

Comparison — ASC 710 vs IAS 19
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 710)IFRS (IAS 19)
Compensated absences accrualFour-part test: attributable to service, vests/accumulates, probable, estimableBroadly similar — accrue the undiscounted expected cost of accumulating paid absences as short-term employee benefits
Rabbi trust as a named conceptExplicitly codified, with specific consolidation guidanceNo equivalent NAMED concept — an economically similar arrangement would likely reach a similar CONSOLIDATED outcome under general IFRS 10 principles, without a dedicated named Topic
The rabbi trust concept is a good example of a genuinely US-specific legal/tax structure the Codification addresses explicitly, where IFRS would simply apply its general consolidation principles to reach a broadly similar economic conclusion.

Compensated absences
Paid time off (vacation, sick leave) earned by employees through service rendered, accrued as a liability when specific vesting/accumulation criteria are met.
Rabbi trust
A trust funding an individual deferred compensation arrangement whose assets remain subject to the employer's general creditors, resulting in consolidation by the employer.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Vesting
The point at which an employee's right to a benefit becomes non-forfeitable, regardless of continued employment.
Accumulating rights
Compensated absence rights that can be carried forward and used in future periods if not used in the period earned.
