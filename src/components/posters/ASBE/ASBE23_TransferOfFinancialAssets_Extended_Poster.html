Conceptual Framework Linkage

Determining when a financial asset should come off the Balance Sheet
ASBE No. 23 governs the DERECOGNITION of FINANCIAL assets – DETERMINING WHEN a TRANSFERRED financial ASSET should be REMOVED from the TRANSFEROR'S Balance SHEET, RATHER than CONTINUING to be RECOGNISED (WHOLLY or PARTIALLY). This STANDARD works ALONGSIDE ASBE No. 22 (CLASSIFICATION and measurement) to FORM the COMPLETE financial ASSET accounting FRAMEWORK, CLOSELY converged with IFRS 9'S own DERECOGNITION model internationally.
2

Scope

Sales, factoring, securitisation, and similar transfer arrangements
This Standard applies to TRANSACTIONS in WHICH an ENTERPRISE TRANSFERS a FINANCIAL asset (or a PART of one) to ANOTHER party – COVERING outright SALES, FACTORING of RECEIVABLES, SECURITISATION arrangements, and REPURCHASE (repo) TRANSACTIONS.
3

Full Derecognition – Risks and Rewards Substantially Transferred

The primary test for derecognition
WHERE an ENTERPRISE TRANSFERS SUBSTANTIALLY ALL the RISKS and REWARDS of OWNERSHIP of a FINANCIAL asset, the ASSET is DERECOGNISED IN FULL, WITH the DIFFERENCE between the CARRYING amount and the CONSIDERATION received RECOGNISED as a GAIN or LOSS.
4

Continued Recognition – Risks and Rewards Substantially Retained

No derecognition, a corresponding liability instead
WHERE an ENTERPRISE RETAINS SUBSTANTIALLY ALL the RISKS and REWARDS of OWNERSHIP (e.g. a SALE with a REPURCHASE agreement at a FIXED price), the ASSET CONTINUES to be RECOGNISED IN FULL, WITH the CONSIDERATION received RECOGNISED as a FINANCIAL liability, RATHER than a SALE.
5

The Control Test – the Fallback for Ambiguous Cases

Applied where risks and rewards are neither clearly transferred nor retained
WHERE it is NOT clear WHETHER SUBSTANTIALLY all RISKS and REWARDS have been TRANSFERRED or RETAINED, the ENTERPRISE assesses WHETHER it has RETAINED CONTROL of the ASSET – IF control is RETAINED, the ASSET continues to be RECOGNISED to the EXTENT of the ENTERPRISE'S continuing INVOLVEMENT (Section 6); IF control PASSES to the TRANSFEREE, the ASSET is DERECOGNISED.
6

Continuing Involvement Accounting

Recognising an asset and liability limited to the retained exposure
WHERE the ENTERPRISE retains a LIMITED, ONGOING exposure to the TRANSFERRED asset (e.g. a GUARANTEE, or a RETAINED subordinated INTEREST), it CONTINUES to RECOGNISE the ASSET (and a CORRESPONDING liability) ONLY to the EXTENT of its CONTINUING involvement – RATHER than either FULL derecognition or FULL continued RECOGNITION.

Worked Example – a Factoring Arrangement Qualifying for Derecognition

A genuine, non-recourse sale of receivables
Example: Non-recourse factoring of trade receivables
A company factors RMB 50,000,000 of trade receivables to a bank on a genuinely NON-RECOURSE basis, receiving RMB 47,000,000 cash, with the bank bearing all credit risk and no ongoing involvement retained by the company.
Since SUBSTANTIALLY all RISKS and REWARDS are TRANSFERRED, the company DERECOGNISES the RMB 50,000,000 receivable IN FULL, recognising a LOSS on transfer of RMB 50,000,000 − RMB 47,000,000 = RMB 3,000,000.
9

Worked Example – Continuing Involvement Under a Retained Guarantee

Partial derecognition, limited to the retained exposure
Example: Factoring with a partial credit guarantee
A company factors RMB 30,000,000 of receivables, but retains a guarantee covering the first RMB 3,000,000 of any credit losses (its maximum possible exposure).
The company CONTINUES to recognise an ASSET (and a CORRESPONDING liability) of RMB 3,000,000 – LIMITED to its CONTINUING involvement – DERECOGNISING the REMAINING RMB 27,000,000 of receivables, since it has NO further EXPOSURE beyond the GUARANTEE.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Derecognition
The removal of a previously recognised financial asset from an entity's Balance Sheet.
Continuing involvement
A retained exposure to a transferred financial asset (such as a guarantee), requiring partial continued recognition limited to that exposure.
