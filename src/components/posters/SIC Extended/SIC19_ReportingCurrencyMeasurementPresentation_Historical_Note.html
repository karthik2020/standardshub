SIC-19 – Reporting Currency–Measurement and Presentation of Financial Statements under IAS 21 and IAS 29
Historical reference note · Issued 2000 · Withdrawn 2003
⚠ WITHDRAWN – Superseded by the 2003 revision of IAS 21 The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates, effective for periods beginning 1 January 2005
What SIC-19 addressed
Under the OLD version of IAS 21 – which used the concept of "reporting currency" rather than "functional currency" – SIC-19 addressed how an entity should select the currency in which to MEASURE and PRESENT its financial statements, including guidance on identifying an appropriate "measurement currency" reflecting the economic substance of the underlying events and circumstances relevant to the entity.
Why the whole framework was replaced
Rather than being folded into a revised version of the same concept, SIC-19's underlying framework was itself REPLACED. The 2003 revision of IAS 21 introduced the modern "FUNCTIONAL CURRENCY" concept – defined as the currency of the primary economic environment in which the entity operates – as a more precisely defined and operationally clearer replacement for the older, less rigorously specified "reporting currency"/measurement-currency approach SIC-19 addressed.
Where this guidance lives today
Superseded byIAS 21 The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates (revised 2003)
Why withdrawnThe functional currency concept, with detailed indicators for its determination, replaced the older reporting-currency framework SIC-19 was built around
Current statusFully superseded – any question about which currency to measure or present financial statements in is now addressed through IAS 21's functional currency determination guidance, not SIC-19
