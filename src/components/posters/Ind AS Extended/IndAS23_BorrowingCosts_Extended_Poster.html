Conceptual Framework Linkage

Identical mandatory capitalisation philosophy to IAS 23
Ind AS 23 applies the IDENTICAL principle as IAS 23 – borrowing costs DIRECTLY ATTRIBUTABLE to the acquisition, construction, or production of a QUALIFYING ASSET are CAPITALISED as part of the asset's cost; all other borrowing costs are expensed as incurred.
HOW IND AS 23 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Faithful representation – reflecting the TRUE total cost of bringing a qualifying asset to its intended use – identical to IAS 23No expensing option – capitalisation is MANDATORY (not a choice), IDENTICAL to IAS 23's own post-2007 revision removing the prior expensing alternative
Ind AS 23 introduced NO substantive change to this model relative to IAS 23 – its practical Indian relevance is most pronounced for the LARGE infrastructure, real estate, and EPC sectors with long-duration qualifying asset construction periods.
The core rule – identical to IAS 23
Borrowing costs directly attributable to a qualifying asset = CAPITALISED as part of its cost
ℹ️ IDENTICAL mandatory rule to IAS 23 – no Indian-specific modification.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 23 covers
Ind AS 23 applies to borrowing costs, EXCLUDING those addressed by Ind AS 102 (share-based payment) or capitalised under Ind AS 41's fair value approach – IDENTICAL scope to IAS 23, and EXCLUDING qualifying assets measured at fair value (e.g. biological assets) or inventories manufactured in large quantities on a repetitive basis.
Key definitions you must know
Qualifying asset
IDENTICAL definition to IAS 23 – an asset that necessarily takes a SUBSTANTIAL period of time to get ready for its intended use or sale.
Capitalisation rate
The weighted average of borrowing costs applicable to GENERAL borrowings outstanding during the period, identical concept to IAS 23.
3

Qualifying Assets

Identical criteria to IAS 23
Examples include inventories requiring substantial time to bring to a saleable condition, manufacturing plants, power generation facilities, and investment properties under construction – IDENTICAL examples to IAS 23, genuinely common for Indian infrastructure and real estate developers.

Capitalisation – Specific Borrowings (with worked example)

Identical to IAS 23
Example 1: Specific loan for a qualifying asset
An Indian real estate developer borrows ₹50,00,00,000 SPECIFICALLY to fund construction of a residential tower, incurring ₹5,00,00,000 of interest during the year, while temporarily investing surplus drawn-down funds to earn ₹40,00,000 of interest income before use.
Borrowing costs ELIGIBLE for capitalisation = ₹5,00,00,000 − ₹40,00,000 (temporary investment income) = ₹4,60,00,000 – an IDENTICAL net capitalisation calculation to how IAS 23 would treat the same specific borrowing scenario globally.
5

Capitalisation – General Borrowings

Identical weighted average capitalisation rate approach to IAS 23
Where general borrowings fund a qualifying asset, a WEIGHTED AVERAGE capitalisation rate is applied to expenditure on the asset, capped at actual borrowing costs incurred – IDENTICAL methodology to IAS 23.
6

Commencement, Suspension, Cessation of Capitalisation

Identical three-condition test to IAS 23
Capitalisation begins when expenditure/borrowing costs are being incurred AND activities to prepare the asset are in progress; SUSPENDED during extended periods of interruption; CEASES when substantially all activities to prepare the asset are complete – IDENTICAL to IAS 23.
7

Indian Infrastructure & Real Estate Sector Context

A practically prominent Indian application area
India's LARGE infrastructure, power, and real estate development sectors frequently involve MULTI-YEAR construction periods for qualifying assets, making Ind AS 23's capitalisation mechanics genuinely significant for these entities' reported asset costs and profitability during the construction phase. Real estate developers in particular must carefully track project-specific borrowings against Ind AS 115's control transfer criteria (see Ind AS 115 poster) to ensure capitalised interest aligns correctly with the asset's construction timeline.
8

Investment Income Offset

Identical to IAS 23
Investment income earned on the TEMPORARY investment of specific borrowings, PENDING their expenditure on the qualifying asset, is DEDUCTED from borrowing costs eligible for capitalisation – IDENTICAL to IAS 23.
