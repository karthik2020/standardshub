Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why presentation itself is a Framework issue
The Conceptual Framework states the objective of general purpose financial statements is to provide information useful to users in making decisions about providing resources to the entity. IAS 1 is the standard that operationalises HOW that information is structured, so it is presented in a way that is understandable and comparable.
HOW IAS 1 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK'S QUALITATIVE CHARACTERISTICS
Comparability — consistent structure, consistent presentation and classification from one period to the next, and comparative information for the prior period Understandability — clear titles, aggregation of items of a similar nature, and separate presentation of dissimilar items when material Faithful representation — fair presentation, achieved through compliance with IFRS Standards (and, in extremely rare cases, a departure where compliance would be misleading)
IAS 1 does not prescribe a rigid single-format template (unlike some local GAAPs) — it sets out minimum line items and overarching principles, leaving considerable structural flexibility to preparers.
Measurement vs presentation — a key distinction
IAS 1 is almost entirely a PRESENTATION standard — it does not prescribe how individual items are recognised or measured (those rules sit in other standards). Its role is to determine structure, classification, and disclosure of information already measured elsewhere.
Framework conceptIAS 1's role
Elements of financial statementsPrescribes WHERE assets, liabilities, equity, income, expenses appear and how they're grouped
Going concernA fundamental PRESENTATION assumption IAS 1 requires management to explicitly assess
Qualitative characteristics in action: the going concern assessment and materiality-driven aggregation/disaggregation decisions required by IAS 1 exist specifically to prevent obscuring material information with immaterial detail (or vice versa) — a direct application of the Framework's materiality concept.

Scope & Applicability

What IAS 1 covers
IAS 1 sets out the overall requirements for the presentation of general purpose financial statements, guidelines for their structure, and minimum requirements for their content, to ensure comparability both with the entity's own prior periods and with other entities.
APPLIES TO
All general purpose financial statements prepared and presented in accordance with IFRS Standards, for profit-oriented entities, including consolidated financial statements of a group.
DOES NOT APPLY TO
The structure and content of condensed interim financial statements → IAS 34 Recognition, measurement, or disclosure of specific transactions and events → governed by the RELEVANT specific standard (e.g. IFRS 15, IAS 16), not IAS 1
IAS 1 also does NOT use the term "balance sheet" or "income statement" as mandatory titles — entities may use these traditional titles instead of "statement of financial position" / "statement of profit or loss," as long as they are not misleading.
Key definitions you must know
General purpose financial statements
Financial statements intended to meet the needs of users who are not in a position to require an entity to prepare reports tailored to their particular information needs.
Material
Information is material if omitting, misstating, or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence decisions that primary users of general purpose financial statements make on the basis of those statements.
Impracticable
A requirement is impracticable when the entity cannot apply it after making every reasonable effort to do so — the threshold used for exemptions such as restating comparatives.
Notes
Information in addition to that presented in the primary statements — providing narrative descriptions or disaggregation of items, and information about items that do not qualify for recognition.
Other comprehensive income (OCI)
Items of income and expense (including reclassification adjustments) that are not recognised in profit or loss as required or permitted by other IFRS Standards.

Overall Considerations — The Core Principles

Six overarching principles governing every set of financial statements
1. Fair presentation & compliance with IFRS
Fair presentation requires faithful representation of transactions per the Framework's definitions and recognition criteria. Compliance with IFRS is presumed to result in fair presentation; an explicit and unreserved statement of IFRS compliance is required.
2. Going concern
Management must assess the entity's ability to continue as a going concern; financial statements are prepared on a going concern basis unless management intends/has no realistic alternative but to liquidate or cease trading.
3. Accrual basis of accounting
All financial statements EXCEPT the statement of cash flows are prepared using the accrual basis.
4. Materiality & aggregation
Each material class of similar items is presented separately; dissimilar items are presented separately unless immaterial. Immaterial information should not obscure material information.
5. Offsetting
Assets and liabilities, and income and expenses, are NOT offset unless required or permitted by an IFRS Standard.
6. Frequency of reporting & comparatives
Complete financial statements are presented at least annually; comparative information is disclosed for all amounts reported in the current period, plus comparative narrative/descriptive information where relevant.
"True and fair override" is available only in extremely rare circumstances — where compliance with a specific IFRS requirement would be so misleading it conflicts with the Framework's objective. When invoked, extensive disclosure of the departure, its nature, and its financial effect is mandatory.

A Complete Set of Financial Statements

The five (or six) required components
A statement of financial position as at the end of the period A statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income for the period A statement of changes in equity for the period A statement of cash flows for the period Notes, comprising significant accounting policies and other explanatory information Comparative information for the preceding period (a "third" statement of financial position at the beginning of the preceding period is required ONLY when a retrospective restatement, reclassification, or change in accounting policy occurs)
The "third SOFP" requirement is one of the most frequently tested technical points in IAS 1 — it applies on retrospective application of a new policy, retrospective restatement, or reclassification, and it must have a material effect.
Worked example — third SOFP trigger
Example 1: Retrospective restatement
A company with a 31 December year end discovers a material prior period error in its 20X2 financial statements during preparation of its 20X3 financial statements. The correction is applied retrospectively per IAS 8.
The 20X3 financial statements must present THREE statements of financial position: as at 31 Dec 20X3, 31 Dec 20X2 (restated), AND 1 Jan 20X2 (restated, i.e. the beginning of the earliest comparative period) — together with related notes.
Only the SOFP triples; the statement of profit or loss/OCI, changes in equity, and cash flows still only require the standard TWO periods (current + one comparative) even when a third SOFP is triggered.

Statement of Financial Position — Structure

Minimum line items (illustrative, not exhaustive)
ASSETS
Property, plant and equipment Investment property Intangible assets Financial assets Investments accounted for using the equity method Biological assets Inventories Trade and other receivables Cash and cash equivalents Assets classified as held for sale (IFRS 5)
LIABILITIES & EQUITY
Trade and other payables Provisions Financial liabilities Current tax liabilities/assets Deferred tax liabilities/assets (always non-current) Liabilities in a disposal group held for sale (IFRS 5) Non-controlling interests, presented within equity Issued capital and reserves attributable to owners of the parent
Presentation format choice
IAS 1 does NOT mandate a specific format or order for the SOFP — entities present either a current/non-current split OR a liquidity-based presentation (broadly in order of liquidity), whichever provides reliable and more relevant information.
Financial institutions (banks, insurers) typically use liquidity-based presentation since a current/non-current split would not provide relevant information for their operating cycle — this is a common exam scenario for justifying the alternative format.
DEFERRED TAX — ALWAYS NON-CURRENT
When a current/non-current split IS used, deferred tax assets/liabilities must always be classified as non-current, regardless of the expected timing of reversal.

Statement of Profit or Loss & Other Comprehensive Income

Structure & presentation choices
SINGLE STATEMENT OR TWO STATEMENTS — POLICY CHOICE
Single statement — one statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, profit/loss as a subtotal flowing into total comprehensive income Two statements — a separate statement of profit or loss, followed by a statement beginning with profit/loss and presenting OCI
EXPENSE CLASSIFICATION — POLICY CHOICE
By nature — e.g. depreciation, raw materials, employee benefits, advertising (no cost-of-sales line) By function — e.g. cost of sales, distribution costs, administrative expenses (requires additional nature-based disclosure of depreciation/employee benefits in the notes)
The "by function" method requires MORE disclosure overall, since nature-based information (e.g. total depreciation) must still be given in the notes even though the primary statement is function-based.
OCI — classification into two groups
OCI itemRecycled to SOPL?
Revaluation surplus (IAS 16/38)Never (may transfer within equity to retained earnings)
Remeasurement of defined benefit plans (IAS 19)Never
FVOCI equity investments (IFRS 9 irrevocable election)Never
Exchange differences on translating foreign operations (IAS 21)Yes, on disposal
Cash flow hedge — effective portion (IFRS 9)Yes, generally
FVOCI debt instruments (IFRS 9)Yes, on derecognition
OCI items are presented grouped by whether they will/will not be reclassified ("recycled") to profit or loss in a future period — a mandatory presentation split under IAS 1.

Statement of Changes in Equity

Required content & worked reconciliation
Total comprehensive income for the period, showing separately amounts attributable to owners of the parent and to non-controlling interests For each component of equity, the effects of retrospective application or restatement recognised per IAS 8 For each component of equity, a reconciliation between the opening and closing carrying amount, separately disclosing changes from: profit or loss; each item of OCI; and transactions with owners in their capacity as owners (contributions, distributions, changes in ownership interests) Amount of dividends recognised as distributions, and the related amount per share, either on the face of the statement or in the notes
Example 2: Simple equity roll-forward
Retained earnings opening balance $500,000; profit for the year $120,000; dividends declared $40,000; a prior period error correction (IAS 8) reduces opening retained earnings by $15,000.
Restated opening balance = 500,000 − 15,000 = $485,000
Closing retained earnings = 485,000 + 120,000 − 40,000 = $565,000
The $15,000 restatement is shown as a separate line, NOT buried within "profit for the year" — a key presentation distinction.

Notes & Accounting Policies

Structure and ordering of notes
Present information about the basis of preparation and specific accounting policies used Disclose information required by IFRS Standards not presented elsewhere in the primary statements Provide information relevant to understanding the primary statements, but not presented within them
Notes are normally presented in a systematic manner, cross-referenced to the related items in the primary statements, though IAS 1 no longer mandates a single fixed ordering — entities have flexibility to present notes in the order that best communicates their financial statements.
Judgements and estimates disclosure
TWO DISTINCT DISCLOSURE REQUIREMENTS — OFTEN CONFUSED
Significant judgements
Judgements management has made in applying accounting policies that have the most significant effect on amounts recognised (e.g. classifying a lease, determining control of an investee)
Sources of estimation uncertainty
Key assumptions about the future, and other major sources of estimation uncertainty, with a significant risk of causing a material adjustment within the NEXT financial year
The "next financial year" time horizon is specific to the estimation-uncertainty disclosure and is a commonly tested distinguishing detail.

Current vs Non-Current Classification

Current asset — any ONE of four tests
Expected to be realised, or is held for sale/consumption, in the entity's normal operating cycle Held primarily for the purpose of trading Expected to be realised within twelve months after the reporting period Is cash or a cash equivalent, unless restricted from being exchanged/used to settle a liability for at least twelve months
All other assets are classified as non-current. The "operating cycle" test means inventory and trade receivables are current even where the cycle exceeds twelve months (e.g. long-term construction contracts).
Current liability — any ONE of four tests
Expected to be settled in the entity's normal operating cycle Held primarily for the purpose of trading Due to be settled within twelve months after the reporting period The entity does NOT have an unconditional right, at the end of the reporting period, to defer settlement for at least twelve months
Loan covenant breach at year end: if a loan becomes repayable on demand due to a covenant breach existing AT the reporting date, it is classified as CURRENT — even if the lender later agrees (after year end but before authorisation for issue) not to demand repayment, UNLESS the right to defer settlement for at least 12 months existed AT the reporting date itself. This is one of the most heavily examined IAS 1 classification traps.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether the going concern basis remains appropriate, and whether material uncertainties exist requiring disclosure Whether an item is material enough to warrant separate presentation or disclosure Classification of the SOFP by current/non-current vs order of liquidity Determining whether the "true and fair override" applies (extremely rare)
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Whether an unconditional right to defer settlement of a liability exists for at least 12 months at the reporting date The length of the "normal operating cycle" where it is not clearly identifiable (default assumed as 12 months)
Material uncertainties related to going concern must be disclosed even when management still concludes the going concern basis is appropriate — disclosure of the uncertainty itself is separate from, and does not necessarily follow, a conclusion that the entity is NOT a going concern.

IFRS vs US GAAP — Key Differences

Comparison — IAS 1 vs US GAAP
FeatureIFRS (IAS 1)US GAAP
SOFP orderFree choice: liquidity order OR current/non-current, whichever more relevantTypically presented in decreasing order of liquidity as standard practice
Extraordinary itemsProhibited — no separate "extraordinary item" classification permittedAlso prohibited (converged since 2015), though unusual/infrequent items may still be separately disclosed
OCI presentationSingle OR two-statement approach, both permittedSingle OR two-statement approach also permitted (largely converged)
Expense classificationFree choice: by nature or by functionSEC rules generally require presentation by function (e.g. cost of sales)
Minimum reporting periodsStatement of financial position: 2 years (3 if restated); SOPL/equity/cash flows: 2 yearsSEC generally requires 2 years for SOFP, 3 years for SOPL/cash flows for larger registrants — a stricter comparative requirement
Despite convergence efforts eliminating major differences (e.g. extraordinary items), the SEC's typically stricter comparative-period requirements for the income statement and cash flow statement remain a practical difference multinational filers must navigate.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Total comprehensive income
The change in equity during a period resulting from transactions and other events, other than those changes resulting from transactions with owners in their capacity as owners — profit/loss plus OCI.
Reclassification adjustment
Amounts reclassified to profit or loss in the current period that were recognised in OCI in the current or previous periods.
Owners
Holders of instruments classified as equity.
Normal operating cycle
The time between acquisition of assets for processing and their realisation in cash/cash equivalents — assumed to be twelve months when not clearly identifiable.
Authorisation date
The date the financial statements are authorised for issue — the cut-off used for classifying liabilities and assessing subsequent events (IAS 10).
Fair presentation
Faithful representation of the effects of transactions, other events, and conditions in accordance with the Framework's definitions and recognition criteria for assets, liabilities, income, and expenses.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position (balance sheet) SOPL Statement of profit or loss (income statement) OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
