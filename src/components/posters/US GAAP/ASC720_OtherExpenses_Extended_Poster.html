Conceptual Framework Linkage

A residual home for specific expense recognition questions
Not every expense recognition question fits neatly into a broader Topic — ASC 720 exists as a "CATCH-ALL" housing several SPECIFIC, narrowly scoped expense questions that arose over time and needed clarification: start-up costs, charitable contributions, real estate taxes, insurance arrangements, and business reengineering costs. Each subtopic answers a genuinely DIFFERENT question, unified only by not belonging anywhere more specific.
2

Scope — a Residual Expense Topic

Several genuinely distinct subtopics
ASC 720 covers: START-UP costs (including organisation costs), INSURANCE costs (including retrospective/loss-sensitive arrangements), CONTRIBUTIONS made (charitable giving), REAL ESTATE TAXES, ADVERTISING costs (cross-referencing ASC 340-20), and BUSINESS/TECHNOLOGY REENGINEERING costs — a genuinely varied collection unified by their residual, "doesn't fit elsewhere" nature.
3

Start-Up Costs (720-15)

Always expensed as incurred — a clear, bright-line rule
Costs of START-UP ACTIVITIES — including ORGANISATION costs (legal and state fees to form a new entity) — are EXPENSED AS INCURRED, with NO capitalisation permitted, regardless of the activity's expected future benefit. IAS 38 similarly generally prohibits capitalising pre-opening/start-up costs (with a specific prohibition on capitalising "start-up costs" as intangible assets), reaching broadly the SAME outcome, even though drafted somewhat differently.
4

Advertising Costs (720-35) — Cross-Reference

Expensed as incurred, with the direct-response exception
Advertising costs are, as a general rule, expensed as incurred — with the narrow DIRECT-RESPONSE advertising capitalisation exception addressed in full in the ASC 340 poster (Section 3–4 there). ASC 720-35 primarily serves as the cross-reference point tying this general expense category together.
5

Contributions Made (720-25)

Recognised when the promise is made, not when paid
An UNCONDITIONAL promise to give (a charitable pledge) is recognised as an EXPENSE (and a corresponding liability) when the PROMISE is MADE — not necessarily when the cash is actually paid. If the promise is CONDITIONAL on a future event or barrier, it is NOT recognised until the condition is substantially met. This is a fairly DETAILED, specific US Topic — IFRS has no single dedicated standard addressing contributions MADE by a for-profit entity in this same explicit detail, with the substance generally following general liability recognition principles instead.
6

Insurance Costs (720-20)

Including retrospective/loss-sensitive arrangements
ASC 720-20 addresses accounting for various insurance cost arrangements, including RETROSPECTIVE or "loss-sensitive" insurance contracts, where the ultimate premium cost depends on the entity's ACTUAL claims experience during the coverage period — requiring an estimate of the expected retrospective premium adjustment to be accrued alongside the base premium expense.
7

Real Estate Taxes (720-30)

Accrued over the taxing authority's fiscal period
Real estate taxes are accrued RATABLY over the fiscal period of the TAXING AUTHORITY they relate to — which may NOT align with the reporting entity's OWN fiscal year — a specific, practical accrual convention ensuring the tax expense is matched to the correct underlying period even when the two fiscal calendars differ.
8

Business and Technology Reengineering Costs (720-45)

Generally expensed, with a software capitalisation carve-out
Costs incurred in connection with BUSINESS PROCESS REENGINEERING activities (often bundled with major IT system implementations) are generally EXPENSED as incurred, UNLESS they meet the SPECIFIC software capitalisation criteria under ASC 350-40 — a narrow clarifying Topic resolving a diversity-in-practice issue that arose during the 1990s wave of large enterprise system implementations.
9

Worked Example — Contribution Expense Recognition

An unconditional multi-year pledge
Example: A $500,000 pledge to a university
An entity's board unconditionally pledges $500,000 to a university, payable $100,000 per year over 5 years, with no conditions attached beyond the passage of time.
Because the promise is UNCONDITIONAL, the FULL present value of the $500,000 pledge is recognised as an EXPENSE and a LIABILITY in the period the pledge is MADE — not spread over the 5 years of actual cash payments.
10

Worked Example — Start-Up Cost Expensing

No capitalisation, regardless of expected benefit
Example: Opening a new retail location
An entity incurs $250,000 of costs preparing to open a new retail store — including staff training, initial marketing, and administrative setup — before the store opens for business.
The FULL $250,000 is EXPENSED AS INCURRED — none of it may be capitalised and deferred to match against the store's future expected revenue, regardless of how confident management is in the store's future success.

ASC 720 vs IFRS — a Dispersed Comparison

Comparison — ASC 720 vs IFRS equivalents
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 720)IFRS equivalent(s)
Start-up cost expensingExplicit, bright-line ruleSimilar outcome under IAS 38's general intangible recognition prohibition for such costs
Contributions madeSpecific, detailed named TopicNo single equivalent — addressed via general liability recognition principles
Real estate tax accrual conventionExplicitly matched to the taxing authority's fiscal periodNo equivalently explicit named convention, though similar accrual practice is common
ASC 720 exemplifies the Codification's granular, sub-topic-by-sub-topic organisational style — IFRS reaches broadly similar substantive outcomes for most of these items, but without maintaining an equally detailed, separately numbered catalogue of narrow expense questions.
