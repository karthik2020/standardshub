Objective, Scope & the Four Categories of Employee Benefits (paras. 1–7)

Objective
Requires an entity to recognize (a) a LIABILITY when an employee has provided service in exchange for future benefits, and (b) an EXPENSE when the entity consumes the economic benefits/service potential from that service.
Applies to ALL employee benefits EXCEPT share-based transactions (governed by the relevant share-based payment standard) Excludes reporting BY retirement benefit plans themselves (IPSAS 49) and non-employment-related social security programs
"Employees" includes KEY MANAGEMENT PERSONNEL as defined in IPSAS 20, and covers full-time, part-time, permanent, casual, and temporary service (para. 7).
The four categories (para. 5)
CategoryExamples
Short-termWages, paid annual/sick leave, bonuses, non-monetary benefits — settled within 12 months
Post-employmentPensions, lump sums, post-employment medical/life insurance
Other long-termLong-service/sabbatical leave, jubilee benefits, long-term disability
TerminationBenefits from ending employment — entity-initiated or employee-accepted offer
Employee benefits arise from FORMAL plans/agreements, LEGISLATIVE requirements (national/state/industry/multi-employer plans, social security), OR informal practices creating a CONSTRUCTIVE obligation — where the entity has no realistic alternative but to pay (para. 4).
2

Definitions (para. 8)

Plan classification
Defined contribution plans
The entity pays FIXED contributions into a separate fund, with NO legal or constructive obligation for further contributions if the fund proves insufficient.
Defined benefit plans
Post-employment benefit plans OTHER than defined contribution plans — the entity bears the risk.
Multi-employer / state plans
Plans pooling assets from unrelated entities (multi-employer), or established by legislation to operate as if multi-employer for defined economic categories (state plans).
The net defined benefit liability (asset) building blocks
Deficit or surplus
Present value of the defined benefit obligation LESS the fair value of plan assets.
Asset ceiling
The present value of economic benefits available as refunds from the plan or reduced future contributions.
Net defined benefit liability (asset)
The deficit or surplus, adjusted for any effect of limiting a net asset to the asset ceiling.
3

Short-Term Employee Benefits — Recognition (paras. 11–12)

Simple, undiscounted, immediate
Recognize the UNDISCOUNTED amount expected to be paid for service rendered: a LIABILITY (accrued expense) net of amounts paid — or an ASSET (prepaid expense) if payments already exceed what's due, to the extent recoverable via future reduction or refund
Recognized as an EXPENSE, unless another Standard requires/permits capitalizing it into an asset's cost (e.g. IPSAS 12 inventory, IPSAS 45 PP&E) (para. 11).
4

Short-Term Paid Absences & Profit-Sharing/Bonus Plans (paras. 13–24)

Accumulating vs non-accumulating absences
TypeTreatment
AccumulatingRecognized as employees render service that increases FUTURE entitlement — whether vesting (cash payable on leaving) or not
Non-accumulatingRecognized only when the ABSENCE actually occurs (e.g. maternity/paternity, jury duty) — no liability accrues from service alone
Accumulating absences are measured at the ADDITIONAL amount expected to be paid from the unused entitlement accumulated at period-end (para. 16).
Profit-sharing & bonus plans — a public-sector-relevant test
A present LEGAL or CONSTRUCTIVE obligation exists (no realistic alternative but to pay) A RELIABLE ESTIMATE can be made — via a formula in the plan's formal terms, a pre-authorization determination, or clear past-practice evidence
Public sector bonus plans are typically tied to SERVICE DELIVERY objectives or financial performance measures (revenue generation, budgetary targets) rather than profit — genuinely less common than in the private sector, but do appear in commercially-operating segments (para. 20). Recognized as an EXPENSE, never a distribution of profit (para. 23). If not settled within 12 months → reclassified as an OTHER LONG-TERM benefit instead (§13) (para. 24).

Post-Employment Benefits — Defined Contribution vs Defined Benefit (paras. 52–56)

Defined contribution — simple, symmetric to short-term benefits
Recognize the CONTRIBUTION PAYABLE as a liability (or asset, if overpaid) and an EXPENSE — discounted only if settlement extends beyond 12 months
The entity's obligation is limited to the amount it agrees to contribute — the FUND bears investment/actuarial risk, not the entity. Disclosure: the expense amount recognized for defined contribution plans (paras. 53–56).
Defined benefit — the entity underwrites the risk
Whether funded or unfunded, the entity is IN SUBSTANCE underwriting the actuarial and investment risk — the fund's ability to pay depends on its own performance AND the entity's ability/willingness to cover any shortfall. Consequently, the expense recognized is NOT simply the period's contribution — it requires the full actuarial apparatus covered in §6–§10 (paras. 57–58).

Defined Benefit Plans — the Four-Step Accounting Process (paras. 59–63)

Step by step (para. 59)
1. Determine the deficit/surplus — projected unit credit method (§7) to estimate the ultimate benefit cost, discounted (§8), less the fair value of plan assets (§10) 2. Determine the net defined benefit liability/asset — the deficit/surplus, adjusted for the ASSET CEILING 3. Amounts to SURPLUS OR DEFICIT — current service cost, past service cost and settlement gains/losses (§9), net interest (§10) 4. REMEASUREMENTS to net assets/equity — actuarial gains/losses, return on plan assets (excluding amounts in net interest), and asset-ceiling effect changes
Each material plan is accounted for SEPARATELY (para. 59). The liability is remeasured with SUFFICIENT REGULARITY that recognized amounts don't materially differ from a period-end valuation (para. 60). A qualified actuary is ENCOURAGED, not required (para. 61). The entity accounts for BOTH its legal obligation AND any CONSTRUCTIVE obligation arising from informal practices (para. 63) — the same test as elsewhere in this library.

Actuarial Valuation — the Projected Unit Credit Method (paras. 68–76)

One additional unit of entitlement, each period
Each period of service = an additional UNIT of benefit entitlement, measured SEPARATELY and built up into the final obligation
MANDATORY method (para. 69). The WHOLE obligation is discounted, even the portion expected to settle within 12 months (para. 71).
Attributing benefit — usually the plan's formula, sometimes straight-line
Default: attribute benefit per the PLAN'S OWN benefit formula EXCEPTION: if later years' service leads to a MATERIALLY HIGHER benefit level than earlier years → attribute STRAIGHT-LINE instead, from first-benefit-accrual date to the date further service yields no material additional benefit
An obligation arises even for NON-VESTED benefits — as vesting requirements shrink each period, a constructive obligation builds; the PROBABILITY some employees won't vest affects MEASUREMENT, not whether the obligation exists at all (para. 74). Where benefit = a constant proportion of FINAL salary, future salary increases affect the SETTLEMENT amount but don't create additional benefit units (para. 76).

Actuarial Assumptions & the Discount Rate (paras. 77, 85–88)

Unbiased and mutually compatible
Actuarial assumptions cover DEMOGRAPHIC variables (turnover, mortality) and FINANCIAL variables (future salary increases, medical cost trends) — all must be UNBIASED (neither imprudently optimistic nor excessively conservative) and MUTUALLY COMPATIBLE (para. 77).
The discount rate — a genuine IPSAS/IAS 19 divergence
The discount rate reflects the TIME VALUE OF MONEY — matched in currency and term to the estimated obligation — but NOT actuarial/investment/entity-specific credit risk
Unlike IAS 19 (which mandates high-quality corporate bond yields), IPSAS 39 leaves the SOURCE open to judgment — government bonds, high-quality corporate bonds, or another instrument, whichever best approximates time value of money in the entity's jurisdiction. Where no deep market exists at the needed maturities, current market rates are used for shorter terms, EXTRAPOLATED along the yield curve for longer maturities (para. 88) — see §16 for the full IAS 19 comparison.

Past Service Cost, Curtailments & Settlements (paras. 101–110)

Remeasure using current everything
When a plan AMENDMENT, CURTAILMENT, or SETTLEMENT occurs, the net defined benefit liability/asset is remeasured using CURRENT fair value of plan assets and CURRENT actuarial assumptions — reflecting both the before-and-after benefit/asset position (para. 101).
PAST SERVICE COST = change in the present value of the obligation from a plan AMENDMENT or CURTAILMENT — recognized as an EXPENSE at the earlier of the amendment/curtailment date, or when related IPSAS 19 restructuring costs/termination benefits are recognized
What past service cost EXCLUDES (para. 110)
Differences between actual vs. assumed salary increases (already captured by projected-salary assumptions) Discretionary pension increase over/under-estimates, where a constructive obligation to grant them exists Surplus-driven benefit improvements the entity is obliged to grant (an ACTUARIAL loss instead) Vested-benefit increases from employees simply completing vesting requirements (already captured as current service cost)
Past service cost can be POSITIVE (benefits introduced/enhanced) or NEGATIVE (benefits withdrawn/reduced) — a simultaneous reduction-and-increase for the same employees is treated as ONE net change (paras. 108–109). A settlement occurring WITH a plan amendment/curtailment need not be separately distinguished (para. 102) — but where amendment precedes settlement, past service cost is recognized FIRST (para. 102).

Plan Assets, Net Interest & the Asset Ceiling (paras. 125–132)

Net interest — a single number to surplus/deficit
Net interest = net defined benefit liability (asset) × the discount rate, determined at the START of the period
Can be viewed as interest EXPENSE on the obligation, interest REVENUE on plan assets, and interest on the asset-ceiling effect, all netted into one figure — recognized in SURPLUS OR DEFICIT.
Actuarial gains/losses & the return on plan assets — remeasurements
ACTUARIAL gains/losses arise from ASSUMPTION changes and EXPERIENCE adjustments — turnover, mortality, salary/medical cost trends, benefit-payment-option assumptions, and discount rate changes (para. 130) They EXCLUDE changes from plan amendments, curtailments, or settlements — those are past service cost or settlement gains/losses instead (§9) (para. 131) RETURN ON PLAN ASSETS is net of the plan's own management costs and any tax payable BY THE PLAN (excluding tax already built into the obligation's actuarial assumptions) (para. 132)
All THREE remeasurement components — actuarial gains/losses, return on plan assets (excluding the net-interest portion), and asset-ceiling effect changes (excluding the net-interest portion) — go to NET ASSETS/EQUITY, never recycled through surplus or deficit later (para. 129).

Presentation — Offsetting & Component Placement (paras. 133–136)

Offsetting across plans — the same two-part test
A LEGALLY ENFORCEABLE right to use one plan's surplus to settle another plan's obligations INTENDS to settle net, or to realize the surplus and settle the obligation SIMULTANEOUSLY
Deliberately mirrors the IPSAS 28 financial-instrument offsetting criteria (§8 of that Standard's poster) (para. 134).
No prescribed current/non-current split, no prescribed line item
IPSAS 39 does NOT specify whether post-employment assets/liabilities should be split current/non-current (para. 135), and does NOT prescribe exactly where service cost and net interest are presented within surplus or deficit — IPSAS 1's general presentation principles govern that placement instead (para. 136).

Disclosure Requirements — Defined Benefit Plans (paras. 137–149)

Three disclosure objectives (para. 137)
EXPLAIN plan characteristics and associated risks IDENTIFY and explain the amounts arising in the financial statements DESCRIBE the effect on the amount, timing, and uncertainty of future cash flows
Same judgment-based approach as elsewhere in this library: consider the level of detail, emphasis, aggregation/disaggregation needed to meet the objective — supplement with additional information if the specified disclosures alone fall short (paras. 138–139).
Disaggregation — separating materially different risks
Entities assess whether plans/plan groups with MATERIALLY DIFFERENT risks warrant disaggregated disclosure — by geography, plan type (flat-salary, final-salary, medical), regulatory environment, reporting segment, or funding arrangement (para. 140). Suggested analyses: active vs. deferred members vs. pensioners; vested vs. accrued-not-vested benefits; conditional benefits vs. future-salary-linked amounts (para. 139).

Other Long-Term Employee Benefits (paras. 155–161)

Examples, and why the method is SIMPLER
Long-term paid/sabbatical leave, jubilee benefits, long-term disability, profit-sharing/bonuses not settled within 12 months, deferred remuneration, and compensation until re-employment (para. 155)
Measurement here is USUALLY less uncertain than post-employment benefits — so this Standard requires a SIMPLIFIED method that does NOT split out net-assets/equity remeasurements the way defined benefit plans do (para. 156). A REBUTTABLE PRESUMPTION exists that long-term disability payments are similarly less uncertain — if rebutted, the FULL defined-benefit machinery (§6–§10) applies instead (para. 157). This is a genuine IPSAS/IAS 19 divergence, covered further in §16.
One net number, straight to surplus or deficit
Recognize the NET TOTAL of service cost + net interest + ALL remeasurements → straight to surplus or deficit (unless capitalized under another IPSAS)
Applies the SAME recognition/measurement mechanics as defined benefit plans (paras. 58–100, 115–121) — but with EVERYTHING routed through surplus or deficit, none split to net assets/equity (para. 158–159). For length-of-service-linked disability benefits, the obligation reflects payment PROBABILITY and expected duration; for flat-rate benefits, cost is recognized only when the disabling EVENT occurs (para. 160).
14

Termination Benefits (paras. 162–170)

Why treated separately — the triggering EVENT differs
Termination benefits arise from TERMINATION of employment, not from SERVICE — that's why they're accounted for separately from every other category. They result EITHER from the entity's decision to terminate, OR the employee's acceptance of an offered exchange for termination (para. 162).
⚠️ Benefits from termination AT THE EMPLOYEE's request (without an entity offer), or from mandatory retirement, are POST-EMPLOYMENT benefits, not termination benefits — though the INCREMENTAL benefit above what an employee-requested termination would receive IS a termination benefit (para. 163).
Recognition — the earlier of two triggers (para. 168)
When the entity can NO LONGER WITHDRAW the offer When the entity recognizes related IPSAS 19 restructuring costs
EMPLOYEE-accepted offers: the earlier of ACCEPTANCE, or when a withdrawal restriction (legal/regulatory/contractual) takes effect (para. 169) ENTITY-initiated terminations: when a termination PLAN has been communicated to affected employees, meeting specific criteria (unlikely to change significantly, identifies affected employees/jobs/locations, and a near-term completion timeframe) (para. 170)

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Comparison with IAS 19

Comparison — IPSAS 39 vs IAS 19
IPSAS 39 is drawn primarily from IAS 19 (issued 2011, amendments to 31 December 2018), Employee Benefits.
TopicDifference
Public sector bonus plansIPSAS 39 adds specific guidance (§4) — a public-sector need IAS 19 doesn't address
Discount rateIAS 19 REQUIRES high-quality corporate bond yields; IPSAS 39 requires a rate reflecting time value of money generally (government bonds, corporate bonds, or another instrument) — PLUS requires disclosure of the basis used (§8)
Long-term disability rebuttable presumptionIPSAS 39 includes one (§13); IAS 19 does not
Remeasurement destinationIPSAS 39: NET ASSETS/EQUITY. IAS 19: OTHER COMPREHENSIVE INCOME — reflecting the different statement architecture between the frameworks
IFRIC 14 guidanceIPSAS 39 embeds it AUTHORITATIVELY as Appendix AA (asset ceiling / minimum funding interaction); under IFRS, IFRIC 14 is a separate document
Terminology"Revenue," "controlling entity," "controlled entities" (IPSAS 39) vs "income," "parent," "subsidiaries" (IAS 19)

Statement / Process Mapping

Classification decision map
1. Is it a TERMINATION benefit (arises from ending employment, not service)? → §14, recognized at the earlier of two specific triggers 2. Otherwise, will it settle WITHIN 12 months of the related service? → SHORT-TERM: undiscounted, simple accrual (§3–§4) 3. Is it POST-EMPLOYMENT (payable after employment ends)? → determine DEFINED CONTRIBUTION (simple) or DEFINED BENEFIT (full actuarial machinery) (§5) 4. Defined benefit → run the four-step process: deficit/surplus → net liability/asset → surplus/deficit amounts → net-assets/equity remeasurements (§6–§10) 5. Neither short-term, post-employment, nor termination → OTHER LONG-TERM benefit — same recognition/measurement mechanics as defined benefit, but ALL routed through surplus or deficit (no remeasurement split) (§13)

Quick-Reference Glossary

Defined contribution plan
A post-employment plan where the entity pays fixed contributions, with no further obligation if the fund proves insufficient.
Defined benefit plan
Any post-employment plan other than a defined contribution plan — the entity bears actuarial and investment risk.
Current service cost
The increase in the present value of the defined benefit obligation from employee service in the current period.
Past service cost
The change in the present value of the defined benefit obligation from a plan amendment or curtailment.
Actuarial gains and losses
Changes in the present value of the defined benefit obligation from assumption changes and experience adjustments.
Termination benefits
Employee benefits provided in exchange for the termination of employment, rather than for service.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOFPerf Statement of financial performance NA/E Net assets/equity (statement of changes)
