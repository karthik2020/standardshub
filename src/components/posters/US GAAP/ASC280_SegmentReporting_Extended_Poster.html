Conceptual Framework Linkage

The "management approach" – seeing the business through management's own eyes
ASC 280 pioneered the "MANAGEMENT APPROACH" to segment reporting: rather than defining segments using externally imposed criteria (e.g. industry classification codes), segments are identified based on the INTERNAL organisational structure management itself actually uses to run the business and allocate resources – maximising relevance by showing users the business the SAME way management sees it.
The direct model IFRS 8 was built on
When the IASB developed IFRS 8, it deliberately modelled it CLOSELY on the pre-existing US standard (SFAS 131, now ASC 280) – making ASC 280 one of the very few Topics in the entire Codification where an IFRS standard was essentially built by ADOPTING an existing US GAAP standard's core methodology, rather than the two frameworks converging independently or from different starting points.
2

Scope & Applicability

Public entities specifically
ASC 280 applies to PUBLIC entities – those with equity or debt securities traded in a public market, or required to file with a regulatory agency for the purpose of issuing securities. Private companies are NOT required to present segment disclosures, closely mirroring IFRS 8's own scope limitation to entities whose securities are (or are in the process of being) publicly traded.
3

Definition of an Operating Segment

Three defining characteristics
CriterionRequirement
(a)Engages in business activities from which it may earn revenues and incur expenses (including intercompany transactions)
(b)Its operating results are REGULARLY REVIEWED by the entity's chief operating decision maker (CODM) to assess performance and allocate resources
(c)DISCRETE financial information is available for it
These are the SAME three defining criteria used under IFRS 8 – a near word-for-word convergence reflecting the direct modelling relationship described in Section 1.
4

The Chief Operating Decision Maker (CODM)

A function, not necessarily a single named individual
The CODM is a FUNCTION – identifying the person or GROUP of persons responsible for allocating resources and assessing the performance of the entity's operating segments. This is typically the CEO, but could be a group such as the executive management committee – the identification depends on FACTS AND CIRCUMSTANCES, not a fixed job title, exactly mirroring IFRS 8's own functional (rather than titular) CODM concept.
5

Aggregation Criteria

Combining similar operating segments
Two or more operating segments may be AGGREGATED into a single reportable segment if they have SIMILAR ECONOMIC CHARACTERISTICS, AND are similar with respect to a further list of qualitative factors: nature of products/services, nature of production processes, type or class of customer, distribution methods, and regulatory environment. This aggregation option is identical in substance to IFRS 8's own aggregation criteria.
6

The 10% Quantitative Thresholds

Identical thresholds to IFRS 8
TestThreshold
Revenue testSegment's reported revenue (including intersegment) is 10% or more of the combined revenue of all operating segments
Profit or loss testSegment's reported profit or loss is 10% or more of the greater, in absolute amount, of (i) combined reported profit of all profitable segments, or (ii) combined reported loss of all loss-making segments
Assets testSegment's assets are 10% or more of the combined assets of all operating segments
ℹ️ A segment meeting ANY ONE of these three thresholds is a REPORTABLE segment – the SAME three tests, at the SAME 10% threshold, used under IFRS 8, making this one of the most precisely converged quantitative mechanics anywhere across the two frameworks.
7

The 75% Reporting Sufficiency Test

Ensuring enough of the business is actually disclosed
If the TOTAL external revenue reported by all identified reportable segments is LESS THAN 75% of the entity's total consolidated revenue, ADDITIONAL operating segments must be identified as reportable (even if they individually fail the 10% tests) until at least 75% of total revenue is captured within reportable segments – again, the SAME 75% threshold used under IFRS 8.

ASU 2023-07 – Significant Segment Expense Disclosure

A genuinely recent enhancement, ahead of an equivalent IFRS 8 amendment
ASU 2023-07 (effective for fiscal years beginning after 15 December 2023) EXPANDED ASC 280's disclosure requirements, requiring disclosure of SIGNIFICANT SEGMENT EXPENSES that are REGULARLY PROVIDED to the CODM and included in the reported segment profit/loss measure, along with a description of "other" segment items not separately disclosed. This is one of the most SUBSTANTIVE recent enhancements to US segment reporting, going beyond what IFRS 8 currently requires in equivalent detail – though the IASB has separately pursued its own primary financial statements project addressing some related disclosure gaps, without yet reaching an identical amendment.
10

Entity-Wide Disclosures

Required even for single-segment entities
Certain ENTITY-WIDE disclosures are required REGARDLESS of the segment structure – including revenue from external customers for each group of similar products/services, revenue and long-lived assets attributed to the entity's home country versus all foreign countries in aggregate (and by individual foreign country if material), and information about MAJOR CUSTOMERS (a single external customer representing 10% or more of total revenue) – again mirroring IFRS 8's own entity-wide disclosure requirements closely.

Worked Example – Applying the 10% Tests

Identifying reportable segments from raw operating segment data
Example: Four operating segments, testing against the revenue threshold
An entity has four operating segments with total revenue (including intersegment) of $50,000,000, $30,000,000, $15,000,000, and $5,000,000 – combined total $100,000,000.
10% threshold = $10,000,000. Segments A ($50M), B ($30M), and C ($15M) EXCEED the threshold and are reportable on the revenue test alone. Segment D ($5M) does NOT meet the revenue test, but must still be checked against the profit/loss and assets tests before being excluded – if it fails all three, it may be combined into an "All Other" category rather than separately reported, PROVIDED the 75% sufficiency test (Section 7) is still satisfied by the three reportable segments already identified.
12

Worked Example – Identifying the CODM

A management committee, not a single executive
Example: A committee-based CODM structure
An entity's CEO, CFO, and Chief Operating Officer jointly review a standard monthly reporting package broken down by product division, and collectively make resource allocation decisions as an executive committee – no single individual makes these decisions unilaterally.
The CODM here is the EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE (CEO, CFO, COO collectively) – a GROUP, not a single individual – and segments are identified based on the product-division breakdown this GROUP regularly reviews for resource allocation and performance assessment, consistent with ASC 280's functional (not titular) CODM definition.
13

Disclosure Requirements

Summary reference (see Sections 8–10 for full detail)
In addition to the per-segment measures in Section 8, entities must provide a RECONCILIATION of total reportable segment revenues, profit/loss, and assets to the corresponding consolidated totals – ensuring the segment disclosures tie back cleanly to the primary financial statements, consistent with IFRS 8's own reconciliation requirement.

ASC 280 vs IFRS 8

Comparison – ASC 280 vs IFRS 8
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 280)IFRS (IFRS 8)
Management approachOriginated here; adopted virtually unchanged by IFRS 8Directly modelled on ASC 280 – near-identical core methodology
10% quantitative thresholds / 75% sufficiency testYes, as describedIdentical thresholds and mechanics
Significant segment expense disclosureREQUIRED since ASU 2023-07 (effective FY2024)No equivalent amendment yet issued – a current, real-time divergence pending further IASB action
ScopePublic entities (SEC filers/publicly traded securities)Entities whose debt/equity is traded (or in the process of being traded) in a public market – closely parallel
ASC 280 and IFRS 8 remain among the most tightly converged pairs in the entire US GAAP/IFRS landscape – the 2023 US-specific expense disclosure enhancement is the first genuinely notable divergence to emerge in years between these two otherwise near-identical standards.

Quick-reference glossary preview
Operating segment
A component of an entity meeting all three defining criteria in Section 3 – the base unit of analysis before aggregation and reportability testing.
Reportable segment
An operating segment (or aggregation of segments) that meets the quantitative thresholds, or is otherwise designated reportable, and is separately disclosed.
