IFRIC 8 – Scope of IFRS 2 (WITHDRAWN)
Extended reference poster · Withdrawn 2009 · Absorbed into IFRS 2 itself via clarifying amendments · Retained for historical/comparative reference
🚫 WITHDRAWN INTERPRETATION – ABSORBED INTO IFRS 2 – IFRIC 8 clarified that IFRS 2's scope extends to transactions where IDENTIFIABLE consideration received appears LESS than the fair value of equity instruments granted, indicating unidentifiable goods/services were also received. This clarification was WITHDRAWN and folded directly into IFRS 2's own scope paragraphs via amendment, rather than retained as a standalone interpretation. This poster is retained for historical/comparative reference.

Conceptual Framework Linkage (Historical)

A scope clarification later absorbed into the standard itself
IFRIC 8 addressed a genuine practical question: IF an entity grants equity instruments and the IDENTIFIABLE consideration received (if any) appears to be LESS than the fair value of those instruments, does IFRS 2 STILL apply? IFRIC 8 CONFIRMED it does – the shortfall indicates that OTHER (unidentifiable) consideration, typically employee or supplier SERVICES, must also have been received.
WHY THIS ABSORPTION HAPPENED
Closing a scope loophole – WITHOUT this clarification, entities could have argued IFRS 2 did not apply to certain equity-settled arrangements simply because NO explicit employee/supplier service was identified in the arrangement's paperworkConceptual continuity – the IASB later concluded this clarification was FUNDAMENTAL enough to IFRS 2's own scope that it belonged IN the standard itself, not in a separate interpretation requiring cross-reference
IFRIC 8 is a genuinely INSTRUCTIVE example of an interpretation whose entire SUBSTANCE was absorbed almost verbatim into its parent standard's scope section.
The core clarifying logic IFRIC 8 established
Identifiable consideration received < Fair value of equity instruments granted → IFRS 2 applies to the shortfall as unidentifiable goods/services received
ℹ️ This exact logic is now embedded DIRECTLY within IFRS 2's own scope paragraphs.
2

Scope & Applicability (Historical)

What IFRIC 8 covered
IFRIC 8 applied to transactions where an entity made SHARE-BASED PAYMENTS for apparently NIL or INSUFFICIENT identifiable consideration, requiring assessment of whether unidentifiable goods/services had ALSO been received.
Key definitions (historical)
Identifiable consideration
Consideration EXPLICITLY specified/documented in an arrangement – the concept IFRIC 8 required comparing against fair value.
Unidentifiable goods or services
Value received by the entity NOT explicitly documented, inferred from a shortfall between identifiable consideration and fair value.
3

The Core Clarification (Historical)

Closing a scope interpretation gap
IFRIC 8 confirmed IFRS 2's scope was NOT limited to arrangements with EXPLICITLY identified employee/supplier services – a shortfall between identifiable consideration and grant-date fair value was ITSELF sufficient evidence that additional, unidentifiable consideration had been (or would be) received, bringing the arrangement within IFRS 2's scope.
4

Identifiable vs Unidentifiable Consideration (Historical, with worked example)

Historical illustration
Example 1: Shares issued to a strategic business partner
A company issued equity shares with a fair value of $500,000 to a strategic business partner, with the documented consideration being ONLY a vague "strategic cooperation" commitment with no specific identifiable services or cash contribution.
Under IFRIC 8, the ENTIRE $500,000 fair value would be assessed for IFRS 2 recognition – since NO clearly identifiable consideration matched the fair value granted, the SHORTFALL itself was treated as evidence of unidentifiable goods/services received, requiring an expense/asset recognition under IFRS 2 – an analysis that would reach the SAME conclusion under IFRS 2's current absorbed scope guidance today.
5

Transactions with Non-Employees (Historical)

Extending beyond typical employee stock compensation
IFRIC 8 was particularly significant for NON-EMPLOYEE arrangements (e.g. shares issued to consultants, business partners, or in connection with acquisitions of INTANGIBLE items not otherwise recognisable), where the absence of a traditional "service agreement" might otherwise have been argued to place the arrangement OUTSIDE IFRS 2's scope.
6

Measurement When Consideration Appears Insufficient (Historical)

Fair value of equity instruments as the measurement anchor
Consistent with IFRS 2's general measurement hierarchy, the FAIR VALUE of the equity instruments granted was used to measure the transaction where the value of the unidentifiable goods/services could not itself be reliably estimated – IDENTICAL to IFRS 2's own general "cannot estimate fair value of goods/services" fallback.
7

How This Guidance Became Part of IFRS 2

A genuinely instructive absorption case
The IASB concluded IFRIC 8's clarification was so INTEGRAL to correctly applying IFRS 2's scope that it should be stated DIRECTLY within the standard, rather than requiring practitioners to cross-reference a separate interpretation. IFRIC 8 was formally WITHDRAWN in 2009, with its substance incorporated into IFRS 2's own scope paragraphs.

Common Historical Application Areas

Where this analysis was most frequently applied
Shares issued to consultants/advisors for below-market or nominal consideration, shares issued in connection with acquisitions where part of the consideration related to future services, and shares issued to charitable organisations or as sponsorship arrangements – ALL contexts where IFRS 2's current absorbed scope guidance remains equally relevant today.
