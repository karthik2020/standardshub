Conceptual Framework Linkage

A broad, foundational standard governing financial assets and liabilities
ASBJ Statement No. 10 is the CENTRAL J-GAAP standard governing the RECOGNITION, CLASSIFICATION, and MEASUREMENT of financial assets and financial liabilities — securities, derivatives, receivables, payables, and investments. It establishes a CLASSIFICATION-BASED model determining WHETHER a financial instrument is measured at FAIR VALUE (through profit or loss, or through Net Assets) or at AMORTISED/HISTORICAL COST, depending on the instrument's NATURE and the entity's HOLDING PURPOSE.
2

Scope

All companies holding financial assets, financial liabilities, or derivatives
This Statement applies to ALL companies holding SECURITIES, DERIVATIVES, receivables, payables, and OTHER financial instruments, establishing the recognition and measurement framework covering the ENTIRE financial instruments landscape.

4

Trading Securities (売買目的有価証券)

Fair value through the Statement of Income
Securities held PRIMARILY for the purpose of generating profits from SHORT-TERM price fluctuations are classified as TRADING SECURITIES, measured at FAIR VALUE at each reporting date, with ALL changes in fair value recognised IMMEDIATELY in the Statement of Income (損益計算書).
5

Held-to-Maturity Debt Securities (満期保有目的の債券)

Amortised cost, given genuine intent and ability to hold to maturity
Debt securities the entity has the GENUINE INTENT and ABILITY to hold UNTIL maturity are classified as HELD-TO-MATURITY, measured at AMORTISED COST — with any DIFFERENCE between acquisition cost and face value amortised OVER the remaining term using the effective interest method, WITHOUT ongoing fair value remeasurement.
6

Available-for-Sale Securities (その他有価証券)

The residual category — fair valued through Net Assets, not the Statement of Income
Securities NOT classified as trading, held-to-maturity, or subsidiary/affiliate investments fall into this RESIDUAL "OTHER SECURITIES" category (その他有価証券, generally translated "Available-for-Sale"). They are measured at FAIR VALUE, but UNREALISED gains/losses are recognised WITHIN Net Assets — specifically within the "Valuation and Translation Adjustments" component (see the companion ASBJ Statement No. 5 poster) — rather than flowing through the Statement of Income, UNTIL the security is SOLD or otherwise disposed of (or impaired), at which point the ACCUMULATED amount is "recycled" through profit or loss.
7

Investments in Subsidiaries and Affiliates

Held at cost, given the strategic (not trading) nature of the holding
Investments in SUBSIDIARIES and AFFILIATES are held at COST in the entity's OWN (non-consolidated) financial statements, SUBJECT to impairment where a decline in value is judged OTHER than TEMPORARY — reflecting that these holdings represent STRATEGIC, CONTROL-oriented or influence-oriented investments, not instruments held for trading or investment yield purposes.
8

Derivatives and Hedge Accounting

Fair value through profit or loss, with deferral hedge accounting available
DERIVATIVES are generally measured at FAIR VALUE, with changes recognised THROUGH the Statement of Income. Where an entity applies HEDGE ACCOUNTING (designating a derivative as a hedge of a specific risk), the PRINCIPAL method under J-GAAP is "DEFERRAL HEDGE ACCOUNTING" — DEFERRING the derivative's gain/loss WITHIN Net Assets until the HEDGED item's own gain/loss is recognised, MATCHING the timing of both — broadly analogous in PURPOSE to cash flow hedge accounting under IFRS 9.
9

THE KEY DIVERGENCE — a Model Closer to the Old IAS 39 Than to IFRS 9

J-GAAP has not undergone its own equivalent of the IFRS 9 overhaul
Internationally, IFRS REPLACED its OLDER financial instruments standard, IAS 39 — with its FOUR-category classification model broadly SIMILAR in STRUCTURE to J-GAAP's current approach — with IFRS 9, which introduced a FUNDAMENTALLY different classification APPROACH (based on an entity's BUSINESS MODEL and the instrument's CONTRACTUAL CASH FLOW CHARACTERISTICS, rather than management's stated HOLDING INTENTION) and a FORWARD-LOOKING EXPECTED CREDIT LOSS impairment model (replacing the OLDER incurred-loss approach). ASBJ Statement No. 10 has NOT undergone an equivalent comprehensive overhaul — J-GAAP's classification model REMAINS conceptually CLOSER to the OLDER IAS 39 four-category structure than to IFRS 9's current approach. This is a RECURRING pattern seen elsewhere in this poster series (UK GAAP's FRS 102 retaining a simplified, pre-IFRS-9-style approach; FRS 103's insurance model predating IFRS 17) — once an international standard MOVES ON, a NATIONAL standard historically converged with an EARLIER version doesn't always immediately follow. NOTABLY, the ASBJ HAS been actively engaged in REVISING this Statement in RECENT periods (including exposure drafts and a revised standard issued during 2025–2026), suggesting this may be an AREA of ONGOING reform — though the FULL extent and final shape of any such revision should be confirmed against ASBJ's current published materials.

Worked Example — Available-for-Sale Securities Through Valuation and Translation Adjustments

Unrealised gains bypassing the Statement of Income
Example: An unrealised gain on an equity security holding
A company holds an equity security classified as "Other Securities" (Available-for-Sale), acquired for ¥200,000,000. At the reporting date, its fair value has risen to ¥230,000,000.
The ¥30,000,000 unrealised gain is recognised WITHIN Net Assets, in the Valuation and Translation Adjustments component (net of any related tax effect) — NOT in the Statement of Income — until the security is eventually SOLD, at which point the gain is RECYCLED through profit or loss.
11

Worked Example — Impairment of an Available-for-Sale Security

A decline judged other than temporary flows through profit or loss
Example: A significant, sustained price decline
A company holds an "Other Securities" equity investment acquired for ¥150,000,000. Its fair value has fallen to ¥70,000,000, and the decline is judged to be OTHER than temporary (significant and sustained).
The company recognises an IMPAIRMENT LOSS of ¥80,000,000 (¥150,000,000 − ¥70,000,000) DIRECTLY in the Statement of Income — UNLIKE an ordinary unrealised fluctuation (Section 10), a decline judged OTHER than temporary is NOT deferred within Net Assets, reflecting that the loss is no longer considered a MERELY temporary market fluctuation.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Other Securities / Available-for-Sale (その他有価証券)
The residual security classification category, measured at fair value with unrealised changes recognised within Net Assets rather than profit or loss.
Deferral hedge accounting
The principal J-GAAP hedge accounting method, deferring a hedging instrument's gain/loss within Net Assets to match the timing of the hedged item's recognition.
Other than temporary decline
A significant and sustained fall in a security's fair value below cost, triggering immediate impairment recognition through profit or loss.
