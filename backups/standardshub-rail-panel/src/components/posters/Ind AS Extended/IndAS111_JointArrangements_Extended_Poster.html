Conceptual Framework Linkage

Identical substance-over-legal-form philosophy to IFRS 11
Ind AS 111 applies the IDENTICAL principle as IFRS 11 — classification depends on the parties' RIGHTS and OBLIGATIONS arising from the arrangement's STRUCTURE, legal form, and contractual terms, NOT merely the vehicle's legal form (e.g. incorporated vs unincorporated).
HOW IND AS 111 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Faithful representation — reflecting genuine rights to assets/obligations for liabilities (joint operation) vs rights to net assets (joint venture) — identical to IFRS 11Relevance to Indian infrastructure consortia — India's extensive PPP (Public-Private Partnership) and EPC (Engineering, Procurement, Construction) consortium structures make this classification GENUINELY significant and frequently applied in Indian practice
Ind AS 111, like IFRS 11, ABOLISHED proportionate consolidation for jointly controlled entities that qualify as joint ventures — a change ALSO carried through into the Indian context without modification.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 111 covers
Ind AS 111 applies to entities that are a PARTY to a joint arrangement — IDENTICAL scope to IFRS 11.
Genuinely significant for Indian construction, infrastructure, and oil/gas sector companies operating through consortium/JV structures.
Key definitions you must know
Joint control
IDENTICAL definition to IFRS 11 — contractually agreed sharing of control, requiring UNANIMOUS consent of controlling parties for relevant activity decisions.
EPC consortium
A common Indian infrastructure structure where multiple contractors jointly undertake a project, often structured to qualify as a joint operation.

Joint Control — The Threshold Test

Identical unanimous consent requirement to IFRS 11
Joint control exists ONLY when decisions about relevant activities require the UNANIMOUS consent of the parties sharing control — IDENTICAL threshold to IFRS 11, distinct from sole control (Ind AS 110) or mere significant influence (Ind AS 28).

Classification — Joint Operation vs Joint Venture (with worked example)

Identical structure/legal form/contractual terms test
Example 1: An Indian EPC consortium classified as a joint operation
Two Indian construction companies form an UNINCORPORATED consortium to jointly execute a highway construction contract awarded by NHAI (National Highways Authority of India), with the consortium agreement specifying each party's SEPARATE scope of work, direct rights to its own portion of contract assets, and direct obligations for its own portion of liabilities.
Given the UNINCORPORATED structure and each party's DIRECT rights/obligations to specific assets/liabilities, this is classified as a JOINT OPERATION — each party recognises its OWN SHARE of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses directly, an IDENTICAL analysis to how IFRS 11 would classify an equivalent global consortium structure.
5

Accounting for Joint Operations

Identical direct recognition approach to IFRS 11
A joint operator recognises its OWN assets (including its share of jointly held assets), liabilities, revenue, and expenses (including its share of jointly incurred expenses) — IDENTICAL to IFRS 11's own treatment, WITHOUT any separate consolidation or equity accounting mechanism.
6

Accounting for Joint Ventures — Equity Method

Identical to IFRS 11 (referencing Ind AS 28)
Joint ventures are accounted for using the EQUITY METHOD per Ind AS 28 — IDENTICAL to IFRS 11's own cross-reference to IAS 28, with NO proportionate consolidation alternative available.

Indian Infrastructure Consortium Context (with worked example)

A genuinely important practical application area
India's LARGE infrastructure sector (roads, ports, power, urban transit) frequently structures projects through consortium/SPV joint arrangements, often in response to specific government tendering requirements (e.g. NHAI, state PWDs). Classification analysis under Ind AS 111 is GENUINELY consequential for how these entities report their infrastructure exposure.
Example 2: A power sector joint venture SPV
Two Indian power companies jointly incorporate a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) to construct and operate a power plant, with the SPV holding all project assets/liabilities in its OWN name, and the joint venture agreement providing NO direct rights to specific underlying assets for either party.
Given the SEPARATE legal entity structure and absence of direct asset/liability rights, this is classified as a JOINT VENTURE, accounted for using the EQUITY METHOD — an IDENTICAL classification outcome to how IFRS 11 would treat an equivalent global infrastructure SPV structure.

Transactions Between a Joint Operator and the Arrangement

Identical to IFRS 11
Gains/losses on sales/contributions of assets to a joint operation are recognised only to the EXTENT of other parties' interests — IDENTICAL to IFRS 11.
