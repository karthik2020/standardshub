Conceptual Framework Linkage

Helping readers understand where next year's numbers could meaningfully change
ASBJ Statement No. 31 requires companies to DISCLOSE information about SIGNIFICANT accounting ESTIMATES made in the CURRENT year's financial statements that carry a genuine RISK of MATERIAL ADJUSTMENT in the FOLLOWING year – helping readers understand WHERE the financial statements are MOST sensitive to changes in JUDGEMENT or underlying assumptions, BEFORE such changes actually occur.
2

Scope

All companies with significant estimation uncertainty in their financial statements
This Statement applies to ALL companies whose financial statements INCLUDE accounting ESTIMATES carrying a genuine risk of MATERIAL adjustment WITHIN the next fiscal year – a genuinely BROAD scope, given how COMMONLY estimation is embedded throughout financial reporting.
3

Identifying Estimates With a Risk of Material Adjustment

A forward-looking risk assessment, not a backward-looking summary
Companies must IDENTIFY which SPECIFIC accounting estimates – for example, impairment assessments, actuarial assumptions, revenue variable consideration estimates, or deferred tax asset recoverability judgements – carry a genuine, FORWARD-LOOKING risk that a MATERIAL adjustment could be required in the NEXT fiscal year, based on how SENSITIVE the estimate is to changes in underlying ASSUMPTIONS or new INFORMATION.
4

What Must Be Disclosed for Each Identified Estimate

The nature, assumptions, and risk factors – not necessarily a full sensitivity analysis
For EACH identified SIGNIFICANT estimate, companies disclose: the NATURE of the estimate and WHY it carries estimation uncertainty; the METHOD and KEY assumptions used; and INFORMATION helping users understand the extent to which the estimate could CHANGE materially in the FOLLOWING year – WITHOUT necessarily requiring a FULL, quantitative sensitivity analysis in every case, reflecting the PRINCIPLES-BASED nature of this requirement (Section 5).
5

A Principles-Based, Not Overly Prescriptive, Requirement

Judgement-driven disclosure, tailored to each company's own circumstances
This Statement is DELIBERATELY principles-based – it does NOT PRESCRIBE a rigid, one-size-fits-all disclosure FORMAT or REQUIRE quantitative sensitivity ranges in every case. Instead, companies EXERCISE judgement in determining WHICH estimates warrant disclosure and WHAT specific information best conveys the NATURE and EXTENT of the risk to READERS, given their OWN particular circumstances.
6

Distinguishing This From Auditor-Reported Key Audit Matters

A management disclosure, not an auditor reporting requirement
This Statement's disclosure requirement is a MANAGEMENT disclosure OBLIGATION, appearing WITHIN the financial statement notes THEMSELVES – GENUINELY DISTINCT from "KEY AUDIT MATTERS" (KAM), which is a SEPARATE AUDITOR reporting requirement appearing in the AUDIT REPORT, reflecting the AUDITOR's OWN professional judgement about WHAT mattered MOST in the audit. Though the TWO concepts often OVERLAP in PRACTICE (an area of significant estimation uncertainty disclosed by MANAGEMENT under this Statement is often ALSO identified as a key audit matter by the AUDITOR), they serve GENUINELY different reporting PURPOSES and come from DIFFERENT reporting PARTIES.
7

Worked Example – Identifying a Significant Estimate

Recognising an estimate with genuine forward-looking risk
Example: A goodwill impairment assessment
A company carries ¥3,000,000,000 of goodwill relating to a business unit whose recent performance has been below plan, making the recoverable amount assessment highly sensitive to revised future cash flow projections.
The company IDENTIFIES this goodwill impairment ASSESSMENT as a SIGNIFICANT estimate carrying a genuine risk of MATERIAL adjustment in the NEXT fiscal year, warranting DISCLOSURE under this Statement.
8

Worked Example – the Resulting Disclosure

Conveying the nature and risk, without necessarily a full sensitivity table
Example: The disclosure note for the goodwill assessment
Continuing the prior example.
The company discloses: the ¥3,000,000,000 goodwill balance and the CASH-GENERATING UNIT it relates to; the KEY assumptions underlying the recoverable amount calculation (growth rates, discount rate); and a QUALITATIVE statement that a REASONABLY POSSIBLE change in these assumptions COULD result in a MATERIAL impairment being recognised in the NEXT fiscal year – giving readers a CLEAR sense of the RISK WITHOUT necessarily providing a full numeric sensitivity table.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Significant accounting estimate
An estimate in the current year's financial statements carrying a genuine risk of material adjustment in the following fiscal year.
Key Audit Matter (KAM)
A separate auditor reporting concept identifying matters of most significance in the audit, distinct from this Statement's management disclosure requirement, though often overlapping in practice.
