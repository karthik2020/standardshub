IFRIC 15 — Agreements for the Construction of Real Estate (WITHDRAWN)
Extended reference poster · Withdrawn 2018 · Superseded by IFRS 15 · A genuinely significant transition for real estate developers globally · Retained for historical/comparative reference
🚫 WITHDRAWN INTERPRETATION — SUPERSEDED BY IFRS 15 — IFRIC 15 addressed WHETHER an agreement for constructing real estate fell within IAS 11's construction contract scope (continuous/over-time recognition) or IAS 18's goods-sale scope (point-in-time recognition), based on the buyer's ability to specify major structural elements. This binary classification was WITHDRAWN and replaced by IFRS 15's own more rigorous over-time vs point-in-time criteria — a change with GENUINELY significant transition impact for real estate developers globally. This poster is retained for historical/comparative reference.

Conceptual Framework Linkage (Historical)

A binary classification later replaced by a more rigorous three-criteria test
IFRIC 15 required entities to determine whether a real estate construction agreement fell WITHIN IAS 11's scope (as a construction contract, generally recognised OVER TIME via percentage-of-completion) or IAS 18's scope (as a sale of goods, generally recognised at a SINGLE POINT IN TIME upon completion/delivery) — based largely on whether the BUYER had the ability to specify major structural elements of the design BEFORE and DURING construction.
WHY IFRS 15 REPLACED THIS APPROACH
A binary test replaced by a rigorous three-criteria test — IFRIC 15's "buyer's ability to specify design" indicator was seen as an IMPERFECT proxy for genuine control transfer, sometimes producing inconsistent outcomes for economically similar arrangementsIFRS 15's stricter control-based test — the standard's own over-time criteria (customer simultaneously receives/consumes benefits; entity's performance creates/enhances a customer-controlled asset; OR the asset has no alternative use PLUS an enforceable right to payment) provide a MORE rigorous, consistently applicable framework
This transition GENUINELY changed reported outcomes for many real estate developers, shifting SOME arrangements from over-time (percentage-of-completion) recognition to point-in-time recognition — one of the MORE consequential IFRIC withdrawals in terms of real-world financial statement impact.

Scope & Applicability (Historical)

What IFRIC 15 covered
IFRIC 15 applied to agreements for the construction of real estate entered into DIRECTLY with buyers, or through intermediaries — historically CENTRAL to real estate developer revenue recognition globally.
Key definitions (historical)
Ability to specify major structural elements
The KEY historical indicator distinguishing construction contract treatment from sale of goods treatment.
Continuous transfer
A concept requiring the buyer to control WORK IN PROGRESS as construction proceeds, for over-time recognition to be appropriate.
3

Construction Contract vs Sale of Goods Classification (Historical, with worked example)

Historical illustration
Example 1: A pre-designed apartment sale vs a customised residence
Developer A sells apartments in a STANDARDISED, pre-designed residential tower, with buyers unable to alter the building's structural design (only minor interior finishes). Developer B constructs a CUSTOM residence where the buyer specifies structural layout changes DURING construction.
Under IFRIC 15's historical test, Developer A's apartments were typically classified as a SALE OF GOODS (point-in-time recognition upon handover), while Developer B's custom residence was typically classified as a CONSTRUCTION CONTRACT (over-time, percentage-of-completion recognition) — an analysis reflecting the buyer's genuine ability to specify structural elements.
4

The "Continuous Transfer" Indicator (Historical)

A precursor to IFRS 15's own over-time criteria
Where the buyer's legal title/possession of the WORK IN PROGRESS transferred CONTINUOUSLY as construction proceeded (rather than only upon final completion), IFRIC 15 permitted over-time recognition — a concept that DIRECTLY foreshadowed IFRS 15's own "customer controls the asset as it is created" over-time criterion.
5

Percentage-of-Completion vs Completed Contract Method (Historical)

Identical mechanics to IFRIC 15's IAS 11 cross-reference
Arrangements classified as construction contracts applied IAS 11's percentage-of-completion method; arrangements classified as sale of goods recognised the ENTIRE sale price at the point of completion/handover — a stark, binary outcome that IFRS 15's more nuanced criteria have since softened.
6

Agreements for Rendering Services (Historical)

A narrower carve-out
Where an entity was NOT required to acquire/supply construction materials (i.e. purely providing construction SERVICES on land the customer already owned), IFRIC 15 applied IAS 18's SERVICES recognition principles instead — a distinction now folded into IFRS 15's own unified performance obligation framework.
7

How This Guidance Became Part of IFRS 15

Replacement by a more rigorous general model
When developing IFRS 15 as a single, comprehensive revenue standard, the IASB determined IFRIC 15's binary "buyer's ability to specify design" test should be REPLACED (not merely absorbed) by the standard's own more rigorous, control-based over-time criteria — applicable UNIFORMLY across all industries, not just real estate. IFRIC 15 was formally WITHDRAWN as IFRS 15 became effective.
8

The Significant Real Estate Sector Transition Impact

A genuinely consequential change for developers globally
The global real estate development sector experienced a SIGNIFICANT transition impact moving from IFRIC 15's binary classification to IFRS 15's stricter over-time criteria — MANY residential developers, previously recognising revenue OVER TIME under the "continuous transfer"/buyer-specification test, found their arrangements did NOT meet IFRS 15's stricter over-time criteria (particularly the "no alternative use + enforceable right to payment" test), shifting recognition to POINT IN TIME (typically at possession/completion) — a MATERIAL change in revenue recognition TIMING for the sector.

An IFRS 15 over-time criterion asking whether the asset could readily be redirected to another customer — a stricter test than IFRIC 15's own indicators.

A further IFRS 15 over-time criterion requiring a right to payment for performance completed to date, including reasonable profit margin.
