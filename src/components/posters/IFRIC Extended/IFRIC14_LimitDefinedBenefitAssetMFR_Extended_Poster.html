1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why a pension "surplus" can be an illusion
An asset must give rise to FUTURE ECONOMIC BENEFITS the entity can actually access. A pension plan can show an accounting surplus (plan assets exceeding the defined benefit obligation) that the sponsoring entity CANNOT genuinely benefit from – for example, if it can neither obtain a refund nor reduce future contributions. IFRIC 14 exists to define precisely what "available economic benefit" means in this context, and to prevent an asset ceiling being wrongly applied – or wrongly ignored.
The genuinely counter-intuitive twist: MFRs can create liabilities
Perhaps the most surprising conceptual contribution of IFRIC 14 is recognising that a MINIMUM FUNDING REQUIREMENT – a legal obligation to pay MORE into a plan – can itself give rise to an additional LIABILITY, where those extra contributions relate to past service and will not be recoverable as a future economic benefit. A funding rule intended to protect members can, in narrow circumstances, worsen the sponsor's reported financial position.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRIC 14 covers
IFRIC 14 applies to all defined benefit plans (and other post-employment defined benefit obligations) within IAS 19's scope, addressing (a) when refunds or reductions in future contributions should be regarded as available, and (b) how a statutory or contractual MINIMUM FUNDING REQUIREMENT (MFR) may affect the availability of those economic benefits, or create an additional liability.
TYPICAL CONTEXTS
Jurisdictions with statutory minimum funding legislation (e.g. UK scheme-specific funding requirements)Plans with a trust deed imposing contractual minimum contribution requirements
Key definitions
Asset ceiling
Under IAS 19, the maximum amount of a net defined benefit ASSET an entity may recognise: the present value of economic benefits available in the form of refunds from the plan or reductions in future contributions.
Minimum funding requirement (MFR)
A statutory or contractual requirement to make specified minimum contributions to a defined benefit plan, potentially in addition to what would otherwise be actuarially determined.
3

The Asset Ceiling Recap

The IAS 19 rule IFRIC 14 elaborates on
IAS 19 requires the net defined benefit asset recognised to be limited to the LOWER of (a) any surplus in the defined benefit plan, and (b) the ASSET CEILING – the present value of economic benefits available in the form of refunds from the plan or reductions in future contributions to the plan. IFRIC 14 does not change this rule; it clarifies HOW to determine what qualifies as an "available" economic benefit under (b), which is often far less obvious than it first appears.
4

Economic Benefits Available as a Refund

Assessed on an unconditional-right basis
A refund is available if the entity has an UNCONDITIONAL RIGHT to a refund – assessed assuming (i) gradual settlement of the plan liabilities over time until all members have left the plan, or (ii) settlement of the plan liabilities in a single event (a full wind-up), WHICHEVER gives the entity the higher benefit – but a right that is contingent on future uncertain events largely outside the entity's control (for example, requiring trustee consent that is not merely a formality) is NOT unconditional and therefore does not qualify.
5

Economic Benefits Available as a Reduction in Contributions

Measured over the shorter of the plan's or the entity's expected life
A reduction in future contributions is available if the entity can determine that it will be able to reduce contributions in the future as a result of the surplus – measured as the present value of the future service cost to the entity for each year over the SHORTER of the expected life of the plan and the expected life of the entity, taking into account any minimum funding requirement that applies to future service accrual (see Section 6).

CategoryEffect

MFR contributions relating to EXISTING (past) service shortfallThe critical category – see Section 7, where these can create an ADDITIONAL LIABILITY rather than merely reducing an asset
7

When an MFR Creates an Additional Liability

The core mechanic
If an entity has a statutory or contractual obligation under an MFR to pay contributions to fund a PAST SERVICE deficit, and those contributions will NOT be available to the entity as a refund or reduction in future contributions once paid, the entity recognises an ADDITIONAL LIABILITY (and a corresponding expense) to the extent the required contributions exceed the economic benefit otherwise available. The 2013 amendments clarified that this assessment considers whether the contribution, once paid, will itself be available as a future economic benefit.
8

Worked Example – MFR Triggering a Liability

A funding surplus that becomes a reported net liability
Example: MFR deficit contributions with no future benefit
A plan shows an IAS 19 accounting surplus of $2,000,000 (plan assets exceed the defined benefit obligation). However, under a statutory MFR based on a more conservative funding basis, the plan shows a funding DEFICIT, and the entity is contractually required to pay $3,500,000 in deficit-recovery contributions over the next three years. The entity has no unconditional right to a refund, and once paid these amounts will not reduce future service contributions (the plan is closed to future accrual).
Because the $3,500,000 in required contributions will provide NO future economic benefit to the entity once paid, an ADDITIONAL LIABILITY of $3,500,000 is recognised – despite the plan showing an accounting surplus on the standard IAS 19 measurement basis. The entity's net position moves from a $2,000,000 asset (before considering the MFR) to a net liability position once IFRIC 14's MFR interaction is applied.

IFRIC 14 vs US GAAP

Comparison – IFRIC 14 vs US GAAP (ASC 715)
FeatureIFRS (IFRIC 14)US GAAP (ASC 715)
Asset ceiling conceptExplicit – capped at present value of refunds/contribution reductionsNo direct equivalent asset ceiling concept – a net funded status asset is generally recognised without an analogous availability-based cap
MFR-triggered additional liabilityExplicitly addressed – can create a liability beyond the funded statusNo direct equivalent mechanism; minimum funding obligations are generally not separately gross-up-liability tested in the same way
Overall philosophySubstance-over-form focus on whether a surplus is genuinely ACCESSIBLE to the sponsorMore mechanical funded-status recognition, without the same accessibility overlay
IFRIC 14 addresses a genuinely IFRS-specific technical problem – US GAAP's pension accounting model does not use an equivalent asset-ceiling mechanism, making this one of the areas with the least direct comparability between the two frameworks.

Quick-reference glossary
Net defined benefit asset/liability
The net of the present value of the defined benefit obligation and the fair value of plan assets, subject to the asset ceiling if in a surplus position.
Remeasurement
Actuarial gains/losses, the return on plan assets (excluding amounts in net interest), and changes in the effect of the asset ceiling – recognised in OCI under IAS 19 and not subsequently reclassified to profit or loss.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Unconditional right
A right not contingent on future uncertain events that are largely outside the entity's control – the threshold IFRIC 14 requires for a refund to count toward the asset ceiling.
Deficit recovery contributions
Contributions required under an MFR specifically to address a past service funding shortfall, as distinct from contributions funding ongoing future service accrual.

SOFP Statement of financial position SOPL Statement of profit or loss OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
