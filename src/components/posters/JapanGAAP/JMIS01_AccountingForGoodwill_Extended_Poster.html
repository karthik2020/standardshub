Conceptual Framework Linkage

A deliberate, narrow carve-out preserving J-GAAP's own long-standing preference
JMIS Modification Accounting Standard No. 1 amends how GOODWILL is subsequently accounted for WITHIN the JMIS framework – requiring SYSTEMATIC AMORTISATION over a determined period, RATHER than the "no amortisation, impairment-only" approach unmodified IFRS 3 and IAS 36 prescribe internationally. This is JMIS's SINGLE most significant, best-known modification, and it directly IMPORTS J-GAAP's OWN long-standing goodwill philosophy (see the companion ASBJ Statement No. 21 poster) into a framework OTHERWISE built on IFRS.
2

What JMIS Is – IFRS With Two Deliberate Modifications

Japan's own "endorsed IFRS," modified in exactly two respects
Japan's Modified International Standards (JMIS) – inaugurated by the ASBJ in 2015 – are DEVELOPED through an "ENDORSEMENT" process applied to IFRS Standards issued by the IASB: JMIS ADOPTS IFRS Standards AS THEY STAND, EXCEPT for a SMALL, DELIBERATE number of specific "MODIFICATIONS" the ASBJ has determined WARRANT departure from the unmodified international text. There are, IN TOTAL, only TWO such modifications – THIS one (goodwill) and JMIS Modification No. 2 (Other Comprehensive Income – see the companion poster) – making JMIS, in PRACTICE, "IFRS with two narrow, well-defined carve-outs," RATHER than a broadly DIFFERENT framework.
3

Scope of This Modification

Companies electing to apply JMIS for consolidated statements
This modification applies to LISTED companies ELECTING to prepare their CONSOLIDATED financial statements under JMIS (one of the FOUR permitted frameworks in Japan, alongside J-GAAP, "Designated IFRS" – unmodified IFRS as endorsed for use in Japan – and US GAAP), specifically GOVERNING the subsequent treatment of GOODWILL recognised in a business combination.
4

The Modification Itself – Mandatory Goodwill Amortisation

A systematic charge, over a determined period
UNDER this modification, GOODWILL recognised in a business combination is AMORTISED SYSTEMATICALLY over its estimated USEFUL LIFE (subject to a MAXIMUM period, broadly CONSISTENT with the approach applied under J-GAAP's own ASBJ Statement No. 21 – see the companion poster) – REPLACING IFRS 3/IAS 36's UNMODIFIED international approach of treating goodwill as an INDEFINITE-life asset, tested ONLY for impairment, NEVER systematically amortised.
5

Why the ASBJ Insisted on This Departure From IFRS

Concerns about impairment-only reliability and earnings management
The ASBJ has LONG expressed CONCERN that an impairment-ONLY approach to goodwill can allow OVERSTATED goodwill balances to PERSIST on the Balance Sheet for EXTENDED periods, given the SUBJECTIVE, judgement-intensive nature of impairment TESTING (discount rate assumptions, cash flow projections) – creating a genuine RISK of DELAYED recognition of an economic DECLINE that has ALREADY occurred, and potential for EARNINGS management through the TIMING of impairment charges. SYSTEMATIC amortisation, by CONTRAST, provides a MORE MECHANICAL, OBJECTIVE, and PREDICTABLE reduction in goodwill's carrying amount over TIME – a PHILOSOPHY the ASBJ considered IMPORTANT enough to preserve EVEN within an otherwise IFRS-based framework.
6

Consistency With J-GAAP's Own Goodwill Treatment

The same underlying philosophy across two different frameworks
This MODIFICATION ensures that GOODWILL is treated CONSISTENTLY whether a Japanese company reports under J-GAAP (ASBJ Statement No. 21) OR elects JMIS – BOTH frameworks REQUIRE systematic amortisation, REFLECTING a SHARED underlying Japanese accounting PHILOSOPHY that transcends the CHOICE of overall reporting framework.
7

Impairment Testing Continues Alongside Amortisation

A dual safeguard – systematic reduction, plus an impairment backstop
EVEN WITH mandatory amortisation, IMPAIRMENT testing CONTINUES to apply WHERE indicators of impairment EXIST – providing a DUAL safeguard: SYSTEMATIC, mechanical reduction over TIME, PLUS an additional impairment CHECK for SIGNIFICANT, UNANTICIPATED declines in value that AMORTISATION alone would not adequately CAPTURE.
8

Worked Example – Goodwill Under JMIS vs Unmodified IFRS

The same acquisition, two very different subsequent treatments
Example: ¥800,000,000 of goodwill, 10-year amortisation period
A company recognises ¥800,000,000 of goodwill on an acquisition, determining a 10-year amortisation period is appropriate.
Under JMIS, the company recognises ¥800,000,000 ÷ 10 = ¥80,000,000 of amortisation EXPENSE per year – a MATERIAL, RECURRING P&L charge. Under unmodified, unamended IFRS (or Designated IFRS as endorsed for Japan), the SAME ¥800,000,000 would SIMPLY remain on the Balance Sheet, tested ANNUALLY for impairment, with NO systematic amortisation charge AT ALL – a genuinely MATERIAL difference in REPORTED profitability between the TWO frameworks for the SAME underlying transaction.
9

Worked Example – an Impairment Trigger Under JMIS

The dual safeguard in action
Example: A significant, unanticipated decline in the acquired business's performance
Continuing the prior example, after 3 years of amortisation (carrying amount: ¥800,000,000 − 3×¥80,000,000 = ¥560,000,000), the acquired business unit's performance deteriorates significantly, triggering an impairment indicator.
DESPITE the ONGOING systematic amortisation, the company STILL performs an IMPAIRMENT test – and, IF the recoverable amount is FOUND to be below ¥560,000,000, recognises an ADDITIONAL impairment LOSS for the shortfall – illustrating that AMORTISATION and IMPAIRMENT testing operate TOGETHER, not as ALTERNATIVES, under this modification.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Japan's Modified International Standards (JMIS)
A framework adopting IFRS Standards as issued by the IASB, subject to exactly two ASBJ-determined modifications, one of which is this goodwill amortisation requirement.
Endorsement process
ASBJ's process of adopting IFRS Standards for JMIS purposes, incorporating them as issued except for the deliberate, narrow modifications.
