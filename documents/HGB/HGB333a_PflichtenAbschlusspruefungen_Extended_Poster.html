Conceptual Framework Linkage

The audit committee's own criminal accountability, closing out this series' review of Book 3's Straftatbestände
§§331, 331a, 332, and 333 have now covered company management, the Bilanzeid signer, and the auditor's own accuracy and confidentiality obligations. §333a addresses a genuinely different party: the audit COMMITTEE established under §324 (covered in the earlier Audit poster) — but only in a narrow, specific circumstance, where an otherwise merely administrative violation is escalated into genuine criminal liability.
HOW §333A MAPS TO GERMAN ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES
Layered severity, matching culpability — an oversight lapse remains an administrative matter UNLESS it is bought (bribery) or becomes a genuine pattern (persistence), at which point the law escalates the response Protecting the audit committee's own integrity — the SAME oversight function §324 established (monitoring auditor independence, non-audit services) is itself now protected from corruption of the overseers themselves
What §333a covers, at a glance
§333a criminalises a member of an audit committee established under §324(1) sentence 1 who EITHER (1) commits an act described in §334(2a) and receives or is promised a Vermögensvorteil (financial/property advantage) for it, OR (2) commits the SAME §334(2a) act persistently/repeatedly (beharrlich wiederholt) — either scenario punishable by imprisonment of up to ONE YEAR or a fine.
2

Scope — Audit Committee Members Specifically

A genuinely narrow Sonderdelikt, distinct from every other party this cluster of provisions covers
§333a's Täterkreis is confirmed to be limited SPECIFICALLY to members of an audit committee (Prüfungsausschuss) established under §324(1) sentence 1 — established in the earlier Audit poster as the body (either a dedicated committee or the supervisory board itself performing this function) responsible for monitoring auditor independence, non-audit services, and the audit process generally. This is neither the auditor themselves (§332, §333), nor company management (§331), nor the specific Bilanzeid signer (§331a) — a genuinely distinct fifth category of potential offender.
3

History — the 2016 AReG, Not the 2021 FISG

A different reform than the one traced through the previous three posters
§333a was introduced by the 2016 Abschlussprüfungsreformgesetz (AReG) — Germany's implementation of the EU's own 2014 Audit Reform Directive and Regulation — several years BEFORE the 2021 FISG reform this series has repeatedly traced through §316a's PIE definition, §323's tiered liability caps, and §331a's standalone Bilanzeid offence. Both reforms share a broadly similar underlying concern (strengthening audit quality and independence), but they are genuinely SEPARATE legislative interventions, several years apart — worth distinguishing clearly rather than assuming every audit-related criminal provision in this cluster traces to the same single reform.

The Core Mechanism — Escalating an Administrative Violation Into a Crime

§334(2a) itself is only an Ordnungswidrigkeit — §333a is what turns it criminal
§334, covered in the NEXT poster in this series, generally defines administrative offences (Ordnungswidrigkeiten — punishable by a fine only, not imprisonment) for various breaches across this Subsection. §334(2a) specifically addresses conduct BY an audit committee member. On its own, this remains a purely administrative matter. §333a's genuinely distinctive contribution is to ESCALATE this same underlying conduct into full CRIMINAL liability, but ONLY in two specific, narrow circumstances — bribery (Nr.1) or persistent repetition (Nr.2) — reflecting a considered legislative judgement that an isolated, non-corrupt lapse should remain administrative, while a bought or genuinely repeated failure warrants the law's strongest response.
5

§333a Nr.1 — Escalation Through Bribery

A financial or property advantage received or promised in exchange for the violation
§333a Nr.1 criminalises an audit committee member who commits a §334(2a) act AND receives, or is promised, a Vermögensvorteil (a financial or property advantage) for doing so — a genuinely corruption-flavoured trigger, structurally similar to the "gegen Entgelt" aggravation this series has now seen repeatedly across §332(2) and §333(2), but here operating as the trigger for CRIMINALITY itself, rather than merely an aggravating factor on top of an already-criminal base offence.

Worked Example — an Audit Committee Member Bribed to Wave Through Prohibited Services

A payment specifically for looking the other way on independence rules
Example 1: Approving a prohibited non-audit service in exchange for a personal benefit
An audit committee member, responsible for overseeing the auditor's independence per §324, improperly approves a non-audit service that §319's independence rules (established in the earlier Audit poster) would ordinarily prohibit — and does so after quietly receiving a personal financial benefit from the auditing firm in exchange.
Assuming this conduct itself qualifies as a §334(2a) violation, the RECEIPT of the personal financial benefit transforms what would otherwise be a purely administrative (Bußgeld) matter into a genuine CRIMINAL offence under §333a Nr.1 — punishable by imprisonment of up to one year or a fine, REGARDLESS of how modest the benefit received actually was.
7

§333a Nr.2 — Escalation Through Persistent Repetition

No bribe required — sheer, sustained repetition is itself enough
§333a Nr.2 supplies a genuinely SEPARATE trigger, requiring NO bribery or corrupt motive at all — an audit committee member who commits the SAME §334(2a) act "beharrlich" (persistently/stubbornly) REPEATEDLY escalates their own conduct into criminal territory purely through the SUSTAINED pattern itself, reflecting a legislative judgement that repeated, wilful disregard of the same oversight duty is qualitatively different from — and more serious than — a single isolated administrative lapse, even absent any financial corruption.
8

Worked Example — Repeated Violations Without Any Bribe at All

Genuine, sustained pattern alone crosses the criminal threshold
Example 2: A committee member who repeatedly ignores the same oversight duty
An audit committee member, without any bribe or corrupt motive whatsoever, simply and repeatedly fails to properly review non-audit service pre-approvals over several consecutive reporting periods — not through a single oversight, but through a genuinely sustained pattern of disregarding the same specific duty each time.
DESPITE the complete absence of any financial benefit or corrupt intent, the PERSISTENT, repeated nature of the same underlying §334(2a) violation itself satisfies §333a Nr.2 — escalating what began as a series of individually administrative lapses into a genuine criminal offence, purely through the sustained pattern.

An Honest Flag — the Precise Content of §334(2a)

The mechanism is confirmed; the exact underlying conduct is worth dedicated verification
This poster's research confirmed §333a's own STRUCTURE (Täterkreis, the two escalation triggers, the one-year maximum penalty) with strong confidence, drawn directly from the current statutory text. What it did NOT fully confirm is the PRECISE, complete definition of the underlying §334(2a) conduct itself — the specific audit-committee-related act that, absent bribery or persistence, would remain a mere administrative matter. Given §334 is the subject of the NEXT poster in this series, this specific point is best resolved there, with dedicated attention, rather than asserted with false confidence here.

Relationship to IFRS

Audit committee corruption sits entirely outside any accounting standard
Precisely as with every other criminal provision this series has documented, IFRS itself has no equivalent — corporate governance oversight bodies and their own criminal accountability are governed entirely by each jurisdiction's own company and criminal law. §333a is a genuinely narrow but distinctive illustration of how far German law extends the audit-quality accountability structure this series has documented — reaching not just the preparer and the auditor, but the AUDIT COMMITTEE that oversees the auditor's own independence.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Prüfungsausschuss
Audit committee, established under §324(1) sentence 1 — §333a's specific Täterkreis.
Vermögensvorteil
A financial or property advantage — the corrupt benefit that triggers §333a Nr.1's escalation.
Beharrlich wiederholen
To persistently/stubbornly repeat — the sustained-pattern trigger under §333a Nr.2, requiring no corrupt motive.
Ordnungswidrigkeit
An administrative offence, punishable by fine only — what §334(2a) conduct remains absent the §333a escalation triggers.
AReG
Abschlussprüfungsreformgesetz — the 2016 reform implementing EU Audit Reform requirements, which introduced §333a.
