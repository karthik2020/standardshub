Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why classification now depends on business purpose AND cash flow nature
The Conceptual Framework requires measurement to faithfully represent how an entity expects to derive economic benefit from an asset. IFRS 9 operationalises this through a PRINCIPLES-based, TWO-part test – the entity's BUSINESS MODEL for managing the asset, and the CONTRACTUAL CASH FLOW characteristics of the instrument itself – replacing IAS 39's rigid four-category, rules-based system.
HOW IFRS 9 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Faithful representation – an asset held to collect simple principal/interest cash flows is genuinely DIFFERENT from one held for short-term trading, and IFRS 9's classification reflects that Timeliness / relevance – the EXPECTED credit loss model (Section 6) recognises credit deterioration ADMISSIONS earlier than IAS 39's incurred loss approach, directly responding to global financial crisis lessons Substance over form – hedge accounting reform (Section 8) aligns accounting more closely with an entity's ACTUAL risk management strategy, rather than a rigid quantitative bright-line test
IFRS 9 was developed in THREE PHASES (classification and measurement; impairment; hedge accounting), each directly responding to specific weaknesses identified in IAS 39 following the 2008 global financial crisis.
The three classification categories
IFRS 9 simplifies IAS 39's four categories into three, driven by the business model and SPPI tests together.
CategoryMeasurement basis
Amortised costHold to collect + SPPI cash flows
FVOCI (debt)Hold to collect AND sell + SPPI cash flows
FVTPLResidual – anything not qualifying for the above two
ℹ️ A THIRD equity-specific option – FVOCI with NO recycling – reflects a deliberate Framework trade-off for equity investments an entity holds for strategic (not trading) reasons.

Scope & Applicability

What IFRS 9 covers
IFRS 9 applies to all financial assets and financial liabilities within its scope, replacing IAS 39 in its entirety. Scope exclusions are LARGELY UNCHANGED from IAS 39: interests in subsidiaries/associates/joint ventures; employee benefit plan rights; most insurance contracts; share-based payment transactions (with limited exceptions).
The general scope architecture carried forward almost UNCHANGED from IAS 39 – the source of IFRS 9's reforms was classification, impairment, and hedge accounting, not scope.
Key definitions you must know
Business model
How an entity manages its financial assets to generate cash flows – by collecting contractual cash flows, selling, or both.
SPPI
Solely Payments of Principal and Interest – the test asking whether contractual cash flows represent basic lending arrangement cash flows only.
Expected credit losses (ECL)
A probability-weighted estimate of credit losses, forward-looking from initial recognition, replacing IAS 39's incurred loss trigger.

Classification – The Business Model Test (with worked example)

Three business models, assessed at a portfolio level
Business modelDescription
Hold to collectObjective is to hold assets to collect contractual cash flows (sales are incidental, infrequent, or insignificant)
Hold to collect AND sellBoth collecting contractual cash flows and selling are integral to achieving the objective
Other (e.g. trading)Managed on a fair value basis, or held for short-term trading/profit-taking
Example 1: Assessing the business model
A bank holds a bond portfolio to collect coupon and principal payments to maturity, but also sells portions of the portfolio periodically in response to changes in credit risk or to manage liquidity needs – sales are more than infrequent but the portfolio is not actively traded for short-term profit.
This is most likely a "hold to collect and sell" business model – both objectives (collecting cash flows and selling) are integral, pointing to FVOCI classification (assuming SPPI is also met).

Classification – The SPPI Test (with worked example)

Are the cash flows "basic lending" in nature?
The SPPI test asks whether contractual cash flows are SOLELY payments of PRINCIPAL and INTEREST on the principal amount outstanding – where "interest" consists of consideration for the time value of money, credit risk, and other basic lending risks/costs plus a profit margin.
Example 2: A convertible bond fails SPPI
An entity holds a bond convertible into a FIXED number of the issuer's ordinary shares at the holder's option.
The conversion feature introduces exposure to EQUITY price risk, unrelated to basic lending – this FAILS the SPPI test, and the ENTIRE instrument must be classified at FVTPL, regardless of the business model.
⚠️ Failing SPPI is DECISIVE – even a "hold to collect" business model cannot rescue an instrument that fails the cash flow characteristics test; FVTPL classification is mandatory.

Financial Liabilities Classification

Largely unchanged from IAS 39 – with one key OCI carve-out
Most financial liabilities continue to be measured at AMORTISED COST. Where an entity elects the fair value option for a liability, changes in fair value attributable to the entity's OWN CREDIT RISK are presented in OCI (not SOPL) – UNLESS this creates or enlarges an accounting mismatch, in which case the full change goes to SOPL. This "own credit risk in OCI" rule is a DIRECT IFRS 9 innovation, addressing the counter-intuitive IAS 39 outcome where a DETERIORATING credit rating produced a PROFIT (since the liability's fair value fell).

Expected Credit Losses – The Three-Stage Model (with worked example)

Forward-looking provisioning from initial recognition
StageRecognition & measurement
Stage 1No significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition – 12-MONTH ECL recognised
Stage 2SIGNIFICANT increase in credit risk since initial recognition, but not yet credit-impaired – LIFETIME ECL recognised
Stage 3Credit-impaired (objective evidence of a loss event) – LIFETIME ECL recognised; interest calculated on the NET carrying amount
Example 3: Moving from Stage 1 to Stage 2
A loan of $1,000,000 has a Stage 1 (12-month) ECL of $8,000 at initial recognition. By the next reporting date, the borrower's credit rating has deteriorated significantly (though not yet in default), triggering a "significant increase in credit risk." The lifetime ECL is now estimated at $45,000.
The loan moves to Stage 2 – the loss allowance INCREASES from $8,000 (12-month ECL) to $45,000 (lifetime ECL), with the $37,000 incremental charge recognised immediately in profit or loss, even though NO actual default has yet occurred.
This FORWARD-LOOKING recognition – based on a SIGNIFICANT INCREASE in credit risk, not an actual loss event – is precisely what IAS 39's incurred loss model prohibited.

Derecognition

Carried forward largely unchanged from IAS 39
A financial asset is derecognised when contractual rights to cash flows EXPIRE, or when the entity TRANSFERS substantially all risks/rewards of ownership. Where risks/rewards are neither substantially transferred nor retained, the test turns to whether CONTROL has been surrendered – the SAME multi-step decision tree IAS 39 used, one of the few areas NOT reformed by IFRS 9.
8

Hedge Accounting (with worked example)

A principles-based "economic relationship" test, replacing the 80-125% bright line
To qualify for hedge accounting, there must be an ECONOMIC RELATIONSHIP between the hedged item and hedging instrument, credit risk must NOT dominate value changes, and the HEDGE RATIO must match that actually used for risk management purposes.
Example 4: A hedge that would have failed IAS 39's bright-line test
An entity hedges jet fuel purchases using a crude oil derivative (since no direct jet fuel derivative market exists). The historical correlation between jet fuel and crude oil prices produces an effectiveness ratio of 78% under a simple retrospective dollar-offset test.
Under IAS 39's strict 80-125% bright line, this hedge would have FAILED and hedge accounting would be prohibited. Under IFRS 9's PRINCIPLES-based economic relationship test, this hedge likely QUALIFIES, since a genuine economic relationship exists between jet fuel and crude oil prices, even though the quantitative dollar-offset falls just outside the old bright line.

Reclassification

Only when the business model genuinely changes
Financial assets are reclassified ONLY when an entity CHANGES its business model for managing them – expected to be a RARE event, determined by senior management as a result of external or internal changes, and demonstrable to external parties. Reclassification is applied PROSPECTIVELY from the reclassification date; NO restatement of previously recognised gains, losses, or interest.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Determining the appropriate business model at a portfolio level Assessing SPPI, particularly for structured or contingent cash flow instruments Identifying a "significant increase in credit risk" triggering the Stage 1 → Stage 2 transition
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Probability-weighted ECL calculations, incorporating forward-looking macroeconomic scenarios Fair value of instruments without observable market prices
⚠️ The ECL model's forward-looking, probability-weighted, multiple-scenario approach is significantly more judgemental and DATA-INTENSIVE than IAS 39's incurred loss trigger – a major implementation challenge for many entities, particularly banks.

IFRS vs US GAAP

Comparison – IFRS 9 vs US GAAP (ASC 326 CECL)
FeatureIFRS (IFRS 9)US GAAP
Impairment modelTHREE-stage ECL – 12-month vs lifetime, based on credit deteriorationCECL ("Current Expected Credit Loss") – LIFETIME expected losses recognised for ALL financial assets from initial recognition, no staging
Classification approachBusiness model + SPPI testBroadly similar categories, though with different terminology and some mechanical differences
Hedge accounting effectivenessPrinciples-based economic relationship testAlso reformed (ASU 2017-12) toward a more principles-based approach, though retaining some distinct mechanics
The BIGGEST remaining difference is impairment – US GAAP's CECL model recognises LIFETIME expected losses IMMEDIATELY for all assets (no staging), a MORE conservative day-one approach than IFRS 9's staged model.

Quick-reference glossary
Significant increase in credit risk
The trigger moving an asset from Stage 1 (12-month ECL) to Stage 2 (lifetime ECL).
Hedge ratio
The relationship between the quantity of the hedging instrument and the quantity of the hedged item, which must match actual risk management practice under IFRS 9.
