Conceptual Framework Linkage

Specialised industry practices layered on general GAAP
ASC 908 doesn't establish an entirely SEPARATE accounting model for airlines – it provides INDUSTRY-SPECIFIC guidance and elaboration on how GENERAL GAAP principles (PP&E capitalisation under ASC 360, revenue recognition under ASC 606) apply to practices UNIQUE to the airline industry, particularly the treatment of MAJOR MAINTENANCE (heavy overhaul) costs and FREQUENT FLYER/mileage programs.
2

Scope

Commercial passenger and cargo airlines
ASC 908 applies to entities operating as commercial AIRLINES, addressing their aircraft maintenance capitalisation practices and customer loyalty program accounting.
3

Major Maintenance (Heavy Overhaul) Costs – Three Acceptable Methods

A genuine policy choice, applied consistently by asset type
MethodDescription
Direct expensingMajor maintenance costs are expensed as incurred, with NO capitalisation – the simplest, most conservative approach
Built-in overhaul (component depreciation)A portion of the aircraft's cost is separately identified as relating to the overhaul component and depreciated over the period until the NEXT scheduled overhaul; actual overhaul costs are then capitalised and depreciated over the following overhaul interval
Deferral methodMajor maintenance costs are capitalised when incurred and amortised over the period until the next scheduled overhaul
Airlines may elect any of these three methods as an accounting POLICY, applied consistently to similar assets – a genuine, sanctioned choice among methods, unlike many other Topics where a single method is mandated.
4

Frequent Flyer / Mileage Programs – Governed by ASC 606

A material right, deferred as a separate performance obligation
Under current guidance, frequent flyer MILEAGE credits earned on a ticket purchase are treated as a MATERIAL RIGHT under ASC 606 – a SEPARATE PERFORMANCE OBLIGATION distinct from the transportation service itself. A PORTION of the ticket transaction price is ALLOCATED to the mileage credits (based on their relative standalone selling price, factoring in expected redemption rates), DEFERRED as a contract liability, and recognised as REVENUE only when the miles are actually REDEEMED (or expire unused). This REPLACED the older "incremental cost" accrual method that predated ASC 606's material rights framework.
5

Route Acquisition Costs and Airframe/Engine Costs

Capitalised where they represent a genuine future economic benefit
Costs of ACQUIRING route authorities/airport slots may be CAPITALISED as intangible assets where they meet general recognition criteria (ASC 350), and airframe/engine costs are capitalised and depreciated under normal ASC 360 PP&E principles, WITH the major maintenance policy election (Section 3) layered on top for subsequent overhaul costs.

Worked Example – Contrasting Major Maintenance Methods

The same overhaul, three different expense patterns
Example: A $2,000,000 engine overhaul, 5-year interval to next overhaul
An airline incurs a $2,000,000 major engine overhaul cost, with the next scheduled overhaul expected in 5 years.
Direct expensing method: the full $2,000,000 is expensed IMMEDIATELY in the year incurred.
Deferral method: the $2,000,000 is CAPITALISED and AMORTISED evenly over the 5-year interval – $400,000 per year.
Built-in overhaul method: a PORTION of the aircraft's original cost (representing the PRIOR overhaul component) would have already been depreciated down to near-zero by this point; the new $2,000,000 cost is now capitalised as the NEW overhaul component and depreciated over the NEXT 5-year interval, similarly to the deferral method's pattern going forward.
7

Worked Example – Frequent Flyer Program Revenue Deferral

Allocating a portion of the ticket price to mileage credits
Example: A $600 ticket earning frequent flyer miles
A passenger purchases a $600 ticket, earning frequent flyer miles with an estimated standalone selling price (factoring in expected redemption) of $30.
The $600 transaction price is ALLOCATED between the transportation service and the mileage material right based on relative standalone selling price: approximately $570 allocated to the flight (recognised as revenue when the flight occurs), and $30 allocated to the mileage credits (deferred as a contract liability, recognised as revenue only as/when the miles are later redeemed for a future flight, upgrade, or other reward, or expire unused).

No Dedicated IFRS Industry Standard – Discussion

A narrower comparison – IFRS addresses these issues through general principles rather than an airline-specific standard
IFRS does NOT have a DEDICATED airline industry standard paralleling ASC 908. Airlines reporting under IFRS instead apply GENERAL principles: major maintenance capitalisation decisions are made under IAS 16's general component depreciation approach (broadly similar in SPIRIT to the "built-in overhaul" method, since IAS 16 explicitly requires SIGNIFICANT PARTS of an asset with different useful lives, such as an engine overhaul component, to be depreciated separately) – while direct expensing and the deferral method as SEPARATE named alternatives are more distinctly a US GAAP construct. Frequent flyer programs were historically addressed by IFRIC 13 (Customer Loyalty Programmes), a narrower interpretation applying essentially the SAME "material right"/deferred revenue logic – IFRIC 13 has since been SUPERSEDED and folded into IFRS 15's general material rights framework, arriving at a broadly CONVERGED outcome with ASC 606 for loyalty programs specifically, even without a dedicated airline standard.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Deferral method
A major maintenance accounting method capitalising overhaul costs and amortising them over the period until the next scheduled overhaul.
Built-in overhaul method
A major maintenance accounting method separately depreciating an asset's overhaul component over the interval to the next scheduled overhaul.
Material right
A separate performance obligation under ASC 606 arising from a customer option (such as frequent flyer miles) that provides a discount or benefit the customer would not receive without entering the original contract.
