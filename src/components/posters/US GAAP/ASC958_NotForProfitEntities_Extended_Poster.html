Conceptual Framework Linkage

Nonreciprocal contributions demand a genuinely different recognition trigger
Not-for-profit entities receive substantial resources through CONTRIBUTIONS — gifts, grants, pledges — where the DONOR receives NOTHING of commensurate value in return, a fundamentally NONRECIPROCAL transaction unlike an ordinary sale. ASC 958 addresses when such contributions should be RECOGNISED (Section 4), how NET ASSETS should be classified based on DONOR-imposed restrictions (Section 3), and requires a genuinely distinctive FUNCTIONAL expense presentation (Section 7).
A genuinely underdeveloped area on the IFRS side
IFRS has NO dedicated, comprehensive standard addressing not-for-profit contribution accounting comparable to ASC 958 — a genuine, substantial GAP, not merely a difference in specific mechanics (Section 13).
2

Scope

Charities, foundations, and other not-for-profit organizations
ASC 958 applies to not-for-profit entities — charities, foundations, membership organisations, religious organisations, and similar entities lacking ownership interests and operating for purposes other than to provide a return to investors.
3

Net Asset Classification — With vs Without Donor Restrictions

Simplified to two categories under ASU 2016-14
ASU 2016-14 SIMPLIFIED net asset classification from the historical THREE-tier system (unrestricted / temporarily restricted / permanently restricted) down to TWO categories: NET ASSETS WITHOUT donor restrictions, and NET ASSETS WITH donor restrictions — with the TIMING and PURPOSE nuances formerly captured by the temporarily/permanently restricted distinction now conveyed primarily through ENHANCED DISCLOSURE (e.g. the nature and amount of restrictions, and when they are expected to be met) rather than through a THIRD balance sheet category.
4

Contribution Recognition — Unconditional vs Conditional Promises to Give

A genuinely distinctive recognition trigger, driven by the presence of a "barrier"
An UNCONDITIONAL promise to give is recognised as CONTRIBUTION REVENUE (and a receivable) when the PROMISE is made — even before cash is received. A CONDITIONAL promise to give — one containing a genuine "BARRIER" that must be overcome (e.g. a matching requirement, a specific measurable performance target, or a right of RETURN/RELEASE for the donor if the condition isn't met) — is recognised ONLY when the CONDITION is substantially MET, treated more like a REFUNDABLE deposit until then. Distinguishing a genuine BARRIER-based condition from a mere donor-imposed RESTRICTION (which affects net asset CLASSIFICATION, not recognition TIMING) is one of the most consequential judgement calls in this entire Topic.
5

Contributions Are Scoped Out of ASC 606 — the Nonreciprocal Transfer Distinction

A separate recognition framework, deliberately outside the general revenue standard
Genuine CONTRIBUTIONS (nonreciprocal transfers, where the donor receives NOTHING of commensurate value) are explicitly SCOPED OUT of ASC 606 — recognised instead under ASC 958-605's OWN contribution recognition framework (Section 4). This distinguishes contributions from EXCHANGE transactions (e.g. a government grant that is genuinely a payment for specific services rendered, which WOULD fall under ASC 606) — requiring not-for-profits to carefully ANALYSE each resource inflow to determine which framework applies, since a grant or sponsorship arrangement can sit ambiguously between the two.
6

Donated Services and Gifts-in-Kind

Recognised only under specific, narrow criteria
DONATED SERVICES are recognised ONLY if they either (a) CREATE or ENHANCE a nonfinancial asset, or (b) require SPECIALISED skills, are provided by someone POSSESSING those skills, and would typically need to be PURCHASED if not donated (e.g. pro bono legal or medical services) — ordinary volunteer labor (e.g. general volunteer fundraising help) is generally NOT recognised, even though it has real economic value. GIFTS-IN-KIND (donated goods, materials, or use of facilities) are recognised at FAIR VALUE when received, with enhanced disclosure requirements (ASU 2020-07) requiring separate presentation from cash contributions and disclosure of the valuation techniques used.
7

Functional Expense Reporting — the By-Nature-and-Function Matrix

A genuinely distinctive, mandatory two-dimensional expense presentation
Not-for-profits must present expenses analysed by BOTH their NATURE (e.g. salaries, occupancy, supplies) AND their FUNCTION (e.g. program services, management and general, fundraising) — typically as a MATRIX/GRID, either on the face of the financial statements or in the notes. This dual-dimension expense disclosure requirement is genuinely DISTINCTIVE to not-for-profit reporting, reflecting donors' and grantors' particular interest in understanding how much of an organisation's spending goes toward its PROGRAM MISSION versus overhead/fundraising.
8

Endowments — Donor-Restricted vs Board-Designated Quasi-Endowments

A meaningful distinction in restriction source
A TRUE DONOR-RESTRICTED endowment is established by a DONOR's explicit stipulation that the principal be maintained in perpetuity (or for a specified term) — classified as NET ASSETS WITH donor restrictions. A "QUASI-ENDOWMENT" (or BOARD-DESIGNATED endowment) is created when the organisation's OWN GOVERNING BOARD elects to set aside otherwise UNRESTRICTED funds to function like an endowment — since this restriction is SELF-IMPOSED (not donor-imposed), quasi-endowment funds remain classified as NET ASSETS WITHOUT donor restrictions, and the board retains the ability to REDIRECT them if circumstances require.

Worked Example — Conditional vs Unconditional Promise to Give

Applying the barrier test
Example: A matching-gift pledge
A donor pledges $500,000 to a nonprofit, CONDITIONED on the nonprofit raising an EQUAL $500,000 in matching gifts from OTHER donors within the next 12 months — with the pledge explicitly RETURNABLE to the donor if the match isn't achieved.
The MATCHING requirement is a genuine BARRIER (a specific, measurable condition with a right of RETURN if not met) — this is a CONDITIONAL promise to give. NO contribution revenue is recognised UNTIL the nonprofit actually RAISES the matching $500,000 — at which point the condition is substantially met, and the FULL $500,000 pledge is recognised as contribution revenue at that time.

Worked Example — Functional Expense Allocation

The by-nature-and-function matrix in practice
Example: Allocating a shared executive director's salary
A nonprofit's executive director spends an estimated 60% of their time on program service delivery, 30% on management and general activities, and 10% on fundraising, with a total annual salary of $150,000.
The $150,000 salary (a NATURE category) is ALLOCATED across FUNCTION categories: $90,000 to program services (60%), $45,000 to management and general (30%), and $15,000 to fundraising (10%) — appearing in the functional expense matrix disaggregated both by its NATURE (salary) and its FUNCTIONAL allocation.

No Dedicated IFRS Not-for-Profit Standard — Discussion

A genuine, substantial gap in the international standards landscape
IFRS has NO dedicated, comprehensive standard addressing not-for-profit contribution accounting comparable to ASC 958 — this is a GENUINE, substantial gap, not merely a difference in specific mechanics. IAS 20 addresses GOVERNMENT grants specifically, but general DONOR contribution recognition, net asset classification, and functional expense reporting of the kind ASC 958 provides simply has NO direct IFRS counterpart. Many not-for-profits reporting internationally instead rely on a PATCHWORK of national GAAP requirements, or on International Public Sector Accounting Standards (IPSAS) — a ENTIRELY SEPARATE standard-setting framework from IFRS, primarily developed for public-sector/governmental entities rather than private charitable organisations. This makes ASC 958 one of the more genuinely DISTINCTIVE, comprehensively developed Topics in the entire US GAAP Codification relative to the international standards landscape.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Barrier
A specific, measurable condition (such as a matching requirement) that must be overcome before a conditional promise to give is recognised.
Quasi-endowment (board-designated endowment)
Funds an organisation's own board designates to function like an endowment, remaining classified as net assets without donor restrictions since the restriction is self-imposed.
Functional expense classification
The required presentation of expenses by both their nature and their function (program, management and general, fundraising).
