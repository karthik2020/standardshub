Conceptual Framework Linkage

The right to be heard, completing §335's own enforcement power
§335 gave the Bundesamt für Justiz genuine power to impose an escalating, repeating coercive fine for disclosure non-compliance. §335a is the essential counterweight: a structured, genuine right to CHALLENGE that decision, with real procedural protections — including, critically, an appeal that automatically PAUSES enforcement while it is being decided.
HOW §335A MAPS TO GERMAN ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES
Rechtsstaatlichkeit (rule of law) — even an administrative body's enforcement power must be subject to genuine, court-based review Proportionate procedural burden — a straightforward two-week Beschwerde for the ordinary case, reserving the more demanding, lawyer-mandatory Rechtsbeschwerde for genuinely significant legal questions the Landgericht itself judges worth escalating

2

Scope

The fined party — and, for the further appeal, the Bundesamt itself too
§335a's Beschwerde right belongs to the party against whom the Ordnungsgeld was imposed. For the SECOND-tier Rechtsbeschwerde specifically, the right ALSO extends to the Bundesamt für Justiz itself — meaning the enforcement authority can, in principle, appeal a Landgericht decision it disagrees with too, not merely the fined company.
3

§335a(1) — the Basic Right to Appeal, and Its Suspensive Effect

A genuinely significant procedural protection
§335a(1) grants a Beschwerde (appeal) against a decision imposing an Ordnungsgeld, rejecting an Einspruch (objection), or rejecting a Wiedereinsetzung (reinstatement) application — governed by the FamFG's own general procedural rules, except where §335a itself provides otherwise. CRITICALLY, §335a(1) sentence 2 gives the Beschwerde SUSPENSIVE EFFECT (aufschiebende Wirkung) specifically where it concerns the imposition of an Ordnungsgeld — meaning filing the appeal AUTOMATICALLY pauses enforcement of the fine while the appeal remains pending.
4

Worked Example — the Practical Significance of Suspensive Effect

The fine cannot be collected while the appeal is genuinely pending
Example 1: A company appealing a €10,000 fine before it becomes payable
The Bundesamt imposes a €10,000 Ordnungsgeld against a company under §335(4). The company believes the fine was wrongly calculated and files a timely Beschwerde.
Because the Beschwerde concerns the IMPOSITION of the Ordnungsgeld itself, it carries automatic suspensive effect under §335a(1) — the Bundesamt CANNOT proceed to collect the €10,000 while the appeal remains genuinely pending before the Landgericht, protecting the company from having to pay a potentially wrongly-imposed fine before its challenge has even been decided.
5

§335a(2) — the Two-Week Deadline and LG Bonn's Exclusive Jurisdiction

A genuinely tight window, decided by a single, specific court nationwide
§335a(2) requires the Beschwerde to be filed within TWO WEEKS, and confirms jurisdiction lies with the Landgericht at the Bundesamt für Justiz's own registered SEAT — confirmed, via the Landgericht Bonn's own published description of its role, to be the Landgericht Bonn specifically, holding EXCLUSIVE nationwide jurisdiction over these Ordnungsgeld appeals, REGARDLESS of where the fined company itself is actually located anywhere in Germany. A regulatory-delegation mechanism ALSO permits the relevant state government to reassign this jurisdiction elsewhere by regulation, to manage caseload or backlog concerns, should that become necessary.
6

Court Composition — the Kammer für Handelssachen

Commercial expertise, where available
Where the Landgericht Bonn has established a Kammer für Handelssachen (Chamber for Commercial Matters), that specialised chamber handles the Beschwerde INSTEAD of the ordinary Zivilkammer (civil chamber). Where the ordinary civil chamber decides, the standard single-judge/panel rules under ZPO §§348-348a apply; where the commercial chamber decides instead, the presiding judge alone decides — a genuinely specific procedural nuance reflecting the commercial, rather than purely civil, character of these disputes.

Worked Example — Filing a Timely Appeal

A tight window, but a nationally consolidated, specialised forum
Example 2: A Bavarian company appealing to a court in North Rhine-Westphalia
A company headquartered in Munich receives an Ordnungsgeld decision and wishes to appeal, believing the fine was miscalculated.
DESPITE the company's own Bavarian location, it must file its Beschwerde within TWO WEEKS specifically with the Landgericht Bonn — the court is NOT determined by the company's own location, but by the Bundesamt für Justiz's OWN seat, ensuring these appeals are consolidated nationally within a single specialised commercial forum rather than scattered across every regional court in Germany.
8

The Cost-Shifting Mechanism

Discretionary reimbursement, including where the Bundesamt itself concedes
The Landgericht may, based on equitable discretion (billiges Ermessen), order that a successful appellant's necessary extrajudicial costs be reimbursed, wholly or partly, from the state treasury — and the SAME reimbursement mechanism applies where the Bundesamt itself simply GRANTS the appeal (abhilft) without the matter needing full court adjudication, ensuring a company that was genuinely wrongly fined isn't left bearing its own defence costs even where the dispute resolves informally.
9

§335a(3) — the Further Appeal (Rechtsbeschwerde), Genuinely Narrow

Permission-only, decided by the Oberlandesgericht, with mandatory legal representation
§335a(3) permits a FURTHER appeal — the Rechtsbeschwerde — against the Landgericht's own Beschwerde decision, but ONLY where the Landgericht ITSELF has expressly ALLOWED it (zugelassen) — genuinely NOT an automatic right. The Rechtsbeschwerde is decided by the Oberlandesgericht (higher regional court) with jurisdiction over the Landgericht's own seat — NOT, as might be assumed, the Bundesgerichtshof — and is available to the Bundesamt itself as well as the fined party, including specifically against a Wiedereinsetzung the Landgericht may have granted. Before the Oberlandesgericht, parties MUST be represented by a lawyer — EXCEPT the Bundesamt, which requires no such representation.
10

Worked Example — Contrasting Beschwerde and Rechtsbeschwerde

A genuinely two-tiered structure, with a real gate between the levels
Example 3: A company that loses its Beschwerde and wants to appeal further
A company's Beschwerde is unsuccessful — the Landgericht Bonn upholds the original Ordnungsgeld. The company, believing the Landgericht made a genuine legal error on a point of general significance, wishes to appeal further.
The company can ONLY pursue a Rechtsbeschwerde if the Landgericht Bonn ITSELF expressly ALLOWS it — the company has NO automatic right to escalate further, unlike the initial Beschwerde itself. IF permitted, the further appeal goes to the Oberlandesgericht (with jurisdiction over Bonn's own seat — the OLG Köln), and the company MUST now be represented by a lawyer, a requirement that did not necessarily apply at the earlier Beschwerde stage.

Critical Judgments & Practical Difficulties

Where judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether a legal question genuinely warrants the Landgericht permitting a Rechtsbeschwerde, versus being confined to that single level of review
PRACTICAL DIFFICULTIES
Meeting the genuinely tight two-week Beschwerde deadline, particularly for companies located far from Bonn who may need to quickly engage local counsel familiar with the specific LG Bonn procedure

Relationship to IFRS

Appeal procedure sits entirely outside any accounting standard
Precisely as with §335 itself, IFRS has no equivalent — the specific mechanics of challenging a disclosure-enforcement fine are governed entirely by each jurisdiction's own procedural law. §335a completes this poster series' documentation of the practical, procedural layer sitting beneath HGB's disclosure enforcement regime, ensuring the power §335 grants remains genuinely subject to judicial check.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Beschwerde
The first-tier appeal against an Ordnungsgeld decision, with automatic suspensive effect for fine impositions.
Rechtsbeschwerde
The second-tier, permission-only further appeal, decided by the Oberlandesgericht.
Aufschiebende Wirkung
Suspensive effect — the automatic pause on fine collection while a timely Beschwerde is pending.
Kammer für Handelssachen
Chamber for Commercial Matters — the specialised court division handling these appeals where established.
Zugelassen
Permitted/allowed — the Landgericht's own gatekeeping decision required before a Rechtsbeschwerde can proceed at all.
Abhilfe
The Bundesamt itself granting/conceding an appeal without requiring full court adjudication.
