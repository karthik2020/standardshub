Conceptual Framework Linkage

Recognizing the future cost of decommissioning a long-lived asset
ASPE Section 3110 requires RECOGNITION of a LIABILITY for a LEGAL obligation ASSOCIATED with the RETIREMENT of a LONG-lived tangible ASSET – SUCH as the FUTURE cost OF decommissioning a FACILITY, RESTORING a SITE, or REMOVING equipment AT the end OF its USEFUL life. THE Section CLOSELY parallels the GENERAL approach ALSO used INTERNATIONALLY under IAS 37/IFRIC 1 and US GAAP ASC 410.
2

Scope

Legal obligations tied to the retirement of a tangible long-lived asset
This Section applies to LEGAL obligations ASSOCIATED with the RETIREMENT of TANGIBLE long-LIVED assets – ARISING from STATUTE, contract, OR common law, RATHER than PURELY discretionary OR voluntary decommissioning PLANS an enterprise MIGHT choose TO undertake.
3

Recognition Criteria

A legal obligation with a reasonably estimable fair value
A LIABILITY is RECOGNIZED when a LEGAL obligation EXISTS, and a REASONABLE estimate OF its FAIR value CAN be MADE – WHERE fair VALUE cannot BE reasonably ESTIMATED at THE time the OBLIGATION is INCURRED, recognition IS deferred UNTIL a REASONABLE estimate BECOMES possible.
4

Initial Measurement – Fair Value

Fair value, or an expected present value technique where fair value is unavailable
THE asset RETIREMENT obligation is INITIALLY measured AT fair VALUE – WHERE a MARKET-based fair VALUE is NOT directly OBSERVABLE, an EXPECTED present VALUE technique is USED instead, DISCOUNTING the ESTIMATED future CASH flows required TO settle the OBLIGATION.
5

Capitalization Into the Related Asset

Increasing the carrying amount of the related tangible asset
THE initial ASSET retirement OBLIGATION liability IS capitalized AS part of the CARRYING amount OF the RELATED long-LIVED asset – SUBSEQUENTLY depreciated OVER that ASSET'S useful LIFE, PER Section 3061'S own GENERAL depreciation PRINCIPLES (see the COMPANION poster).
6

Subsequent Measurement – Accretion of the Liability

A period expense reflecting the passage of time
THE liability INCREASES over TIME through "ACCRETION" – RECOGNIZING a PERIOD expense (SIMILAR in NATURE to INTEREST expense) that REFLECTS the PASSAGE of TIME as the DISCOUNTED liability MOVES closer TO its UNDISCOUNTED, ULTIMATE settlement AMOUNT.
7

Changes in the Estimated Liability

Adjusting both the liability and the related asset
WHERE the TIMING or AMOUNT of ESTIMATED future CASH flows CHANGES (e.g. a REVISED cost ESTIMATE, or a CHANGE in the EXPECTED settlement DATE), BOTH the LIABILITY and the CARRYING amount OF the RELATED asset ARE adjusted – CONSISTENT with the GENERAL approach ALSO used INTERNATIONALLY under IFRIC 1.
8

Settlement of the Obligation

Recognizing a gain or loss on settlement
UPON settling the OBLIGATION (e.g. ACTUALLY performing the DECOMMISSIONING or RESTORATION work), the ENTERPRISE recognizes a GAIN or LOSS FOR any DIFFERENCE between the LIABILITY'S carrying AMOUNT and the ACTUAL cost INCURRED to SETTLE it.
9

Worked Example – Initial Recognition and Capitalization

Adding the discounted future cost to the asset's carrying amount
Example: A mining site restoration obligation
A mining company has a legal obligation to restore a site upon closure in 15 years. The estimated future restoration cost is $2,000,000, with a present value (using an appropriate discount rate) of $780,000.
The COMPANY recognizes a $780,000 ASSET retirement OBLIGATION liability, CAPITALIZING the SAME $780,000 into the CARRYING amount OF the RELATED mining ASSET – SUBSEQUENTLY depreciated OVER the ASSET'S remaining USEFUL life.
10

Worked Example – Accretion Expense Over Time

The liability grows each period, reflecting the discount unwinding
Example: Accreting the liability in year one
Continuing the prior example, the $780,000 liability carries an effective discount rate of 6% per year.
ACCRETION expense FOR the FIRST year = $780,000 × 6% = $46,800, INCREASING the LIABILITY to $826,800 AT year-end – RECOGNIZED as a PERIOD expense, SIMILAR in NATURE to INTEREST expense, REFLECTING the PASSAGE of TIME.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Accretion
The periodic increase in an asset retirement obligation liability reflecting the passage of time, recognized as an expense.
Expected present value technique
A measurement approach discounting probability-weighted estimated future cash flows, used when a market-based fair value is not directly observable.
Financial statement key (ASPE terminology)
BS Balance Sheet IS Income Statement SRE Statement of Retained Earnings / Statement of Changes in Equity SCF Cash Flow Statement
