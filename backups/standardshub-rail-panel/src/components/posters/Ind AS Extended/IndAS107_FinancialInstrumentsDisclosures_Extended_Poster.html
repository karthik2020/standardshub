Conceptual Framework Linkage

Identical disclosure philosophy to IFRS 7
Ind AS 107 applies the IDENTICAL Conceptual Framework logic as IFRS 7 — recognised amounts alone tell users little about risk exposure or measurement reliability; genuine understanding requires SEPARATE, DEDICATED risk disclosures (credit, liquidity, market) alongside the fair value hierarchy.
HOW IND AS 107 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Relevance — risk disclosures give users forward-looking insight beyond recognised balances — IDENTICAL rationale to IFRS 7 Faithful representation — the fair value hierarchy distinguishes observable from unobservable inputs — IDENTICAL to IFRS 7 Dual regulatory layer for financial sector entities — for Indian banks and NBFCs, Ind AS 107's disclosures operate ALONGSIDE the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) own extensive prudential disclosure norms — a genuinely India-specific dual-track feature, similar in spirit to IRDAI's role for insurers under Ind AS 104
Ind AS 107 was one of the LATER standards to become fully effective for Indian banks specifically (given phased regulatory transition), but its accounting SUBSTANCE mirrors IFRS 7 closely once applicable.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 107 covers
Ind AS 107 applies to ALL entities, for ALL types of financial instruments — IDENTICAL scope to IFRS 7, with the SAME scope exclusions (subsidiary/associate/JV interests, employee benefit plans, insurance contracts except embedded derivatives).
In practice, DETAILED sensitivity analysis and fair value hierarchy disclosures are most extensively applied by Indian banks, NBFCs, and other financial-sector-heavy entities.
Key definitions you must know
Credit risk / Liquidity risk / Market risk
IDENTICAL definitions to IFRS 7.
RBI
Reserve Bank of India — India's central bank and banking/NBFC regulator, prescribing its own extensive prudential disclosure requirements alongside Ind AS 107.

Significance of Financial Instruments

Identical disclosure requirements to IFRS 7
Carrying amounts by Ind AS 109 category, net gains/losses, interest income/expense (effective interest method), impairment (ECL) reconciliation, reclassifications, collateral — ALL identical to IFRS 7's own requirements.

Credit Risk Disclosures

Identical ECL-staging disclosures to IFRS 7
Credit risk management practices, quantitative/qualitative ECL information, three-stage movement analysis (12-month/lifetime not credit-impaired/lifetime credit-impaired), and concentration disclosure — IDENTICAL to IFRS 7's requirements, aligned with Ind AS 109's impairment model.

Liquidity Risk Disclosures (with worked example)

Identical undiscounted maturity analysis to IFRS 7
Example 1: An Indian company's undiscounted maturity analysis
An Indian company has a term loan requiring ₹40,00,000 principal plus ₹3,20,000 interest in Year 1, and ₹40,00,000 principal plus ₹1,60,000 interest in Year 2.
Disclosed maturity analysis (UNDISCOUNTED): ₹43,20,000 due within 1 year, ₹41,60,000 due in 1-2 years — an IDENTICAL calculation methodology to IFRS 7.

Market Risk & Sensitivity Analysis

Identical mandatory sensitivity analysis to IFRS 7
For EACH type of market risk, Ind AS 107 requires sensitivity analysis showing the effect of reasonably possible changes in the relevant risk variable on profit/loss and equity — IDENTICAL requirement to IFRS 7, commonly applied by Indian companies to interest rate risk on floating-rate borrowings and foreign currency risk given India's significant import/export exposure.

RBI's Regulatory Role for Banks and NBFCs (with worked example)

A parallel prudential disclosure framework
The Reserve Bank of India prescribes DETAILED prudential disclosure norms for banks and NBFCs — including capital adequacy (Basel III-aligned), asset classification and provisioning (NPA norms), and specific format requirements for financial statements under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 and RBI Master Directions. These operate ALONGSIDE Ind AS 107's own disclosures, similar in spirit to IRDAI's role for insurers.
Example 2: RBI vs Ind AS 107 disclosure layers for a bank
An Indian scheduled commercial bank prepares its annual report with Ind AS 107's credit/liquidity/market risk disclosures, PLUS RBI-mandated Basel III Pillar 3 disclosures and asset quality (NPA) disclosures under RBI's prudential norms.
BOTH disclosure sets are required SIMULTANEOUSLY — Ind AS 107 governs the ACCOUNTING STANDARD-based disclosures, while RBI's prudential framework governs SUPERVISORY, capital-adequacy-focused disclosures — a genuinely India-specific dual reporting burden for financial sector entities, with NO direct single-standard equivalent in a typical IFRS 7 jurisdiction (though comparable prudential regimes, e.g. Basel III itself, exist globally alongside IFRS 7 too).

The Fair Value Hierarchy

Identical three-level hierarchy to IFRS 7/IFRS 13
Level 1 (quoted prices), Level 2 (observable inputs), Level 3 (unobservable inputs) — IDENTICAL to IFRS 7/IFRS 13, with Ind AS 113 (the Ind AS equivalent of IFRS 13) supplying the underlying fair value measurement framework.
