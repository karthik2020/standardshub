SIC-11 – Foreign Exchange–Capitalisation of Losses from Severe Currency Devaluations
Historical reference note · Issued 1998 · Withdrawn 2003
⚠ WITHDRAWN – Superseded by the 2003 revision of IAS 21 The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates, effective for periods beginning 1 January 2005
What SIC-11 addressed
SIC-11 addressed a narrow question: could exchange LOSSES on foreign currency liabilities incurred to acquire an asset – arising from a SEVERE currency devaluation against which there was no practical means of hedging – be CAPITALISED into the carrying amount of the related asset, rather than expensed immediately, where the liability could not have been settled before the devaluation occurred?
The conclusion it reached, and why it was later reversed
SIC-11 permitted such capitalisation, but only in RESTRICTED circumstances: the loss had to result directly from a recent, severe devaluation of a currency for which no practical hedging existed, and had to relate to a liability that could not have been settled before the devaluation and arose directly from acquiring the asset. When the IASB revised IAS 21 in 2003, this capitalisation option was REMOVED ENTIRELY – under current IAS 21, ALL exchange differences, including those from severe devaluations, are recognised in PROFIT OR LOSS, with no exception allowing capitalisation into a related asset's carrying amount.
Where this guidance lives today
Superseded byIAS 21 The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates (revised 2003)
Why withdrawnThe capitalisation option SIC-11 governed was eliminated outright by the IASB, not merely folded into the main standard's text
Current statusNo longer applicable – even in cases of severe currency devaluation, exchange losses on related liabilities must be expensed through profit or loss under current IFRS, not capitalised
