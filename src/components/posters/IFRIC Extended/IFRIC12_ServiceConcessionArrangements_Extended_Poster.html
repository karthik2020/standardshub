Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why the operator often does NOT control the infrastructure
Recognition of an asset under the Conceptual Framework depends on control – the ability to direct the use of, and obtain substantially all the remaining benefits from, a resource. In a public-to-private service concession, the GRANTOR retains control over the underlying infrastructure's use, pricing, and ultimate disposition. IFRIC 12 concludes the operator, despite building and operating the infrastructure, typically does not control it in the IAS 16 sense – its real asset is a RIGHT (to cash or to charge users), not the infrastructure itself.
Faithful representation of what the operator actually holds
Recognising the infrastructure as the operator's own PP&E would overstate the operator's control and misrepresent the substance of the arrangement – the operator's real economic position is a contractual right to be paid for the services it provides, which IFRIC 12's financial-asset/intangible-asset models represent more faithfully.
2

Scope – The Two-Part Control Test

Both conditions must be met for IFRIC 12 to apply
ConditionRequirement
(a)The grantor CONTROLS OR REGULATES what services the operator must provide with the infrastructure, to whom, and at what price
(b)The grantor CONTROLS – through ownership, beneficial entitlement, or otherwise – any significant residual interest in the infrastructure at the end of the arrangement
⚠️ Infrastructure the operator uses under an arrangement that does NOT meet BOTH conditions – for example, if the operator can freely price its services or keeps significant residual value – falls OUTSIDE IFRIC 12's scope and is accounted for as ordinary PP&E or a lease instead.
3

Why Infrastructure Stays Off the Operator's PP&E

The central mechanic
Infrastructure within IFRIC 12's scope is NOT recognised as property, plant and equipment of the OPERATOR, even though the operator builds, maintains, and operates it – because the grantor's control over usage and residual value means the operator lacks the control IAS 16 requires for asset recognition. The operator instead recognises a FINANCIAL ASSET, an INTANGIBLE ASSET, or a combination of both, reflecting what it actually has: a contractual right arising from the services it provides.
4

The Financial Asset Model (with worked example)

When it applies
The operator recognises a FINANCIAL ASSET to the extent it has an UNCONDITIONAL CONTRACTUAL RIGHT to receive cash or another financial asset from (or at the direction of) the grantor – because the grantor has GUARANTEED payment for the construction/upgrade services, regardless of actual usage of the infrastructure by end users.
Worked example – grantor-guaranteed toll road
Example: Availability-payment concession
An operator builds a $200,000,000 toll road under an arrangement where the grantor unconditionally guarantees fixed availability payments over 25 years, regardless of actual traffic volumes.
The operator recognises a FINANCIAL ASSET (typically a receivable measured under IFRS 9) for the construction services provided, since the grantor bears demand risk entirely and the operator's right to payment does not depend on usage.
5

The Intangible Asset Model (with worked example)

When it applies
The operator recognises an INTANGIBLE ASSET to the extent it receives a RIGHT to charge users of the public service – because the grantor does NOT guarantee payment, and the operator instead bears DEMAND RISK for usage of the infrastructure.
Worked example – user-pays toll road
Example: Pure demand-risk concession
An operator builds a $150,000,000 toll road and is granted the right to collect and retain tolls directly from drivers for 30 years, with no minimum revenue guarantee from the grantor.
The operator recognises an INTANGIBLE ASSET for the right to charge users (accounted for under IAS 38), amortised over the concession period, since it bears the full risk that usage – and therefore recovery of its investment – may fall short of expectations.
6

The Bifurcated Model

When consideration is split between guaranteed and demand-based
Where the operator is compensated PARTLY through an unconditional guarantee and PARTLY through a right to charge users, EACH component is accounted for separately: the guaranteed portion as a financial asset, and the demand-risk portion as an intangible asset – requiring the entity to allocate the fair value of consideration receivable between the two components at inception, and apply the respective subsequent measurement models to each going forward.
7

Construction & Upgrade Revenue

Recognised under IFRS 15, like any other service
Construction or upgrade services the operator provides to the grantor are RECOGNISED AS REVENUE under IFRS 15, measured at the fair value of the consideration receivable (whether that consideration takes the form of the financial asset, the intangible asset, or a combination). Operating and maintenance services provided during the operating phase are also recognised as revenue under IFRS 15, separately from the construction/upgrade revenue.
8

Borrowing Costs & Restoration Obligations

Two distinct interactions to track
BORROWING COSTS (IAS 23)
Generally expensed as incurred UNLESS the operator has a contractual right to receive an INTANGIBLE ASSET (a right to charge users) – in which case borrowing costs attributable to the construction phase may be capitalised, consistent with IAS 23's general treatment of qualifying assets.
RESTORATION OBLIGATIONS (IAS 37)
A contractual obligation to restore the infrastructure to a specified level of serviceability before it is handed back to the grantor is recognised and measured in accordance with IAS 37, best estimate of the expenditure required to settle the obligation at the reporting date.

IFRIC 12 vs US GAAP

Comparison – IFRIC 12 vs US GAAP (ASC 853)
FeatureIFRS (IFRIC 12)US GAAP (ASC 853)
Overall approachFinancial asset / intangible asset / bifurcated model, based on demand-risk allocationBroadly converged – ASC 853 (Service Concession Arrangements) was substantially modelled on IFRIC 12, reaching similar off-balance-sheet-infrastructure conclusions
Revenue recognition for construction servicesUnder IFRS 15Under ASC 606 (the US GAAP revenue standard), reaching largely converged outcomes given the two revenue standards' close alignment
Terminology"Financial asset model" / "intangible asset model"Same conceptual split, broadly consistent terminology
Service concession accounting is one of the more CONVERGED areas between IFRS and US GAAP, since ASC 853 was developed with direct reference to IFRIC 12's framework rather than independently.

Quick-reference glossary
Grantor
The public sector body (or private entity to which the concession has been contracted out) that grants the service arrangement to the operator and retains control over the infrastructure's use and residual value.
Demand risk
The risk that actual usage of infrastructure by end users falls short of expectations, borne by the operator under the intangible asset model and by the grantor under the financial asset model.
14

Quick-Reference Glossary

Public-to-private service concession arrangement
An arrangement whereby a government or public sector body (grantor) contracts with a private operator to develop, finance, operate, and maintain infrastructure used to provide a public service.
Unconditional contractual right to receive cash
A right the grantor cannot avoid, typically because payment is guaranteed regardless of the extent to which the public actually uses the service – the defining feature of the financial asset model.

SOFP Statement of financial position SOPL Statement of profit or loss OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
