Objective, Scope & Definitions (paras. 1–7)

Objective & scope
Prescribes financial reporting requirements for revenue arising from NON-EXCHANGE transactions — other than those giving rise to a public sector combination. Covers recognizing and measuring such revenue, including identifying contributions from owners.
A public sector combination that is itself a non-exchange transaction (IPSAS 40) Contributions to social benefit schemes under the insurance approach (IPSAS 42, paras. 26–31)
Sister standard: IPSAS 9 covers EXCHANGE transaction revenue. Together they cover most public sector revenue, pending IPSAS 47's eventual broader rollout.
Key definitions (para. 7)
Taxes
Economic benefits/service potential compulsorily paid or payable to public sector entities, per laws/regulations established to provide government revenue — EXCLUDING fines or other penalties.
Transfers
Inflows of future economic benefits/service potential from non-exchange transactions, OTHER than taxes.
Control of an asset
Arises when the entity can use/benefit from the asset in pursuit of its objectives, AND can exclude or regulate others' access to that benefit.
The taxable event
The event that government/legislature/other authority has determined will be subject to taxation.

Non-Exchange vs Exchange Transactions (paras. 8–11)

Three transaction patterns
Clearly EXCHANGE — approximately equal value both ways (governed by IPSAS 9) Clearly NON-EXCHANGE — the entity receives resources and provides no/nominal consideration directly in return (governed by IPSAS 23) MIXED — some consideration given, but not approximating fair value → SPLIT into an exchange component (IPSAS 9) and a non-exchange component (IPSAS 23), each recognized separately
Taxpayers pay taxes because the law mandates it — not in exchange for the public services the government happens to provide with that revenue (para. 9).
Worked example — a concessionary loan is really TWO transactions
Multi-lateral agency provides CU6m; entity must repay CU5m over 10 years at 5% (market rate 11%)
The entity has effectively received a grant PLUS a below-market loan.
Non-exchange component: CU1m grant (CU6m − CU5m to be repaid), PLUS the off-market portion of the interest (6% below market). Exchange component: the CU5m contractual capital and interest payments at 5% over the loan's life.
Subsidized-price sales are also split by substance, not form — a "sale" at well below fair value has a non-exchange component too (para. 11).

Stipulations on Transferred Assets — Conditions vs Restrictions (paras. 14–24)

Conditions — impose a PRESENT OBLIGATION (a liability)
Require the recipient to CONSUME the future economic benefits/service potential as specified, OR RETURN them to the transferor if breached. Because the recipient cannot avoid this outflow, a LIABILITY is recognized alongside the asset on initial recognition (para. 17).
A stipulation is a CONDITION only if the return obligation is genuinely ENFORCEABLE and would actually be ENFORCED — assessed on the transferor's past behavior, or (absent history) presumed enforceable (paras. 20–21).
Restrictions — limit USE, but no return obligation
Limit or direct the PURPOSES an asset may be used for, but do NOT require return if not deployed as specified. Gaining control does NOT create a present obligation — breach may trigger a PENALTY (court action, administrative directive), but that penalty stems from the breach itself, not from acquiring the asset (para. 19).
Substance over form governs throughout: merely LABELING a stipulation as requiring consumption/return isn't sufficient by itself to create a liability (para. 20). Constructive obligations do NOT arise from stipulations — those are governed separately by IPSAS 19 (para. 16).
What a valid condition needs (para. 23) & conditional-on-a-future-event transfers
Nature/quantity of goods, services, or assets to be provided/acquired, specified clearly Performance periods, if relevant, AND ongoing monitoring by or on behalf of the transferor An outflow that is genuinely PROBABLE, with performance capable of being assessed
Where an asset must be RETURNED if a future event does NOT occur (e.g. a national government grants matching funds subject to the recipient raising its own contribution), no return obligation — and no liability — arises UNTIL it's expected the stipulation WILL be breached (para. 24).

Recognition of Assets (paras. 29–36)

The two-part test (para. 31)
PROBABLE that future economic benefits/service potential will flow to the entity Fair value can be measured RELIABLY
Exception: when a creditor forgives a liability, the entity DECREASES the carrying amount of the liability rather than recognizing a new asset (para. 29).
Control & the past event — announcements aren't enough
✕ Not sufficient: a mere ANNOUNCEMENT of an intention to transfer resources does NOT itself identify controlled resources. A destroyed school doesn't recognize a receivable the moment government announces it will fund rebuilding (para. 33).
Where a transfer AGREEMENT is required, resources aren't controlled until the agreement is BINDING (para. 33) The past event may be a purchase, a taxable event, or a transfer — an INTENTION to levy tax is never itself a past event (para. 34) An item meeting the asset definition but failing recognition criteria may warrant CONTINGENT ASSET disclosure under IPSAS 19 (para. 36)

Contributions from Owners & Mixed Exchange/Non-Exchange Components (paras. 37–43)

Substance over form — is it really equity?
A FORMAL DESIGNATION by the contributor (or its controlling entity) as part of the recipient's contributed net assets/equity A formal agreement establishing/increasing a sellable, transferable, or redeemable financial interest Issuance of sellable/transferable/redeemable equity instruments
⚠️ If a transaction is LABELED a contribution but specifies fixed distributions with an eventual return of the "investment" at a set date — that's economically a LOAN, and is accounted for as such (para. 37).
Measuring assets on initial recognition (para. 42)
An asset acquired through a non-exchange transaction → measured initially at CURRENT VALUE as at the date of acquisition
Inventories, investment property, intangibles, financial instruments → FAIR VALUE at acquisition. PP&E → DEEMED COST at acquisition, where the deemed-cost basis (current operational value vs. fair value) follows the same primary-objective test used under IPSAS 45 (paras. 43, 43A).

Recognition & Measurement of Revenue from Non-Exchange Transactions (paras. 44–58)

The core recognition rule
Revenue = the increase in net assets recognized — an asset inflow is revenue EXCEPT to the extent a liability is ALSO recognized for the same inflow
As a recognized LIABILITY (a condition) is satisfied, the entity REDUCES the liability and recognizes REVENUE equal to that reduction (para. 45) Timing follows the nature of conditions and their settlement — e.g. revenue recognized as goods/services are actually provided under the condition (para. 47)
A transaction satisfying the definition of contributions from owners is NEVER recognized as a liability or revenue (para. 46).
Present obligations recognized as liabilities (para. 50)
A present obligation arising from a non-exchange transaction that meets the liability definition and satisfies the recognition criteria is recognized as a LIABILITY — measured at the BEST ESTIMATE of the amount required to settle it at the reporting date (para. 57).
Revenue from non-exchange transactions = the amount of the increase in NET ASSETS recognized by the entity

Taxes (paras. 59–75)

Recognition trigger & who recognizes it
Recognize an asset when the TAXABLE EVENT occurs AND asset recognition criteria are met
Revenue accrues ONLY to the government that IMPOSES the tax — a collecting AGENCY acting on another government's behalf recognizes nothing (para. 61) A national government passing 100% of collected sales tax to states recognizes ITS OWN asset/revenue, plus an expense for the transfer; the STATES recognize the transfer as their own asset/revenue (para. 61)
Taxes do NOT satisfy the contributions-from-owners definition — paying tax gives no distribution rights or ownership interest (para. 62).
The taxable event, by tax type (para. 65)
Tax typeTaxable event
Income taxEarning of assessable income during the tax period
VATUndertaking a taxable activity during the tax period
Goods and services taxPurchase/sale of taxable goods and services during the period
Customs dutyMovement of dutiable goods/services across the customs boundary
Death dutyDeath of a person owning taxable property
Property taxPassing of the levy date, or the levy period if periodic
Measurement — statistical models, and when reliability is delayed
Measured at the BEST ESTIMATE of the inflow — often via statistical models based on collection history, filing patterns, and economic relationships (para. 68). A separation between the taxable event and collection timing is normal and doesn't itself block recognition; ESTIMATE revisions follow IPSAS 3 (para. 69).
Exceptional cases (e.g. a large deceased estate needing specialist antique/artwork valuations for death duty) may delay recognition until receipt/receivability is achieved several periods after the taxable event (para. 70).
Advance receipts, expenses paid through the tax system & tax expenditures
Tax received BEFORE the taxable event occurs → recognize an ASSET and a LIABILITY (advance receipt); revenue only when the taxable event later occurs (para. 66) Taxation revenue is measured GROSS — never reduced for "expenses paid through the tax system" (amounts available to beneficiaries regardless of whether they pay tax); those are separately recognized as government EXPENSES (paras. 71–72) Taxation revenue is never GROSSED UP for tax EXPENDITURES (concessions only available to taxpayers) — these are foregone revenue, not expenses; no asset/liability/revenue/expense arises at all (paras. 73–74)
Offsetting tax revenue against expenses paid through the tax system is explicitly PROHIBITED (para. 75, cross-ref IPSAS 1).

Transfers — Debt Forgiveness & Fines (paras. 76, 84–89)

Debt forgiveness
Revenue = the CARRYING AMOUNT of the debt forgiven
Recognized when the former debt no longer meets the liability definition or recognition criteria — provided it doesn't itself satisfy the CONTRIBUTIONS FROM OWNERS definition (para. 85). A controlling entity forgiving a WHOLLY-OWNED controlled entity's debt, or assuming its liabilities, may instead be a contribution from owners (para. 86).
Fines
Economic benefits/service potential received or receivable, as determined by a COURT or other LAW ENFORCEMENT body, following a breach of laws/regulations. A negotiated penalty-instead-of-trial payment IS recognized as a fine (para. 88).
Normally require a fixed CASH transfer, with no offsetting government obligation → no liability recognized Recognized as revenue when the receivable meets the asset definition/recognition criteria; measured at the BEST ESTIMATE of the inflow Collected as an AGENT → not revenue of the collecting entity (para. 89)

Transfers — Bequests, Gifts & Donations, Including Goods In-Kind (paras. 90–97)

Bequests
A transfer per the provisions of a deceased person's WILL. The past event = when the entity gains an ENFORCEABLE CLAIM (e.g. on death, or on grant of probate, per jurisdiction). Recognized as assets/revenue when probable AND reliably measurable — probability may be affected by elapsed time, estate solvency, and disputed wills (paras. 90–91).
Fair value follows the same approach as gifts/donations (§9 right) — sometimes already determined by a tax authority for deceased-estate tax purposes (para. 92).
Gifts, donations & goods in-kind
VOLUNTARY transfers, normally free from stipulations, from an entity or individual. For cash/monetary gifts and goods in-kind, the past event is normally RECEIPT of the gift.
Goods in-kind: recognized as assets when received, or when a BINDING arrangement to receive them exists No conditions attached → revenue recognized IMMEDIATELY Conditions attached → a liability is recognized, reduced (with revenue recognized) as conditions are satisfied
Initial measurement (para. 97): inventories, investment property, intangibles, financial instruments → FAIR VALUE at acquisition. PP&E → DEEMED COST, using the current operational value or fair value basis per its primary holding objective (same test as IPSAS 45).

Services In-Kind & Pledges (paras. 98–105)

Services in-kind — optional recognition
ℹ️ An entity MAY, but is NOT REQUIRED to, recognize services in-kind as revenue and as an asset (para. 98).
These technically meet the asset definition — but are IMMEDIATELY consumed, so an equal-value transaction (usually an expense) is recognized alongside the revenue. If used to CONSTRUCT an asset instead, the amount is included in that asset's cost (para. 99).
TYPICAL EXAMPLES (PARA. 100)
Technical assistance from other governments/international organizations Convicted offenders performing community service Public hospital or school volunteers; volunteer firefighters
Why recognition is optional, and pledges
Uncertainty over CONTROL and RELIABLE MEASUREMENT of services in-kind is pervasive — many are rendered by untrained volunteers, fundamentally different from services the entity would otherwise purchase (para. 102). Disclosure of nature/type is ENCOURAGED regardless of recognition (para. 108).
✕ Pledges are never assets: a pledge is an UNENFORCEABLE undertaking to transfer assets — the recipient cannot control the transferor's access to the pledged item. Never recognized as an asset or revenue; may warrant IPSAS 19 contingent-asset disclosure. If the pledge is subsequently ACTUALLY transferred, it's then recognized as a gift/donation (para. 104).

Disclosure Requirements (paras. 106–108)

Face or notes (para. 106)
Revenue by major class — taxes (by major tax class) and transfers (by major transfer class), shown separately Receivables recognized in respect of non-exchange revenue Liabilities recognized for transferred assets subject to CONDITIONS (and, separately, concessionary-loan conditions) Assets subject to RESTRICTIONS, and the nature of those restrictions Existence and amounts of any advance receipts Any liabilities FORGIVEN
Notes only (para. 107) & encouraged extras
Accounting policies for recognizing non-exchange revenue Basis used to measure fair value of inflowing resources, by major revenue class Nature of taxes the entity CANNOT reliably measure during the taxable-event period Nature and type of major bequest/gift/donation classes, with goods-in-kind shown separately
✓ Disclosure of the nature/type of major services-in-kind classes RECEIVED is encouraged — including those NOT recognized (para. 108). Disaggregation of the conditions-liability disclosure by class is also encouraged (para. 111).

Relationship to IPSAS 9 & IPSAS 47 — No IAS/IFRS Equivalent

A genuinely public-sector-only Standard, now in transition
IPSAS 23 has NO IFRS counterpart — non-exchange revenue (taxes, fines, gifts) is largely a public-sector-specific concept that private-sector accounting standards don't need to address at this scale. It was DEVELOPED independently by the IPSASB, drawing on the Conceptual Framework rather than converging with any existing IASB Standard.
IPSAS 9 is the sister standard covering EXCHANGE transaction revenue — together the two Standards cover essentially all public sector revenue IPSAS 47, Revenue (issued May 2023, IFRS 15-style five-step model) is the IPSASB's eventual unified successor, covering BOTH exchange and non-exchange revenue in one framework
⚠️ IPSAS 23 remains in the Handbook and continues to receive consequential amendments — it has NOT been formally withdrawn. Preparers should track their jurisdiction's transition plan toward IPSAS 47, which is available for early adoption.

Statement / Process Mapping

The full non-exchange inflow analysis, end to end
1. Is this an EXCHANGE, NON-EXCHANGE, or MIXED transaction? Split mixed transactions into components (§2) 2. Does the inflow meet the ASSET definition and recognition criteria — probable, reliably measurable, past event occurred, control established? If no → no asset (§4) 3. Does the transaction satisfy CONTRIBUTIONS FROM OWNERS (substance test)? → not revenue at all, recognized in net assets/equity directly (§5) 4. Is the transferred asset subject to a STIPULATION? CONDITION → recognize a liability too. RESTRICTION → asset only, no liability (§3) 5. Recognize revenue = the net increase in net assets; as any liability/condition is later satisfied, reduce it and recognize revenue equal to the reduction (§6) 6. Route to the SPECIFIC transfer-type guidance — taxes (§7), debt forgiveness/fines (§8), bequests/gifts/donations (§9), services in-kind/pledges (§10) — each with its own past-event and measurement nuances

Quick-Reference Glossary

Conditions on transferred assets
Stipulations requiring consumption as specified, or return of benefits, if breached — create a liability on initial recognition.
Restrictions on transferred assets
Stipulations limiting purpose of use, without a return-on-breach requirement — no liability arises.
Fines
Economic benefits received/receivable as determined by a court or law enforcement body, following a breach of laws or regulations.
Expenses paid through the tax system
Amounts available to beneficiaries regardless of whether they pay taxes — recognized as a separate government expense.
Tax expenditures
Preferential tax law provisions available only to taxpayers — foregone revenue, not expenses; no accounting entries arise.
Pledges
Unenforceable undertakings to transfer assets — never recognized as an asset or revenue.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOFPerf Statement of financial performance NA/E Net assets/equity (statement of changes)
