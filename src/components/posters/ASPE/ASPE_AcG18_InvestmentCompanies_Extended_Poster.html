ASPE Accounting Guideline AcG-18 – Investment Companies
Extended reference poster · Canadian GAAP (ASPE – Part II of the CPA Canada Handbook) · Overriding consolidation and the equity method with fair value throughout – conceptually parallel to IFRS 10's investment entity exception · CAD examples
A GUIDELINE THAT OVERRIDES THE NORMAL FRAMEWORK: For a qualifying investment company, AcG-18 displaces the ordinary consolidation (Section 1591) and equity method (Section 3051) requirements entirely – every investee, regardless of the degree of control or influence held, is measured at fair value through net income.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Fair value information, not consolidated line items, is what investors in an investment company actually want
AcG-18 addresses HOW a QUALIFYING investment COMPANY accounts FOR its INVESTMENTS – REGARDLESS of the DEGREE of INFLUENCE or CONTROL held OVER a PARTICULAR investee, ALL investments ARE measured AT fair VALUE through NET income. THIS Guideline OVERRIDES the NORMAL ownership-BASED accounting FRAMEWORK (Sections 1591 and 3051 – see the COMPANION posters) an INVESTMENT company WOULD otherwise HAVE to APPLY.
2

Scope – What Qualifies as an Investment Company

An entity whose business purpose is investing for returns, not operational integration
An INVESTMENT company is AN entity WHOSE business PURPOSE is INVESTING funds FOR returns IN the FORM of INTEREST, dividends, OR capital APPRECIATION – WITH no PLAN to HOLD its INVESTMENTS indefinitely FOR strategic OR operational purposes.
3

Qualifying Criteria

Investment purpose, portfolio management, and fair-value-based evaluation
Business purpose is investing funds for capital appreciation, investment income, or bothNo plans for investees to become operationally integrated with the investment companyOwnership interests may be represented by units entitling the holder to a share of net assetsInvestees are managed and evaluated collectively as a portfolio, on a fair value basis
4

THE CORE PRINCIPLE – Fair Value Throughout, Regardless of Influence or Control

No consolidation, no equity method – even for controlled investees
A QUALIFYING investment COMPANY measures ALL of its INVESTMENTS at FAIR value, WITH changes RECOGNIZED directly IN net INCOME – THIS applies EVEN to INVESTMENTS where the INVESTMENT company HOLDS control (WHICH would OTHERWISE trigger CONSOLIDATION under Section 1591) or SIGNIFICANT influence (WHICH would OTHERWISE trigger the EQUITY method under SECTION 3051). NEITHER consolidation NOR the EQUITY method IS applied by a QUALIFYING investment COMPANY – FAIR value THROUGH net INCOME is the SOLE measurement BASIS, REGARDLESS of OWNERSHIP percentage OR degree OF influence.
5

Why This Override Exists

Fair value is simply more decision-useful for this specific business model
FOR an ENTITY whose ENTIRE business MODEL is BUYING and SELLING investments FOR a RETURN, CONSOLIDATING a CONTROLLED investee'S individual ASSETS and LIABILITIES line-BY-line would OBSCURE, RATHER than illuminate, the INFORMATION genuinely RELEVANT to the INVESTMENT company'S own INVESTORS – WHO care ABOUT the AGGREGATE fair VALUE of the PORTFOLIO, NOT the OPERATIONAL details OF each UNDERLYING investee. FAIR value THROUGHOUT is SIMPLY the MORE decision-USEFUL measurement BASIS for THIS particular BUSINESS model.
6

Worked Example – Measuring a Controlled Investee at Fair Value

No consolidation, despite a controlling stake
Example: A private equity fund holding a controlling stake
A qualifying investment company holds a 70% controlling interest in a portfolio company, originally invested at $4,000,000, now with an estimated fair value of $6,500,000.
DESPITE holding a CONTROLLING interest, the INVESTMENT company does NOT consolidate the PORTFOLIO company – it SIMPLY measures the INVESTMENT at its $6,500,000 FAIR value, RECOGNIZING a $6,500,000 − $4,000,000 = $2,500,000 gain DIRECTLY within NET income.
