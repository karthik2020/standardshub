1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

One activity, two distinct economic benefits
Waste removal (stripping) in an open-pit mine produces TWO benefits simultaneously: it exposes ore extracted in the CURRENT period (an inventory-type benefit), and it improves access to ore that will be extracted in FUTURE periods (a longer-term, asset-type benefit). Before IFRIC 20, practice varied on whether to expense all stripping costs, capitalise them all, or something in between — the interpretation exists to split the cost between these two genuinely different economic benefits.
Matching cost recognition to benefit recognition
Recognising all stripping costs as an expense as incurred would understate assets and overstate current-period costs relative to the future benefit obtained; capitalising it all would defer costs that genuinely relate to current inventory production. IFRIC 20's split mirrors the two distinct future economic benefits obtained, consistent with the Conceptual Framework's matching of cost recognition to the pattern of benefit consumption.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRIC 20 covers
IFRIC 20 addresses the accounting for waste removal (stripping) costs incurred in surface mining activity during the PRODUCTION phase of a mine (as distinct from the DEVELOPMENT phase, before production begins, which is addressed by general IAS 16/38 capitalisation principles instead).
TYPICAL CONTEXTS
Open-pit metal and mineral mines requiring ongoing removal of overburden and waste rockSurface coal mines with progressive stripping ratios as the pit advances
Key definitions
Stripping costs
Costs of removing waste materials (overburden and other waste) to gain access to mineral ore deposits during the production phase of a surface mine.
Component of the ore body
A specific, identifiable part of the ore body that is made more accessible by a particular stripping campaign, forming the unit of account for the stripping activity asset.
3

Splitting the Benefit — Inventory vs Improved Access

The central mechanic
To the extent the benefit from stripping activity is REALISED IN THE FORM OF INVENTORY produced, the entity accounts for the cost of that stripping activity in accordance with IAS 2. To the extent stripping activity IMPROVES ACCESS to ore to be mined in the FUTURE, the entity recognises the cost as a NON-CURRENT ASSET — the "stripping activity asset" — subject to the recognition criteria in Section 4.
4

Recognition Criteria for the Stripping Activity Asset

All three conditions must be met
ConditionRequirement
(a)It is PROBABLE that the future economic benefit (improved access to the ore body component) associated with the stripping activity will flow to the entity
(b)The entity can IDENTIFY the component of the ore body for which access has been improved
(c)The costs relating to the stripping activity associated with that component can be measured RELIABLY
5

Initial Measurement

Cost accumulation, treated as an addition to an existing asset
The stripping activity asset is initially measured at COST — the accumulation of costs directly incurred to perform the stripping activity that improves access to the identified component of ore, plus an allocation of directly attributable overhead. It is accounted for as an ADDITION TO, OR ENHANCEMENT OF, an existing asset (the mine itself), and its classification as property, plant and equipment or an intangible asset follows the NATURE of the existing asset it enhances.
6

Subsequent Measurement & Amortisation

Systematically depreciated over the component's useful life
After initial recognition, the stripping activity asset is carried at cost (or revalued amount, if that model is applied to the underlying asset class) less depreciation/amortisation and impairment, and is depreciated or amortised on a SYSTEMATIC BASIS over the EXPECTED USEFUL LIFE of the identified component of the ore body that becomes more accessible as a result of the stripping activity — typically using a units-of-production method tied to the reserves in that specific component.
7

Allocating Costs Between Inventory and the Asset

A relevant production measure, if costs cannot be specifically identified
If the costs of the inventory-related benefit and the asset-related benefit are NOT separately identifiable, the entity allocates production stripping costs between the two using a RELEVANT PRODUCTION MEASURE — commonly, the ratio of ore extracted to waste removed for a specific component, compared against an expected strip ratio for that component over its life — recognising the cost as an asset only to the extent the actual strip ratio exceeds the expected average strip ratio for the component.
8

Worked Example — Component-Based Recognition

Allocating stripping costs using an expected strip ratio
Example: Actual strip ratio exceeding the expected average
A mine's identified ore body component has an expected average strip ratio of 3:1 (three units of waste per unit of ore) over its life. In the current period, the entity incurs $6,000,000 of stripping costs, removing waste and ore at an ACTUAL ratio of 4.5:1 for this component — above the expected average, indicating some of this period's stripping activity is improving FUTURE access rather than solely benefiting current production.
The PORTION of costs corresponding to the excess above the 3:1 expected ratio (i.e. the extra waste removed relative to what a steady-state 3:1 ratio would imply) is capitalised as an addition to the stripping activity asset for that component; the remainder, reflecting the expected "normal" stripping level, is allocated to inventory production costs under IAS 2.

IFRIC 20 vs US GAAP

Comparison — IFRIC 20 vs US GAAP (ASC 930)
FeatureIFRS (IFRIC 20)US GAAP (ASC 930)
Overall approachSplit cost between inventory (IAS 2) and a non-current stripping activity asset based on the future-access benefitBroadly similar — production stripping costs are generally capitalised to the extent they benefit identifiable ore reserves accessed in the future, expensed to the extent they relate to current production
Unit of accountIdentified "component" of the ore bodySimilar component-based approach, generally consistent in substance
Amortisation basisUnits of production over the component's expected useful lifeAlso units of production, broadly converged
Stripping cost accounting is a reasonably converged area between the two frameworks, reflecting a shared industry-driven view of the underlying economics, even though the codified guidance developed somewhat independently in each framework.

Quick-reference glossary
Strip ratio
The ratio of waste material removed to ore extracted, used to distinguish stripping costs relating to current production from those improving access to future ore.
Overburden
The waste rock and soil layers that must be removed to expose the underlying ore deposit in a surface (open-pit) mine.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Stripping activity asset
The non-current asset recognised under IFRIC 20 representing the future economic benefit of improved access to an identified ore body component, classified as PP&E or intangible depending on the nature of the asset it enhances.
Production phase
The period during which a mine is actively extracting and selling ore, as distinct from the earlier development phase — the specific period IFRIC 20's stripping cost guidance applies to.
