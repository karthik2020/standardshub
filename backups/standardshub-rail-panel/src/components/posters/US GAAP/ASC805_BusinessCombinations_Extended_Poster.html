Conceptual Framework Linkage

Fair value at the moment control changes hands
When an entity obtains CONTROL of a business, that transaction is treated as a genuinely NEW measurement event — assets acquired and liabilities assumed are recognised at their ACQUISITION-DATE FAIR VALUES, capturing the full economics of the deal rather than carrying forward the target's old historical costs. ASC 805 governs this "acquisition method."
A genuine joint-project standard, with one well-known remaining divergence
ASC 805 and IFRS 3 emerged from a JOINT FASB-IASB convergence project (completed around 2008-2009) and share the SAME fundamental acquisition-method structure. The single most significant remaining divergence is the NONCONTROLLING INTEREST measurement choice (Section 7) — worth knowing well, since it's the standout comparison point in an otherwise closely converged standard.
2

Scope — the Definition of a "Business"

A simplified screen test, post-ASU 2017-01
ASU 2017-01 introduced a SCREEN test to simplify the often-contentious "is this a business or just an asset group" determination: if SUBSTANTIALLY ALL of the fair value of the gross assets acquired is CONCENTRATED in a single identifiable asset (or group of similar assets), the transaction is NOT a business — it is an ASSET ACQUISITION instead (accounted for differently, with acquisition costs typically capitalised rather than expensed). Notably, IFRS 3 followed with its OWN broadly similar concentration test amendment in 2020 — a rare instance of IFRS adopting a structure closely modelled on a prior US GAAP change, making this a genuinely CONVERGED area today.

Identifying the Acquirer and Acquisition Date

Substance over legal form
The ACQUIRER is the entity that obtains CONTROL — not necessarily the legally acquiring entity (a "reverse acquisition" can occur when the legal acquirer is the accounting ACQUIREE). The ACQUISITION DATE is the date control is obtained, generally the closing date — the same identification principles IFRS 3 applies.
5

Recognising and Measuring Identifiable Assets, Liabilities, and NCI

Fair value, with specific exceptions
Identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed are recognised at ACQUISITION-DATE FAIR VALUE, with a handful of specific EXCEPTIONS to this general rule (e.g. income taxes measured per ASC 740 rather than fair value, certain employee benefit obligations measured per ASC 715, indemnification assets measured on the same basis as the indemnified item) — broadly the same general approach and exception list IFRS 3 applies.
6

Goodwill Measurement

The same formula as IFRS 3
ComponentSign
Consideration transferred (fair value)+
Fair value of any noncontrolling interest+
Fair value of any previously held equity interest (step acquisitions)+
Fair value of net identifiable assets acquired−
The residual, if positive, is goodwill; if negative, it's a bargain purchase gain (Section 8).
7

Noncontrolling Interest — Full Goodwill vs the IFRS 3 Choice

The standout remaining divergence
US GAAP REQUIRES noncontrolling interest (NCI) to be measured at FAIR VALUE — which effectively means the "FULL GOODWILL" method is the ONLY option: goodwill is measured as if the ENTIRE target (100%) were acquired at its implied fair value, with a proportionate share attributed to the NCI. IFRS 3 offers a GENUINE CHOICE, made on an ACQUISITION-BY-ACQUISITION basis: NCI may be measured EITHER at FAIR VALUE (the "full goodwill" method, matching the mandatory US approach), OR at the NCI's PROPORTIONATE SHARE of the acquiree's identifiable NET ASSETS (the "PARTIAL GOODWILL" method, which excludes the NCI's share of goodwill entirely). This is the single most significant divergence remaining in an otherwise closely converged standard — under IFRS, two seemingly similar acquisitions can produce DIFFERENT total reported goodwill purely based on which NCI measurement option is elected.
8

Bargain Purchase Gains

Converged — immediate income recognition
If the net identifiable assets acquired EXCEED the consideration transferred (plus NCI and previously held interests) — a "bargain purchase" — the resulting GAIN is recognised IMMEDIATELY in EARNINGS at the acquisition date, after first reassessing whether all assets/liabilities were correctly identified and measured. The same immediate income statement recognition applies under IFRS 3.
9

Acquisition-Related Costs

Expensed, not capitalised — converged
Costs the acquirer incurs in connection with the acquisition (legal, advisory, due diligence fees) are EXPENSED AS INCURRED, and are NOT included in the consideration transferred or capitalised as part of the acquired assets — the same treatment IFRS 3 requires.
10

Contingent Consideration

Fair value at acquisition, then classification-dependent subsequent treatment
Contingent consideration (e.g. an earnout) is measured at FAIR VALUE at the acquisition date and included in consideration transferred. Subsequently, LIABILITY-classified contingent consideration is REMEASURED each period with changes through EARNINGS; EQUITY-classified contingent consideration is NOT remeasured — the same classification-dependent approach IFRS 3 applies.
11

The Measurement Period

Up to one year to finalise provisional amounts
If initial accounting for a business combination is INCOMPLETE at the reporting date, the acquirer reports PROVISIONAL amounts, subsequently ADJUSTED retrospectively as new information about facts existing at the acquisition date comes to light — up to a MEASUREMENT PERIOD of ONE YEAR from the acquisition date — the same one-year measurement period IFRS 3 allows.

Worked Example — Full vs Partial Goodwill (NCI Choice)

The same acquisition, two different IFRS outcomes
Example: An 80% acquisition
An acquirer purchases 80% of a target for $8,000,000 cash. The target's identifiable net assets have a fair value of $9,000,000. The remaining 20% NCI has an implied fair value of $2,000,000 (based on the same per-share price paid for the 80% stake).
Under US GAAP (mandatory full goodwill): Goodwill = ($8,000,000 + $2,000,000 NCI at fair value) minus $9,000,000 = $1,000,000.
Under IFRS 3, full goodwill election: SAME calculation, SAME $1,000,000.
Under IFRS 3, partial goodwill election: NCI measured at its proportionate share of net assets = 20% times $9,000,000 = $1,800,000 (not $2,000,000 fair value). Goodwill = ($8,000,000 + $1,800,000) minus $9,000,000 = $800,000 — a LOWER total goodwill figure, and NCI itself is presented at a DIFFERENT amount, purely due to the election IFRS 3 permits but US GAAP does not.
13

Worked Example — a Bargain Purchase Gain

Recognising a gain instead of goodwill
Example: An acquisition below net asset fair value
An acquirer purchases 100% of a distressed target for $5,000,000 cash. After reassessing all identifiable assets and liabilities, the target's net identifiable assets have a fair value of $6,200,000.
Bargain purchase gain = $6,200,000 minus $5,000,000 = $1,200,000, recognised IMMEDIATELY in earnings at the acquisition date, after confirming the identification and measurement of all acquired items was performed correctly.

Quick-reference glossary preview
Full goodwill method
Measuring goodwill as if the entire target were acquired at implied fair value, with NCI measured at fair value — mandatory under US GAAP, optional under IFRS 3.
Partial goodwill method
An IFRS 3-only option measuring NCI at its proportionate share of identifiable net assets, excluding NCI's share of goodwill entirely.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Measurement period
The period, up to one year from the acquisition date, during which provisional amounts may be retrospectively adjusted as new information about acquisition-date facts emerges.
Bargain purchase
An acquisition where the fair value of net identifiable assets exceeds the consideration transferred, resulting in an immediate gain rather than goodwill.
