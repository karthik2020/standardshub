Objective, Scope & Out-of-Scope Circumstances (paras. 1–6)

Objective — a bridge, not a permanent home
Guides a FIRST-TIME ADOPTER preparing financial statements on transition to accrual basis IPSAS, aiming for high-quality information that is (a) transparent about the transition, (b) a suitable starting point for accrual accounting regardless of the entity's PREVIOUS basis, and (c) worth its cost.
Applies from the DATE OF ADOPTION and throughout the transition period — NOT to ordinary accounting policy changes by an entity ALREADY on accrual IPSAS (those follow IPSAS 3 instead) (para. 6).
When this Standard does NOT apply (para. 3)
Stops presenting statements under prescribed requirements, having previously ALSO presented a set with an explicit unreserved IPSAS compliance statement Presented statements in the PRIOR period with an explicit unreserved compliance statement Presented such a compliant statement even if auditors modified their opinion on it
⚠️ Applying transitional exemptions in this Standard can genuinely PREVENT an entity from making an unreserved IPSAS compliance statement — full compliance resumes only once the relevant exemptions expire, or the items concerned are recognized/measured under the applicable IPSAS, whichever is earlier (para. 5).
2

Key Definitions (para. 9)

The transition vocabulary
Date of adoption of IPSAS
The date accrual basis IPSAS is FIRST adopted — the start of the reporting period for the first transitional or first IPSAS financial statements.
First-time adopter
An entity adopting accrual basis IPSAS for the first time, presenting its first transitional or first IPSAS financial statements.
Period of transition
The period during which a first-time adopter applies one or more exemptions in this Standard, before it can make an unreserved compliance statement.
Previous basis of accounting
Whatever basis (cash, accrual, a modified version of either, or another prescribed basis) the entity used immediately before adopting accrual IPSAS.
Two DIFFERENT kinds of "first" financial statements
TermMeaning
Transitional IPSAS financial statementsStatements where the entity applies exemptions that AFFECT fair presentation — it CANNOT yet make an unreserved compliance statement
First IPSAS financial statementsThe first statements where the entity CAN make the unreserved compliance statement — either because it never used fair-presentation-affecting exemptions, or because those exemptions have now expired
The OPENING STATEMENT OF FINANCIAL POSITION is the entity's SOFP at the date of adoption — the starting point for everything that follows (§3).
3

The Opening Statement of Financial Position (paras. 15–19)

The starting point for everything that follows
A first-time adopter MUST prepare and present an opening statement of financial position at the date of adoption — the foundation for all subsequent accrual-basis accounting (para. 15). On that date, IPSAS requirements are applied RETROSPECTIVELY, except where this Standard requires or permits otherwise (para. 16).
The SAME accounting policies apply in the opening SOFP and throughout ALL periods presented — except where a §5–§9 exemption is used (para. 17) Policies must comply with EVERY IPSAS effective AT the date of adoption (not earlier or later versions) — though early application of a not-yet-mandatory new IPSAS is allowed if that IPSAS itself permits it (para. 19) Using an exemption means AMENDING accounting policies once that exemption expires, or the relevant items are recognized/measured under the applicable IPSAS (whichever is earlier) (para. 18)
4

General Recognition & Accounting Policy Principles (paras. 20–23)

The default rule (para. 20) — except as exempted
RECOGNIZE all assets and liabilities IPSAS requires Do NOT recognize items IPSAS doesn't permit as assets/liabilities RECLASSIFY items recognized differently under the previous basis, if IPSAS treats them as a different type of asset/liability/net-assets-component Apply IPSAS in MEASURING everything recognized
Where the adjustments land
Accounting policy differences between the new and previous basis produce adjustments arising from PRE-ADOPTION transactions/events/conditions. These go DIRECTLY to the opening balance of ACCUMULATED SURPLUS OR DEFICIT (or another net assets/equity category, if appropriate) — recognized in the period the items are recognized/measured, at the EARLIEST period presented (para. 21).
Estimates made under IPSAS at the date of adoption must be CONSISTENT with those made under the previous basis (after adjusting for differing accounting policies), unless there's objective evidence those earlier estimates were in error (para. 23).
5

Three-Year Transitional Relief — Assets & Liabilities (paras. 33–41B)

The headline exemption — AFFECTS fair presentation
A first-time adopter is NOT REQUIRED to recognize and/or measure the following, for up to THREE YEARS from the date of adoption (para. 36)
Inventories (IPSAS 12) · Investment property (IPSAS 16) · PP&E (IPSAS 45) Defined benefit plans / other long-term employee benefits (IPSAS 39) — the obligation AND any related plan assets must be recognized TOGETHER, not selectively (para. 37) Biological assets and agricultural produce (IPSAS 27) · Intangible assets (IPSAS 31) Right-of-use assets and related lease liabilities (IPSAS 43) Service concession assets and related liabilities, under EITHER model (IPSAS 32) Financial instruments (IPSAS 41) · Social benefits (IPSAS 42)
⚠️ This exemption CANNOT be applied by analogy to other items not on this list (para. 33). A first-time adopter is ENCOURAGED, but not required, to comply in full as soon as possible (para. 34) — and while an item remains unrecognized, its ASSOCIATED presentation/disclosure requirements don't apply either (para. 35, §10).
Two flavors of relief, and their knock-on effects
NEVER recognized under the previous basis → the whole three-year window to recognize/measure per para. 36 ALREADY recognized under the previous basis → no REQUIREMENT to change the measurement policy for three years either (para. 38) — existing (even non-IPSAS-compliant) policies may continue Deferring a FINANCIAL ASSET's recognition under this exemption also defers related REVENUE recognition under IPSAS 47 (para. 41); deferring a financial LIABILITY similarly defers related expense recognition under IPSAS 48 (para. 41B)
6

Three-Year Relief — Revenue & Transfer Expenses, Plus Consequential Exemptions (paras. 42–48)

Revenue and transfer expenses — incremental adoption allowed
A first-time adopter needn't change its revenue (IPSAS 47) or transfer-expense (IPSAS 48) accounting policy for three years — and may apply the new Standards INCREMENTALLY, class by class (paras. 42–43). Example: property tax revenue might be IPSAS 47-compliant from day one, while income tax or binding-arrangement revenue takes the full three years to model reliably.
Consequential relief for related Standards
IPSAS 5 (Borrowing Costs) — no requirement to capitalize borrowing costs on qualifying assets under the allowed-alternative treatment while those assets remain outside the §5 relief (para. 44) IPSAS 43 (Leases) — deferring PP&E/investment property/intangible/service-concession recognition under §5 automatically defers lease recognition too, since the underlying asset framework isn't yet in place (para. 46) IPSAS 19 (Provisions) — similar consequential relief where PP&E recognition is deferred (para. 48)
These consequential exemptions exist because several Standards' mechanics are INTERDEPENDENT — deferring one naturally defers others that build on it.

Deemed Cost — the Core Transitional Tool (paras. 64–70)

What deemed cost solves
Deemed cost = a substitute for acquisition cost or depreciated cost, used ONLY where reliable historical cost information is genuinely UNAVAILABLE
May be elected for inventory, investment property (if the entity uses IPSAS 16's historical cost model), right-of-use assets, most intangible assets, financial instruments, service concession assets, and PP&E (para. 64, 64A). Subsequent depreciation/amortization is based on the DEEMED cost, as if it WERE the acquisition cost.
Not a revaluation — and where the adjustment lands
⚠️ Using deemed cost is explicitly NOT treated as a revaluation, or as adopting the current value model for subsequent measurement under other Standards (para. 66) — it's purely a ONE-TIME transitional measurement device. The resulting adjustment is recognized DIRECTLY in accumulated surplus or deficit, in the period the deemed cost is determined (whether at the date of adoption, or later within the three-year window) (IG39, para. 76).
8

Deemed Cost — Specific Applications (paras. 64A, 67–75)

PP&E — current operational value or fair value
Follows the SAME primary-objective test as IPSAS 45 itself: held for OPERATIONAL capacity → current operational value; held for FINANCIAL capacity → fair value (para. 64A). A PRE-EXISTING revaluation under the previous basis can be used as deemed cost if it was broadly comparable to the appropriate current-value basis at the time (para. 67) — including EVENT-DRIVEN valuations from a specific past event, whether that measurement date falls before OR during the transition period (para. 68).
Non-exchange acquisitions & investments in controlled entities
An asset acquired via a NON-EXCHANGE transaction may use fair value (or current operational value/fair value for PP&E) as deemed cost, where reliable cost data is unavailable (para. 71) Investments in controlled entities/joint ventures/associates, measured at cost in SEPARATE financial statements (IPSAS 34) → elect COST or DEEMED COST (fair value at the date of adoption) (para. 72)
If observable current-value inputs aren't available for inventory, specialized investment property, or PP&E, other IPSAS 46 measurement techniques may be used to determine deemed cost (para. 70).
9

Exemptions That Do NOT Affect Fair Presentation (paras. 63, 153)

A second, gentler category — deemed cost lives here
Paragraphs 64–134 house a SEPARATE category of exemptions (including the deemed-cost provisions in §7–§8) that DO NOT prevent an entity from making an unreserved IPSAS compliance statement. An entity is REQUIRED to adopt some, and MAY ELECT others — but, like the §5 exemptions, none may be applied by analogy to items outside their stated scope (para. 63).
Where a first-time adopter had ALREADY adopted transitional provisions under OTHER individual IPSAS (e.g. a Standard's own first-application relief), it continues applying THOSE until they expire — but if it instead elects THIS Standard's exemptions, the relief period cannot exceed what THIS Standard provides (para. 153).
10

Disclosure During the Period of Transition (paras. 151–152F)

Disclosure relief tracks recognition relief
Where a §5 three-year exemption applies to NOT recognize/measure an item → its ASSOCIATED IPSAS 1/18/other disclosure requirements don't apply EITHER, until the same trigger (expiry or recognition, whichever is earlier)
Early voluntary disclosure is ENCOURAGED regardless (para. 152).
Current value measurement disclosures — a familiar package
Mirrors the fair value hierarchy disclosure packages seen throughout this library (IPSAS 12, 16, 27, 31): for NON-RECURRING current operational value/fair value measurements, disclose the measurement at period end and reasons, the hierarchy level (or observable/unobservable basis), techniques/inputs used (with reasons for any technique change), and — for Level 3/unobservable-input measurements — the valuation PROCESSES used (paras. 152A–152F). Quantitative disclosures are presented in TABULAR format unless another format is clearly more appropriate.

Worked Example — Deemed Cost of Investment Property (Implementation Guidance IG39)

Entity X adopts IPSAS on 1 January 20X4; deemed cost applied to investment property
Previously recognized investment property: CU300,000. Deemed cost (fair value at adoption date): CU1,800,000. No comparative information presented.
Investment property reconciliationCU
Opening balance under previous basis of accounting300,000
Additional value recognized per deemed cost (IPSAS 33)1,500,000
Restated opening balance, 1 Jan 20X41,800,000
The CU1,500,000 adjustment is recognized DIRECTLY in accumulated surplus/deficit — NOT through surplus or deficit for the period. Opening net assets/equity moves from CU220,000 to a restated CU1,720,000 (accumulated surplus/deficit rises from CU210,000 to CU1,710,000; the CU10,000 "Other Reserves" balance is unaffected). The period's ordinary surplus of CU5,000 is added on top, giving a closing balance of CU1,725,000 at 31 December 20X4.

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Comparison with IFRS 1

A conceptual parallel, not a direct derivation
Unlike most IPSAS, IPSAS 33 is NOT drawn from a specific IFRS Standard — the Handbook contains no "Comparison with IFRS 1" section, and no Acknowledgment tying it to that pronouncement. The IPSASB developed it independently, though IFRS 1, First-time Adoption of International Financial Reporting Standards, addresses a conceptually SIMILAR problem in the private sector.
IFRS 1 typically bridges a GAAP-to-GAAP transition (already-accrual entities switching frameworks); IPSAS 33 typically bridges a much LARGER leap — CASH to ACCRUAL basis entirely, for entities that may never have measured many asset/liability classes before This scale difference explains IPSAS 33's much MORE GENEROUS relief — a full THREE-YEAR window to not recognize entire asset/liability categories, versus IFRS 1's narrower, more surgical exemptions Both share the core "deemed cost" concept — a fair-value substitute for unavailable historical cost data — though IPSAS 33 extends it more broadly given public sector entities' frequent lack of any prior asset registers

Statement / Process Mapping

Transition decision map
1. Establish the DATE OF ADOPTION and prepare an opening SOFP — the default is FULL retrospective application (§3, §4) 2. For each major asset/liability class, is reliable historical cost data AVAILABLE? If no → consider whether a THREE-YEAR relief exemption applies (§5) or DEEMED COST can be used instead (§7–§8) 3. Does the exemption chosen AFFECT fair presentation? → the entity produces TRANSITIONAL IPSAS financial statements, and cannot yet make an unreserved compliance statement (§1, §2) 4. Is the exemption one that does NOT affect fair presentation (deemed cost, and similar)? → the entity can still assert full compliance while using it (§9) 5. Track WHEN each exemption expires, or the relevant items are recognized/measured under the applicable IPSAS — that's when the entity's FIRST IPSAS financial statements arrive (§2)
