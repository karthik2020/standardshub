Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why offsetting the wrong way would mislead
Contributors to decommissioning funds often assume their obligation and their fund interest are simply two sides of the same coin and could be presented net. IFRIC 5 exists because that intuition conflicts with IAS 1's general prohibition on offsetting assets and liabilities — the contributor's obligation to a THIRD PARTY (the ultimate site owner/regulator) and its INTEREST in a separately-managed fund are, in substance, distinct rights and obligations against different counterparties.
Faithful representation of a restricted asset
A contributor typically cannot access fund assets freely — access is restricted to reimbursement of decommissioning expenditure. Recognising the fund interest as an unrestricted asset, or netting it against the liability, would obscure that restriction from users of the financial statements. IFRIC 5's gross presentation preserves that information.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRIC 5 covers
IFRIC 5 applies to a contributor's accounting for interests arising from decommissioning funds ("funds") that have BOTH of these characteristics: (a) the fund's assets are administered separately from the contributor's own assets, and (b) the contributor's access to fund assets is restricted.
TYPICAL FACT PATTERNS
Nuclear decommissioning trust fundsMine rehabilitation funds jointly funded by multiple operatorsEnvironmental remediation funds administered by an independent trustee
Key definitions
Fund
Assets segregated in a separate, restricted vehicle to fund some or all of the costs of decommissioning, restoring, or rehabilitating an asset or site.
Contributor
An entity that makes contributions to a fund, separately from its own recognised decommissioning obligation under IAS 37.
3

Two Separate Items, Not One Net Balance

The central mechanic
A contributor recognises TWO separate items: (1) its OBLIGATION to pay decommissioning costs as a liability under IAS 37, and (2) its INTEREST in the fund, recognised separately UNLESS the contributor is required to make good any shortfall in the fund, in which case a right of reimbursement may instead be recognised under IAS 37's own reimbursement provisions.
⚠️ The two items are NOT offset against one another on the statement of financial position — IFRIC 5 requires gross presentation, consistent with IAS 1's general offsetting prohibition.
4

Accounting for the Fund Interest (with worked example)

The default treatment
Where the contributor's right to receive reimbursement from the fund is NOT unconditional, the interest is recognised as a reimbursement right under IAS 37, measured at the LOWER of: (a) the amount of the related decommissioning obligation, and (b) the contributor's share of the fair value of the net assets of the fund attributable to contributors.
Worked example — reimbursement right measurement
Example: Applying the lower-of test
A contributor's share of the decommissioning obligation is $5,000,000. Its share of the fair value of the fund's net assets is $4,200,000.
The reimbursement right is recognised at $4,200,000 — the LOWER of the two amounts. The full $5,000,000 obligation remains recognised gross as a liability; the $800,000 shortfall is not reduced by any asset recognition, reflecting genuine funding risk.
5

Reimbursement Rights vs Consolidation

When the fund itself must be consolidated or equity accounted
IFRIC 5's reimbursement-right mechanics apply only where the contributor does NOT control, jointly control, or have significant influence over the fund. Where the contributor DOES have such a relationship with the fund — for example, where it is the sole or dominant contributor and effectively directs the fund's activities — it instead accounts for its interest under IFRS 10 (consolidation), IFRS 11 (joint arrangements), or IAS 28 (associates), as applicable, in place of the reimbursement-right approach.
6

Obligations to Make Additional Contributions

A separate liability, recognised on its own terms
Where a contributor has an obligation to make potential additional contributions — for example, if other contributors default, or the fund's assets prove insufficient — that obligation is recognised as a SEPARATE liability, assessed under IAS 37's own recognition criteria (probable outflow, reliably estimable amount), independently of the reimbursement right described in Section 4.
7

Rights to Surplus Distributions

A high recognition threshold
A contributor's right to a refund of any surplus from the fund — after all decommissioning obligations are settled — is recognised as an ASSET only if the surplus is VIRTUALLY CERTAIN to be available to the contributor. This deliberately sets a much higher bar than the "probable" threshold used elsewhere in IAS 37, reflecting the genuine uncertainty of a residual claim that ranks behind the fund's primary decommissioning purpose.
8

Changes in the Carrying Amount of the Interest

Where subsequent movements are recognised
Changes in the carrying amount of the reimbursement right — OTHER than contributions paid into the fund and payments received/receivable from the fund for decommissioning costs incurred — are recognised in profit or loss in the period they occur. This captures, for example, changes in the fair value of the fund's net assets that affect the lower-of measurement outcome.

IFRIC 5 vs US GAAP

Comparison — IFRIC 5 vs US GAAP (ASC 410-20, ASC 715-30 trust analogy)
FeatureIFRIC 5 (IFRS)US GAAP
Measurement of the fund interestLower of the obligation and the contributor's share of the fund's net asset fair valueUS GAAP asset retirement obligation trust guidance generally values the trust interest at fair value directly, without an equivalent "lower of" cap tied to the obligation
Surplus refund recognition thresholdVirtually certain — a high barNo single codified "virtually certain" threshold — evaluated under general asset recognition principles, which can produce different outcomes in practice
PresentationGross — obligation and interest presented separatelyGenerally also gross, consistent presentation philosophy
Gross presentation is a shared principle across both frameworks; the more consequential divergence is in how the contributor's INTEREST in the fund is measured and capped.

Quick-reference glossary
Reimbursement right
An asset recognised under IAS 37 when a third party is expected to reimburse some or all of the expenditure required to settle a provision — capped so as not to exceed the amount of the related provision.
Virtually certain
A very high probability threshold, well above "probable," used sparingly across IFRS — here applied to a contributor's right to a residual fund surplus.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Restricted assets
Fund assets to which the contributor's access is limited, typically to reimbursement of qualifying decommissioning expenditure — the defining feature that brings a fund within IFRIC 5's scope.
Net assets attributable to contributors
The fair value of the fund's assets less its liabilities to parties other than contributors, used as the basis for measuring each contributor's share under the "lower of" test.
