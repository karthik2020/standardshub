Conceptual Framework Linkage

Segment disclosure as management actually sees the business
ASBJ Statement No. 17 requires disclosure of FINANCIAL information about an entity's OPERATING SEGMENTS – organised around a "MANAGEMENT APPROACH": segments are identified based on the entity's OWN INTERNAL organisational structure, the way MANAGEMENT actually allocates resources and evaluates PERFORMANCE – rather than an EXTERNALLY imposed, standardised industry classification. This lets readers see the business through the SAME lens management itself uses.
2

Scope

Companies with publicly traded securities preparing consolidated (or individual) financial statements
This Statement applies to companies (typically LISTED companies) with PUBLICLY TRADED securities, requiring segment disclosure as part of their FINANCIAL statements.
3

The Management Approach – Segments as Management Sees Them

Identified by internal reporting structure, not external classification
Operating segments are identified based on COMPONENTS of the entity for which SEPARATE FINANCIAL information is available and REGULARLY REVIEWED by the entity's CHIEF OPERATING DECISION-MAKING body (typically the board of directors or an equivalent senior management group) for the purpose of ALLOCATING resources and ASSESSING performance – a genuinely MANAGEMENT-DRIVEN identification process, MOVING AWAY from the OLDER approach of segmenting purely by RISK-AND-REWARD industry/geographic categories.
4

Identifying Reportable Segments – the Quantitative Thresholds

The 10% test, applied across revenue, profit/loss, or assets
An operating segment is a "REPORTABLE SEGMENT" requiring separate disclosure if it meets AT LEAST ONE of several QUANTITATIVE thresholds – generally, its REVENUE, its PROFIT OR LOSS (in absolute terms), or its ASSETS represent 10% OR MORE of the COMBINED total for all operating segments – broadly MIRRORING IFRS 8's own 10% quantitative thresholds internationally.

6

Reconciliation to Consolidated Totals

Bridging segment totals back to the consolidated financial statements
Because segment measures are based on MANAGEMENT's own internal reporting (which may use DIFFERENT accounting policies or allocation methods than the CONSOLIDATED financial statements), a RECONCILIATION is required, bridging the TOTAL of all reportable segments' revenue, profit/loss, and assets BACK to the corresponding CONSOLIDATED financial statement totals, identifying and explaining any MATERIAL reconciling items.
7

Entity-Wide Disclosures – Products, Geography, and Major Customers

Additional disclosures required regardless of segment structure
EVEN where an entity has only a SINGLE reportable segment, certain ENTITY-WIDE disclosures are still required: revenue from EXTERNAL customers by MAJOR product/service category; revenue and non-current assets by GEOGRAPHIC area; and information about SIGNIFICANT reliance on any SINGLE external customer (generally where revenue from one customer represents 10% or more of total revenue).
8

Worked Example – Applying the 10% Reportable Segment Threshold

Determining which segments must be separately reported
Example: A company with four operating segments
A company's four operating segments report revenue of ¥600,000,000, ¥250,000,000, ¥90,000,000, and ¥60,000,000 respectively (total: ¥1,000,000,000).
The 10% threshold = ¥100,000,000. Segments 1 (¥600,000,000, 60%) and 2 (¥250,000,000, 25%) EXCEED the threshold and must be SEPARATELY reported. Segments 3 (¥90,000,000, 9%) and 4 (¥60,000,000, 6%) FALL BELOW the threshold – though they may still be VOLUNTARILY disclosed separately, or COMBINED into an "all other segments" category.
9

Worked Example – a Segment Reconciliation

Bridging internal management figures to the consolidated total
Example: Reconciling segment profit to consolidated profit
The total of all reportable segments' profit is ¥180,000,000. Consolidated profit before tax is ¥165,000,000, with the ¥15,000,000 difference arising from unallocated corporate expenses not attributed to any segment.
The company discloses the RECONCILIATION: ¥180,000,000 (total segment profit) LESS ¥15,000,000 (unallocated corporate expenses) = ¥165,000,000 (consolidated profit before tax) – clearly explaining the NATURE of the reconciling item.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Management approach
Identifying operating segments based on an entity's actual internal organisational and reporting structure, as used by its chief operating decision-making body.
Reportable segment
An operating segment meeting at least one of the quantitative 10% thresholds (revenue, profit/loss, or assets), requiring separate disclosure.
