Purpose of This Introduction & the IPSASB's Mandate

A short preamble, not a Standard in its own right
This Introduction is a BRIEF preamble to the Cash Basis IPSAS — it carries no numbered paragraphs, no requirements, and no authoritative force of its own. Its role is purely to set context before the actual Standard (Financial Reporting Under the Cash Basis of Accounting) begins on the following page.
The IPSASB develops accounting standards for public sector entities — International Public Sector Accounting Standards (IPSAS) Its stated goal: achieving CONSISTENT and COMPARABLE financial information ACROSS jurisdictions It strongly ENCOURAGES governments and national standard-setters to engage with its Exposure Drafts and Consultation Papers during development
2

Two Bases, One Standard-Setter

A deliberately dual-track body of literature
The IPSASB issues IPSAS covering BOTH the CASH BASIS of accounting AND the ACCRUAL BASIS — this Introduction sits at the head of the cash-basis track specifically, distinct from the 50-Standard accrual-basis body of literature covered elsewhere in this library (IPSAS 1–50).
Why a cash-basis Standard exists at all
Many governments and public sector entities, particularly in developing jurisdictions, report on a cash basis as a practical matter — often as a stepping stone toward eventual accrual adoption. Rather than leaving this reporting basis entirely unregulated, the IPSASB provides a dedicated Standard so that even cash-basis financial statements can achieve a baseline of consistency, comparability, and credibility.
3

Adoption, Jurisdictional Autonomy & the Compliance Threshold

Improving quality and comparability — while respecting sovereignty
Adoption of IPSAS is expected to improve BOTH the quality and comparability of public sector financial information globally. At the same time, the IPSASB explicitly RECOGNIZES the right of governments and national standard-setters to establish their OWN accounting standards and guidelines for their jurisdictions — this Standard is offered as an important step forward, not a mandate.
The all-or-nothing compliance rule
Financial statements may be described as complying with this IPSAS ONLY IF they comply with ALL the requirements of PART 1
A genuinely important threshold: partial compliance, or compliance with only the Standard's ENCOURAGED disclosures (Part 2), does not entitle an entity to claim conformity with the Cash Basis IPSAS. This mirrors the accrual-basis IPSAS's own "unreserved compliance statement" concept (see IPSAS 1 and IPSAS 33 in this library) — compliance is binary, not a matter of degree.
4

The Path Forward — Encouraging Transition to Accrual Basis

This Standard is framed as a stepping stone, not a destination
The IPSASB explicitly ENCOURAGES governments to progress from cash-basis reporting to the ACCRUAL BASIS of accounting, and to harmonize national requirements with the accrual-basis IPSAS body of literature (IPSAS 1–50, covered extensively elsewhere in this library) — the Cash Basis IPSAS is positioned as an interim improvement, not a permanent alternative.
For entities planning an eventual transition, the Introduction points to a further IPSASB resource: Study 14, Transition to the Accrual Basis of Accounting: Guidance for Governments and Government Entities — a practical implementation guide sitting outside the Handbook's numbered Standards, but part of the IPSASB's broader publication suite alongside Recommended Practice Guidelines (RPGs) and Studies.
