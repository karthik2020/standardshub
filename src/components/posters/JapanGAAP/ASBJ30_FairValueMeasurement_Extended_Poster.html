Conceptual Framework Linkage

A single, consistent definition and hierarchy across all of J-GAAP
ASBJ Statement No. 30 establishes a SINGLE, UNIFIED definition and MEASUREMENT hierarchy for "FAIR VALUE" (時価) APPLICABLE across EVERY other J-GAAP standard that REQUIRES a fair value measurement – financial instruments (ASBJ Statement No. 10), business combinations (ASBJ Statement No. 21), impairment testing, and more – REPLACING what was PREVIOUSLY scattered, standard-by-standard fair value guidance WITHOUT a single, consistent framework.
2

Scope

Applies wherever another J-GAAP standard requires fair value measurement
This Statement applies WHEREVER another J-GAAP standard REQUIRES (or permits) a fair value measurement – it does NOT itself DETERMINE which items should be measured at fair value (that remains the ROLE of the individual standard concerned), but rather supplies the CONSISTENT definition and methodology for HOW that fair value measurement should be PERFORMED.
3

Fair Value Defined – an Exit Price Notion

Directly mirroring IFRS 13's definition
FAIR VALUE is DEFINED as the price that WOULD be RECEIVED to SELL an asset, or PAID to TRANSFER a liability, in an ORDERLY transaction between MARKET PARTICIPANTS at the MEASUREMENT date – a genuine "EXIT PRICE" notion, DIRECTLY MIRRORING IFRS 13's own DEFINITION internationally, WORD-FOR-WORD in SUBSTANCE.
4

The Three-Level Fair Value Hierarchy

Prioritising observable market inputs over unobservable estimates
LevelDescription
Level 1QUOTED prices in ACTIVE markets for IDENTICAL assets/liabilities – the HIGHEST quality, most reliable input
Level 2OBSERVABLE inputs OTHER than quoted prices (e.g. quoted prices for SIMILAR assets, or observable market data such as interest rates/yield curves)
Level 3UNOBSERVABLE inputs, used ONLY when observable market data is NOT available – requiring the GREATEST degree of judgement
This THREE-LEVEL hierarchy DIRECTLY MIRRORS IFRS 13's OWN structure internationally.
5

Unifying Previously Scattered Guidance

A single reference point, replacing standard-by-standard fair value rules
BEFORE this Statement, fair value MEASUREMENT guidance was SCATTERED across the INDIVIDUAL J-GAAP standards that HAPPENED to require it – WITHOUT a SINGLE, consistent DEFINITION or HIERARCHY applicable UNIFORMLY across ALL of them. ASBJ Statement No. 30 CONSOLIDATED this into ONE unified reference framework – CLOSELY paralleling how IFRS 13 ITSELF consolidated PREVIOUSLY scattered fair value guidance that existed across MULTIPLE individual IFRS standards before ITS OWN introduction internationally.
6

A NOTABLE CONTRAST – Another Case of Direct Current-Standard Adoption

Alongside Revenue Recognition, a genuine "kept pace" example
LIKE ASBJ Statement No. 29 (Revenue Recognition), this Statement represents a case where J-GAAP DIRECTLY ADOPTED the CURRENT international framework (IFRS 13) from the OUTSET, RATHER than remaining anchored to an OLDER, SCATTERED approach for an extended period. TOGETHER, these two Statements illustrate that J-GAAP's convergence PACE is genuinely UNEVEN across TOPIC areas – SOME standards (financial instruments, leases, insurance-adjacent areas) have LAGGED behind superseded international models for YEARS, while OTHERS (revenue recognition, fair value measurement) were built DIRECTLY around the CURRENT standard from the START.

Worked Example – Classifying a Level 1 Measurement

The highest-quality fair value input
Example: Listed equity securities
A company holds shares in a company listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange, with a readily available, actively traded quoted market price.
This is classified as a LEVEL 1 fair value measurement – the QUOTED market price for an IDENTICAL asset in an ACTIVE market provides the HIGHEST-quality, most DIRECTLY observable fair value input available.
8

Worked Example – Classifying a Level 3 Measurement

Relying on unobservable inputs
Example: An unlisted equity investment
A company holds a small equity stake in an unlisted start-up, with no observable market price and no comparable recent transactions. Fair value is estimated using a discounted cash flow model based on internally projected future cash flows.
This is classified as a LEVEL 3 fair value measurement – since the valuation relies on genuinely UNOBSERVABLE inputs (management's own internal projections), requiring the GREATEST degree of JUDGEMENT among the three hierarchy levels.

The price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date.
Fair value hierarchy
A three-level classification (observable to unobservable inputs) indicating the reliability of a fair value measurement.
