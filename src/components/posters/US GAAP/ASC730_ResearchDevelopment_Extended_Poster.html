Conceptual Framework Linkage

Too uncertain to capitalise, in the FASB's view
The FASB's historical position (dating back to SFAS 2 in 1974, now codified in ASC 730) is that the FUTURE benefit of research and development activity is simply TOO UNCERTAIN, at the time costs are incurred, to justify capitalising it as an asset – favouring a simple, conservative, uniformly applied "expense as incurred" rule over attempting to distinguish which specific projects will ultimately succeed.
One of THE most consequential US GAAP/IFRS divergences anywhere
IAS 38 draws a sharp line between RESEARCH (always expensed) and DEVELOPMENT (CAPITALISED once specific criteria are met) – a fundamentally different philosophy from ASC 730's blanket expense rule. This single difference can produce materially different reported ASSETS, EXPENSES, and EARNINGS for economically identical R&D-intensive businesses (software, pharmaceuticals, technology), making Section 13 one of the most important comparisons in this entire library.
2

Scope & Applicability

Self-generated research and development activities
ASC 730 applies to SELF-GENERATED (internal) research and development costs – expenditures aimed at discovering new knowledge or translating that knowledge into new or improved products/processes. It does NOT govern software development costs subject to their own specific guidance (ASC 350-40 for internal-use software, or ASC 985-20 for software to be sold/licensed), nor ACQUIRED in-process R&D in a business combination (Section 7).
3

The General Rule – All R&D Expensed as Incurred

A blanket, bright-line rule
ALL research and development costs are EXPENSED AS INCURRED under US GAAP – with NO capitalisation permitted, regardless of the project's stage, its technical feasibility, or the probability of future commercial success. Unlike IAS 38, US GAAP draws NO distinction between "research" and "development" phases for the purpose of THIS general rule – both are treated IDENTICALLY, as an immediate expense.
4

Why This Is a Headline Divergence From IAS 38

IAS 38's contrasting two-phase model
IAS 38 REQUIRES expensing RESEARCH costs (consistent with US GAAP), but REQUIRES CAPITALISING DEVELOPMENT costs once ALL of the following criteria are met (often remembered by the mnemonic "PIRATE"): Probability of future economic benefit; Intention to complete the asset; Resources (technical, financial, other) adequate to complete; Ability to use or sell the resulting asset; Technical feasibility of completing the asset; and Expenditure can be reliably measured. Once these six criteria are met, DEVELOPMENT costs MUST be capitalised as an intangible asset and subsequently amortised – a fundamentally different treatment from US GAAP's blanket expense rule for the SAME underlying activity.
5

What Counts as R&D – Definitions

Similarly defined activities, differently accounted for
RESEARCH is planned search or critical investigation aimed at discovering new knowledge. DEVELOPMENT is the translation of research findings into a PLAN or DESIGN for a new or significantly improved product or process. These DEFINITIONS are broadly similar in substance to IAS 38's own research/development definitions – the divergence lies entirely in the ACCOUNTING CONSEQUENCE (expense vs potential capitalisation), not in how the underlying activities themselves are defined.
6

Costs Included in R&D

Materials, personnel, facilities, and allocable indirect costs
R&D costs include materials, equipment, and facilities used for R&D (unless they have an ALTERNATIVE FUTURE USE, in which case they are CAPITALISED and DEPRECIATED, with the depreciation charge itself included in R&D expense), personnel costs for those engaged in R&D, costs of services performed by others, and INDIRECT costs reasonably allocable to R&D activities.
7

Acquired In-Process R&D (IPR&D) – the Business Combination Exception

A notable, largely converged exception to the general rule
R&D projects ACQUIRED in a business combination are treated DIFFERENTLY from self-generated R&D: acquired in-process R&D (IPR&D) is CAPITALISED at FAIR VALUE as an INDEFINITE-LIVED intangible asset (NOT immediately expensed), and tested for impairment under ASC 350 until the project is either COMPLETED (at which point it becomes a finite-lived asset, amortised over its useful life) or ABANDONED (expensed at that point). This is a genuine EXCEPTION to the "expense as incurred" rule, specific to the business combination context – and it is broadly CONVERGED with IFRS 3's own treatment, since ACQUIRED intangible assets meeting recognition criteria in a business combination are capitalised under IFRS 3 too, REGARDLESS of whether they would have been capitalised if self-generated. This specific carve-out is one of the few genuinely converged corners within an otherwise sharply divergent Topic.
8

R&D Funding Arrangements

Assessing genuine risk transfer vs disguised financing
Where a THIRD PARTY funds an entity's R&D activities (e.g. in exchange for future royalties or a repayment obligation contingent on the project's success), ASC 730 provides specific guidance for assessing whether the arrangement represents a GENUINE transfer of risk to the funding party (accounted for accordingly, potentially avoiding expense recognition by the researching entity) or is, IN SUBSTANCE, a FINANCING arrangement (requiring the researching entity to recognise a LIABILITY for the funds received, since it hasn't genuinely transferred the risk of the R&D's failure).

Worked Example – Contrasting R&D Capitalisation (US vs IFRS)

The same software development project, two very different balance sheets
Example: A technically feasible software development project
A technology company spends $8,000,000 over a year developing a new product. By mid-year, the project has reached TECHNICAL FEASIBILITY, management has demonstrated the intention and resources to complete it, and there's a clear ability to sell the finished product – meeting all of IAS 38's PIRATE criteria from that point forward. Of the $8,000,000, $3,000,000 was spent before mid-year and $5,000,000 after.
Under US GAAP: the FULL $8,000,000 is EXPENSED as incurred – NOTHING is capitalised, regardless of the project's technical feasibility.
Under IAS 38: the $3,000,000 spent BEFORE the PIRATE criteria were met is expensed (research-phase-equivalent spending), but the $5,000,000 spent AFTER the criteria were met is CAPITALISED as a development intangible asset, subsequently amortised once the product launches – producing a materially different balance sheet (a $5,000,000 asset under IFRS that simply doesn't exist under US GAAP) and a materially different current-period expense ($8,000,000 vs $3,000,000) for the identical underlying project.
10

Worked Example – Acquired IPR&D in a Business Combination

Capitalised despite the general expense rule
Example: Acquiring a pharmaceutical company mid-trial
A pharmaceutical company acquires a target with a promising drug candidate still in clinical trials, with an estimated fair value of $60,000,000 attributable to that in-process R&D project as part of the purchase price allocation.
Despite ASC 730's general "expense as incurred" rule for SELF-GENERATED R&D, the ACQUIRED $60,000,000 IPR&D asset is CAPITALISED as an indefinite-lived intangible asset, tested annually for impairment under ASC 350, until the drug trial either succeeds (converting to a finite-lived, amortised asset) or fails (written off entirely) – a genuine exception, and one broadly MATCHED by IFRS 3's own treatment of the same acquired asset.

Research costsExpensed as incurredExpensed as incurred – converged
Development costs (self-generated)ALSO expensed as incurred – no capitalisation pathCAPITALISED once the six PIRATE criteria are met – MANDATORY, not optional
Acquired IPR&D in a business combinationCapitalised at fair value as an indefinite-lived intangibleAlso capitalised at fair value under IFRS 3 – converged
Practical effect for R&D-intensive industriesLOWER reported assets, HIGHER current-period expense volatility, but simpler/more conservativeHIGHER reported assets for successful projects, SMOOTHER expense recognition via amortisation, but requires ongoing judgement about when criteria are met

An R&D project acquired in a business combination, capitalised at fair value as an indefinite-lived intangible until completed or abandoned.
PIRATE criteria (IAS 38)
The six conditions IAS 38 requires before development costs can be capitalised: Probability of benefit, Intention to complete, Resources adequate, Ability to use/sell, Technical feasibility, Expenditure reliably measurable.

Research
Planned search or critical investigation aimed at discovering new knowledge, always expensed as incurred under both frameworks.
Development
The translation of research findings into a plan or design for a new or improved product/process, expensed under US GAAP but capitalised under IAS 38 once specific criteria are met.
