Ind AS 27 – Separate Financial Statements
Extended reference poster · Converged with IAS 27 · Mandatory standalone FS filing – a genuine Indian regulatory divergence · Worked examples
🇮🇳 IND AS – CONVERGED WITH IAS 27, WITH A GENUINE MANDATORY FILING DIVERGENCE – Ind AS 27's measurement options for investments in subsidiaries/JVs/associates mirror IAS 27 closely. The distinctive Indian feature is that the Companies Act, 2013 MANDATES every Indian company to prepare and file STANDALONE (separate) financial statements, REGARDLESS of whether it also prepares consolidated financial statements – unlike many IFRS jurisdictions where separate FS preparation may not be a legal requirement. Section 11 covers the full comparison.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Same measurement choice as IAS 27, mandatory Indian filing overlay
Ind AS 27 shares IAS 27's core content – when an entity PREPARES separate financial statements, investments in subsidiaries, joint ventures, and associates are accounted for EITHER at COST, in accordance with Ind AS 109 (fair value), or using the EQUITY METHOD. Where Ind AS 27 genuinely diverges in PRACTICE is that the Companies Act, 2013 makes standalone financial statement preparation MANDATORY for every Indian company, a legal requirement IAS 27 itself does not impose (since IFRS defers to LOCAL jurisdictional law on whether separate FS are required at all).
HOW IND AS 27 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Accountability – mandatory standalone reporting ensures EVERY Indian company (not just consolidating parents) provides a discrete, entity-level financial picture – a company-law-driven choice, not an Ind AS 27 accounting principleComparability – the measurement OPTIONS themselves (cost/Ind AS 109/equity method) are IDENTICAL to IAS 27
This mandatory filing requirement is a COMPANY LAW divergence, not a divergence in Ind AS 27's own accounting content – Ind AS 27's substantive MEASUREMENT rules are IDENTICAL to IAS 27.

Scope & Applicability

What Ind AS 27 covers
Ind AS 27 prescribes accounting/disclosure for investments in subsidiaries, joint ventures, and associates WHEN an entity elects, or is required, to prepare separate financial statements – IDENTICAL scope to IAS 27.
In India, EVERY company must apply this standard, since standalone FS preparation is compulsory under the Companies Act, 2013.
Key definitions you must know
Separate financial statements
IDENTICAL definition to IAS 27 – statements presented by a parent (or investor) in which investments are accounted for at cost, per Ind AS 109, or using the equity method, rather than on the basis of reported results and net assets of investees.
Standalone financial statements
The Companies Act, 2013 terminology for an Indian company's separate financial statements, MANDATORY regardless of consolidation status.
3

When Separate Financial Statements Are Prepared

Optional under IAS 27, mandatory under Indian company law
IAS 27 itself is NEUTRAL on whether separate financial statements must be prepared at all – this is left to jurisdictional law/regulation. Ind AS 27 (via Indian company law) makes them MANDATORY for every Indian company, in ADDITION to consolidated financial statements where applicable (see Section 7).

Measurement Options – Cost, Ind AS 109, or Equity Method (with worked example)

Identical choice to IAS 27
Example 1: Choosing a measurement basis for a subsidiary investment
An Indian parent company holds a 100% investment in a subsidiary, originally acquired for ₹50 crore, with the subsidiary's fair value now estimated at ₹80 crore.
The parent may elect to carry this investment in its STANDALONE financial statements at COST (₹50 crore less any impairment), at FAIR VALUE under Ind AS 109 (₹80 crore, with changes through P&L or OCI depending on the election), or using the EQUITY METHOD – an IDENTICAL choice to what IAS 27 offers globally, with the SAME accounting consequences for each option.

Dividends from Investees

Identical to IAS 27
Dividends from a subsidiary, JV, or associate are recognised in P&L when the right to receive payment is established, with an assessment of whether the dividend indicates POSSIBLE impairment of the investment – IDENTICAL to IAS 27.
6

Group Reorganisations

Identical to IAS 27
Specific guidance for group reorganisations meeting defined criteria (e.g. establishing a new parent) applies IDENTICALLY to IAS 27, allowing the new parent to measure cost at the PREVIOUS carrying amount of its share of equity in the original parent.

Mandatory Standalone Filing – The Indian Regulatory Divergence

The single most important Ind AS 27-related Indian practical point
Under the Companies Act, 2013, EVERY Indian company – even a wholly-owned subsidiary within a large group, or a parent that ALSO prepares consolidated financial statements – MUST prepare, have audited, and FILE its own STANDALONE financial statements with the Registrar of Companies (RoC), applying Ind AS 27's measurement options at the ENTITY level. This is a distinctly Indian statutory REQUIREMENT – many IFRS jurisdictions do NOT mandate separate financial statements for every entity in a group, particularly where only consolidated reporting is legally required.
