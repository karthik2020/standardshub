Conceptual Framework Linkage

Website spend looked new, but the underlying question wasn't
When entities began building and operating their own websites, a question arose whether website development spend was fundamentally different from other internally generated intangibles IAS 38 already addressed — such as software. SIC-32 concludes it is NOT fundamentally different: a website is simply another form of internally generated intangible asset, subject to IAS 38's existing recognition criteria, including the same discipline around demonstrating genuine future economic benefit before capitalising anything.
Guarding against speculative capitalisation of intangible spend
IAS 38 already imposes strict conditions before internally generated intangibles can be capitalised, precisely because such expenditure is often difficult to distinguish from the cost of maintaining or enhancing internally generated goodwill (which can NEVER be capitalised). SIC-32 extends that same discipline to websites, preventing entities from capitalising speculative or promotional website spend simply because it happens to relate to a technology asset.
2

Scope & Applicability

What SIC-32 covers
SIC-32 applies to the internal expenditure on the development and operation of an entity's OWN web site for internal or external access. It does NOT apply to expenditure on purchasing, developing, and operating HARDWARE (servers, etc.), which is accounted for under IAS 16, and it does not apply to a website developed for SALE to another entity, which is dealt with as inventory or under other applicable guidance.
TYPICAL CONTEXTS
An e-commerce entity building and operating its own transactional websiteA corporate website developed for information, marketing, or internal intranet purposes
Key definitions
Web site
Software and other coded instructions, and typically graphics and content in the form of text or pictures, embedded in the software or coded instructions, developed and operated for internal or external access.
Internally generated intangible asset
An intangible asset arising from an entity's own development activity, subject to IAS 38's stricter research/development recognition split, rather than being separately purchased.
3

A Website Is an Internally Generated Intangible Asset

The threshold conclusion
A web site arising from development IS an internally generated intangible asset subject to IAS 38's requirements — meaning expenditure on a website is recognised as an asset ONLY if it meets BOTH the general DEFINITION of an intangible asset (identifiable, controlled by the entity as a result of past events, and expected to generate future economic benefits) AND the SPECIFIC recognition criteria in IAS 38, most critically including the requirement to demonstrate HOW the website will generate probable future economic benefits.

5

Planning Stage — Always Expensed

No capitalisation, regardless of eventual success
Expenditure incurred during the PLANNING stage — undertaking feasibility studies, defining objectives and specifications, evaluating alternative products and suppliers, and selecting preferences — is, in nature, similar to the RESEARCH phase under IAS 38, and is EXPENSED AS INCURRED. This applies even where the planning ultimately leads to a successful, revenue-generating website — the nature of the activity, not its eventual outcome, drives the treatment.
6

Development Stage — Conditionally Capitalised (with worked example)

Where the real judgement lies
Expenditure incurred in the APPLICATION AND INFRASTRUCTURE DEVELOPMENT stage, the GRAPHIC DESIGN stage, and the CONTENT DEVELOPMENT stage (to the extent content is developed for purposes OTHER than advertising and promoting an entity's own products/services) is capitalised ONLY if the entity can satisfy IAS 38's general recognition criteria — most importantly, demonstrating how the website will generate PROBABLE future economic benefits, such as by being capable of generating revenue directly (e.g. through orders placed via the site).
Worked example — e-commerce site development
Example: Capitalising a revenue-generating transactional site
An entity incurs $80,000 planning an online store (feasibility studies, requirements gathering), then $450,000 building the transactional infrastructure, payment processing integration, and product catalogue functionality — a site designed to generate direct sales revenue.
The $80,000 PLANNING cost is expensed as incurred. The $450,000 development cost MAY be capitalised as an intangible asset, since the site is demonstrably capable of generating revenue directly through online sales — satisfying IAS 38's probable-future-economic-benefit criterion — PROVIDED the other IAS 38 recognition criteria (technical feasibility, intention and ability to complete, reliable cost measurement) are also met.
7

Operating Stage

Ongoing maintenance is generally expensed
Once a website is complete and enters the OPERATING stage, subsequent expenditure is assessed against the SAME criteria as during development: capitalised only if it enables the website to generate FURTHER future economic benefits BEYOND the level originally assessed, and meets the IAS 38 recognition criteria. Routine activities such as REVIEWING content for accuracy, correcting errors, and general maintenance are generally EXPENSED as incurred, since they typically maintain rather than enhance the asset's originally assessed level of performance.
8

The Advertising/Promotion Carve-Out

A firm exclusion, regardless of development stage
If a website — or a specific PORTION of content within it — is developed PRIMARILY for the purpose of advertising and promoting an entity's own products and services, ALL related expenditure is EXPENSED AS INCURRED, consistent with IAS 38's general prohibition on capitalising internally generated brands, mastheads, and advertising/promotional costs (whose future economic benefits cannot be reliably distinguished from the cost of developing the business as a whole). This carve-out applies even to expenditure that would otherwise fall within the "development stage" bucket in Section 6.

SIC-32 vs US GAAP

Comparison — SIC-32 vs US GAAP (ASC 350-50)
FeatureIFRS (SIC-32)US GAAP (ASC 350-50)
Overall structureStage-based model mapped onto IAS 38's research/development split, requiring probable future economic benefit demonstrationA very SIMILAR stage-based model (planning, application/infrastructure development, graphics, content) with broadly parallel capitalisation conclusions per stage — one of the more closely converged specific interpretations
Advertising/promotional content carve-outExplicitly excluded from capitalisationSimilarly excluded — advertising costs are generally expensed under US GAAP as well
Operating stage maintenanceGenerally expensed unless it creates further future benefitSimilarly, ongoing maintenance/minor upgrades are generally expensed under US GAAP
SIC-32 and ASC 350-50 reach highly convergent outcomes — both apply essentially the same stage-based logic to website development costs, reflecting a shared underlying view of how internally generated software/website assets should be distinguished from research-type and promotional spend.

Related Standards

IAS 2Applies instead where a website is developed for SALE to a customer, rather than for the entity's own internal or external use
Quick-reference glossary
Research phase analogy
IAS 38's principle that research-type activity cannot demonstrate a probable future economic benefit and must be expensed — SIC-32 maps a website's planning stage onto this same logic.
Development phase analogy
IAS 38's principle that development-type activity may be capitalised once specific recognition criteria are met — SIC-32 maps a website's application/infrastructure, graphic design, and non-promotional content stages onto this logic.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Content development
Creating, purchasing, preparing, and uploading information onto a website — capitalisable if non-promotional and meeting IAS 38 criteria, otherwise expensed.
Internally generated goodwill
Goodwill an entity builds through its own ongoing activities, NEVER capitalised under IAS 38 — the underlying reason advertising/promotional website content cannot be capitalised either, since its economic benefit cannot be separated from goodwill generation.
