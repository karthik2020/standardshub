Conceptual Framework Linkage

The dividing line between short-term and long-term claims
ASPE Section 1510 defines WHAT qualifies AS a CURRENT asset OR current LIABILITY – the DISTINCTION underlying the CLASSIFIED Balance SHEET presentation MOST ASPE preparers USE. THIS Section INCLUDES a GENUINELY distinctive rule GOVERNING debt SUBJECT to COVENANT violations (Section 6), WORTH particular ATTENTION given ITS practical SIGNIFICANCE for PRIVATE, lender-financed enterprises.
2

Scope – the Classified Balance Sheet Distinction

Applies wherever a classified presentation is used
This Section applies to ENTERPRISES presenting a CLASSIFIED Balance SHEET, DISTINGUISHING current FROM non-current ASSETS and LIABILITIES – the MOST common PRESENTATION approach FOR ASPE preparers, THOUGH an UNCLASSIFIED, liquidity-ORDERED presentation MAY be used WHERE it PROVIDES more RELIABLE and RELEVANT information.
3

Definition of Current Assets

Cash, or realizable within one year or the operating cycle
CURRENT assets COMPRISE cash, and OTHER assets that ARE readily CONVERTIBLE into a KNOWN amount OF cash, or ARE held for SALE or CONSUMPTION in the NORMAL course of OPERATIONS, WITHIN one YEAR from the BALANCE sheet date, OR within the NORMAL operating CYCLE when LONGER than a YEAR.
4

Definition of Current Liabilities

Due within one year, the operating cycle, or on demand
CURRENT liabilities COMPRISE liabilities DUE to be SETTLED within ONE year of the BALANCE sheet DATE (or the NORMAL operating CYCLE, when LONGER), AND liabilities that ARE payable ON demand.

THE DISTINCTIVE RULE – Callable Debt Following a Covenant Violation

A covenant breach can force a current classification, even for long-term debt
WHERE long-TERM debt BECOMES callable BY the LENDER because OF a COVENANT violation – EVEN if the LENDER has NOT actually EXERCISED that RIGHT – the DEBT is GENERALLY classified as CURRENT, UNLESS the LENDER has WAIVED, or SUBSEQUENTLY lost, the RIGHT to DEMAND repayment FOR a period of MORE than ONE year FROM the balance SHEET date, or the VIOLATION is CURED within a SPECIFIED grace PERIOD. THIS is a GENUINELY distinctive, PRACTICALLY significant rule – a SINGLE covenant BREACH can PUSH an ENTIRE long-term LOAN balance into CURRENT liabilities, MATERIALLY affecting WORKING capital presentation and LENDER covenant CALCULATIONS themselves.
7

Refinancing After the Balance Sheet Date

A narrow exception where genuine long-term refinancing has occurred
WHERE debt that WOULD otherwise be CLASSIFIED as CURRENT (e.g. DUE to a MATURITY within ONE year, OR a COVENANT violation) is REFINANCED on a GENUINELY long-term BASIS AFTER the balance SHEET date but BEFORE the FINANCIAL statements are COMPLETED, it MAY still be CLASSIFIED as long-TERM, PROVIDED the SPECIFIC conditions FOR this treatment ARE met.
8

The Normal Operating Cycle

A longer-than-one-year measuring stick for certain industries
WHERE an ENTERPRISE'S normal OPERATING cycle EXCEEDS one YEAR (COMMON in SECTORS such as LONG-term construction OR certain MANUFACTURING processes), the CURRENT/non-current DISTINCTION is MEASURED against THAT longer OPERATING cycle, RATHER than a FLAT one-year THRESHOLD.
9

Worked Example – Classifying Debt Callable Due to a Covenant Breach

A single breach reclassifies the entire balance
Example: A breached debt-to-equity covenant
A private company has a $6,000,000 term loan maturing in 4 years. At the balance sheet date, the company breaches a debt-to-equity covenant, giving the lender the contractual right to demand immediate repayment – a right the lender has not yet exercised or waived.
DESPITE the LOAN'S 4-year CONTRACTUAL maturity, the COMPANY classifies the FULL $6,000,000 as a CURRENT liability, SINCE the LENDER retains the RIGHT to CALL the DEBT and has NEITHER waived NOR lost THAT right as OF the balance SHEET date.
10

Worked Example – Refinancing Before the Financial Statements Are Completed

A genuine long-term refinancing restores long-term classification
Example: Curing the breach through refinancing
Continuing the prior example, the company refinances the loan on a new, genuinely long-term basis 6 weeks after the balance sheet date, before its financial statements are completed, meeting the specific conditions required for long-term classification.
GIVEN the QUALIFYING refinancing OCCURRED before the FINANCIAL statements were COMPLETED, the COMPANY may CLASSIFY the $6,000,000 as NON-CURRENT – the SUBSEQUENT refinancing RESTORES the LONG-term classification THAT would otherwise HAVE been LOST due to the COVENANT breach.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Callable debt
Debt subject to a lender's contractual right to demand immediate repayment, commonly triggered by a covenant violation, requiring current classification absent a waiver or cure.
Normal operating cycle
The typical time span between the acquisition of materials for a process and their conversion into cash, used as the current/non-current threshold when longer than one year.
