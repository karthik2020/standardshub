Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why grants aren't simply cash income
The Conceptual Framework's matching-oriented approach to income recognition underpins IAS 20 — a grant that compensates for specific costs should be recognised in the SAME period as those costs, not simply when cash is received, so profit or loss faithfully represents underlying performance.
HOW GRANTS MAP TO THE FRAMEWORK'S ELEMENTS
Income recognition — a grant increases assets or reduces a liability, giving rise to income, but only once reasonable assurance exists that conditions will be met Liability (deferred income) — cash or an asset received before the related conditions/costs are satisfied is initially a liability, not income Systematic recognition — income is recognised over the periods that match the related costs the grant is intended to compensate
IAS 20 predates the current Conceptual Framework and is one of the oldest, least-amended IFRS standards — its "income approach" (rather than treating grants as a direct credit to equity) reflects a long-standing IASB view that grants are not contributions from owners.
Measurement principles applied by IAS 20
Grants are measured based on what is RECEIVED or RECEIVABLE, not at some independently assessed fair value of the benefit conferred (except for non-monetary grants).
Framework basisUsed by IAS 20 for…
Historical/transaction amountMonetary grants — measured at the amount received or receivable
Fair valueNon-monetary grants (e.g. free land, equipment) — usually recorded at the fair value of the non-monetary asset
ℹ️ Qualitative characteristics in action: IAS 20 prioritises faithful representation of performance by deferring grant income to match related expenditure — recognising the full grant as income on receipt (a "capital approach" some jurisdictions use) would distort the period's profit and is explicitly rejected by the standard.

Scope & Applicability

What IAS 20 covers
IAS 20 applies to accounting for, and disclosure of, government grants and other forms of government assistance. A government grant is assistance by government in the form of a transfer of resources to an entity in return for past or future compliance with certain conditions relating to the entity's operating activities.
THE CORE RECOGNITION TEST
(a) reasonable assurance the entity will comply with any conditions attached; AND
(b) reasonable assurance the grant will be received
SCOPE EXCLUSIONS — GOVERNED BY OTHER STANDARDS
Government assistance provided in the form of tax breaks, tax holidays, or tax credits → IAS 12 (income tax accounting), not IAS 20 Government participation in the ownership of the entity → outside IAS 20's scope Government grants covered by specific standards — e.g. grants related to biological assets measured at fair value less costs to sell → IAS 41 (which has its own, simpler rule, see Section 13) Government-mandated price support / trade-policy benefits without a clear transfer of resources → typically outside IAS 20's definition of a "grant"
Key definitions you must know
Government grants
Assistance by government in the form of transfers of resources to an entity in return for past or future compliance with certain conditions relating to the entity's operating activities — excludes forms of government assistance that cannot reasonably have a value placed on them, and transactions with government that cannot be distinguished from the entity's normal trading transactions.
Government
Government, government agencies, and similar bodies, whether local, national, or international.
Government assistance
Action by government designed to provide an economic benefit specific to an entity or range of entities qualifying under certain criteria — broader than "grants" and includes things like free technical/marketing advice and loan guarantees.
Grants related to assets
Government grants whose primary condition is that an entity qualifying for them should purchase, construct, or otherwise acquire long-term assets.
Grants related to income
Government grants other than those related to assets — typically compensating for expenses already incurred or providing immediate financial support with no future related costs.
3

Recognition Criteria

The two-condition recognition test
A government grant — whether monetary or non-monetary — is NOT recognised until both conditions are met. Recognition based on mere receipt of cash, without reasonable assurance over conditions, is not permitted.
Condition 1 — Reasonable assurance of compliance
The entity will comply with the conditions attached to the grant.
Condition 2 — Reasonable assurance of receipt
The grant will be received.
⚠️ Systematic recognition, not immediate income: once recognition criteria are met, IAS 20 requires the grant to be recognised in profit or loss on a SYSTEMATIC BASIS over the periods in which the entity recognises the related costs as expenses, for which the grant is intended to compensate — never all at once unless it compensates for costs already incurred with no future related costs.

Grants Related to Assets — Two Presentation Methods

Method 1 — Deferred income approach
MECHANICS
Grant recognised as deferred income (a liability); released to SOPL over the useful life of the related asset, usually matching the depreciation pattern
The asset itself is recorded at full cost, gross of the grant, and depreciated normally. The deferred income liability is released to income in proportion to depreciation recognised.
Method 2 — Deduction from asset's carrying amount
Grant deducted from the asset's cost; asset carried at (Cost − Grant); reduced depreciation charge over the asset's life effectively recognises the grant in profit
Both methods are permitted under IAS 20 — the choice is an accounting POLICY CHOICE applied consistently to similar grants, and must be disclosed. Total profit over the asset's life is identical either way; only presentation and the timing pattern of individual line items differ.
Worked example — grant toward a machine, both methods compared
Example 1: $200,000 machine, $50,000 government grant, 10-year useful life, straight-line, nil residual value
A manufacturing entity purchases a machine for $200,000 and receives a related government grant of $50,000, with reasonable assurance over both compliance and receipt established at acquisition.
Method 1 (deferred income):
• Machine recognised at $200,000 (SOFP, non-current asset); annual depreciation = 200,000 ÷ 10 = $20,000 (SOPL)
• Deferred income liability of $50,000 released at $5,000 p.a. (50,000 ÷ 10) to other income (SOPL)
• Net annual SOPL impact: $20,000 depreciation − $5,000 grant income = $15,000 net expense

Method 2 (net of asset):
• Machine recognised at 200,000 − 50,000 = $150,000 (SOFP); annual depreciation = 150,000 ÷ 10 = $15,000 net expense
• No separate grant income line — the benefit is embedded within a lower depreciation charge

Both methods produce IDENTICAL net annual profit impact ($15,000) — only the gross presentation (asset carrying amount, and whether "grant income" appears as a separate SOPL line) differs.

Grants Related to Income

Recognition & presentation choices
Grants related to income are recognised in profit or loss over the period(s) in which the related expenses are incurred, and can be presented in either of two acceptable ways.
PRESENTATION OPTIONS — POLICY CHOICE
Separate credit / "other income" — presented as a distinct line item, often called "other income" Netted against the related expense — deducted from the related expense line it compensates for
As with asset-related grants, both presentation choices are acceptable and must be applied consistently and disclosed — profit for the period is identical under either.
Worked example — training cost subsidy
Example 2: Grant compensating for employee training costs already incurred
An entity incurs $40,000 of staff retraining costs during the year in a designated development region and receives a government grant of $15,000 specifically to reimburse these already-incurred costs, with reasonable assurance of receipt confirmed by year end.
Since the costs the grant compensates for have ALREADY been incurred at the point recognition criteria are met, the grant is recognised in profit or loss immediately (not deferred) as the conditions are satisfied at the point of recognition.

Repayment of Grants — A Change in Estimate

Repayment mechanics — treated prospectively under IAS 8
A government grant that becomes repayable (e.g. because a condition was breached) is accounted for as a CHANGE IN ACCOUNTING ESTIMATE, not a prior period error — this is a frequently tested point.
GRANT RELATED TO INCOME — REPAYABLE
Applied first against any unamortised deferred credit balance remaining for the grant; any excess is recognised immediately as an expense.
GRANT RELATED TO ASSETS — REPAYABLE
Recorded by increasing the carrying amount of the asset (Method 2) or reducing the deferred income balance (Method 1) by the repayment amount — the CUMULATIVE additional depreciation that would have been recognised to date, absent the grant, is recognised immediately as an expense.
Example 3: Repayment after breach of condition
A grant of $50,000 (Method 1, from Example 1) becomes fully repayable at the start of Year 3 due to a condition breach. At that point, $10,000 of deferred income remains unamortised (2 years × $5,000 already released).
Repayment: Dr Deferred income $10,000 (clears remaining balance); Dr Expense (immediately, SOPL) $40,000; Cr Cash/payable $50,000.
The $40,000 "excess" reflects the grant income already recognised in Years 1–2 that must now effectively be reversed through expense recognition.

Government Assistance — Beyond Grants

Forms of government assistance excluded from "grant" accounting
Some forms of government assistance cannot reasonably have a value placed on them, or cannot be distinguished from the entity's normal trading transactions — these are NOT accounted for as grants, but disclosure of their nature, extent, and duration may still be required.
Free technical or marketing advice Provision of guarantees (loan guarantees) Government procurement policies responsible for a portion of an entity's sales Below-market-rate government loans — the BENEFIT of the below-market rate IS treated as a government grant (see callout below), even though the loan principal itself is a financial liability under IFRS 9
Below-market-rate government loans
Grant benefit = Loan proceeds (fair value using market rate) − Actual cash received/carrying amount at initial recognition under IFRS 9
The loan is initially recognised and measured under IFRS 9 (at fair value, which for a below-market loan is LESS than the cash actually received). The DIFFERENCE between that fair value and the cash proceeds received is accounted for as a government grant under IAS 20.
This is a common intersection point between IAS 20 and IFRS 9 in exams — the loan liability itself follows IFRS 9's amortised cost rules; only the embedded subsidy follows IAS 20.

Disclosure Requirements

Required disclosures
GOVERNMENT GRANTS
The accounting policy adopted, including the method of presentation used in the financial statements The nature and extent of government grants recognised in the financial statements, and an indication of other forms of government assistance from which the entity has directly benefited Unfulfilled conditions and other contingencies attaching to government assistance that has been recognised
GOVERNMENT ASSISTANCE (BROADER)
Nature, extent, and duration of government assistance significant enough that disclosure is needed to understand the entity's financial statements — even where the assistance cannot be reasonably valued and is not recognised Repayment obligations, if a previously recognised grant becomes repayable

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether "reasonable assurance" over compliance and receipt genuinely exists — a lower, more subjective threshold than the "virtually certain"/"probable" language used elsewhere in IFRS Distinguishing a grant related to assets from one related to income when a single grant serves mixed purposes Determining whether a below-market government loan exists and quantifying the market rate benchmark used to measure the embedded grant
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
The systematic basis (usually matching depreciation or the pattern of related expense recognition) used to allocate grant income across periods Fair value of non-monetary grants (e.g. donated land) where no observable market price exists
⚠️ IAS 20 does NOT permit grants to be credited directly to equity/shareholders' funds — a treatment sometimes seen in local GAAPs. Under IFRS, ALL government grants pass through profit or loss over the relevant periods (or reduce an asset's carrying amount, itself later reflected in reduced depreciation through SOPL).

IFRS vs US GAAP — Key Differences

Comparison — IAS 20 vs US GAAP
FeatureIFRS (IAS 20)US GAAP
Dedicated standardSingle comprehensive standard (IAS 20)No single comprehensive standard — guidance is fragmented; many entities analogise to IAS 20 or apply industry/contribution-accounting guidance by analogy
Recognition threshold"Reasonable assurance" of compliance and receiptGenerally requires grant terms to be unconditional or conditions substantially met before recognition, often a higher bar
Presentation of asset-related grantsFree choice: deferred income OR net against asset costDiversity in practice; net presentation against the asset is common where guidance is analogised, but no single codified rule
Not-for-profit/contribution guidanceN/A — IAS 20 covers all entities uniformlyASC 958 addresses contributions received by not-for-profits specifically, a different recognition model (based on conditions/restrictions) than IAS 20
The lack of a single comprehensive US GAAP standard for government grants received by for-profit entities is a well-known convergence gap — this makes IAS 20 disclosures and presentation choices a common comparability discussion point for multinational groups reporting under both frameworks.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Grants related to assets
Grants whose primary condition is the purchase, construction, or acquisition of long-term assets.
Grants related to income
All government grants other than those related to assets.
Forgivable loans
Loans which the lender undertakes to waive repayment of under certain prescribed conditions — treated as a government grant when there is reasonable assurance the entity will meet the terms for forgiveness.
Fair value
Used to measure non-monetary grants (e.g. donated land or equipment), consistent with the IFRS 13 definition.
Deferred income
A liability representing grant amounts received/receivable but not yet recognised in profit or loss, pending satisfaction of the matching condition.
Reasonable assurance
The recognition threshold used by IAS 20 — a lower bar than "virtually certain," requiring credible evidence conditions will be met and the grant received.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position (balance sheet) SOPL Statement of profit or loss (income statement) OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
