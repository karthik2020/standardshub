Conceptual Framework Linkage

Hedge accounting must track a genuine economic exposure
Hedge accounting is only appropriate where an entity is managing a REAL risk exposure. A net investment in a foreign operation creates genuine foreign currency risk between the operation's functional currency and the parent's functional currency — but groups often present consolidated results in a DIFFERENT presentation currency again, and IFRIC 16 exists largely to stop entities treating that further translation step as if it were itself a hedgeable risk.
Faithful representation across a multi-entity group
Because large groups routinely hold hedging instruments in whichever group entity has the most efficient access to the relevant currency markets — not necessarily the parent that owns the foreign operation — IFRIC 16 confirms hedge accounting should follow the ECONOMIC substance of the hedge (does it genuinely offset the net investment's currency risk?) rather than an arbitrary rule about which legal entity must hold the instrument.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRIC 16 covers
IFRIC 16 applies to an entity that hedges the foreign currency risk arising from its net investments in foreign operations and wishes to qualify for hedge accounting under IFRS 9. It addresses three specific application questions: the nature of the hedged risk and eligible hedged amount, where within the group the hedging instrument may be held, and how to determine amounts reclassified from equity to profit or loss on disposal.
TYPICAL CONTEXTS
Multinational groups hedging translation exposure on foreign subsidiaries using FX forwards or foreign-currency borrowingsGroups centralising treasury/hedging activity in a entity other than the direct parent of the foreign operation
Key definitions
Net investment in a foreign operation
The amount of the reporting entity's interest in the net assets of a foreign operation, per IAS 21 — the item whose currency risk may be hedged.
Presentation currency
The currency in which the financial statements are presented, which may differ from any entity's functional currency within the group — NOT itself a source of hedgeable risk (see Section 4).
3

What Risk Can Actually Be Hedged

Only functional-currency-to-functional-currency risk qualifies
The hedged risk that may be designated is the foreign currency risk arising between the FUNCTIONAL CURRENCY of the foreign operation and the functional currency of ANY PARENT entity of that foreign operation — this need not be the ultimate parent; an intermediate parent's functional currency can equally be used as the basis for the hedged risk, provided the designation is made consistently.
ℹ️ This flexibility — hedging against an intermediate parent's functional currency rather than only the ultimate parent's — is one of IFRIC 16's most practically useful clarifications for complex multi-tier group structures.
4

Presentation Currency Is Not an Exposure

A firm boundary on eligible hedged risk
IFRIC 16 explicitly clarifies that the risk arising from the difference between the GROUP'S PRESENTATION currency and a foreign operation's functional currency does NOT, on its own, create an exposure to which hedge accounting can be applied. Presentation currency is simply a reporting convention (a translation exercise for consolidation purposes) — it does not represent a genuine economic exposure the group is managing, so it cannot be designated as the hedged risk.
5

Where the Hedging Instrument Can Be Held

Any entity within the group, subject to the usual hedge accounting conditions
The hedging instrument (or instruments) may be held by ANY entity or entities within the group — it does not have to be held by the parent that has the investment in the foreign operation being hedged, nor by the foreign operation itself. What matters is that the designation, documentation, and effectiveness requirements of IFRS 9's hedge accounting model are satisfied at the CONSOLIDATED level, regardless of which group entity is the legal counterparty to the hedging instrument.
6

Effectiveness Testing

Comparing like-for-like currency movements
Hedge effectiveness is assessed by comparing the change in value of the hedging instrument attributable to the designated foreign currency risk against the change in value of the net investment attributable to that SAME foreign currency risk. Because more than one entity's functional currency can be used to define the hedged risk (Section 3), effectiveness testing must be performed CONSISTENTLY with the specific functional currency pairing that was designated at inception.
7

Amounts Reclassified on Disposal

Reclassifying both legs consistently
On disposal of the hedged foreign operation, the CUMULATIVE amount recognised in other comprehensive income in respect of BOTH the hedging instrument and the net investment's foreign currency translation is reclassified from equity to profit or loss as a reclassification adjustment, consistent with IAS 21's general treatment on disposal of a foreign operation. IFRIC 16 clarifies this reclassification must be determined using the SAME functional currency pairing that was used for effectiveness testing throughout the life of the hedge.
8

Worked Example — Intragroup Hedging Instrument

Hedging instrument held by a non-parent group entity
Example: Centralised treasury hedge
A US-dollar-functional-currency parent owns a Japanese yen-functional-currency subsidiary through an intermediate UK sterling-functional-currency holding company. The group's centralised treasury function, based in a separate Swiss entity, enters into a yen/dollar forward contract to hedge the parent's net investment exposure. The forward is designated against the USD/JPY risk (parent's functional currency vs the operation's functional currency).
This qualifies for hedge accounting under IFRIC 16: the hedging instrument does not need to be held by the parent OR the intermediate UK holding company — the Swiss treasury entity holding the forward is acceptable, provided the designation (USD as the parent's functional currency, JPY as the foreign operation's) and effectiveness testing are properly documented and consistently applied at the consolidated level.

IFRIC 16 vs US GAAP

Comparison — IFRIC 16 vs US GAAP (ASC 815-35)
FeatureIFRS (IFRIC 16)US GAAP (ASC 815-35)
Eligible hedged risk basisFunctional currency of the operation vs functional currency of ANY parent in the chainBroadly similar flexibility — net investment hedges can be designated using an intermediate parent's functional currency under US GAAP too
Where the hedging instrument can be heldAny group entity, provided consolidated-level conditions are metSimilarly flexible in practice, though documentation conventions differ
Presentation currency as a hedgeable riskExplicitly excludedAlso not treated as a hedgeable risk under US GAAP's net investment hedge model
Net investment hedge accounting is another relatively converged area between the two frameworks — the practical mechanics of documentation and effectiveness testing differ in detail, but the core conclusions IFRIC 16 reaches are broadly mirrored in US GAAP practice.

Quick-reference glossary
Foreign currency translation reserve
The cumulative OCI balance representing exchange differences arising on translating a foreign operation's results and financial position into the presentation currency.
Reclassification adjustment
An amount reclassified from OCI to profit or loss in the current period, such as the cumulative translation and hedge gains/losses released on disposal of a foreign operation.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Net investment hedge
A hedge of the foreign currency exposure arising from a reporting entity's net investment in a foreign operation, one of the three hedge accounting categories recognised under IFRS 9.
Functional currency
The currency of the primary economic environment in which an entity operates, determined per IAS 21 — the basis for identifying the eligible hedged risk under IFRIC 16.
