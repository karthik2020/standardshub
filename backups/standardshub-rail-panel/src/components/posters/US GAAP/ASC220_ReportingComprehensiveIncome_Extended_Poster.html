Conceptual Framework Linkage

Capturing value changes net income alone misses
Net income does not capture every economic gain or loss an entity experiences in a period — certain items (foreign currency translation, certain pension remeasurements, cash flow hedge gains/losses, available-for-sale debt security fair value changes) are recognised OUTSIDE net income, in OTHER COMPREHENSIVE INCOME (OCI), while remaining part of the entity's TOTAL comprehensive income for the period. ASC 220 governs how this broader picture is reported.
A presentation Topic, not a recognition Topic
ASC 220 does NOT itself determine WHICH items go through OCI versus net income — that determination is made by the specific Topic governing each transaction type (e.g. ASC 815 for cash flow hedges, ASC 715 for pension remeasurements, ASC 830 for foreign currency translation). ASC 220 governs only how those OCI items, once identified elsewhere, are PRESENTED and RECLASSIFIED.
2

Scope & Applicability

Applies to all entities with OCI items
ASC 220 applies to any entity that reports items of other comprehensive income. An entity with NO OCI items in a given period is not required to report comprehensive income separately from net income — though most entities of any size have at least occasional pension or foreign currency translation activity bringing them within scope.

No Equity-Security FVOCI Category in US GAAP

A significant divergence from IFRS 9
Under ASC 321 (Investments—Equity Securities), equity securities are measured at FAIR VALUE with changes recognised THROUGH NET INCOME — there is NO US GAAP equivalent to IFRS 9's irrevocable FVOCI ELECTION for equity instruments. This means, unlike under IFRS, a US GAAP preparer holding a long-term strategic equity stake has NO option to route fair value changes through OCI instead of net income — every equity security fair value change flows through the income statement, full stop.
5

Presentation Options — One or Two Statements

The same structural choice IFRS offers
An entity may present comprehensive income EITHER (a) in a SINGLE CONTINUOUS STATEMENT, with net income as a subtotal followed immediately by OCI components and a total comprehensive income figure, OR (b) in TWO SEPARATE BUT CONSECUTIVE STATEMENTS — an Income Statement immediately followed by a Statement of Comprehensive Income beginning with net income — mirroring IAS 1's own single-statement/two-statement choice almost exactly.
6

Reclassification Adjustments — the ASU 2013-02 Tabular Format

A specific, prescribed disclosure format
When an amount previously recognised in OCI is RECLASSIFIED into net income (e.g. a cash flow hedge gain realised when the hedged transaction occurs, or an available-for-sale security sold), ASC 220 (as amended by ASU 2013-02) requires a SPECIFIC, DETAILED tabular disclosure showing, BY COMPONENT of accumulated OCI: the amount reclassified, and the specific INCOME STATEMENT LINE ITEM it was reclassified into. This is a more prescriptive, standardised format than IAS 1's more general reclassification adjustment disclosure requirement.
7

Tax Effects of Each OCI Component

Presented net of tax, or gross with a single aggregate tax line
An entity may present each component of OCI either NET OF related tax effects, or presented BEFORE related tax effects with ONE aggregate income tax line shown for the total of all OCI items — with the tax effect of EACH component disclosed either on the face of the statement or in the notes either way. This mirrors IAS 1's own equivalent flexibility for presenting OCI items net of tax or gross with an aggregate tax line.
8

Worked Example — Two-Statement Presentation

Illustrating the structure
Example: Building the second statement from net income
An entity reports net income of $12,000,000 for the year. During the year it recognises a $900,000 favourable foreign currency translation adjustment and a $350,000 unrealised loss on available-for-sale debt securities, both net of tax.
Statement of Comprehensive Income: Net income $12,000,000 + Foreign currency translation adjustment $900,000 − Unrealised loss on AFS debt securities $350,000 = Total comprehensive income $12,550,000. This second statement follows immediately after the Income Statement (which independently arrives at the $12,000,000 net income figure) under the two-statement approach.

Worked Example — Reclassification Out of AOCI

A cash flow hedge gain realised into net income
Example: A commodity hedge settling into cost of goods sold
An entity previously deferred a $500,000 gain on an effective cash flow hedge of forecasted raw material purchases in accumulated OCI. During the current period, the hedged purchases occur and are subsequently sold as finished goods.
The $500,000 is RECLASSIFIED out of accumulated OCI and into NET INCOME, specifically within Cost of Goods Sold — the tabular disclosure required by ASC 220 shows this $500,000 reclassification, by component (cash flow hedges) and by the SPECIFIC income statement line it landed in, exactly the level of specificity ASU 2013-02's format requires.

ASC 220 vs IAS 1 — the Equity FVOCI Divergence

Comparison — ASC 220 vs IAS 1's OCI presentation requirements
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 220 / ASC 321)IFRS (IAS 1 / IFRS 9)
Equity securities — OCI optionNONE — always through net income (ASC 321)Irrevocable FVOCI election available for equity instruments not held for trading, per IFRS 9
Recycling of equity OCI gains on disposalNot applicable — no equity OCI category exists to recycleNEVER recycled to profit or loss even on disposal, if the FVOCI election was made — a "permanent OCI" treatment
Presentation format (one vs two statements)Free choice, per ASC 220Free choice, per IAS 1 — fully converged
Reclassification disclosure specificityHighly prescriptive tabular format (ASU 2013-02)General principle-based disclosure requirement, less rigidly formatted
The equity-instrument OCI divergence is one of the more consequential differences between the frameworks for entities holding long-term strategic equity stakes — identical economics can produce materially different reported net income VOLATILITY purely due to this classification difference.

Quick-reference glossary preview
Reclassification adjustment
An amount reclassified from accumulated OCI into net income in the current period, to avoid double-counting the item in total comprehensive income across periods.
Accumulated other comprehensive income (AOCI)
The cumulative balance of OCI items recognised over time, a distinct component of total equity.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Comprehensive income
The change in equity of an entity during a period from transactions and other events/circumstances from non-owner sources — net income plus other comprehensive income.
Two-statement approach
Presenting the Income Statement and the Statement of Comprehensive Income as two separate but consecutive statements, rather than combining them into one continuous statement.
