ASPE Section 3065 — Leases
Extended reference poster · Canadian GAAP (ASPE — Part II of the CPA Canada Handbook) · Retains the dual capital/operating lease model — ASPE has NOT adopted the single on-balance-sheet lessee model · CAD examples
A MAJOR DIVERGENCE FROM THE GLOBAL CONVERGENCE TREND: ASPE has not adopted a single on-balance-sheet lessee model. It retains the older dual classification approach (capital vs operating leases) for both lessees and lessors — standing in genuine contrast to IFRS 16, ASBE No. 21, and Japan's ASBJ No. 34, all of which moved lessees toward recognizing right-of-use assets and lease liabilities for virtually all leases.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

One of the most significant structural divergences in this entire poster library
ASPE Section 3065 governs LEASE accounting FOR both LESSEES and LESSORS — RETAINING the OLDER, dual-CLASSIFICATION approach (CAPITAL leases VERSUS operating LEASES) that PRE-DATES the GLOBAL shift TOWARD a SINGLE, on-BALANCE-sheet lessee MODEL. THIS represents ONE of the MOST significant STRUCTURAL divergences FROM the CONVERGENCE trend RUNNING throughout the REST of this ENTIRE poster LIBRARY (Section 3).
2

Scope

Agreements conveying the right to use an asset for a period in exchange for payment
This Section applies to LEASE agreements — CONTRACTS conveying the RIGHT to USE a SPECIFIC asset FOR a period OF time in EXCHANGE for PAYMENT — for BOTH the LESSEE and LESSOR sides OF such an ARRANGEMENT.
3

THE CENTRAL DIVERGENCE — Dual Classification, Not a Single On-Balance-Sheet Model

Two categories, with genuinely different accounting outcomes
EVERY lease is CLASSIFIED, at ITS inception, as EITHER a CAPITAL lease OR an OPERATING lease — a CAPITAL lease is RECOGNIZED on the LESSEE'S own BALANCE Sheet (an ASSET and a CORRESPONDING liability); an OPERATING lease is NOT recognized on the BALANCE Sheet AT all, WITH lease PAYMENTS simply EXPENSED as INCURRED. THIS stands IN stark CONTRAST to IFRS 16, ASBE No. 21, and JAPAN'S ASBJ No. 34 (see the COMPANION posters ELSEWHERE in this LIBRARY), ALL of WHICH require LESSEES to RECOGNIZE a right-OF-use asset and LEASE liability for VIRTUALLY all LEASES, REGARDLESS of CLASSIFICATION.
4

Capital Lease Classification Criteria

Transferring substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership
Ownership of the asset transfers to the lessee by the end of the lease termThe lease contains a bargain purchase option the lessee is reasonably certain to exerciseThe lease term is for the major part of the asset's remaining economic lifeThe present value of minimum lease payments amounts to substantially all of the asset's fair value at inception
5

Lessee Accounting for a Capital Lease

An asset and liability at the present value of minimum lease payments
A LESSEE recognizes a CAPITAL lease as BOTH an ASSET (RECORDED at the LOWER of the ASSET'S fair VALUE and the PRESENT value OF minimum LEASE payments) and a CORRESPONDING lease OBLIGATION — the ASSET is SUBSEQUENTLY depreciated (PER Section 3061'S own PRINCIPLES), and the LEASE obligation is REDUCED as PAYMENTS are MADE, WITH an IMPUTED interest COMPONENT recognized AS an EXPENSE.
6

Lessee Accounting for an Operating Lease

Simply expensed, with no balance sheet recognition
A LESSEE under an OPERATING lease SIMPLY recognizes LEASE payments as an EXPENSE, GENERALLY on a straight-LINE basis OVER the LEASE term — WITH NO asset OR liability RECOGNIZED on the BALANCE Sheet WHATSOEVER, REGARDLESS of the LEASE'S total COMMITMENT or DURATION.
7

Lessor Accounting — a Parallel Dual Classification

Sales-type, direct financing, or operating leases
A LESSOR similarly CLASSIFIES each LEASE — as a SALES-type lease (WHERE the LESSOR is EFFECTIVELY the MANUFACTURER or DEALER, recognizing a PROFIT at LEASE commencement), a DIRECT financing LEASE (WHERE the LESSOR is EFFECTIVELY financing the ASSET'S acquisition, WITHOUT a manufacturer'S PROFIT), or an OPERATING lease (RETAINING the ASSET on ITS OWN balance SHEET, recognizing LEASE income OVER the lease TERM).
8

Why ASPE Retained the Older Model

Cost-benefit considerations for typically simpler private-enterprise leasing arrangements
THE global SHIFT toward a SINGLE on-BALANCE-sheet lessee MODEL (IFRS 16, ASC 842, ASBJ No. 34) was LARGELY motivated BY enhancing COMPARABILITY for PUBLIC capital MARKETS investors, WHO had HISTORICALLY struggled TO compare enterprises USING extensive OFF-balance-sheet OPERATING leases AGAINST those FINANCING similar ASSETS through ON-balance-sheet DEBT. FOR private enterprises, WHOSE primary USERS (LENDERS, tax AUTHORITIES, owner-MANAGERS) OFTEN already ADJUST for OPERATING lease COMMITMENTS through OTHER means (e.g. DIRECT inquiry, NOTE disclosure REVIEW), ASPE'S standard-SETTERS concluded the SUBSTANTIAL implementation COST of adopting a SINGLE model WOULD likely EXCEED its INCREMENTAL benefit TO this NARROWER user BASE — a DELIBERATE, cost-CONSCIOUS choice TO retain the OLDER, more FAMILIAR dual-CLASSIFICATION approach.

Worked Example — Classifying and Accounting for a Capital Lease

On-balance-sheet recognition, given the lease's genuine ownership-like nature
Example: A lease covering substantially all of an asset's economic life
A company leases specialized equipment for 8 years, matching the equipment's full 8-year estimated economic life, with the present value of minimum lease payments equal to $480,000 (the equipment's approximate fair value).
GIVEN the LEASE term COVERS the MAJOR part OF the asset'S economic LIFE, and the PRESENT value OF payments APPROXIMATES fair VALUE, the LEASE is CLASSIFIED as a CAPITAL lease — the COMPANY recognizes a $480,000 ASSET and a CORRESPONDING $480,000 LEASE obligation on ITS Balance SHEET.
10

Worked Example — an Operating Lease, Off Balance Sheet

No balance sheet recognition whatsoever, regardless of total commitment
Example: A short-term office space lease
A company leases office space for 3 years, representing a small fraction of the building's overall economic life, with no ownership transfer, bargain purchase option, or payments approximating the property's fair value. Total lease payments over the term are $360,000.
GIVEN NONE of the CAPITAL lease CRITERIA are MET, the LEASE is CLASSIFIED as an OPERATING lease — the COMPANY simply EXPENSES $360,000 ÷ 3 = $120,000 per YEAR, with NO asset OR liability recognized ON the Balance SHEET at ALL.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Capital lease
A lease transferring substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership, recognized as an asset and liability on the lessee's Balance Sheet.
Operating lease
A lease not meeting the capital lease criteria, with payments simply expensed and no Balance Sheet recognition.
