Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why property held for rental/capital gain is measured differently
The Conceptual Framework permits CURRENT VALUE measurement where it provides more relevant information than historical cost. Property held purely to earn rentals or for capital appreciation generates economic benefits INDEPENDENTLY of the entity's other operations — its FAIR VALUE, not its depreciated cost, is what genuinely reflects the resource's economic potential to a user assessing the entity's investment performance.
HOW IAS 40 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Relevance — fair value changes in investment property are a core measure of the entity's investment performance, unlike owner-occupied property where physical USE, not market value change, drives economic benefit Purpose-driven classification — the SAME physical asset (a building) can be classified completely differently depending on the PURPOSE it is held for — a direct application of the Framework's substance-over-form principle Faithful representation — allowing (but not mandating) fair value gives entities the choice best suited to representing their genuine business model, while still requiring FULL fair value disclosure even under the cost model
IAS 40 is one of the clearest illustrations in IFRS of how PURPOSE, not physical characteristics, drives classification — the identical building can be PPE (IAS 16), investment property (IAS 40), or inventory (IAS 2), purely based on WHY the entity holds it.
Measurement principles applied by IAS 40
IAS 40 offers a genuine ACCOUNTING POLICY CHOICE between two models — a rare degree of flexibility in modern IFRS, applied consistently to ALL investment property.
ModelKey feature
Fair value modelRemeasured to fair value EVERY period; gains/losses through SOPL; NO depreciation charged
Cost modelCost less accumulated depreciation and impairment, per IAS 16's cost model mechanics; fair value STILL disclosed in the notes
Qualitative characteristics in action: mandating fair value DISCLOSURE even under the cost model choice ensures comparability is preserved for users, regardless of which measurement policy an entity elects.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IAS 40 covers
IAS 40 applies to the recognition, measurement, and disclosure of investment property — property (land or a building, or part of a building, or both) held (by the owner, or by a lessee as a right-of-use asset) to earn rentals or for capital appreciation, or both.
EXAMPLES OF INVESTMENT PROPERTY
Land held for long-term capital appreciation, rather than short-term sale in the ordinary course of business Land held for a currently undetermined future use (NOT yet decided whether it will be owner-occupied or sold in the near term) A building owned by the entity (or held under a right-of-use asset) and leased out under one or more operating leases A vacant building held to be leased out under one or more operating leases Property being constructed or developed for future use as investment property
A property interest held by a LESSEE under a lease may be classified as investment property if it meets the definition and the entity uses the fair value model for ALL its investment property.
Key definitions you must know
Investment property
Property held to earn rentals or for capital appreciation, or both, rather than for use in the production/supply of goods or services or for administrative purposes, or sale in the ordinary course of business.
Owner-occupied property
Property held (by the owner or lessee under a right-of-use asset) for use in the production/supply of goods or services or for administrative purposes — governed by IAS 16, NOT IAS 40.
Fair value
The price that would be received to sell an asset in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date, per IFRS 13.

Recognition Criteria & Ancillary Services

The two-condition recognition test, plus the "significant portion" tie-breaker
Investment property is recognised as an asset when, and only when: (a) it is PROBABLE that the future economic benefits associated with it will flow to the entity; AND (b) the cost of the property can be measured RELIABLY.
MIXED-USE JUDGEMENT — ANCILLARY SERVICES
Some properties comprise a portion held to earn rentals/capital appreciation, and another portion held for use in production/administrative purposes. If these portions could be SOLD SEPARATELY (or leased out separately under a finance lease), the entity accounts for them SEPARATELY (part IAS 40, part IAS 16). If NOT separable, the property is classified as investment property ONLY IF the owner-occupied portion is INSIGNIFICANT.
Where an entity provides ANCILLARY SERVICES to occupants (e.g. security, maintenance) and these services are a RELATIVELY INSIGNIFICANT component of the arrangement as a whole, the property is treated as investment property — e.g. an office building owner providing security and maintenance to lessees. If the services provided are SIGNIFICANT (e.g. a hotel managed by the owner, with substantial guest services), the property is treated as OWNER-OCCUPIED (IAS 16), not investment property.

Initial Measurement (with worked example)

At cost, including transaction costs
Initial cost = Purchase price + directly attributable transaction costs (e.g. legal fees, property transfer taxes, other transaction costs)
This mirrors IAS 16's initial cost recognition principles closely — the DIVERGENCE between the two standards begins at SUBSEQUENT measurement, not initial recognition.
Worked example — initial recognition
Example 1: Purchasing a rental property
An entity purchases an office building to lease out under operating leases, for $2,000,000. Legal fees of $40,000 and a property transfer tax of $60,000 are also incurred.
Initial cost recognised = 2,000,000 + 40,000 + 60,000 = $2,100,000

The Fair Value Model (with worked example)

Gains and losses always through SOPL — never OCI
Under the fair value model, investment property is REMEASURED to fair value at EVERY reporting date, with gains/losses recognised in profit or loss for the period. NO depreciation is charged — the fair value movement itself already captures any decline (or increase) in value that depreciation would otherwise attempt to approximate.
Example 2: Fair value gain recognised
The office building from Example 1, initially recognised at $2,100,000, has a fair value of $2,350,000 at the following year end, based on an independent valuation.
Fair value gain = 2,350,000 − 2,100,000 = $250,000, recognised IN FULL through profit or loss

A frequently tested contrast with IAS 16's revaluation model: under IAS 16, an upward revaluation typically goes to OCI (revaluation surplus); under IAS 40's fair value model, ALL gains and losses go DIRECTLY through profit or loss, with no OCI route at all.
6

The Cost Model

Applying IAS 16's cost model mechanics
Carrying amount = Cost − Accumulated depreciation − Accumulated impairment losses
Depreciated over the property's useful life exactly as PPE would be under IAS 16, with impairment testing under IAS 36 as normal.
Mandatory fair value disclosure — even under the cost model
Fair value MUST still be disclosed in the notes, even if the cost model is chosen for measurement purposes — this is a mandatory disclosure requirement REGARDLESS of the elected measurement model, ensuring users always have access to the current market value information, whichever policy the entity applies to the primary statements.

Transfers To/From Investment Property (with worked example)

Transfers triggered ONLY by a genuine change of use, evidenced by specific events
Transfers to, or from, investment property are made when, and only when, there is a CHANGE IN USE, evidenced by: commencement of owner-occupation (transfer from investment property to owner-occupied); commencement of development with a view to sale (transfer to inventory); end of owner-occupation (transfer to investment property); or commencement of an operating lease to another party (transfer to investment property).
Example 3: Transfer from investment property to owner-occupied (fair value model)
A property carried under the fair value model at $1,800,000 (fair value at the transfer date) is transferred to owner-occupied use, as the entity begins using it for its own head office.
The property's fair value at the DATE OF TRANSFER ($1,800,000) becomes its DEEMED COST for subsequent accounting under IAS 16 — any fair value gain/loss UP TO the transfer date is recognised in profit or loss as normal under the fair value model, and the property is then depreciated under IAS 16 from that new deemed-cost basis going forward.
This "fair value becomes deemed cost" mechanic applies symmetrically to ALL transfers into or out of investment property measured under the fair value model — the transfer itself is always at fair value, with any gain/loss recognised in the SAME manner as an ordinary fair value remeasurement.
8

Disposals & Mixed-Use Property Considerations

Derecognition
An investment property is DERECOGNISED on disposal, or when it is permanently withdrawn from use and no future economic benefits are expected from its disposal. Gains/losses on disposal are determined as the difference between the NET DISPOSAL PROCEEDS and the CARRYING AMOUNT, recognised in profit or loss.
Property under construction/development
Investment property BEING CONSTRUCTED or developed for future use as investment property IS within IAS 40's scope, and (if the fair value model is used) is measured at fair value where this can be reliably determined — if fair value cannot yet be reliably measured for a property under construction, it is measured at COST until either fair value becomes reliably determinable or construction is complete.

Disclosure Requirements

Required disclosures
GENERAL DISCLOSURES
Whether the fair value model or cost model is used Criteria used to distinguish investment property from owner-occupied property and property held for sale, where classification is difficult Rental income and direct operating expenses (including repairs/maintenance) arising from investment property during the period
MODEL-SPECIFIC DISCLOSURES
Fair value model: a reconciliation of the carrying amount at the beginning/end of the period (additions, disposals, fair value gains/losses, transfers) Cost model: depreciation methods, useful lives/rates, gross carrying amount and accumulated depreciation, and — critically — the FAIR VALUE of investment property, even though it is not used for measurement

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Classifying a property as investment property vs owner-occupied vs inventory, especially for mixed-use buildings and where ancillary services are provided Whether ancillary services provided to occupants are "insignificant" (investment property) or "significant" (owner-occupied) Identifying the specific triggering event for a transfer to/from investment property
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Fair value of investment property, whether used for measurement (fair value model) or disclosure only (cost model) Useful life and depreciation method under the cost model
Once an entity elects the fair value model for its investment property, that choice must be applied CONSISTENTLY to ALL its investment property, with an extremely limited exception for property under construction whose fair value cannot yet be reliably determined — switching between models thereafter is expected to be rare.

IFRS vs US GAAP — Key Differences

Comparison — IAS 40 vs US GAAP
FeatureIFRS (IAS 40)US GAAP
Dedicated standard for investment propertyYes — IAS 40 is a standalone standard with a genuine fair value model electionNO equivalent standalone standard — investment property is generally accounted for under the SAME historical cost/depreciation model as other real estate (no fair value option)
Fair value gains/lossesRecognised through SOPL (fair value model)Not applicable, since fair value remeasurement is not generally used for held real estate under US GAAP
Classification conceptExplicit "investment property" category distinct from owner-occupied PPENo distinct classification category — real estate is generally accounted for uniformly, regardless of purpose held
This is one of the STARKEST IFRS/US GAAP divergences of any standard in this library — IAS 40's entire fair-value-through-profit-or-loss investment property model has NO direct US GAAP counterpart, since US GAAP does not generally permit fair value remeasurement of held real estate at all, making comparability adjustments significant for real estate-heavy entities reporting under both frameworks.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Owner-occupied property
Property held for use in the production/supply of goods or services, or for administrative purposes — accounted for under IAS 16, not IAS 40.
Ancillary services
Services provided by the property owner to occupants (e.g. security, maintenance) — if insignificant relative to the arrangement, the property remains investment property; if significant, it is treated as owner-occupied.
Deemed cost
The fair value of a property AT THE DATE OF TRANSFER to/from investment property, used as its new "cost" basis under the receiving standard (IAS 16 or IAS 2) going forward.
Right-of-use asset
A lessee's asset representing its right to use an underlying leased asset (IFRS 16) — may itself qualify as investment property if held to earn rentals/capital appreciation and measured at fair value.
Fair value model
The IAS 40 measurement policy under which investment property is remeasured to fair value each period, with gains/losses recognised in profit or loss.
Cost model
The alternative IAS 40 measurement policy, following IAS 16's cost-less-depreciation-less-impairment mechanics, with mandatory fair value disclosure in the notes.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position (balance sheet) SOPL Statement of profit or loss (income statement) OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
