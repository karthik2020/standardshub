Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why a debt-for-equity swap is not a "non-event"
A distressed debtor issuing shares to a creditor in place of cash might appear to just move an amount from one part of equity/liabilities to another. IFRIC 19 rejects that framing: the entity is SETTLING a liability using consideration whose value may differ substantially from the liability's carrying amount, and the Conceptual Framework requires that genuine economic difference to be recognised, not buried.
Fair value of what is actually given up
Consistent with derecognition principles elsewhere in IFRS, the consideration given to extinguish a liability should be measured at FAIR VALUE — here, the fair value of the equity instruments issued — so that the resulting gain or loss faithfully represents the economic substance of the restructuring, rather than an arbitrary carrying-value roll-forward.
2

Scope & Applicability

What IFRIC 19 covers
IFRIC 19 applies where the terms of a financial liability are RENEGOTIATED, resulting in the entity issuing equity instruments to a creditor to extinguish all or part of the liability. It does NOT apply to transactions where the creditor is also a shareholder acting in that capacity, nor where the same parties are under common control, nor to extinguishments that occur upon the liability's original contractual terms (e.g. a convertible bond converted per its original terms).
TYPICAL FACT PATTERNS
Distressed debt restructuring where creditors accept newly issued shares instead of cash repaymentRenegotiation of loan terms during a financial difficulty, resulting in a debt-for-equity swap
Key definitions
Equity instruments issued to extinguish a liability
Shares or other equity instruments issued by the debtor entity, given to a creditor in settlement of some or all of a financial liability, per the renegotiated terms.
Extinguishment
The point at which a financial liability is discharged, cancelled, or expires, resulting in its derecognition under IFRS 9.
3

Does an Extinguishment Even Occur?

The threshold question
IFRIC 19 first confirms that issuing equity instruments to a creditor to extinguish a financial liability IS consideration paid, within the scope of IFRS 9's derecognition requirements — i.e. the equity instruments issued ARE "consideration paid" in the same sense as a cash payment would be, triggering derecognition of the liability to the extent it is extinguished.
4

Measurement — Fair Value of Equity Issued

The default measurement basis
Equity instruments issued to a creditor to extinguish a financial liability are measured at the FAIR VALUE OF THE EQUITY INSTRUMENTS ISSUED, unless that fair value cannot be reliably measured (see Section 5). The DIFFERENCE between the carrying amount of the financial liability extinguished and the fair value of equity instruments issued is recognised in PROFIT OR LOSS.
Worked example — basic debt-for-equity swap
Example: Straightforward extinguishment
A creditor agrees to accept new shares from a debtor in full settlement of a $4,000,000 loan. The fair value of the shares issued at settlement date is $3,200,000.
Entry: Debit Loan payable $4,000,000 / Credit Share capital (fair value) $3,200,000 / Credit Gain on extinguishment (profit or loss) $800,000.
5

When Fair Value of the Equity Cannot Be Measured Reliably

The fallback measurement basis
If the fair value of the equity instruments issued cannot be reliably measured — a rare circumstance, given equity issued in a genuine restructuring is usually measurable — the equity instruments are instead measured at the FAIR VALUE OF THE FINANCIAL LIABILITY EXTINGUISHED, as at the date the liability is extinguished. In that case, by construction, no gain or loss arises from the measurement of the equity itself (though any difference between the liability's fair value and its carrying amount is still recognised in profit or loss).
6

Partial Extinguishment

Allocating consideration between extinguishment and modification
Where equity instruments are issued to extinguish only PART of a financial liability, the entity assesses whether some of the consideration given also relates to a MODIFICATION of the terms of the remaining liability. If so, the consideration is ALLOCATED between the portion relating to the part extinguished and the portion relating to the modification of terms on the remaining liability, applying judgement where the two are not clearly separable in the renegotiated agreement.
7

Worked Example — Debt-for-Equity Swap

Fair value of liability used as fallback
Example: Equity fair value not reliably measurable
A private company in financial distress issues new preference shares to a creditor to settle a $1,500,000 liability. Because the company is closely held and thinly traded, the fair value of the newly issued shares cannot be reliably estimated. The fair value of the extinguished liability, however, is reliably estimated at $1,100,000 (reflecting the distressed pricing a similar liability would trade at).
The equity instruments are measured at $1,100,000 — the fair value of the LIABILITY extinguished. Entry: Debit Loan payable $1,500,000 / Credit Share capital $1,100,000 / Credit Gain on extinguishment (profit or loss) $400,000.
8

Distinguishing Extinguishment from Modification

Why the distinction matters
A pure MODIFICATION of a financial liability's terms (with no equity issued) is accounted for under IFRS 9's own modification/extinguishment guidance — typically based on whether the change in present value of cash flows exceeds a 10% threshold. IFRIC 19 applies specifically where EQUITY is issued as consideration; the two frameworks interact where equity is issued alongside a broader renegotiation that also changes the remaining debt's terms, requiring the allocation discussed in Section 6.

IFRIC 19 vs US GAAP

Comparison — IFRIC 19 vs US GAAP (ASC 470-60 / ASC 405-20)
FeatureIFRS (IFRIC 19)US GAAP
Measurement basis for equity issuedFair value of equity issued (primary), fair value of liability extinguished (fallback)Also generally fair value of equity issued, provided it is reliably determinable — broadly converged in principle
Gain/loss recognitionDifference between liability carrying amount and equity fair value, recognised in profit or lossSimilarly recognised as a gain/loss on debt extinguishment, generally in earnings
Troubled debt restructuring specificsNo separate "troubled debt" category — IFRIC 19 applies uniformlyASC 470-60 has a distinct troubled debt restructuring framework with some different mechanics for certain restructurings involving continuing debt
The core measurement conclusion is well aligned between frameworks; the more notable US GAAP feature is its separate troubled-debt-restructuring guidance, which does not have a directly equivalent standalone category under IFRS.

Quick-reference glossary
Debt-for-equity swap
A restructuring in which a creditor agrees to accept equity instruments of the debtor in place of cash repayment of a financial liability, wholly or in part.
Troubled debt restructuring
A renegotiation of debt terms granted by a creditor to a debtor experiencing financial difficulty — the typical context in which IFRIC 19 fact patterns arise.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Consideration paid
Under IFRS 9's derecognition model, the amount given by the debtor to extinguish a liability — IFRIC 19 confirms newly issued equity instruments qualify as consideration paid.
Reliable measurement
A threshold assessment of whether an entity can determine fair value with sufficient confidence — where it cannot for the equity issued, IFRIC 19 requires falling back to the fair value of the liability extinguished instead.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOPL Statement of profit or loss OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
