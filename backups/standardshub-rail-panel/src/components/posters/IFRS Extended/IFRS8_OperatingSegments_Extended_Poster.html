Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why segment reporting uses "the management approach"
The Conceptual Framework values information that helps users understand a business the way MANAGEMENT itself does. IFRS 8 adopts this "MANAGEMENT APPROACH" directly: segments are defined by how the entity is ACTUALLY organised and managed internally, not by an externally imposed classification scheme.
HOW IFRS 8 MAPS TO THE FRAMEWORK
Relevance — reporting segments the way management ACTUALLY monitors them gives users the SAME lens management uses to run the business Faithful representation — externally imposed, standardised segment categories risk distorting how the business is genuinely structured and managed Cost constraint — segment information is DERIVED from data management ALREADY produces internally
IFRS 8 replaced IAS 14's earlier "business/geographical segment" model specifically to align segment reporting more closely with how entities are ACTUALLY managed.
Measurement principles applied by IFRS 8
Unlike most IFRS Standards, IFRS 8 does NOT prescribe how segment items are measured — it requires reporting the SAME measures management ITSELF uses internally, even if those measures differ from IFRS.
Framework conceptIFRS 8's application
Measurement basisWHATEVER basis management uses internally — may differ from IFRS
Reconciliation requirementMandatory reconciliation of segment totals to consolidated IFRS totals
ℹ️ Permitting NON-IFRS internal measures, but requiring a mandatory reconciliation, balances relevance against comparability.

Scope & Applicability

What IFRS 8 covers
IFRS 8 applies to the SEPARATE or INDIVIDUAL financial statements of an entity, and the CONSOLIDATED financial statements of a group with a parent, whose DEBT or EQUITY instruments are traded in a public market (or that FILES its financial statements with a regulatory organisation for the purpose of issuing such instruments).
Unlike most IFRS Standards, IFRS 8's scope is EXPLICITLY tied to PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY — private entities without publicly traded instruments are generally NOT required to apply IFRS 8.
Key definitions you must know
Operating segment
A component engaging in business activities generating revenues/expenses, whose results are REGULARLY REVIEWED by the CODM, with DISCRETE financial information available.
Chief operating decision maker (CODM)
A FUNCTION, not necessarily a job title — allocates resources to, and assesses performance of, operating segments.
Reportable segment
An operating segment (or aggregation) meeting one of the quantitative thresholds, or voluntarily reported.
3

Identifying Operating Segments (with worked example)

The three-part definition, applied
A component is an operating segment ONLY if it meets ALL THREE conditions: (1) engages in business activities generating revenue/expenses; (2) results REGULARLY reviewed by the CODM; and (3) DISCRETE financial information is available.
Example 1: Identifying operating segments via internal reporting
A diversified group's CEO (the CODM) receives monthly reports broken into three divisions: Electronics, Textiles, and Logistics — each with discrete revenue/expense/profit figures. A fourth cost centre, "Corporate HQ," generates no revenue and is not reviewed for resource allocation.
Electronics, Textiles, and Logistics EACH qualify as operating segments. Corporate HQ does NOT (no revenue-generating activity, not assessed for resource allocation).

Aggregation of Segments

Combining similar segments — a strict five-factor test
Two or more operating segments MAY be aggregated if consistent with IFRS 8's core principles, with SIMILAR ECONOMIC CHARACTERISTICS, and similar in EACH respect:
Nature of products and services Nature of the production processes Type or class of customer Methods used to distribute products/services Nature of the regulatory environment, if applicable
Aggregation is genuine JUDGEMENT, not mechanical calculation.
5

The Quantitative Thresholds — 10% Tests (with worked example)

Reportable if it meets ANY ONE of three 10% tests
TestThreshold
Revenue testReported revenue (external + intersegment) is 10%+ of COMBINED revenue of all segments
Profit or loss testAbsolute profit/loss is 10%+ of the GREATER of (a) combined profit of profit-making segments, (b) combined loss of loss-making segments
Assets testSegment assets are 10%+ of combined assets of all segments
Example 2: Applying the profit/loss 10% test
4 segments: A ($800k profit), B ($600k profit) → combined profits $1,400,000. C ($300k loss), D ($900k loss) → combined losses $1,200,000.
Benchmark = GREATER of $1,400,000 and $1,200,000 = $1,400,000. 10% threshold = $140,000. Segment D's $900,000 loss exceeds this → REPORTABLE.
6

The 75% Reporting Sufficiency Test (with worked example)

A backstop ensuring adequate overall coverage
If TOTAL EXTERNAL REVENUE reported by operating segments is LESS THAN 75% of total consolidated external revenue, ADDITIONAL segments must be added as reportable until 75% is reached.
Example 3: Applying the 75% test
Total external revenue $50,000,000. Segments meeting 10% thresholds account for $34,000,000 (68%).
68% is below 75% — additional segments must be added until at least $37,500,000 (75%) is captured.
Segments not separately reportable are combined in "ALL OTHER SEGMENTS," with revenue sources described.
7

Measurement of Segment Information

Reported "the way management sees it" — including non-IFRS figures
Each segment item is measured as REPORTED TO THE CODM — this can be a NON-IFRS measure, provided the basis and any differences from consolidated totals are EXPLAINED.
⚠️ Segment figures may NOT be directly comparable ACROSS different entities, even in the same industry.
8

Reconciliations to Consolidated Totals

Bridging segment figures back to the consolidated financial statements
Reconciliations required: total segment revenues to entity revenue; total segment profit/loss to entity profit/loss before tax; total segment assets/liabilities to entity totals; and other material items.
These mandatory reconciliations make the management approach work alongside comparability.
9

Entity-Wide Disclosures

Required regardless of segment structure — a final backstop
PRODUCTS/SERVICES & GEOGRAPHY
Revenue from external customers by product/service, even if segments organised differently Revenue attributed to country of domicile vs all foreign countries in total Non-current assets located in domicile vs foreign countries in total
MAJOR CUSTOMERS
If revenue from a SINGLE customer is 10%+ of total revenue, disclose that fact, the amount, and the segment(s) involved
⚠️ These apply EVEN IF the entity has only ONE reportable segment.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where management judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Identifying the CODM function correctly in complex structures Whether operating segments meet the five-factor aggregation criteria
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Allocating shared/corporate costs between segments Estimating revenue attributable to a customer's parent for concentration disclosure
⚠️ Changes to internal reporting structures can trigger CHANGES in reportable segments year over year.

Defining segments based on internal management structure, not an external classification.
All other segments
Residual category combining non-reportable operating segments.
Intersegment transactions
Transactions between segments within the same entity, included in the 10% revenue test.
