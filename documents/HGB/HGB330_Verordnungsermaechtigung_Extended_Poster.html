HGB §330 — Verordnungsermächtigung (Regulatory Authorization)
Extended reference poster · Book 3, Section 2, Subsection 5 (entire subsection — a single provision) · The delegation power behind specialised statement formats · German commercial law
A DELIBERATELY NARROW POSTER: §330 is a single, purely enabling provision — it does not itself prescribe any substantive accounting rule. It authorises the federal government to issue detailed regulations (Rechtsverordnungen) filling in technical specifics this Book has left to delegated rulemaking, particularly for specialised industries. Given its narrow legal text, this poster is scaled accordingly rather than padded to match the anchor posters.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

A single hinge connecting primary law to detailed, specialised technical rules
Nearly everything this poster series has covered lives directly in HGB's own statutory text. §330 is different in kind: it is the LEGAL HINGE authorising the federal government to issue separate, delegated regulations (Rechtsverordnungen) — most notably prescribing detailed Formblätter (standard forms) for the Bilanz and GuV of certain specialised company types, particularly where Book 3's own general presentation rules don't naturally fit an industry's genuine operational reality.
HOW §330 MAPS TO GERMAN ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES
Klarer Einblick, for specialised industries — the SAME underlying transparency objective this whole series has documented, achieved here through TAILORED, industry-specific presentation formats rather than a one-size-fits-all approach Legislative efficiency — highly technical, frequently-updated presentation details are better suited to delegated regulation than to primary statute, which requires the full parliamentary process to amend
What §330 does, at a glance
§330 authorises the competent federal ministry to issue regulations PRESCRIBING detailed standard forms and further supplementary technical rules for the Bilanz and GuV of certain Kapitalgesellschaften — most notably those in specialised sectors (credit institutions, insurance undertakings, and comparable industries) whose operational realities the GENERAL §266/§275 formats established earlier in this series don't naturally accommodate.

Scope

Primarily specialised-industry Kapitalgesellschaften
§330's practical significance concentrates on Kapitalgesellschaften in specialised sectors — credit institutions, insurance undertakings, and comparable industries covered by Book 3, Section 4's own further supplementary provisions — whose genuine business structure the GENERAL Bilanz/GuV formats this series has documented in detail were never designed to accommodate cleanly.
3

What §330 Actually Does — a Delegation, Not a Rule

Empowering, not itself prescribing
§330 does not itself set out a Formblatt, a measurement rule, or a disclosure requirement — it EMPOWERS the responsible federal authority to do so through separate, delegated regulations. This is a genuinely different KIND of provision than nearly everything else this poster series has covered: rather than a substantive rule readers apply directly, §330 is the constitutional-style hinge explaining WHERE certain specialised technical detail actually comes from, even though it doesn't appear in HGB's own text.
4

Why Delegate at All — the Practical Case for Regulatory Flexibility

Technical detail that changes faster than primary legislation reasonably can
Highly specific, industry-tailored presentation formats — particularly for credit institutions and insurers, whose regulatory and operational environment evolves considerably faster than general commercial law — are genuinely better suited to DELEGATED regulation than to primary statute. Amending a Rechtsverordnung is procedurally faster and more technically flexible than amending HGB itself through the full parliamentary legislative process, allowing specialised presentation rules to be kept reasonably current without requiring Book 3's own core text to be constantly revised.

Worked Example — Where a Formblatt Regulation Would Matter

A specialised balance sheet structure the general format was never built for
Example 1: A credit institution's balance sheet structure
A licensed credit institution's balance sheet is dominated by items — customer deposits, interbank lending, loan portfolios, trading securities — that the GENERAL §266 asset/liability categories established earlier in this series (Anlagevermögen/Umlaufvermögen, ordinary Verbindlichkeiten) describe only awkwardly, if at all.
Rather than forcing the credit institution into the GENERAL Kapitalgesellschaft format, a Formblatt regulation issued under the §330 authorisation prescribes a SPECIALISED, industry-tailored Bilanz and GuV structure specifically fitted to a bank's own genuine balance sheet composition — the practical mechanism §330 exists to enable, even though the SPECIFIC form itself lives in a separate regulation rather than in HGB's own statutory text.

Critical Judgments & Practical Difficulties

Where judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether a specific industry's presentation needs genuinely warrant a specialised Formblatt, versus simply applying the general format with the flexibility §265(4) already provides
PRACTICAL DIFFICULTIES
Locating the actual, current delegated regulation itself — since it exists SEPARATELY from HGB's own statutory text, a practitioner working only from HGB directly would not find the specific Formblatt content there at all
7

Relationship to IFRS

A structural difference in how specialised presentation gets handled
IFRS has no direct equivalent delegation mechanism of this kind — where a specialised industry's presentation needs diverge from IAS 1's general minimum-line-item approach, IFRS typically addresses this through INDUSTRY-SPECIFIC STANDARDS (e.g. IFRS 17 for insurance contracts) issued by the IASB itself through its own standard-setting process, rather than through a separate, government-delegated regulatory mechanism sitting outside the core standards altogether. Both approaches solve the SAME underlying problem — general formats don't fit every industry — through genuinely different institutional architectures.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Verordnungsermächtigung
Regulatory authorization — a statutory provision empowering the executive to issue delegated regulations on a specific technical topic.
Rechtsverordnung
A delegated regulation issued by the executive under statutory authorization, without requiring the full parliamentary legislative process.
Formblatt
Standard form — a prescribed, industry-tailored Bilanz/GuV presentation format issued under a §330 regulation.
Ermächtigungsnorm
An enabling provision — the general legal category §330 belongs to, granting delegated rulemaking power rather than stating a substantive rule itself.
