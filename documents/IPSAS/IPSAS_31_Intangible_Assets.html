Objective, Scope & Intangible Heritage Assets (paras. 1–15)

Objective & scope
Prescribes the accounting treatment for intangible assets not dealt with specifically by another Standard — requiring recognition only when specified criteria are met, prescribing measurement, and requiring specified disclosures.
Financial assets (IPSAS 28) Exploration/evaluation assets (IPSAS 50); mineral/oil/gas development and extraction expenditure Powers and rights conferred by legislation, a constitution, or equivalent means Deferred tax assets; insurance-contract deferred acquisition costs and intangibles Inventories held for sale (IPSAS 12); leased intangibles (IPSAS 43); employee benefit assets (IPSAS 39) Goodwill (IPSAS 40); held-for-sale intangibles (IPSAS 44); binding-arrangement assets (IPSAS 47) Initial recognition/measurement of service concession assets (IPSAS 32) — though subsequent measurement/disclosure of those assets IS within scope
Tangible vs intangible judgment, and intangible heritage assets
Where an asset has both physical AND intangible elements (e.g. fighter-jet navigation software), judgment determines which element is more significant — integral software is PP&E; standalone software is intangible (para. 7).
Intangible HERITAGE assets — cultural, environmental, or historical significance (e.g. historical event recordings, rights to a public figure's likeness on stamps/coins) Value often not fully reflected in market price; may face legal/statutory disposal restrictions; value may increase over time; useful life can be very hard to estimate (para. 12)
Where an intangible heritage asset has economic benefits BEYOND its heritage value (e.g. royalties from a historical recording), it may be recognized/measured like a cash-generating intangible; otherwise its future benefit is limited to heritage characteristics (para. 14).

Definitions & the Three-Part Intangible Asset Test (paras. 16–27)

The definition, and its three components
Intangible asset = an IDENTIFIABLE, NONMONETARY asset WITHOUT PHYSICAL SUBSTANCE
Identifiability — separable (can be sold/transferred/licensed/rented/exchanged, alone or with a related item) OR arises from binding arrangements, regardless of transferability Control — power to obtain the future benefits AND restrict others' access, normally via enforceable legal rights (though legal enforceability isn't strictly required) Future economic benefits or service potential — revenue, cost savings, or other benefits from use
Where control usually fails
A skilled staff team, or training-derived skills — usually insufficient control (staff can leave) Specific management/technical talent — unlikely to qualify unless legally protected Portfolios of service users, market share, or user loyalty — usually insufficient control, UNLESS an arm's-length exchange transaction for similar non-contractual relationships provides evidence of both control AND separability
An item failing the intangible-asset definition is expensed as incurred — UNLESS acquired in a public sector combination, in which case it forms part of goodwill (para. 18).

Recognition & Separate Acquisition (paras. 28–39)

The two-part recognition test (para. 28)
PROBABLE that expected future economic benefits/service potential attributable to the asset will flow to the entity Cost or fair value can be measured RELIABLY
Initial measurement is at COST — or, for non-exchange acquisitions, FAIR VALUE at the acquisition date (para. 31). For separately acquired intangibles, the probability criterion is ALWAYS considered satisfied — the price paid inherently reflects benefit-flow expectations (para. 32).
Cost of a separately acquired intangible (paras. 34–38)
INCLUDES: purchase price (net of discounts/rebates, plus import duties/non-refundable taxes), and directly attributable preparation costs (testing, professional fees, directly-related employee benefits) EXCLUDES: new-product introduction costs, new-location/new-user-class costs (incl. staff training), and general administration/overhead Cost capitalization STOPS once the asset is capable of operating as management intends — subsequent use/redeployment costs, and initial operating deficits, are NOT capitalized

Acquisition as Part of a Public Sector Combination & via Non-Exchange Transactions (paras. 39A–47)

Public sector combinations (IPSAS 40 interaction)
Cost = fair value at the acquisition date; both recognition criteria are ALWAYS deemed satisfied
The acquirer recognizes an intangible SEPARATELY from goodwill, whether or not the acquired operation had recognized it — INCLUDING an in-process R&D project if it's identifiable (separable or arising from binding arrangements) (paras. 39A–39B).
Non-exchange acquisitions & exchanges of assets
Intangible acquired through a NON-EXCHANGE transaction → initial cost = FAIR VALUE at the date of acquisition (para. 31, 42–43) Exchange of assets → measured at fair value UNLESS the exchange lacks commercial substance, or neither asset's fair value is reliably measurable
✕ Internally generated goodwill is NEVER recognized as an asset — it isn't an identifiable resource controlled by the entity that can be measured reliably at cost (para. 48-adjacent guidance).

Internally Generated Intangible Assets — Research vs Development Phase (paras. 49–62)

Research phase — NEVER capitalized
✕ No intangible asset arising from research (or a project's research phase) is EVER recognized — expensed as incurred (para. 52).
Examples: obtaining new knowledge; searching for/evaluating applications of research findings; searching for material/product/process alternatives; formulating and selecting design alternatives (para. 54). If research can't be distinguished from development, treat the WHOLE project as research (para. 51).
Development phase — capitalized if ALL SIX criteria are met (para. 55)
TECHNICAL FEASIBILITY of completing the asset for use or sale INTENTION to complete and use or sell it ABILITY to use or sell it HOW it will generate probable future benefits — demonstrable market or internal usefulness AVAILABILITY of adequate technical, financial, and other resources to complete development ABILITY to measure reliably the expenditure attributable to the asset
Internally generated BRANDS, mastheads, publishing titles, and service-user lists are NEVER recognized — indistinguishable from the cost of developing the business as a whole (para. 61).
6

Cost of an Internally Generated Intangible Asset (paras. 63–70)

What counts, from WHEN
Cost = sum of expenditure incurred from the date the asset FIRST meets ALL recognition criteria — never reinstated retroactively
INCLUDES: materials/services consumed, directly attributable employee benefits, legal-right registration fees, amortization of patents/licenses used to generate the asset EXCLUDES: selling/admin/overhead (unless directly attributable), inefficiencies and initial operating deficits, staff training costs
The one-way door — no retroactive capitalization
✕ Once expensed, always expensed: expenditure initially recognized as an expense is NEVER later reinstated as part of an intangible asset's cost (para. 70) — even if the project subsequently meets the recognition criteria. Only expenditure incurred AFTER the criteria are first met is capitalized.

Subsequent Measurement — Historical Cost & Current Value Models (paras. 71–86)

One policy per class, consistently applied
Historical cost model: cost LESS accumulated amortization and impairment
Current value model: fair value at revaluation date LESS subsequent accumulated amortization and impairment
If current value is chosen, ALL other assets in that CLASS must also use it, unless no active market exists for them (para. 71). Fair value under this model is measured by reference to an ACTIVE MARKET — genuinely rare for intangibles (brands, mastheads, patents are typically unique, so no active market exists) (paras. 74, 77).
Revaluation gains/losses — mirrors IPSAS 45's PP&E model
Carrying amount INCREASE → revaluation surplus (net assets/equity) directly, UNLESS reversing a prior decrease on the SAME asset previously in surplus/deficit — that portion goes to surplus/deficit Carrying amount DECREASE → surplus or deficit, UNLESS a credit balance exists in that asset's revaluation surplus — offset there first Cumulative revaluation surplus may transfer to accumulated surpluses/deficits when realized (on disposal, or progressively as used) — NEVER through surplus or deficit itself (para. 86)
If an active market CEASES to exist for a revalued asset, its carrying amount FREEZES at the last active-market-referenced revalued amount (less subsequent amortization/impairment) — and the disappearance of that market may itself be an impairment indicator (paras. 81–82).

Useful Life — Finite vs Indefinite (paras. 87–95A)

The indefinite test — "no foreseeable limit"
INDEFINITE useful life = no foreseeable limit to the period the asset is expected to generate net cash inflows or provide service potential
⚠️ "Indefinite" does NOT mean "infinite." An indefinite conclusion should reflect ONLY the maintenance spend needed to sustain current performance — never planned FUTURE expenditure in excess of that level (para. 90).
Binding-arrangement-derived useful life — capped at the arrangement's term
Useful life shall NOT exceed the binding arrangement's period — though it MAY be shorter, depending on expected use (para. 93) RENEWAL periods are included only with evidence of renewal WITHOUT SIGNIFICANT COST — evidence of past renewal, satisfaction of conditions, and renewal cost being insignificant relative to expected benefits (para. 95) A license/right granted between combining operations, later recognized in an amalgamation — or a reacquired right recognized in an acquisition — uses the REMAINING period only, EXCLUDING renewal periods (para. 93A)
Factors considered: expected usage, product life cycles, obsolescence, industry stability, competitor actions, required maintenance spend, legal/similar limits, and dependency on other assets' useful lives (para. 89).

Amortization, Impairment & Retirements/Disposals (paras. 96–116)

Amortization mechanics — finite-life assets only
Begins when the asset is AVAILABLE FOR USE; ceases at the earlier of held-for-sale classification (IPSAS 44) or derecognition Method reflects the expected CONSUMPTION pattern of benefits — straight-line if that pattern can't be reliably determined REBUTTABLE PRESUMPTION that revenue-based amortization is inappropriate — revenue reflects too many unrelated factors (price, volume, inflation). Overcome only where the asset IS expressed as a revenue measure (e.g. a toll-road right capped at CU100m cumulative tolls), or benefit consumption is demonstrably highly correlated with revenue (paras. 97A–97C)
RESIDUAL VALUE is assumed ZERO unless a third party is committed to purchase the asset at end-of-life, or a reliable active market is expected to exist then (para. 99). Both amortization period/method AND residual value are reviewed AT LEAST each reporting date, as accounting ESTIMATE changes under IPSAS 3 (paras. 101, 103).
Indefinite-life assets, impairment, and derecognition
Indefinite-life assets are NEVER amortized — but tested for impairment ANNUALLY, and whenever an indication arises (para. 106–107, cross-ref IPSAS 21/26) Useful life is reassessed EVERY period — a shift from indefinite to FINITE is itself an impairment indicator (paras. 108–109) Derecognition: on disposal, OR when no further benefits are expected from use or disposal — gain/loss = net disposal proceeds LESS carrying amount, to surplus or deficit (paras. 111–112)
Amortization does NOT stop merely because an asset is no longer actively used — only on full depreciation or held-for-sale classification (para. 116).

Disclosure Requirements (paras. 117–127)

General — per class, split internally-generated vs other (para. 117)
Whether useful lives are indefinite or finite (and if finite, the lives/amortization rates) Amortization methods used Gross carrying amount and accumulated amortization (+ impairment) at period start/end SOFPerformance line item(s) containing amortization A full RECONCILIATION — additions (internal/separate/combination), disposals, revaluation changes, impairment losses/reversals, amortization, exchange differences, other changes
Also required: indefinite-life carrying amount + supporting reasons; description/carrying amount/remaining amortization period of any INDIVIDUALLY MATERIAL asset; restricted-title assets and pledged-as-security amounts; acquisition commitments (paras. 121, 121(d)-(e)).
Current value model, R&D, and encouraged extras
Revaluation-specific: effective date, revalued carrying amount vs. the historical-cost-model equivalent, and the revaluation surplus movement with any distribution restrictions (para. 123) Fair value hierarchy package mirroring IPSAS 12/16/27: Level 1/2/3 classification, techniques/inputs, and Level 3 reconciliations (paras. 123A–123C) AGGREGATE research and development expenditure expensed during the period (para. 125)
✓ ENCOURAGED (not required): a description of fully-amortized assets STILL in use, and significant CONTROLLED-BUT-UNRECOGNIZED intangible assets that failed the recognition criteria (para. 127).

Statement / Process Mapping

Recognition decision map
1. Does the item meet the three-part test — identifiable, controlled, future benefit? If no → expense, or fold into goodwill if acquired in a combination (§2) 2. Is it internally generated? RESEARCH phase → always expensed. DEVELOPMENT phase → capitalize only if all six para. 55 criteria are met (§5) 3. Choose ONE measurement policy per class: historical cost, or current value (only where an active market genuinely exists) (§7) 4. Assess useful life — FINITE → amortize systematically, review each period. INDEFINITE → no amortization, test for impairment ANNUALLY (§8, §9) 5. On disposal or when no further benefit is expected → derecognize, recognizing any gain/loss in surplus or deficit (§9)

Quick-Reference Glossary

Intangible asset
An identifiable nonmonetary asset without physical substance.
Amortization
The systematic allocation of the depreciable amount of an intangible asset over its useful life.
Research
Original and planned investigation undertaken to gain new scientific or technical knowledge and understanding.
Development
Applying research findings or other knowledge to a plan or design for new or substantially improved materials, devices, products, processes, systems, or services.
Intangible heritage assets
Intangible assets with cultural, environmental, or historical significance.
Carrying amount
The amount at which an asset is recognized after deducting accumulated amortization and impairment losses.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOFPerf Statement of financial performance NA/E Net assets/equity (statement of changes)
