Conceptual Framework Linkage

The entry point to the entire German commercial accounting edifice
§§238–241a are not a narrow technical rule — they are the FOUNDATION on which every other HGB accounting provision rests. Before any question of RECOGNITION, MEASUREMENT, or DISCLOSURE can even arise, an obligation must exist to keep books AT ALL, and a physical INVENTORY must exist from which a balance sheet can be derived. Everything in Book 3 presupposes §§238–241a have already been satisfied.
HOW §§238–241A MAP TO GERMAN ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES
Gläubigerschutz (creditor protection) — reliable, tamper-evident books are the FIRST line of defence protecting creditors, who rely on the Kaufmann's own records when extending credit Grundsätze ordnungsmäßiger Buchführung (GoB) — §238 explicitly INCORPORATES this judicially-developed body of principles BY REFERENCE, making it legally binding rather than a voluntary professional norm Nachprüfbarkeit (verifiability) — a THIRD party must be able to reconstruct transactions "within a reasonable time," a requirement with NO direct parallel in the IFRS Conceptual Framework's own qualitative characteristics
§§238–241a apply REGARDLESS of legal form, size, or industry — they bind every Kaufmann (merchant), unlike most of HGB Book 3 which is layered on top ONLY for Kapitalgesellschaften.
What these provisions actually require, at a glance
Four distinct but interlocking obligations, each building on the last.
§Core requirement
238Keep books showing the state of assets and debts, per GoB
239Keep them in a living language, chronologically, without obscuring alterations
240Physically count and itemise all assets/liabilities (Inventar) at start and each year-end
241Simplified stocktaking methods are permitted where GoB allows
ℹ️ §241a then EXEMPTS very small sole traders from all four obligations at once — see Section 12.

Scope — Who Is Bound (Der Kaufmann)

Kaufmannseigenschaft — merchant status is what triggers the whole chapter
The bookkeeping obligation attaches to anyone qualifying as a "Kaufmann" under HGB §§1 et seq. — this includes individual sole traders operating a commercial business (Einzelkaufleute), and, CRUCIALLY, ALL corporations (Kapitalgesellschaften: AG, GmbH, KGaA) REGARDLESS of whether they would otherwise qualify as merchants by their activity, since company law itself deems them merchants ("Formkaufmann").
BOUND BY §§238–241
Einzelkaufleute (sole traders) above the §241a thresholds OHG and KG (partnerships) engaged in commercial activity ALL Kapitalgesellschaften (AG, GmbH, KGaA) — automatically, by legal form
NOT BOUND, OR SEPARATELY GOVERNED
Freelancers and members of the liberal professions (Freiberufler) — generally outside HGB's merchant concept entirely Small sole traders below the §241a thresholds (see Section 12) Farmers and foresters below certain size thresholds, per separate provisions

§238 — The Basic Bookkeeping Obligation (with worked example)

Books that show the true state of assets and business dealings
§238(1) requires the Kaufmann to keep books and, IN them, to make visible ("ersichtlich machen") their commercial transactions and the state of their assets, IN ACCORDANCE with GoB. The bookkeeping must be such that it gives an EXPERT THIRD PARTY an overview of the transactions and the state of the business WITHIN a reasonable time — and each transaction must be traceable from its ORIGIN through to its final settlement (§238(1) sentence 3).
Example 1: Determining who is bound by §238
A freelance graphic designer operates as a sole proprietor with no registered trade (Gewerbe), invoicing clients directly for design services. Separately, a small GmbH with two employees runs an online retail shop selling handmade candles, generating €120,000 revenue and €15,000 profit annually.
The FREELANCE designer is generally OUTSIDE HGB's merchant concept (a liberal profession, not a commercial trade) and is NOT bound by §238. The GmbH, by contrast, is bound by §238 AUTOMATICALLY — its legal form alone makes it a Kaufmann under company law, REGARDLESS of how small its revenue or profit actually is (the §241a exemption applies ONLY to Einzelkaufleute, never to a Kapitalgesellschaft — see Section 12).
4

The "Expert Third Party" Standard (Sachverständiger Dritter)

A specific, named legal test — not a vague aspiration
The books must be intelligible to a KNOWLEDGEABLE outsider — not the Kaufmann themselves, and not a completely untrained layperson — WITHIN A REASONABLE TIME. This is a genuinely NAMED legal standard German courts have interpreted repeatedly, unlike IFRS's broader "understandability" qualitative characteristic, which is aimed at USERS of financial statements rather than at the underlying bookkeeping records themselves.
Traceability from origin to settlement
§238(1) sentence 3 additionally requires that transactions be traceable in their ENTSTEHUNG (origin) and ABWICKLUNG (settlement/processing) — meaning the audit trail must run continuously from the initial business event through to its final accounting resolution, with no unexplained gaps.
⚠️ A bookkeeping system that LOOKS complete but cannot be traced back to source documents within a reasonable time FAILS §238, even if the final balances happen to be numerically correct.
5

§239 — How the Books Must Be Kept

Language, abbreviations, chronology, and timeliness
§239 sets out the MECHANICAL requirements for how the books are physically or electronically maintained.
Records must be kept in a LIVING language (lebende Sprache) — a dead or invented language would not satisfy the expert-third-party test of §238 Abbreviations, digits, letters, or symbols MAY be used, PROVIDED their meaning is clearly established for each specific case Entries must be made COMPLETELY, ACCURATELY, PROMPTLY, and in an ORDERLY, chronological manner An entry or record may NOT be altered in a way that makes the ORIGINAL content unrecognisable — whether this happened at the time of the original entry or only later cannot be established (the "Radierverbot," Section 6)

The Alteration Prohibition (Radierverbot, with worked example)

Corrections must remain visible, never erased
§239(3) does NOT forbid correcting an error — it forbids correcting it in a way that DESTROYS the ability to see what was originally recorded. A correction must be made so that BOTH the original entry AND the correction remain legible and datable.
Example 2: A permissible correction vs a prohibited one
A bookkeeper mistakenly records a €4,500 supplier invoice as €5,400 in the ledger. Two different corrections are considered: (A) using correction fluid to fully obscure "5,400" and writing "4,500" cleanly over the blank space; (B) drawing a single line through "5,400" so it remains legible, writing "4,500" alongside, and initialling/dating the change.
Correction (A) VIOLATES §239(3) — the original erroneous entry can NO LONGER be reconstructed, which is precisely what the Radierverbot exists to prevent. Correction (B) is FULLY COMPLIANT — both the error and its correction remain visible and traceable, satisfying the expert-third-party and traceability requirements of §238 simultaneously.
🚫 In electronic bookkeeping systems, this principle requires an immutable audit trail or version history — simply overwriting a database field with no record of the prior value is the digital equivalent of using correction fluid, and equally non-compliant.

§240 — The Inventory Obligation

A physical, itemised count — at the start of business and every year-end
§240(1) requires the Kaufmann, at the START of their commercial activity, to prepare an ITEMISED statement of their assets and liabilities showing their respective VALUES (the "Inventar," or inventory) — and §240(2) requires this same itemised statement to be prepared AGAIN for the close of EVERY subsequent business year.
WHAT §240 ACTUALLY REQUIRES
An itemised list — not a summary — of every asset and every liability A value assigned to each individual item Preparation at the opening of the business AND at each subsequent fiscal year-end
§240(3)–(4): PRACTICAL FLEXIBILITY
Similar items of raw materials, consumables, and goods MAY be combined into groups and valued at a weighted average, where individually itemising each unit would be impracticable Items of comparatively minor value MAY be combined and valued together, on a reasonable estimate

Inventar vs Bilanz — a Critical Distinction

Two related but genuinely different documents — a frequently confused distinction
Inventar (§240)Bilanz (§242 onward)
A detailed, ITEMISED physical listing of every individual asset and liabilityA CONDENSED summary financial statement, aggregating items into standardised line items
Internal working document, not published or filedExternally-facing; forms part of the filed/published Jahresabschluss
No prescribed presentation formatLegally PRESCRIBED, line-by-line format under §266
The Inventar is essentially the DETAILED WORKING PAPER from which the summarised Bilanz is then constructed — the Bilanz could not exist, in principle, without an underlying Inventar to support it.

§241 — Simplified Stocktaking Procedures

Four permitted simplification methods
Recognising that a literal, full physical count of EVERY item at EVERY year-end would often be disproportionately costly, §241 permits four specific alternative approaches, WHERE consistent with GoB.
Stichprobeninventur — statistical, sample-based counting methods, extrapolated using recognised mathematical/statistical techniques Permanente Inventur — a perpetual inventory system: a physical count may occur AT ANY POINT during the year (not necessarily the balance sheet date), PROVIDED a properly organised ongoing record allows the year-end quantities to be reliably rolled forward Festwert — a fixed/standing value for certain low-value, regularly-replenished asset categories (Section 10) Gruppenbewertung — group valuation of similar items using a weighted average, cross-referenced to §240(4)
Why simplification is permitted at all
The underlying rationale is a genuinely PRACTICAL, cost-conscious one: GoB itself contains an implicit cost-benefit constraint, and §241 gives that constraint concrete, statutory form for the specific case of stocktaking — closely paralleling the SAME cost-benefit reasoning we've seen drive numerous ASPE simplifications for private enterprises.
ℹ️ All four methods remain subject to the overriding requirement that the RESULTING inventory be at least as reliable as a full physical count would have been — simplification is a method of ACHIEVING the §240 standard more efficiently, not a relaxation of the standard itself.

Worked Example — the Festwert (Fixed Value) Method

Freezing a value where the underlying stock is stable and low in significance
A Festwert may be used for items of property, plant, raw materials, or supplies that are REGULARLY REPLACED, whose overall quantity is SUBJECT TO ONLY MINOR fluctuations, and whose total value is of SUBORDINATE importance to the enterprise as a whole.
Example 3: Applying the Festwert method to small tools
A machine-tool manufacturer holds a large population of small, low-value hand tools (spanners, screwdrivers, gauges) used across its workshop floor, worth approximately €38,000 in aggregate — a genuinely immaterial figure against the company's €14,000,000 balance sheet. The tool population is constantly replaced (worn tools discarded, new ones purchased) but its TOTAL value fluctuates only slightly year to year.
The company adopts a FESTWERT of €38,000 for this tool category, carrying it at that fixed figure YEAR AFTER YEAR — REGARDLESS of the individual tools actually on hand at each balance sheet date — until a PHYSICAL recount (required at least every 3 years under established practice) confirms the fixed value remains appropriate, or a MATERIAL change in the underlying stock justifies an adjustment.

Worked Example — Sample-Based Stocktaking

Extrapolating from a statistically valid sample
Example 4: Stichprobeninventur in a large warehouse
A wholesale distributor holds approximately 40,000 individual SKUs across a large warehouse. A full physical count of every SKU at year-end would require the warehouse to close for several days, at substantial cost. The company instead engages a statistician to design a random sampling procedure covering a representative subset of SKUs, extrapolating the sampled results to the full population using accepted statistical methods, with a documented confidence level and margin of error.
PROVIDED the sampling methodology is genuinely sound and properly documented, this qualifies as a valid Stichprobeninventur under §241(1) — the company need NOT physically count all 40,000 SKUs, so long as the STATISTICAL reliability of the extrapolated result is comparable to what a full count would have achieved.
German commercial law scholarship treats the statistical VALIDITY of the sampling method (not merely its existence) as the decisive test — a sloppy or unrepresentative "sample" would not satisfy §241, even if labelled as one.

§241a — The Small Business Exemption (with worked example)

A genuine, complete opt-out for very small sole traders — with a 2024 threshold increase
§241a exempts Einzelkaufleute (sole traders) from §§238–241 IN THEIR ENTIRETY — no bookkeeping obligation, no inventory obligation whatsoever — WHERE, at the balance sheet date of two consecutive fiscal years, revenue does NOT exceed €800,000 and annual profit does NOT exceed €80,000. These thresholds were RAISED from their earlier €500,000/€50,000 levels by the Wachstumschancengesetz (Growth Opportunities Act), effective 28 March 2024.
⚠️ CRITICAL LIMITATION: §241a applies ONLY to Einzelkaufleute — sole traders. It does NOT extend to partnerships (OHG, KG) or to ANY Kapitalgesellschaft, no matter how small. A one-person GmbH with €50,000 revenue remains FULLY bound by §§238–241; incorporation itself forecloses this exemption.
Example 5: Testing whether §241a applies
A sole-trader artisan bakery reports revenue of €720,000 and €65,000 profit in Year 1, and €780,000 revenue and €72,000 profit in Year 2 — both years below the current thresholds. In Year 3, revenue rises to €850,000 with €90,000 profit.
Since BOTH Year 1 and Year 2 (the two CONSECUTIVE prior years) stayed below €800,000/€80,000, the bakery is EXEMPT from §§238–241 going into Year 3, DESPITE Year 3 itself exceeding the thresholds — the test looks BACKWARD at the two most recently completed years, not at the current, still-open year.

Consequences of Non-Compliance

This is not merely a paperwork failing — criminal exposure is real
A failure to keep books at all, or keeping them in a way that OBSCURES the true state of assets, can constitute the criminal offence of Bankrott (bankruptcy fraud) under §283 StGB (Criminal Code) WHERE the enterprise subsequently becomes insolvent — the commercial bookkeeping obligation and CRIMINAL liability are directly linked.
🚫 Deliberately destroying, damaging, or concealing commercial books BEFORE the expiry of the statutory retention period (§257 — see the companion poster on §§257–263) is itself a SEPARATE criminal offence under §283 StGB, independent of any underlying insolvency.
Civil-law consequences within a commercial dispute
Books kept in violation of §§238–241 lose much of their practical value if a court ever orders their production in litigation (§258 — see the companion poster on §§257–263) — properly kept commercial books can serve as credible evidence when produced; defectively kept, gap-ridden, or altered books carry far less persuasive weight under a court's general, free evaluation of the evidence.
14

The Tax-Law Linkage (§140 AO)

Commercial bookkeeping "radiates" directly into tax obligations
§140 of the Abgabenordnung (AO, Germany's General Fiscal Code) provides that WHOEVER is obligated to keep books and records under OTHER laws (i.e., under HGB §238) is ALSO obligated to keep them for TAX purposes — the commercial bookkeeping duty is "hung onto" the tax system directly, WITHOUT tax law needing to separately re-legislate the same requirement.
ℹ️ This is an early, foundational instance of the broader Maßgeblichkeitsprinzip (authoritative-principle) linkage between commercial and tax accounting we introduced earlier in this series — even at this most basic level, HGB's bookkeeping obligation directly determines whether a tax bookkeeping obligation exists at all.

Critical Judgments & Practical Difficulties

Where judgement — and practical difficulty — arises most often
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether a given activity genuinely constitutes commercial trade (Gewerbe) triggering Kaufmann status, as opposed to a liberal profession outside HGB's scope Whether a proposed stocktaking simplification under §241 is genuinely as reliable as a full physical count would have been Whether an electronic audit-trail/versioning system genuinely satisfies the alteration prohibition of §239(3)
PRACTICAL DIFFICULTIES
Applying the §241a thresholds correctly at the transition point between exempt and non-exempt status, particularly for a newly-founded business (special first-year rules apply) Designing a statistically defensible Stichprobeninventur methodology that would survive challenge by tax or commercial auditors
⚠️ A newly-founded Einzelkaufmann tests the §241a thresholds against the FIRST balance sheet date after founding alone — the "two consecutive years" rule does not apply to a brand-new business, allowing immediate qualification for the exemption from inception if the first year's figures alone are within the limits.

Relationship to IFRS — a Genuine Structural Absence

Comparison — HGB §§238–241a vs IFRS
FeatureHGB (§§238–241a)IFRS
Legal bookkeeping mandateA DIRECT statutory obligation, with criminal consequences for breachNO equivalent — IFRS presupposes underlying records already exist, and never legislates HOW they are kept
Physical inventory requirementExplicit, named legal obligation (§240)IAS 2 addresses INVENTORY measurement, not the physical stocktaking PROCESS itself
Small-entity exemption from bookkeeping itselfYES — §241a, a genuine opt-out from keeping books at allNOT applicable — IFRS has no bookkeeping-obligation layer to exempt anyone FROM
This is a genuinely different KIND of comparison than we have drawn for any other framework in this poster library. IFRS is a FINANCIAL REPORTING standard — it tells preparers how to MEASURE and PRESENT what is already recorded. HGB §§238–241a sit ONE LEVEL BELOW that: they are the underlying LEGAL bookkeeping infrastructure a jurisdiction's commercial or company law must separately supply before any accounting standard — IFRS included — has anything to operate on. An IFRS preparer in Germany STILL relies on §§238–241 to establish that reliable books and an inventory exist in the first place; IFRS itself is simply silent on the question.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Kaufmann
A merchant under HGB — anyone conducting a commercial trade, or automatically deemed one by legal form (e.g. any Kapitalgesellschaft).
Sachverständiger Dritter
The "expert third party" standard §238 uses to test whether bookkeeping is sufficiently clear and traceable.
Inventar
The detailed, itemised physical listing of every asset and liability with individual values, distinct from the condensed Bilanz.
Radierverbot
The prohibition on altering an entry in a way that destroys the ability to reconstruct the original content.
Festwert
A fixed value carried unchanged year to year for low-value, regularly-replenished, immaterial asset categories.
Stichprobeninventur
Statistical, sample-based stocktaking, extrapolated to the full population using recognised methods.
