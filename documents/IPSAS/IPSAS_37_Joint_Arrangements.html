Objective, Scope & Definitions (paras. 1–7)

Objective & scope
Establishes principles for financial reporting by entities with an interest in JOINTLY controlled arrangements. Requires an entity party to a joint arrangement to determine its TYPE by assessing its rights and obligations, and to account for those rights/obligations ACCORDINGLY.
Applies to ALL entities that are a party to a joint arrangement
Six core definitions (para. 7)
Joint arrangement
An arrangement of which two or more parties have JOINT CONTROL.
Joint operation
A joint arrangement where the jointly controlling parties have rights to the ASSETS, and obligations for the LIABILITIES, relating to the arrangement.
Joint venture
A joint arrangement where the jointly controlling parties have rights to the NET ASSETS of the arrangement.
Separate vehicle
A separately identifiable financial structure — including separate legal entities or statutory entities, whether or not they have legal personality.
2

Joint Control — the Unanimous Consent Test (paras. 9–18)

Two required characteristics (para. 10)
The parties are bound by a BINDING ARRANGEMENT That arrangement gives TWO OR MORE parties JOINT CONTROL
A joint arrangement is EITHER a joint operation OR a joint venture (para. 11) — never both at once for the same activity.
The two-stage assessment
Stage 1 — collective control: do all parties (or a group of them) control the arrangement TOGETHER — i.e., must they ACT TOGETHER to direct the relevant activities? (para. 13) Stage 2 — joint control: once collective control is established, does exercising it require the UNANIMOUS CONSENT of the parties sharing control? (para. 14)
No single party controls a joint arrangement alone — any joint-control party can BLOCK the others from controlling it unilaterally (para. 15). Some parties may participate WITHOUT joint control (para. 16). Judgment is applied throughout, considering all facts/circumstances, and REASSESSED whenever those change (paras. 17–18).
3

Types of Joint Arrangement — Operation vs Venture (paras. 19–22)

Classification depends on RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS, not just structure
An entity determines its type by considering the arrangement's STRUCTURE and LEGAL FORM, the terms agreed by the parties (or established by legislative/executive authority), and — where relevant — OTHER facts and circumstances (para. 20).
A single FRAMEWORK AGREEMENT can spawn MULTIPLE joint arrangements of DIFFERENT types — where the parties' rights/obligations differ across the specific activities it covers, joint operations and joint ventures can genuinely COEXIST under one umbrella agreement (para. 21) Classification is REASSESSED whenever facts and circumstances change (para. 22)
4

Joint Operations — the "Look-Through" Recognition Model (paras. 23–24)

Recognize YOUR OWN share directly — no equity-method wrapper
A joint operator recognizes ITS OWN assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses (including its SHARE of any held/incurred jointly), applying the IPSAS otherwise applicable to each item
Its OWN assets, including its share of any assets held JOINTLY Its OWN liabilities, including its share of any liabilities incurred JOINTLY Revenue from selling ITS OWN share of the joint operation's output Its SHARE of revenue from the joint operation's OWN sale of output Its OWN expenses, including its share of jointly incurred expenses
ℹ️ This is fundamentally different from the joint venture's equity method (§6) — there's no single "investment" line item. Each joint operator's SOFP and SOFPerformance directly reflect its proportionate share of the underlying assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses, item by item, using whichever Standard normally governs each item (para. 24).
5

Non-Controlling Parties & Acquisitions of Interests in Joint Operations (paras. 24A–26)

Participating without joint control
Has rights to assets AND obligations for liabilities of the joint operation, despite lacking joint control → applies the SAME §4 recognition mechanics (para. 26) Does NOT have such rights/obligations → accounts for its interest under whichever IPSAS ordinarily applies to that interest instead (para. 26)
Acquiring an interest where the activity is an "operation" (IPSAS 40 sense)
Where the joint operation's activity constitutes an OPERATION (IPSAS 40) → apply IPSAS 40's acquisition-accounting principles, to the extent of the entity's own share
Applies to BOTH the initial interest AND any additional interests acquired later (para. 24A). Transactions between an entity and a joint operation it participates in (sales, contributions, purchases of assets) follow specific Application Guidance (para. 25) — not repeated in the black-letter text itself.
6

Joint Ventures — the Equity Method (paras. 27–28)

Joint venturers — equity method by default
A joint venturer recognizes its interest as an INVESTMENT, accounted for using the EQUITY METHOD (IPSAS 36) — unless exempt
This is the mirror image of §4's joint operation treatment — here, ONE investment line item captures the venturer's interest, rather than proportionate line-by-line recognition (para. 27).
Non-controlling parties to a joint venture
No joint control, no significant influence → financial instruments Standards (IPSAS 28, 30, 41) No joint control, but DOES have significant influence → IPSAS 36 (equity method) instead
A joint arrangement classified as a joint VENTURE never gets the joint operation's line-by-line recognition treatment — the split is total, based purely on whether the parties have rights to NET ASSETS (venture) or to specific assets/liabilities (operation) (para. 28, cross-ref §3).

Transitional Provisions — Joint Ventures: Proportionate Consolidation to the Equity Method (paras. 31–36)

Building the opening investment balance
Opening investment (start of immediately preceding period) = AGGREGATE of the carrying amounts of all previously proportionately-consolidated assets/liabilities, INCLUDING any purchased goodwill
Goodwill previously belonging to a LARGER cash-generating unit is allocated to the joint venture based on RELATIVE carrying amounts (para. 32). This opening balance is then treated as the DEEMED COST at initial recognition — immediately tested for impairment under IPSAS 36 paras. 43–48, with any resulting loss adjusted against opening accumulated surplus/deficit (para. 33).
Negative net assets, and required disclosure
If aggregating the previously-consolidated items yields NEGATIVE net assets → assess whether legal/constructive obligations exist; if so, recognize a LIABILITY; if not, adjust opening accumulated surplus/deficit instead — WITHOUT recognizing a liability (para. 34) Disclose: that fact, the CUMULATIVE unrecognized share of joint venture losses (both at the start of the preceding period AND at first application), and a BREAKDOWN of everything aggregated into the single investment line (paras. 34–35)
After this one-time transition, the investment is accounted for using the ordinary equity method going forward (para. 36).

Transitional Provisions — Joint Operations: Equity Method to Assets & Liabilities (paras. 37–41)

Derecognize the investment, recognize the shares
DERECOGNIZE the equity-method investment (and any other net-investment items) → RECOGNIZE the entity's share of each underlying asset/liability, including any embedded goodwill
The interest in assets/liabilities is determined per the binding arrangement's specified proportion; initial carrying amounts are DISAGGREGATED from the former investment's carrying amount, using the same information the entity used applying the equity method (paras. 37–38).
Where the difference goes, and the reconciliation
Net recognized assets/liabilities (incl. goodwill) HIGHER than the derecognized investment → offset against any existing goodwill FIRST; any remainder adjusts opening accumulated surplus/deficit Net recognized assets/liabilities LOWER than the derecognized investment → the whole difference adjusts opening accumulated surplus/deficit (para. 39)
A RECONCILIATION between the derecognized investment and the recognized assets/liabilities is required, with any remaining difference disclosed (para. 40). An entity previously using cost or IPSAS 41 for a joint operation interest in its own SEPARATE statements (under the old IPSAS 6 para. 58) follows the SAME derecognition/recognition mechanics, with its own reconciliation (para. 41).

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Comparison with IFRS 11

Comparison — IPSAS 37 vs IFRS 11
IPSAS 37 is drawn primarily from IFRS 11 (issued 2011, amendments to 31 December 2014), Joint Arrangements.
TopicDifference
Terminology"Controlling entity," "surplus or deficit," "accumulated surplus or deficit" (IPSAS 37) vs "parent," "profit or loss," "retained earnings" (IFRS 11)
Binding arrangementDefined (via IPSAS 35) more BROADLY than IFRS 11's "contractual arrangement" — capturing statutory/legislative mechanisms alongside contracts
Illustrative examplesIPSAS 37 adds ADDITIONAL examples reflecting the public sector context
A notably SHORT comparison — IPSAS 37 tracks IFRS 11 closely, since the joint-arrangement classification logic (rights to assets/liabilities vs. rights to net assets) translates cleanly across sectors.

Statement / Process Mapping

Classification & accounting decision map
1. Is there a BINDING ARRANGEMENT giving two or more parties JOINT CONTROL (unanimous consent required)? If no → not a joint arrangement at all (§2) 2. Assess RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS: rights to specific ASSETS and obligations for specific LIABILITIES → JOINT OPERATION. Rights to the NET ASSETS only → JOINT VENTURE (§3) 3. Joint operation → recognize YOUR OWN share of assets/liabilities/revenue/expenses directly, line by line, under whichever IPSAS normally governs each item (§4) 4. Joint venture → recognize a SINGLE investment, equity-accounted under IPSAS 36, unless exempt (§6) 5. Does the joint operation's activity constitute an "operation" under IPSAS 40? → apply IPSAS 40 acquisition accounting to your own share, for both initial and additional interests (§5) 6. Apply the SAME split in the entity's own separate financial statements (§7)

Quick-Reference Glossary

Joint arrangement
An arrangement of which two or more parties have joint control.
Joint control
The agreed sharing of control, existing only when relevant-activity decisions require unanimous consent.
Joint operation
A joint arrangement where the parties have rights to the assets and obligations for the liabilities relating to the arrangement.
Joint operator
A party to a joint operation that has joint control of that joint operation.
Joint venture
A joint arrangement where the parties have rights to the net assets of the arrangement.
Separate vehicle
A separately identifiable financial structure, including separate legal entities or statutory entities.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOFPerf Statement of financial performance NA/E Net assets/equity (statement of changes)
