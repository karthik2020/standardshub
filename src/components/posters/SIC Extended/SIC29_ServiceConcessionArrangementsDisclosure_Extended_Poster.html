Conceptual Framework Linkage

Complex, long-lived arrangements need narrative context
Service concession arrangements often run for decades and involve significant, long-term rights and obligations that are not fully captured by the balance sheet and income statement figures alone. SIC-29 exists to ensure users receive enough NARRATIVE and QUANTITATIVE context – beyond the bare recognition and measurement outcomes IFRIC 12 produces – to genuinely understand the nature, timing, and uncertainty of the cash flows these arrangements generate.
Symmetric transparency for both sides of the arrangement
Because BOTH the operator and the grantor have significant stakes in a service concession arrangement, SIC-29 requires disclosure from both parties – reflecting that a faithful picture of the arrangement's significance requires understanding it from both perspectives, not just the operator's recognised assets.
2

Scope & Applicability

What SIC-29 covers
SIC-29 applies to both OPERATORS and GRANTORS of public-to-private service concession arrangements, requiring disclosure of information that enables users of the financial statements to evaluate the nature and extent of such arrangements, and their effect on the entity's financial position, performance, and cash flows.
TYPICAL CONTEXTS
Toll roads, bridges, tunnels, and similar transport infrastructure concessionsWater and utility supply concessions between a government grantor and a private operatorAny arrangement within IFRIC 12's recognition and measurement scope, from either the operator's or grantor's perspective
Key definitions
Operator
The entity that constructs, operates, and/or maintains infrastructure used to provide a public service under a service concession arrangement.
Grantor
The government or public sector body (or private entity to which the concession has been contracted out) that grants the service arrangement to the operator.
3

Disclosure, Not Recognition

A crucial scope distinction
SIC-29 is a pure DISCLOSURE interpretation – it does NOT govern recognition or measurement, which is addressed by IFRIC 12 (for the operator's perspective) and other relevant standards. SIC-29's role is purely to ensure adequate NOTES disclosure surrounding whatever recognition and measurement outcome the substantive standards produce, including for the grantor, for whom IFRS does not prescribe a specific comprehensive recognition and measurement model in the way IFRIC 12 does for operators.
4

Who Must Disclose – Both Operator and Grantor

Symmetric obligations, asymmetric recognition guidance
SIC-29's disclosure requirements apply to BOTH the operator and the grantor of a service concession arrangement – even though IFRIC 12's detailed recognition and measurement guidance addresses primarily the OPERATOR'S accounting. This means a grantor (often a public sector entity applying IFRS, or a private entity party to a sub-concession) must still provide the disclosures SIC-29 requires, even absent a fully symmetric recognition framework of its own.

6

Description of the Arrangement

Narrative context users need to interpret the numbers
Beyond the checklist items in Section 5, SIC-29 expects sufficient NARRATIVE description for users to understand how the arrangement operates – what public service is being provided, the general structure of the concession (financial asset, intangible asset, or bifurcated model, per IFRIC 12), and how the entity's obligations and rights evolve over the concession's life.
7

Changes During the Period

Keeping the disclosure current year over year
SIC-29 specifically requires disclosure of CHANGES in the service concession arrangement occurring during the reporting period – for example, a renegotiation of pricing terms, an extension of the concession period, or a material change in construction/upgrade obligations – ensuring the disclosure reflects the CURRENT state of the arrangement rather than becoming stale boilerplate carried forward unchanged year after year.
8

Worked Example – Toll Road Disclosure Note

Illustrative disclosure structure
Example: Operator's SIC-29 note for a 30-year toll concession
An operator holds a 30-year concession to build, operate, and maintain a toll road, compensated through a right to charge users (intangible asset model under IFRIC 12), with a contractual obligation to hand the road back to the grantor at the end of the term in a specified condition.
The note would typically disclose: the 30-year term and its start/end dates; the intangible-asset classification and the basis for that conclusion; the toll-setting mechanism and any government re-pricing controls; the construction obligation and estimated completion date; the handback condition obligation (linking to an IAS 37 provision, if recognised); and any material renegotiation or extension occurring during the current period.
9

Aggregation of Disclosures

Individually or by class of arrangement
SIC-29 permits disclosures to be given either for EACH individual service concession arrangement, or AGGREGATED by class for arrangements entered into under substantially similar terms – for example, an entity with many broadly similar water utility concessions across different municipalities may disclose them as a class, while a single large, distinctive toll road concession would typically warrant individual disclosure.

SIC-29 vs US GAAP

Comparison – SIC-29 vs US GAAP (ASC 853)
FeatureIFRS (SIC-29)US GAAP (ASC 853)
Dedicated disclosure interpretationYes – SIC-29 specifically, as a companion to IFRIC 12ASC 853 integrates disclosure requirements directly within the standard itself, rather than as a separate companion interpretation, but covers broadly similar ground
Disclosure by both operator and grantorExplicitly required from both partiesASC 853 is primarily focused on the operator's disclosures, with less explicit parallel guidance for the grantor
Both frameworks converge on requiring rich narrative and quantitative disclosure for service concession arrangements – the structural difference is that IFRS separates disclosure (SIC-29) from recognition/measurement (IFRIC 12) into two documents, while ASC 853 integrates both into a single standard.

Quick-reference glossary
Class of arrangements
A grouping of substantially similar service concession arrangements that SIC-29 permits to be disclosed together rather than individually.
Re-pricing date
A contractually specified date on which pricing terms of a service concession arrangement may be revised, a significant term SIC-29 requires disclosure of.
14

Quick-Reference Glossary

Handback obligation
A contractual requirement for the operator to return infrastructure to the grantor in a specified condition at the end of the concession term – a significant term typically disclosed under SIC-29.
Public-to-private service concession arrangement
An arrangement whereby a government or public sector body (grantor) contracts with a private operator to develop, finance, operate, and maintain infrastructure used to provide a public service – the subject matter of both IFRIC 12 and SIC-29.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOPL Statement of profit or loss OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
