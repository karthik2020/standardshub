Glossary
1

Objective, Scope & the Public Sector Combinations Carve-Out (paras. 1–6)

Objective
Establishes principles for presenting and preparing consolidated financial statements when an entity controls one or more other entities. To achieve this, the Standard: requires controlling entities to present consolidated statements; DEFINES control as the basis for consolidation; sets out how to APPLY the control principle; prescribes accounting requirements for consolidation; and defines an INVESTMENT ENTITY exception (para. 2).
What's carved out
Accounting for PUBLIC SECTOR COMBINATIONS and their consolidation effects, including goodwill (IPSAS 40) — para. 4 Post-employment and other long-term employee benefit plans (IPSAS 39) — para. 6
Scope applies to the preparation of consolidated statements for the ECONOMIC ENTITY — a controlling entity and its controlled entities (para. 3). A controlled entity's DISSIMILAR activities (e.g. commercial entities alongside budget-sector entities) do NOT exclude it from consolidation — segment disclosures (IPSAS 18) instead explain the differing activities (para. 8).
2

Presentation of Consolidated Financial Statements — the Consolidation Exemption (paras. 5, 7, 9–10)

Every controlling entity presents consolidated statements — UNLESS all four conditions hold (para. 5)
It is ITSELF a controlled entity, and user needs are met by ITS OWN controlling entity's consolidated statements — and (for a partially-owned entity) all OTHER owners have been informed and don't object Its debt/equity instruments are NOT publicly traded (domestic/foreign exchange, over-the-counter, local/regional) It has NOT filed, and is not in the process of filing, statements with a securities regulator for public-market issuance Its ultimate or any intermediate controlling entity produces PUBLICLY AVAILABLE, IPSAS-compliant consolidated (or investment-entity fair-value) statements
⚠️ The exemption does NOT apply where a controlled entity's users' needs would NOT be met by its controlling entity's consolidated statements — e.g. whole-of-government statements may not satisfy users interested in a specific sector or activity, and jurisdictions often legislate separate reporting for exactly this reason (para. 9).
Investment entities, and aggregated statements outside this Standard
A controlling entity that IS an investment entity does NOT present consolidated statements if REQUIRED (para. 56) to measure ALL controlled entities at fair value through surplus or deficit (para. 7) An entity legally required to prepare AGGREGATED statements for a DIFFERENT economic entity than this Standard defines falls OUTSIDE its scope — though the Standard's guidance may still usefully inform that preparation (para. 10)
3

Definitions (para. 14)

The central concept
Control
An entity controls another entity when it is EXPOSED, or has RIGHTS, to VARIABLE BENEFITS from its involvement, AND has the ABILITY to affect the nature or amount of those benefits through its POWER over the other entity.
Power
Existing rights that give the CURRENT ability to direct the RELEVANT ACTIVITIES of another entity.
Benefits
Advantages an entity obtains from its involvement with other entities — financial OR non-financial, positive OR negative.
The supporting vocabulary
Economic entity
A controlling entity and its controlled entities — also called an administrative, financial, or consolidated entity, or a group.
Non-controlling interest
Net assets/equity in a controlled entity NOT attributable, directly or indirectly, to a controlling entity.
Relevant activities
Activities of the potentially controlled entity that SIGNIFICANTLY AFFECT the nature or amount of benefits the assessing entity receives.
Protective rights
Rights designed to protect the HOLDER's interest without giving that party POWER over the entity.
Investment entity
An entity that (a) obtains funds from investors to provide investment management services, (b) invests solely for capital appreciation/investment revenue, and (c) measures substantially all investments at fair value.
4

Control — the Three-Element Test (paras. 18–22)

ALL three elements required, simultaneously
Control exists if, and ONLY if, an entity has: (a) POWER over the other entity; (b) exposure/rights to VARIABLE BENEFITS from involvement; AND (c) the ABILITY to use its power to AFFECT those benefits
Every entity, regardless of the NATURE of its involvement, must assess whether it controls another entity using this test (para. 18). ALL facts and circumstances are considered — and the assessment is REASSESSED whenever facts and circumstances suggest a change to any of the three elements (para. 21).
Collective control — nobody controls alone
Where TWO OR MORE entities must ACT TOGETHER to direct the relevant activities, NO SINGLE entity controls the other — since no one party can direct activities without the others' cooperation. Each party instead accounts for its interest under IPSAS 36, 37, or the financial instruments Standards (IPSAS 28/30/41), as appropriate (para. 22).
5

Power — Rights & Public Sector Nuances (paras. 23–29)

Where power comes from
The right to direct another entity's FINANCIAL AND OPERATING policies is the most common way power is demonstrated in the public sector (para. 23). Power can arise from voting rights (straightforward) — OR, more often in the public sector, from BINDING ARRANGEMENTS conferring rights WITHOUT any equity instrument at all (para. 24).
✕ Two things that do NOT, by themselves, create power: REGULATORY control (e.g. a pollution authority's power to shut down non-compliant operators; a gaming board's licensing authority — these protect the PUBLIC, not establish a controlling interest), and ECONOMIC DEPENDENCE (para. 26).
Statutory independence does NOT preclude control
An entity can have power even WITHOUT day-to-day operational responsibility. Legislation may give statutory bodies (an Auditor-General, a central bank) independence to carry out FUNCTIONS without recourse to government — but that independence does NOT, of itself, preclude the government having power over the entity's operating/financial policies to obtain benefits. ALL facts and circumstances still matter (para. 25).
An entity has power even if its rights to direct HAVEN'T yet been exercised — e.g. rights exercised once, at an entity's ESTABLISHMENT, with predetermined activities thereafter (para. 27) Where multiple entities each unilaterally direct DIFFERENT relevant activities, power belongs to whichever entity directs the activities that MOST SIGNIFICANTLY affect benefits (para. 28) Holding only PROTECTIVE rights (see §3) never confers power, even alongside another party's significant influence (para. 29)
6

Benefits — Financial & Non-Financial (paras. 30–34)

Financial benefit examples (para. 32)
Dividends, variable debt-security interest, other distributions Exposure to investment value increases/decreases Exposure to loss from financial-support agreements (e.g. major project backing) Cost savings from economies of scale or synergies Residual interests on the other entity's liquidation
Non-financial benefit examples — distinctly public sector (para. 33)
Benefiting from another entity's specialized knowledge Another entity's activities assisting the assessing entity's OWN objectives Improved or more efficient outcomes; more efficient goods/services delivery Assets/services available EARLIER, or at a HIGHER service quality, than otherwise
Benefits can be CONGRUENT (in agreement with the entity's objectives) or COMPLEMENTARY (adding to and completing them) — voluntary OR directed. Only ONE entity can control another, but MULTIPLE parties can SHARE in that entity's benefits (e.g. non-controlling interest holders sharing in surpluses) (para. 34).
7

Link Between Power and Benefits — Principal vs Agent (paras. 35–37)

Power alone isn't enough — it must be USABLE
Control requires the ABILITY to USE power to affect benefits — not merely holding power and separately being exposed to benefits. Congruent OBJECTIVES alone are insufficient; the entity must be able to DIRECT the other entity to further ITS OWN objectives using that power (paras. 35–36).
Agents never control, even while exercising decision-making rights
An AGENT is primarily engaged to act ON BEHALF OF and for the BENEFIT of a principal — and therefore does NOT control the entity when exercising its decision-making authority
Every entity with decision-making rights must determine whether IT is a principal or agent — AND whether ANOTHER entity with decision-making rights is acting as ITS agent. A principal's power may be HELD and EXERCISED by an agent, but always ON THE PRINCIPAL's behalf (para. 37).
8

Accounting Requirements & Consolidation Procedures (paras. 38–41)

The mechanics — three steps (para. 40)
Combine — like items of assets, liabilities, net assets/equity, revenue, expenses, and cash flows of the controlling entity WITH those of controlled entities, line by line Offset — eliminate the controlling entity's INVESTMENT carrying amount against its portion of each controlled entity's net assets/equity (IPSAS 40 governs any resulting goodwill) Eliminate — INTRA-ECONOMIC-ENTITY balances/transactions in full — surpluses/deficits embedded in assets like inventory or fixed assets are eliminated entirely; intra-entity LOSSES may signal an impairment requiring separate recognition
Uniform policies & consolidation window
Uniform accounting POLICIES apply for like transactions/events across the whole economic entity — a member using DIFFERENT policies gets adjusted before consolidation (para. 38, 41) Consolidation begins the date CONTROL is obtained, and ceases the date control is LOST (para. 39)
9

Measurement Timing, Reporting Dates & Potential Voting Rights (paras. 42–46)

Measurement window & reporting dates
Revenue/expenses of a controlled entity are included from the date control is GAINED to the date it's LOST — based on asset/liability amounts recognized in the consolidated statements AT the acquisition date (e.g. post-acquisition depreciation is based on acquisition-date asset values) (para. 42). Component statements used for consolidation are prepared as of the SAME reporting date — via additional information, or the most recent statements ADJUSTED for significant intervening transactions (para. 46).
Potential voting rights — existing interests only, with one exception
Surplus/deficit and net assets/equity change allocations use ONLY existing ownership interests — NOT potential voting rights or derivatives, UNLESS those rights currently give SUBSTANTIVE access to ownership benefits
Where an instrument DOES currently give substantive access to ownership benefits, the eventual exercise/conversion IS taken into account (paras. 43–44). Such instruments fall OUTSIDE IPSAS 28/41's scope in that case; otherwise they're accounted for under those Standards normally (para. 45).
10

Non-Controlling Interests (paras. 47–50)

Presentation & attribution
Non-controlling interests presented WITHIN net assets/equity on the consolidated SOFP, SEPARATELY from the controlling entity's owners
Surplus/deficit and EVERY gain/loss recognized directly in net assets/equity is attributed BETWEEN the controlling entity's owners and non-controlling interests — even where this gives non-controlling interests a DEFICIT balance (para. 49).
Cumulative preference shares held by non-controlling interests
Where a controlled entity has outstanding cumulative preference shares CLASSIFIED as equity and held by non-controlling interests, the entity computes ITS OWN share of surplus/deficit AFTER adjusting for dividends on those shares — WHETHER OR NOT those dividends have actually been declared (para. 50).
Ownership CHANGES that do NOT result in losing control are treated as TRANSACTIONS WITH OWNERS in their capacity as owners — not as gains/losses in surplus or deficit (para. 48, expanded in §11).
11

Changes in the Proportion Held by Non-Controlling Interests (para. 51)

A pure equity transaction — never surplus or deficit
When the non-controlling proportion changes (control retained throughout) → adjust the carrying amounts of BOTH controlling and non-controlling interests to reflect their new relative interests
Any DIFFERENCE between the amount by which non-controlling interests are adjusted, and the fair value of consideration paid/received, is recognized DIRECTLY in net assets/equity, attributed to the CONTROLLING entity's owners (para. 51). This is the consolidation-level parallel to §10's "transactions with owners" framing — buying or selling a partial stake WITHOUT losing control never touches the performance statement.
12

Loss of Control — Derecognition & Gain/Loss Mechanics (paras. 52–55A)

The four-part sequence (paras. 52, 54)
DERECOGNIZE the former controlled entity's assets (including goodwill) and liabilities at their carrying amounts, plus any non-controlling interest carrying amount, at the date control is lost RECOGNIZE the fair value of any consideration received, any share distribution to owners, and any RETAINED investment at its fair value at that date TRANSFER directly to accumulated surplus/deficit any amounts previously in net assets/equity, on the SAME basis as if the entity had directly disposed of the underlying assets/liabilities RECOGNIZE any resulting difference as a GAIN OR LOSS in surplus or deficit, attributable to the controlling entity
A retained investment's remeasured fair value at the loss-of-control date becomes its DEEMED initial fair value under IPSAS 41, or deemed COST if it becomes an equity-method associate/joint venture (para. 52(b)).
Multiple arrangements treated as ONE transaction
Control can be lost across two or more arrangements — but where indicators suggest they're economically linked, they're accounted for as a SINGLE transaction (para. 53). Indicators: entered at the same time or in contemplation of each other; designed for one overall commercial effect; one arrangement's occurrence depends on another; or one arrangement alone isn't economically justified but IS when combined (e.g. a below-market disposal offset by a later above-market one).
Where the retained interest becomes an equity-method associate/joint venture NOT containing an "operation" (IPSAS 40), the gain/loss is recognized ONLY to the extent of UNRELATED investors' interests — the remainder is eliminated against the retained investment's carrying amount (para. 55A).
13

Investment Entities — the Fair Value Exception & Status Changes (paras. 56–64)

The core exception, and its own carve-out
An investment entity does NOT consolidate its controlled entities (or apply IPSAS 40) → instead measures each at FAIR VALUE THROUGH SURPLUS OR DEFICIT under IPSAS 41 (para. 56)
⚠️ Exception to the exception: a controlled entity that is NOT itself an investment entity, and whose main purpose is providing SERVICES related to the investment entity's own investment activities, IS consolidated normally under §8 (para. 57).
A non-investment-entity parent of an investment-entity subsidiary — a blended approach
Such a controlling entity presents consolidated statements measuring the CONTROLLED INVESTMENT ENTITY's own investments at fair value through surplus or deficit, while CONSOLIDATING that investment entity's other assets/liabilities/revenue/expenses normally (para. 58) — a hybrid presentation unique to this scenario.
CEASES to be an investment entity → the change date becomes a DEEMED ACQUISITION date; fair value at that date becomes deemed consideration for any IPSAS 40 goodwill; full consolidation resumes going forward (para. 63) BECOMES an investment entity → stops consolidating (except §57 service-entities); treated AS IF it had lost control of those entities at that date, applying the §12 mechanics (para. 64)
Determining investment-entity status considers ALL facts and circumstances, including purpose and design — reassessed whenever circumstances change (para. 59). Absent one or more of three characteristics (funds from multiple investors; equity-like ownership interests; more than one investment), the entity must disclose the JUDGMENTS made in reaching its conclusion (IPSAS 38 para. 15) (paras. 61–62).

Worked Scenarios — Power & Control Assessment Judgment (Illustrative Examples IE2–IE6)

Partial funding ≠ power
A state government partially funds a local government's activities (library services, leisure facilities, refuse/wastewater, building/safety enforcement) — a council elected every 4 years decides how to use resources.
Despite the partial funding, the state government does NOT have power to direct the local government's relevant activities. The local council's own rights preclude state control (Example 1).
Regulatory power ≠ power over relevant activities
A gaming control board (GCB) regulates casinos — licensing, accounting systems, game rules, auditing — and can grant or deny operating licenses based on character/integrity standards.
The GCB does NOT have power over the relevant activities of the gaming establishments it regulates. The regulations protect the PUBLIC and apply uniformly to all licensees, who each retain a genuine choice whether to operate at all (Example 5).
Two entities, two decision rights — who has power over the combined venture?
Entities A and B jointly form Entity C to develop and market a medical product. A has UNILATERAL authority over development/regulatory approval; B has unilateral authority over manufacturing/marketing once approved.
Since BOTH sets of activities could be "relevant activities," A and B must each determine which activity MOST SIGNIFICANTLY affects Entity C's benefits — considering the purpose/design of C, what drives its surplus/value, the effect of each entity's decisions on performance, and their own exposure to variable benefits. In this case: the uncertainty and effort of obtaining regulatory approval (weighed against A's track record), and which entity controls the product once development succeeds, are the deciding factors (Example 6).

Disclosure — Deferred Entirely to IPSAS 38

A structural note: no black-letter disclosure section in IPSAS 35 itself
Unlike most Standards in this library, IPSAS 35 contains NO dedicated "Disclosure" section of its own. All disclosure requirements relating to interests in controlled entities, structured entities, joint arrangements, and associates — including investment-entity-specific disclosures such as the significant-judgments disclosure referenced in §13 — are consolidated into a SEPARATE, DEDICATED Standard: IPSAS 38, Disclosure of Interests in Other Entities.
This is a deliberate structural design choice by the IPSASB, carried over directly from IFRS 10/12's own split — recognition/measurement principles sit in one Standard, while disclosure for the WHOLE family of "interests in other entities" (controlled entities, joint arrangements, associates, structured entities, investment entities) sits together in one place, avoiding repetition across IPSAS 34, 35, 36, and 37.

Transitional Provisions, Effective Date & Amendment Timeline (paras. 65–81)

Transition — largely retrospective, with a grandfathering rule
Applied RETROSPECTIVELY under IPSAS 3, with specific exceptions (paras. 65–78). Notably: at the date of initial application, NO adjustment is required to previous accounting for entities that were ALREADY consolidated under the old IPSAS 6 AND remain consolidated under IPSAS 35 — or for entities that were NOT consolidated under either (para. 68). Only entities whose consolidation STATUS actually CHANGES require restatement.

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Comparison with IFRS 10

Comparison — IPSAS 35 vs IFRS 10
IPSAS 35 is drawn primarily from IFRS 10 (issued 2011, amendments to 31 December 2014), Consolidated Financial Statements. At initial issuance the IPSASB had not yet considered the public sector applicability of IFRS 5 (superseded by IPSAS 44, May 2022) or IFRS 9.
TopicDifference
Terminology"Economic entity," "controlling entity," "controlled entity" (IPSAS 35) vs "group," "parent," "subsidiary" (IFRS 10) — "investor"/"investee" mostly replaced by "an entity"/"another entity," retained only in investment-entity guidance
Binding arrangementIPSAS 35 defines this BROADER concept, capturing rights from statutory/legislative mechanisms — not just contracts, as IFRS 10's "contractual arrangement" does
Investment entity characteristicsIFRS 10 lists "typical characteristics" separately from the definition; IPSAS 35 folds relevant characteristics INTO the definition discussion instead
Non-financial benefitsIPSAS 35 contains substantially MORE guidance — reflecting that public sector "returns" are so often non-financial (§6)
Non-investment-entity parent of an investment-entity subsidiaryIPSAS 35 requires the BLENDED presentation described in §13; IFRS 10's equivalent provision differs in detail
Illustrative examplesIPSAS 35 adds ADDITIONAL examples reflecting the public sector context (regulatory control, statutory independence, joint public-sector ventures) — see §14

Consolidation decision map

Is the controlling entity an INVESTMENT ENTITY? → fair value through surplus or deficit instead of consolidation, except for service-providing controlled entities (§13) 4. Ownership proportion changes WITHOUT losing control → net assets/equity transaction only (§11). Quick-Reference Glossary

Control
Exposure/rights to variable benefits from involvement with another entity, plus the ability to affect those benefits through power over it.
Power
Existing rights giving the current ability to direct the relevant activities of another entity.
Relevant activities
Activities of the potentially controlled entity that significantly affect the nature or amount of benefits to the assessing entity.
Non-controlling interest
Net assets/equity in a controlled entity not attributable, directly or indirectly, to the controlling entity.
Investment entity
An entity obtaining funds from investors to provide investment management services, investing solely for capital appreciation or investment revenue, measured at fair value.
Protective rights
Rights protecting the holder's interest without conferring power over the entity to which they relate.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position SOFPerf Statement of financial performance NA/E Net assets/equity (statement of changes)
