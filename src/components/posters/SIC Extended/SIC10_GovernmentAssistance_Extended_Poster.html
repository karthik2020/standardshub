Conceptual Framework Linkage

A grant is a grant, regardless of how loosely it is targeted
IAS 20 requires government grants to be recognised in profit or loss over the periods necessary to match them with the costs they are intended to compensate. Some general government assistance schemes – regional investment incentives, industry-wide subsidies – are not tied to specific expenditure, tempting some preparers to treat them as capital contributions credited directly to equity instead. SIC-10 closes that gap by confirming the SUBSTANCE (a transfer of resources from government) governs, not the absence of a specific expenditure link.
Preventing an inconsistent capital-contribution loophole
Allowing assistance to bypass profit or loss merely because it lacks a specific operating tie would create an inconsistency – economically similar transfers of government resources would be accounted for completely differently based on a technicality of how the scheme happened to be drafted, undermining comparability across entities receiving different forms of government support.
2

Scope & Applicability

What SIC-10 covers
SIC-10 applies to government assistance that does NOT relate to specific operating activities of an entity – for example, general subsidies designed to encourage investment in a particular region or industry sector, rather than assistance tied to specific identifiable expenditure the entity has incurred or will incur.
TYPICAL FACT PATTERNS
Regional development grants encouraging investment in economically disadvantaged areasIndustry-wide subsidies intended to support a particular sector generally, without reference to specific expenditure items
Key definitions
Government grant
Assistance by government in the form of transfers of resources to an entity in return for past or future compliance with certain conditions relating to the operating activities of the entity, per IAS 20.
Assistance not specifically related to operating activities
Government support provided without a direct link to identifiable expenditure – the specific category SIC-10 confirms remains within IAS 20's scope as a grant.
3

The Question SIC-10 Answers

The narrow, precise issue
Should government assistance that is NOT tied to specific operating expenditure be accounted for as a government GRANT under IAS 20 (recognised in profit or loss consistent with the costs it compensates), or should it instead be treated as a CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION credited directly to equity, on the basis that it lacks the specific expenditure link IAS 20's ordinary examples typically assume?
4

The Core Conclusion – Still a Grant, Still Through Profit or Loss

SIC-10's firm answer
Government assistance that does not relate to specific operating activities is nonetheless a GOVERNMENT GRANT within the scope of IAS 20 and shall be RECOGNISED IN PROFIT OR LOSS, consistent with IAS 20's general recognition principles – it is NOT credited directly to equity merely because it is not tied to specific identifiable expenditure.
5

Why Equity Treatment Was Tempting, and Wrong

The flawed argument SIC-10 rejects
Some preparers argued that, absent a specific expenditure to match the grant against, the "matching" logic underlying IAS 20's profit-or-loss recognition simply could not apply – implying equity treatment by default. SIC-10 rejects this: IAS 20 provides a general recognition framework for ALL government grants, and the absence of a SPECIFIC expenditure link does not remove the assistance from that framework; it simply means the entity must identify an appropriate systematic basis for recognition (see Section 6) rather than a direct cost-matching link.
6

Recognition Pattern

Systematic recognition, absent a specific cost to match
Where assistance is not linked to specific expenditure, the entity determines a SYSTEMATIC basis for recognising it in profit or loss over the periods that bear the related costs the assistance is broadly intended to compensate – for example, over the period the underlying regional investment or industry-wide activity is expected to generate benefits, consistent with IAS 20's general principle that grants are recognised in the periods necessary to match them with the costs they are intended to compensate.
7

Worked Example – Regional Investment Incentive

General assistance, not tied to a specific asset or expense
Example: Grant to encourage general regional investment
A government agency pays an entity $5,000,000 as an incentive to establish operations in an economically disadvantaged region, with conditions requiring the entity to maintain a minimum level of employment in the region for 10 years, but with no requirement to spend the funds on any specific identified asset or cost category.
Despite the LACK of a specific expenditure link, the $5,000,000 remains a government GRANT under IAS 20/SIC-10. It is recognised in PROFIT OR LOSS on a systematic basis – commonly straight-line over the 10-year employment condition period, i.e. $500,000 per year – rather than credited directly to equity.
8

Distinguishing from Capital Contributions by Owners

The genuine boundary that remains
SIC-10 does not eliminate the concept of a genuine capital contribution – where the GOVERNMENT itself is acting as an OWNER/SHAREHOLDER of the entity (rather than in its capacity as a public authority granting assistance to the broader economy), a transfer of resources may still properly be treated as a capital contribution to equity. The distinction turns on the CAPACITY in which the government is acting, not on whether the assistance happens to be tied to specific expenditure.

SIC-10 vs US GAAP

Comparison – SIC-10 vs US GAAP (ASC 832)
FeatureIFRS (IAS 20 / SIC-10)US GAAP (ASC 832)
General recognition modelRecognised in profit or loss on a systematic basis matching the costs the assistance compensatesASC 832 (a relatively recent DISCLOSURE-focused standard, not a comprehensive recognition/measurement model) does not prescribe a single recognition model – entities often analogise to a grant model like IAS 20 or another framework by policy election
Assistance not tied to specific activitiesExplicitly still a grant, recognised through profit or loss per SIC-10No equivalent explicit interpretation; outcome depends on the recognition framework the entity has elected to apply by analogy
US GAAP notably lacks a comprehensive, prescriptive government grant recognition and measurement standard comparable to IAS 20 – ASC 832 is primarily a disclosure standard, making this an area of genuine structural divergence between the two frameworks, not just a difference in specific mechanics.

Quick-reference glossary
Deferred income
A liability recognised for government grant amounts received before the related systematic recognition in profit or loss has occurred.
Systematic basis
A rational, consistent method (e.g. straight-line over a condition period) for recognising a government grant in profit or loss when no specific expenditure link exists to match against.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Government assistance
Action by government designed to provide an economic benefit specific to an entity or range of entities qualifying under certain criteria, a broader concept than a government grant under IAS 20.
Capital contribution
A transfer of resources from a government acting in its capacity as an OWNER of the entity, credited directly to equity – distinct from a government grant received in the government's public-authority capacity.
