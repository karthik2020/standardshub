Conceptual Framework Linkage

Fair-value acquisition accounting, with one of J-GAAP's most significant divergences
ASBJ Statement No. 21 requires the ACQUISITION METHOD (fair value-based purchase accounting) for VIRTUALLY ALL business combinations – identifying an ACQUIRER, measuring consideration TRANSFERRED at fair value, and recognising the acquiree's IDENTIFIABLE assets and liabilities at fair value, with GOODWILL recognised for any excess. It is the NATURAL companion to ASBJ Statement No. 7 (Business Divestitures), addressing the OTHER side of the SAME transactions. It is ALSO home to one of the MOST significant, well-known divergences from IFRS and US GAAP in the ENTIRE J-GAAP suite: the MANDATORY AMORTISATION of goodwill (Section 5).
2

Scope

Business combinations where one entity obtains control of another
This Statement applies to BUSINESS COMBINATIONS – transactions or events in which an ACQUIRER obtains CONTROL of one or more businesses – whether structured through share acquisition, asset acquisition, or a Companies Act corporate reorganisation mechanism (see the companion ASBJ Statement No. 7 poster for the corporate split backdrop).
3

The Acquisition Method – Identifying the Acquirer

Every combination has an identifiable acquirer
The ACQUISITION METHOD requires IDENTIFYING which combining entity is the "ACQUIRER" – generally the entity obtaining CONTROL – based on indicators such as RELATIVE size, WHO issues consideration, and WHO dominates management of the combined entity AFTERWARD. Once identified, the acquirer applies purchase accounting FROM the acquirer's own perspective.
4

Measuring Consideration and Identifiable Net Assets at Fair Value

Fair value throughout – consideration, and acquired assets/liabilities
Consideration TRANSFERRED (cash, shares issued, contingent consideration) is measured at FAIR VALUE at the acquisition date. The acquiree's IDENTIFIABLE assets acquired and liabilities assumed are ALSO recognised at their INDIVIDUAL fair values – REGARDLESS of their PREVIOUS carrying amounts in the acquiree's own books – with GOODWILL recognised for any EXCESS of consideration over the fair value of net identifiable assets acquired.
5

THE KEY DIVERGENCE – Goodwill Is Always Amortised

A systematic charge over a period not exceeding 20 years – no indefinite-life exception
UNLIKE IFRS 3 (and US GAAP's ASC 350, post-2001), which treat GOODWILL as an INDEFINITE-life asset TESTED ONLY for impairment (never systematically amortised), J-GAAP requires goodwill to be SYSTEMATICALLY AMORTISED – generally on a STRAIGHT-LINE basis – over a period the entity determines is APPROPRIATE, but NOT EXCEEDING 20 YEARS, in ADDITION to being tested for IMPAIRMENT where indicators exist. This is one of the MOST SIGNIFICANT, FREQUENTLY-CITED divergences between J-GAAP and international practice – it means Japanese companies' reported profit is SYSTEMATICALLY REDUCED by goodwill amortisation EVERY period following an acquisition, in a way that companies reporting under IFRS or US GAAP simply do NOT experience (their profit is affected ONLY if and when an impairment is triggered).
6

Negative Goodwill (Bargain Purchase Gain)

Recognised immediately in profit or loss – converged with IFRS 3
Where the fair value of NET IDENTIFIABLE assets acquired EXCEEDS the consideration transferred (a "BARGAIN PURCHASE"), the RESULTING gain is recognised IMMEDIATELY in the Statement of Income – CONSISTENT with IFRS 3's OWN treatment of negative goodwill, a genuinely CONVERGED area despite goodwill's OTHERWISE divergent treatment.
7

Non-Controlling Interests – the Partial Goodwill Approach Only

No "full goodwill" election available, unlike IFRS 3
Non-controlling interests are measured at their PROPORTIONATE SHARE of the acquiree's IDENTIFIABLE net assets (the "PARTIAL GOODWILL" method) – meaning goodwill is recognised ONLY in respect of the PARENT's OWN acquired interest, NOT the non-controlling interest's share. This is J-GAAP's ONLY available approach – UNLIKE IFRS 3, which OFFERS a transaction-by-transaction ELECTION between the partial goodwill method and the "FULL GOODWILL" method (measuring non-controlling interests at FAIR VALUE, recognising goodwill attributable to the ENTIRE acquiree, including the non-controlling portion).
8

The Narrowing of Pooling-of-Interests-Style Accounting

A convergence-driven elimination, mirroring IFRS 3 and ASC 805
Japan historically permitted a "UNITING OF INTERESTS" (pooling-of-interests-style) method for combinations meeting NARROW criteria (no identifiable acquirer, broadly EQUAL relative size, consideration entirely in voting shares) – but this eligibility has been SIGNIFICANTLY NARROWED over successive revisions, moving J-GAAP TOWARD requiring acquisition accounting for VIRTUALLY ALL business combinations, MIRRORING how BOTH IFRS 3 and US GAAP's ASC 805 ALSO eliminated pooling-of-interests accounting internationally around a broadly SIMILAR period.
9

Worked Example – Goodwill Recognition and Amortisation

A systematic annual charge, unlike an impairment-only approach
Example: An acquisition generating ¥600,000,000 of goodwill
A company acquires 100% of a target for ¥2,000,000,000 cash. The fair value of the target's identifiable net assets is ¥1,400,000,000. The acquirer determines a 15-year amortisation period is appropriate.
GOODWILL = ¥2,000,000,000 − ¥1,400,000,000 = ¥600,000,000. This is AMORTISED on a straight-line basis: ¥600,000,000 ÷ 15 = ¥40,000,000 PER YEAR, recognised as an expense EVERY year for 15 years – REGARDLESS of whether any impairment indicator exists – a MATERIAL, RECURRING charge that would NOT arise under IFRS 3 or ASC 805, where the SAME ¥600,000,000 goodwill would simply be tested ANNUALLY for impairment.
10

Worked Example – a Bargain Purchase Gain

Immediate recognition, converged with IFRS 3
Example: Acquiring a distressed target below fair value
A company acquires 100% of a distressed target for ¥500,000,000 cash. The fair value of the target's identifiable net assets is ¥620,000,000.
The company recognises a BARGAIN PURCHASE GAIN of ¥620,000,000 − ¥500,000,000 = ¥120,000,000, IMMEDIATELY in the Statement of Income – a treatment CONSISTENT with IFRS 3's own approach to negative goodwill.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Partial goodwill method
Measuring non-controlling interests at their proportionate share of identifiable net assets, recognising goodwill only for the parent's own acquired interest – J-GAAP's only available approach.
Bargain purchase gain
The gain recognised immediately when the fair value of net identifiable assets acquired exceeds the consideration transferred.
