1

Conceptual Framework Linkage

A deliberate convergence move — bringing J-GAAP into line with IAS 2
ASBJ Statement No. 9 governs the MEASUREMENT of inventories (棚卸資産) — the COST formulas an entity may use, and the requirement to write inventories DOWN when their value has genuinely DECLINED below cost. A NOTABLE feature of this Statement is that its DEVELOPMENT specifically ELIMINATED the LAST-IN, FIRST-OUT (LIFO) cost formula from acceptable J-GAAP practice — a DELIBERATE convergence move aligning Japanese practice with IAS 2's LONG-STANDING prohibition of LIFO internationally.
2

Scope

All companies holding inventories
This Statement applies to ALL companies holding inventories — merchandise, finished goods, work in progress, raw materials, and supplies — held for SALE in the ordinary course of business or CONSUMPTION in producing goods or services for sale.
3

Lower of Cost and Net Realisable Value

The core measurement principle
Inventories are measured at the LOWER of their determined COST and NET REALISABLE VALUE (正味売却価額, "net selling value") — where an item's net realisable value has FALLEN below its recorded cost, the carrying amount must be WRITTEN DOWN to reflect the LOWER, currently-recoverable amount, consistent with the general principle that assets should NOT be carried above their recoverable economic value.
4

Acceptable Cost Formulas — and the Elimination of LIFO

FIFO, weighted average, and specific identification — LIFO no longer permitted
Acceptable cost formulas under this Statement include FIRST-IN, FIRST-OUT (FIFO), WEIGHTED AVERAGE cost, and SPECIFIC IDENTIFICATION for uniquely identifiable items. LAST-IN, FIRST-OUT (LIFO) — historically PERMITTED under EARLIER Japanese practice — was SPECIFICALLY ELIMINATED as an acceptable cost formula through this Statement's development, a deliberate step bringing J-GAAP INTO ALIGNMENT with IAS 2, which has LONG PROHIBITED LIFO internationally.
5

Write-Down to Net Realisable Value — Presentation

Generally within cost of sales, unless the decline is unusual in nature or scale
A write-down to net realisable value is generally presented WITHIN cost of sales — reflecting the ORDINARY, recurring nature of typical inventory valuation declines. Where the DECLINE is UNUSUAL in nature or SIGNIFICANT in scale (e.g. reflecting a natural disaster or an extraordinary market disruption), it may instead be presented as a SEPARATE, distinctly labelled loss.
6

Reversal of Write-Downs

Permitted where net realisable value subsequently recovers
Where an item's NET REALISABLE VALUE subsequently RECOVERS in a LATER period (after a previous write-down), the write-down may be REVERSED — but ONLY up to the ORIGINAL cost figure, NEVER above it — consistent with IAS 2's own reversal requirement, and reflecting that the write-down was always intended to capture a genuine, though not necessarily PERMANENT, decline in value.
7

Worked Example — a Write-Down to Net Realisable Value

Writing inventory down to its recoverable amount
Example: Obsolete finished goods inventory
A company holds finished goods inventory with a recorded cost of ¥60,000,000. Due to a sudden shift in market demand, the estimated net realisable value has fallen to ¥45,000,000.
The company writes the inventory DOWN by ¥15,000,000 (¥60,000,000 − ¥45,000,000), recognising the amount generally WITHIN cost of sales, bringing the carrying amount to ¥45,000,000 — reflecting the LOWER of cost and net realisable value.
8

Worked Example — Reversal of a Prior Write-Down

Recovering value, capped at original cost
Example: Demand recovers in a later period
Continuing the prior example, market demand RECOVERS in the following period, and the SAME inventory's net realisable value rises back to ¥58,000,000 (still below the original ¥60,000,000 cost).
The company REVERSES ¥13,000,000 of the prior write-down (¥58,000,000 − ¥45,000,000), bringing the carrying amount up to ¥58,000,000 — but NOT above the ORIGINAL ¥60,000,000 cost, even if net realisable value were to recover further still.

Critical Judgments & Estimates

Where judgement matters most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether an inventory value decline is ordinary/recurring versus unusual, affecting presentation
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Estimated net realisable value, based on expected selling price less estimated costs to complete and sell

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — and the Contrast With US GAAP

Comparison across all three major frameworks
FeatureJ-GAAP (ASBJ Statement No. 9)IFRS (IAS 2) / US GAAP
LIFO permittedNO — eliminatedIFRS: NO (never permitted). US GAAP: YES — LIFO remains permitted
Reversal of write-downsPERMITTED, capped at original costIFRS: PERMITTED, capped at original cost — converged. US GAAP: NOT permitted once written down
Japan's elimination of LIFO was a DELIBERATE step TOWARD IFRS, moving AWAY from where US GAAP still stands — one of the clearer examples of J-GAAP choosing IFRS convergence over alignment with US practice on a SPECIFIC measurement question.
