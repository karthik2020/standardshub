Objective, Scope & Definitions (paras. 1–8)

Objective & scope
Requires disclosures enabling users to evaluate (a) the SIGNIFICANCE of financial instruments for the entity's financial position and performance, and (b) the NATURE AND EXTENT of risks arising from financial instruments, and how the entity manages them. Complements IPSAS 28 (presentation) and IPSAS 41 (recognition/measurement).
Applies to BOTH recognized and unrecognized financial instruments — e.g. some loan commitments are unrecognized under IPSAS 41 but still within THIS Standard's disclosure scope (para. 4).
Key definitions (para. 8)
Credit risk
The risk one party to a financial instrument will cause a financial loss for the other by failing to discharge an obligation.
Liquidity risk
The risk of encountering difficulty meeting obligations associated with financial liabilities settled by delivering cash/another financial asset.
Market risk
The risk fair value or future cash flows will fluctuate from market price changes — comprising CURRENCY risk, INTEREST RATE risk, and OTHER PRICE risk.
Loans payable
Financial liabilities OTHER THAN short-term trade payables on normal credit terms.

Classes of Financial Instruments & Significance for Financial Position (paras. 9–11)

Grouping into classes (para. 9)
Where disclosure BY CLASS is required, the entity groups instruments into classes appropriate to the NATURE of the information disclosed, taking account of the instruments' characteristics — providing sufficient information to permit RECONCILIATION to the SOFP line items.
Mandatory SOFP categories (para. 11) — IPSAS 41-aligned
Financial assets at fair value through surplus or deficit — designated vs. mandatorily so classified, shown separately Financial liabilities at fair value through surplus or deficit — designated vs. held-for-trading, shown separately Financial assets measured at AMORTIZED COST Financial liabilities measured at AMORTIZED COST Financial assets at fair value through NET ASSETS/EQUITY — showing separately those under IPSAS 41 para. 41 vs. equity instruments designated under para. 106

Fair Value Designation Disclosures (paras. 12–17)

Designated financial ASSETS (para. 12)
Maximum exposure to credit risk at period-end Amount by which related credit derivatives/similar instruments MITIGATE that exposure Change in fair value (period + cumulative) attributable to changes in the asset's OWN credit risk Change in fair value of related credit derivatives (period + cumulative)
Designated financial LIABILITIES (para. 13)
Where the entity presents credit-risk-attributable fair value changes in net assets/equity (per IPSAS 41 para. 108), disclose the cumulative amount of that change, and any transferred amount within net assets/equity, plus the difference between the liability's carrying amount and the contractually required amount at maturity.
4

Significance for Financial Performance — Statement of Financial Performance Disclosures

Items of revenue, expense, gains, and losses — by category
An entity discloses items of revenue, expense, gains, or losses, EITHER on the face of the statements or in the notes, showing separately for each of the SOFP measurement categories (§2): net gains/losses (with an indication of whether interest revenue/expense is included), total interest revenue and expense (calculated using the effective interest method), fee revenue/expense (other than amounts in effective-interest calculations), and impairment losses for each class of financial asset.
5

Other Disclosures — Accounting Policies, Hedge Accounting & Fair Value

Accounting policies & hedge accounting
Measurement basis (or bases) used, and other accounting policies relevant to understanding the statements (IPSAS 1) Hedge accounting: a description of each hedge TYPE, the hedging instruments and their fair values, and the nature of hedged risks — separately for fair value, cash flow, and net investment hedges (cross-ref IPSAS 29 §3–§6) For cash flow hedges: periods when cash flows are expected, when they'll affect surplus/deficit, and any amounts previously in net assets/equity now expected to NOT be recoverable
Fair value disclosures
Fair value for EACH class of financial asset/liability, comparable to its carrying amount The methods and (when a valuation technique is used) assumptions applied in estimating fair value Whether fair value measurements use a QUOTED market price or are ESTIMATED using a valuation technique
Fair value disclosure is NOT required when the carrying amount is a reasonable approximation of fair value (e.g. short-term trade receivables), or for instruments where fair value cannot be reliably measured.

Concessionary Loans (paras. 37–37A)

What they are
Loans granted on BELOW-MARKET terms — e.g. loans to developing countries, small farms, student loans for university/college, and housing loans for low-income families.
Required disclosure — for loans at amortized cost (para. 37) or at fair value (para. 37A)
A RECONCILIATION between opening and closing carrying amounts — nominal value of new loans, fair value adjustment on initial recognition, loans repaid, impairment losses (or the fair value adjustment during the period, for fair-value-measured loans), discount unwinding, other changes The NOMINAL VALUE of loans at period-end Purpose and terms of the various loan types — including, for fair-value loans, the NATURE OF THE CONCESSION VALUATION ASSUMPTIONS used

Nature & Extent of Risks Arising from Financial Instruments (paras. 38–42)

Qualitative disclosures — per risk type (para. 40)
The EXPOSURES to risk, and how they arise OBJECTIVES, POLICIES, and PROCESSES for managing the risk, and methods used to measure it Any CHANGES from the previous period
Pairing qualitative WITH quantitative disclosures helps users link related information and build an overall risk picture (para. 39A).
Quantitative disclosures — per risk type (para. 41)
Summary quantitative data on exposure at period-end, BASED ON information reported internally to key management personnel (IPSAS 20) — e.g. the governing body or CEO The specific disclosures required by §8–§10 (credit, liquidity, market risk), to the extent not already covered by the internal-reporting-based disclosure CONCENTRATIONS of risk, if not already apparent
If period-end quantitative data is UNREPRESENTATIVE of the entity's risk exposure during the period, further representative information must be provided (para. 42).
8

Credit Risk (paras. 42A–43)

Objective & three disclosure pillars (para. 42B)
Credit risk management PRACTICES, and how they relate to expected credit loss recognition/measurement — including methods, assumptions, and information used Quantitative and qualitative information on amounts arising from EXPECTED CREDIT LOSSES, including changes and reasons for those changes The entity's credit risk EXPOSURE, including significant concentrations
Cross-referenced incorporation from a management commentary or risk report (available on the same terms, at the same time) is permitted — without it, the financial statements are INCOMPLETE (para. 42C).
Key required disclosures
How significant credit risk INCREASES since initial recognition are determined, including "low credit risk" treatment and rebutting the 30-days-past-due presumption (IPSAS 41 paras. 82–83) Definitions of DEFAULT, write-off policy, and credit-impairment determination A RECONCILIATION of the loss allowance, opening to closing, by class and by expected-credit-loss stage (12-month vs. lifetime) Credit risk EXPOSURE by credit risk rating grade, split by ECL stage Maximum credit risk exposure, and collateral/credit enhancement description, for instruments OUTSIDE the IPSAS 41 impairment model (para. 43) Assets obtained by taking possession of collateral — nature, carrying amount, and disposal policy (para. 45)

Liquidity Risk (para. 46)

The three required disclosures
A MATURITY ANALYSIS for non-derivative financial liabilities (including issued financial guarantee contracts), showing REMAINING CONTRACTUAL maturities A maturity analysis for DERIVATIVE financial liabilities, where contractual maturities are essential to understanding cash flow timing A description of how the entity MANAGES the liquidity risk inherent in both of the above
The maturity analysis convention — undiscounted contractual cash flows bucketed by remaining time band — mirrors standard practice for liquidity risk transparency in both public and private sector financial reporting.

Market Risk — Sensitivity Analysis & Transfers of Financial Assets (paras. 47–49H)

Two routes to satisfying the sensitivity analysis requirement
STANDARD ROUTE (PARA. 47)
A sensitivity analysis per market risk TYPE, showing the impact on surplus/deficit and net assets/equity from reasonably possible changes in the relevant risk variable Methods and assumptions used, and any period-to-period changes with reasons
ALTERNATIVE ROUTE (PARA. 48)
If the entity manages risk using an INTERDEPENDENCY-reflecting method (e.g. value-at-risk), it may use that instead — disclosing the method, main parameters/assumptions, its objective, and any limitations affecting how fully it reflects the assets/liabilities' fair value.
If year-end sensitivity data is unrepresentative of exposure DURING the year, that fact and the reason must be disclosed (para. 49).
Transfers of financial assets (paras. 49A–49H) — a single dedicated note
Where an asset is transferred but NOT derecognized in its entirety, or the entity has CONTINUING INVOLVEMENT, disclosures (presented together in ONE note) must let users understand the relationship between the transferred assets and associated liabilities, and evaluate the nature/risks of any continuing involvement.
Nature of the transferred assets, and the risks/rewards of ownership retained Nature of the relationship between transferred assets and associated liabilities, including USE RESTRICTIONS Where the liability counterparty has recourse ONLY to the transferred assets — a schedule of fair values (assets, liabilities, and the net position)

Effective Date & Amendment Timeline (paras. 50–53)

Amendment history at a glance
Base Standard: periods beginning on/after 1 January 2013 — may not be applied before that date unless the entity also applies IPSAS 28 and IPSAS 29 (para. 51).
EffectiveChange
1 Jan 2017First-time-adoption amendments (IPSAS 33); IPSAS 35, 37 & 38 scope references
1 Jan 2018Paras. 6, 7 deleted (Applicability of IPSAS); para. 3 amended (IPSAS 39)
1 Jan 2023Extensive rewrite — IPSAS 41, Financial Instruments (Aug 2018): new credit-risk expected-credit-loss disclosure package (paras. 42A–42N, §8), transfers-of-assets requirements, and measurement-category alignment throughout (§2)
1 Jan 2025Para. 8 — IPSAS 46, Measurement
Unlike IPSAS 29, IPSAS 30 was substantially REWRITTEN rather than gutted when IPSAS 41 arrived — its disclosure framework needed updating to match IPSAS 41's expected-credit-loss impairment model, but the Standard itself remains fully live.

Relationship to IAS/IFRS — Comparison with IFRS 7

Comparison — IPSAS 30 vs IFRS 7
IPSAS 30 is drawn primarily from IFRS 7, Financial Instruments: Disclosures (originally issued 2005, amendments to April 2009).
TopicDifference
Concessionary loansIPSAS 30 requires specific disclosures (§6) — a public-sector-specific need; IFRS 7 has no equivalent
Terminology"Revenue," "statement of financial performance," "net assets/equity" (IPSAS 30) vs "income," "statement of comprehensive income," "equity" (IFRS 7)
Notably brief comparison — IPSAS 30 tracks IFRS 7 closely in structure and substance, since financial instrument disclosure principles translate well across sectors once the concessionary-loan carve-out is addressed.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Credit risk
The risk one party to a financial instrument causes a financial loss for the other by failing to discharge an obligation.
Credit risk rating grades
A rating of credit risk based on the risk of a default occurring on the financial instrument.
Liquidity risk
The risk of difficulty meeting obligations from financial liabilities settled by delivering cash or another financial asset.
Market risk
The risk fair value or future cash flows fluctuate from market price changes — currency, interest rate, and other price risk.
Concessionary loans
Loans granted on below-market terms — e.g. to developing countries, students, or low-income households.
Loans payable
Financial liabilities other than short-term trade payables on normal credit terms.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position Notes Notes to the financial statements
