SIC-21 – Income Taxes–Recovery of Revalued Non-Depreciable Assets
Historical reference note · Issued 2000 · Withdrawn 2012
⚠ WITHDRAWN – Superseded by amendments to IAS 12 ("Deferred Tax: Recovery of Underlying Assets"), effective for periods beginning 1 January 2012
What SIC-21 addressed
SIC-21 addressed how to measure DEFERRED TAX relating to a NON-DEPRECIABLE asset – such as land – carried under the IAS 16 revaluation model, specifically which tax rate and tax base to use when recovery of the asset's carrying amount would normally be expected to occur through SALE rather than through ongoing use.
The conclusion it reached
Deferred tax on such non-depreciable revalued assets should be measured on the basis of the tax consequences of recovering the carrying amount through SALE – since, unlike depreciable assets, a non-depreciable asset such as land does not generate economic benefit through consumption/use in a way that would justify measuring deferred tax on a "use" basis.
Where this guidance lives today
Superseded byAmendments to IAS 12 Income Taxes, "Deferred Tax: Recovery of Underlying Assets" (2010)
Why withdrawnThe 2010 amendments GENERALISED SIC-21's approach into a rebuttable presumption that the carrying amount of investment property measured at fair value (and, by extension, other non-depreciable assets under the revaluation model) will be recovered through sale – broader in scope than SIC-21's original non-depreciable-asset-specific guidance
Current statusThe underlying "recovery through sale" logic remains good law today – it simply now sits in IAS 12 itself, extended to a wider population of assets
