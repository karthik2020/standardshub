IAS 35 — Discontinuing Operations
Extended reference poster · Historical standard · Initial disclosure event · Worked examples · Mapping to IFRS 5
⚠️ SUPERSEDED STANDARD — IAS 35 was withdrawn and replaced by IFRS 5 Non-current Assets Held for Sale and Discontinued Operations, effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2005. It is retained here for historical reference and because its "discontinuing operations" concept is the direct ancestor of IFRS 5's "discontinued operations" and "held for sale" classification. For current requirements, always apply IFRS 5.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

Why users needed visibility into operations being wound down
The Conceptual Framework's predictive value objective requires users to distinguish results that will CONTINUE from those that will NOT — mixing a discontinuing division's results anonymously into total continuing performance would badly mislead a user trying to forecast future cash flows. IAS 35 required specific DISCLOSURE (not separate recognition/measurement) once a discontinuance decision crossed a defined threshold.
HOW IAS 35 MAPPED TO THE FRAMEWORK
Predictive value — separately disclosing discontinuing operations' results helped users project FUTURE performance based only on operations that would actually continue Relevance — the moment an operation crossed IAS 35's "initial disclosure event" threshold (Section 4), its results became separately relevant information, distinct from the entity's core continuing business A DISCLOSURE-focused, not measurement-focused, standard — IAS 35 notably did NOT introduce special recognition or measurement rules; it required disclosure of information ALREADY measured under other Standards, structured to highlight the discontinuance
IAS 35's key conceptual limitation — a genuinely DISCLOSURE-only standard, with no dedicated measurement or asset held-for-sale classification concept — is precisely what IFRS 5 later introduced. IFRS 5 added the "held for sale" classification and lower-of-carrying-amount-and-fair-value-less-costs-to-sell MEASUREMENT rule that IAS 35 entirely lacked.
Why "no separate measurement basis" was IAS 35's defining feature
Unlike its successor, IAS 35 deliberately did NOT change how assets/liabilities of a discontinuing operation were measured — it operated PURELY at the disclosure and presentation level.
Framework conceptIAS 35's treatment
Recognition/measurementUNCHANGED — assets/liabilities continued to be measured under their normal applicable Standards (IAS 16, IAS 2, IAS 36, etc.)
Presentation/disclosureSEPARATE disclosure required once the initial disclosure event occurred, to isolate discontinuing operation results from continuing operations
ℹ️ Qualitative characteristics in action: IAS 35's disclosure-only approach traded off some relevance (no forced write-down to fair value less costs to sell, unlike IFRS 5) for simplicity — a limitation that eventually justified full replacement.

Scope & Applicability (Historical)

What IAS 35 covered
IAS 35 established principles for reporting information about discontinuing operations, aiming to enhance the ability of users of financial statements to make projections about an entity's cash flows, earnings-generating capacity, and financial position — by separating information about discontinuing operations from information about continuing operations.
This "enhancing predictive ability" objective statement was carried forward almost verbatim into IFRS 5's own stated purpose for discontinued operation reporting.
Key definitions you must know
Discontinuing operation
A component of an entity that the entity, pursuant to a SINGLE PLAN, is either disposing of substantially in its entirety, or disposing of piecemeal, or terminating through abandonment — AND that represents a separate major line of business or geographical area of operations, and can be distinguished operationally and for financial reporting purposes.
Initial disclosure event
The date of a binding sale agreement or the date the entity's board (or equivalent) approves and announces a detailed formal plan — whichever occurs EARLIER — triggering IAS 35's disclosure requirements.
3

Identifying a Discontinuing Operation

The definition's three cumulative requirements
Disposal pursuant to a SINGLE PLAN — whether through a single transaction, piecemeal disposal, or abandonment Represents a SEPARATE MAJOR line of business or geographical area of operations — a genuinely significant portion of the entity, not a minor asset or department Can be DISTINGUISHED operationally and for financial reporting purposes, both physically and operationally, and for internal financial reporting purposes
⚠️ The "SEPARATE MAJOR line of business or geographical area" threshold was a genuinely HIGH bar — routine restructurings, closures of individual stores, or minor product line discontinuations typically did NOT qualify, requiring instead ordinary IAS 8/IAS 37 treatment rather than IAS 35's special disclosure regime.
4

The Initial Disclosure Event (with worked example)

The trigger date, precisely defined
Initial disclosure event = EARLIER of: (a) the entity entering into a BINDING SALE AGREEMENT for substantially all the assets attributable to the discontinuing operation; or (b) the entity's BOARD approving a DETAILED, FORMAL PLAN for discontinuance, and making an ANNOUNCEMENT of the plan
A detailed, formal plan had to include, at minimum, all major features of the plan — including the assets/liabilities to be disposed of, expected disposal method/timing, gains/losses expected, and expected date of completion.
Worked example — identifying the disclosure trigger date
Example 1: Board approval vs binding sale agreement
A company's board approves and publicly announces a detailed plan to discontinue its industrial machinery division on 15 March 20X2. A binding sale agreement for the division is signed with a buyer on 30 September 20X2.
Initial disclosure event = 15 March 20X2 (the EARLIER date — board approval and announcement) — the entity must begin IAS 35 disclosures in the FIRST financial statements (interim or annual) covering the period in which the initial disclosure event occurred, NOT waiting until the binding sale agreement is signed six months later.
5

Recognition & Measurement — No Separate Basis

IAS 35 explicitly did NOT introduce new recognition/measurement rules
IAS 35 required an entity to apply RELEVANT IFRS Standards in accounting for and reporting the effects of discontinuance — INCLUDING recognising and measuring any impairment (IAS 36), recognising the initial recognition and any changes to provisions per IAS 37 (e.g. restructuring provisions), and recognising employee termination benefits per IAS 19. The standard's OWN, dedicated contribution was purely additional DISCLOSURE requirements laid on top of these ordinarily applicable Standards.
✕ There was NO IAS 35 concept equivalent to IFRS 5's "held for sale" classification with its lower-of-carrying-amount-and-fair-value-less-costs-to-sell measurement floor — assets of a discontinuing operation continued to be depreciated, amortised, and tested for impairment entirely under their NORMAL applicable Standards throughout the disposal process.
6

Presentation on the Face of the Statements

Separate line item presentation, once the initial disclosure event occurred
Once the initial disclosure event occurred, an entity presented, on the face of the SOPL, the amount of PRE-TAX PROFIT OR LOSS from ordinary activities attributable to the discontinuing operation, together with the related INCOME TAX EXPENSE — either as a single amount, or with further analysis provided in the notes.
This "single net amount, further analysed in the notes" presentation approach is directly reflected in IFRS 5's own current requirement to present discontinued operation results as a single line on the face of the SOPL, with detailed analysis in the notes.

Disclosure Requirements (Historical)

Required disclosures once the initial disclosure event occurred
GENERAL DISCLOSURES
A description of the discontinuing operation, and the business/geographical segment(s) it was reported in The date and nature of the initial disclosure event The date/expected date of completion of the disposal, if known/determinable The carrying amounts, as of the reporting date, of the total assets and total liabilities to be disposed of
FINANCIAL EFFECT DISCLOSURES
Revenue, expenses, pre-tax profit/loss from ordinary activities, and the income tax expense attributable to the discontinuing operation for the current period Net cash flows attributable to operating, investing, and financing activities of the discontinuing operation Amounts of any expected gain/loss on the disposal, or a statement that no such estimate can be made
8

Updating Disclosures in Subsequent Periods

Continued disclosure until the disposal was complete
An entity was required to include, in its financial statements for periods SUBSEQUENT to the one in which the initial disclosure event occurred, a description of any significant changes in the AMOUNT OR TIMING of cash flows relating to the assets/liabilities to be disposed of, or any other resources to be transferred, together with the CIRCUMSTANCES causing those changes — this continued until disposal was substantially COMPLETE, essentially maintaining a running commentary on the disposal's progress across successive reporting periods.

Worked Example — Full Disclosure Timeline

Tracking a discontinuance across two reporting periods
Example 2: A retail chain discontinuing its overseas division
A company's board approves and announces a detailed formal plan to discontinue its overseas retail division on 1 November 20X1 (year end 31 December). The division's revenue for the period 1 January–31 December 20X1 was $12,000,000, with a pre-tax loss of $800,000 and related tax benefit of $160,000. Total assets to be disposed of at 31 December 20X1: $6,500,000; total liabilities: $2,100,000. By the following year end (31 December 20X2), the division has been fully sold at a $500,000 pre-tax loss on disposal, and the timeline was updated for a 3-month delay caused by regulatory approval issues.
20X1 financial statements (year the initial disclosure event occurred): disclose the description, initial disclosure event date (1 November 20X1), carrying amounts of assets ($6,500,000) and liabilities ($2,100,000) to be disposed of, and the full-year revenue/pre-tax loss/tax figures for the division, presented as a single net line on the face of the SOPL.

20X2 financial statements: disclose the completed disposal, the actual $500,000 loss on disposal, and an explanation of the 3-month timeline delay caused by regulatory approval — the required ongoing "commentary on changes" disclosure.

Critical Judgments & Estimates (Historical)

Where management judgement mattered most
SIGNIFICANT JUDGEMENTS
Whether an operation genuinely represented a "separate MAJOR line of business or geographical area" — a materiality-type judgement with no bright-line quantitative threshold Whether a board plan was sufficiently "detailed and formal," and had been genuinely "announced," to constitute the initial disclosure event
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES
Expected gain/loss on disposal, where reasonably estimable Expected timing of completion of the disposal plan
⚠️ Because IAS 35 lacked a dedicated MEASUREMENT trigger (unlike IFRS 5's held-for-sale write-down), the ONLY consequence of crossing the initial disclosure event threshold was disclosure — a materially LESS consequential accounting event than under the current IFRS 5 regime, where classification triggers an immediate measurement change.

IAS 35 vs IFRS 5 — What Changed

Comparison — the transition from IAS 35 to IFRS 5
FeatureIAS 35 (superseded)IFRS 5 (current)
Measurement change on classificationNONE — assets continued to be measured under their normal Standards throughoutMANDATORY — assets classified as held for sale are measured at the LOWER of carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell; depreciation/amortisation CEASES
Trigger event"Initial disclosure event" — binding sale agreement OR board approval/announcement, whichever earlier"Highly probable" sale criteria met — a more detailed, prescriptive set of conditions (active marketing, reasonable price, expected sale within 12 months)
Presentation of held-for-sale ASSETS on the SOFPNo separate SOFP presentation requirement for individual assetsMANDATORY separate presentation of held-for-sale assets/liabilities as distinct SOFP line items
Scale/threshold for qualification"Separate major line of business or geographical area""Component of an entity" that either has been disposed of or is classified as held for sale, representing a SEPARATE major line of business or geographical area (similar language, refined criteria)
Individual non-current assets not part of a "major" disposalOutside IAS 35's scope entirely (no held-for-sale concept existed)WITHIN IFRS 5's scope as an individual "held for sale" asset, even without qualifying as a full discontinued operation
The single biggest change was IFRS 5's introduction of a genuine MEASUREMENT consequence (the held-for-sale write-down floor and depreciation cessation) — something IAS 35 never had, since it operated purely as a disclosure standard layered on top of unchanged underlying measurement. IFRS 5 also broadened scope to cover INDIVIDUAL assets held for sale, not just entire major disposal groups.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Component of an entity
Operations and cash flows that can be clearly distinguished, operationally and for financial reporting purposes, from the rest of the entity — the building block of the "discontinuing operation" definition.
Binding sale agreement
An enforceable agreement that includes all necessary terms to allow completion of the sale, and, given only a REMOTE likelihood of failure, is expected to be completed.
Detailed formal plan
A plan that includes, at minimum: assets/liabilities to be disposed of; expected disposal method; timing; disposal proceeds/gains-losses expected; and the expected completion date.
Ordinary activities
Activities undertaken by an entity as part of its business, and related activities in which the entity engages in furtherance of, incidental to, or arising from those activities.
Abandonment
Terminating the use of assets, or ceasing operations, without a formal sale — one of the three ways a discontinuance could occur under IAS 35's definition (single sale, piecemeal disposal, or abandonment).
Geographical segment
A distinguishable component providing products/services within a particular economic environment, subject to risks/returns different from other economic environments — one of the two qualifying "major" categories under IAS 35.
Financial statement key
SOFP Statement of financial position (balance sheet) SOPL Statement of profit or loss (income statement) OCI Other comprehensive income Equity Statement of changes in equity
