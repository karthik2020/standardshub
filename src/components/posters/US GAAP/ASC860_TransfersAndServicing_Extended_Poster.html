Conceptual Framework Linkage

Was it really a sale, or just borrowing dressed up as one?
When an entity TRANSFERS financial assets (receivables, loans, mortgage pools) to another party, a fundamental question arises: has the transferor genuinely GIVEN UP its economic stake, warranting DERECOGNITION (sale accounting) – or has it merely borrowed money using those assets as COLLATERAL, in which case the assets should REMAIN on its balance sheet (secured borrowing accounting)? ASC 860 answers this through a "SURRENDER OF CONTROL" test (Section 3).
A genuinely different analytical starting point than IFRS 9
ASC 860's CONTROL-based test is essentially the SOLE gatekeeping analysis for derecognition under US GAAP – there's no separate risks-and-rewards gate. IFRS 9, by contrast, uses a RISKS-AND-REWARDS test as its PRIMARY analysis, falling back to a control test only in specific circumstances – a genuine STRUCTURAL divergence in how each framework approaches the same underlying question (Section 7).
2

Scope

Transfers of financial assets, servicing, and secured borrowings
ASC 860 governs the accounting for TRANSFERS of financial assets (including participations, securitizations, and factoring arrangements), the accounting for SERVICING of financial assets (whether retained by a transferor or acquired separately), and the distinction between SALE and SECURED BORROWING treatment for such transfers.
3

The Three Conditions for Sale Accounting – Surrender of Control

ALL THREE conditions must be met
ConditionRequirement
1The transferred assets have been ISOLATED from the transferor – put presumptively beyond the reach of the transferor AND its creditors, even in bankruptcy or receivership (a genuine "TRUE SALE" legal analysis)
2The TRANSFEREE has the RIGHT to pledge or exchange the transferred assets (or, for a securitization entity, its beneficial interest holders have that right), free of conditions that constrain that right and provide the transferor more than a trivial benefit
3The transferor does NOT maintain EFFECTIVE CONTROL over the transferred assets – e.g. no agreement requiring the transferor to repurchase the assets before maturity, and no unilateral ability to reclaim specific transferred assets
ALL THREE conditions must be satisfied for SALE accounting; failing even ONE means the transfer is accounted for as a SECURED BORROWING instead (Section 9).
4

Legal Isolation – the "True Sale" Analysis

A genuinely legal question, often requiring a legal opinion
Legal isolation asks whether the transferred assets would genuinely be BEYOND THE REACH of the transferor and its creditors – INCLUDING in the event of the transferor's bankruptcy – often requiring a formal LEGAL "TRUE SALE" opinion from counsel, particularly for securitization structures, confirming that a bankruptcy court would NOT treat the transfer as merely a secured financing subject to substantive consolidation with the transferor's estate.
5

Transferee's Right to Pledge or Exchange

Genuine, unconstrained rights over the transferred assets
The TRANSFEREE (or, for securitizations, the beneficial interest holders) must have the practical ability to PLEDGE or EXCHANGE the transferred assets, WITHOUT conditions that both CONSTRAIN that ability AND provide the TRANSFEROR more than a trivial benefit – a condition designed to catch arrangements where the transferor retains an effectively controlling degree of influence over what the "transferee" can do with the assets.
6

Absence of Effective Control by the Transferor

No repurchase agreements or unilateral reclaim rights
The transferor must NOT maintain effective control – indicators of RETAINED effective control include an agreement that BOTH obligates the transferor to repurchase the assets AND provides the transferee with the right to require repurchase (before maturity, at a fixed/determinable price), or the transferor's UNILATERAL ability to reclaim SPECIFIC transferred assets (as opposed to merely substantially-the-same assets in a general sense).
7

THE KEY DIVERGENCE – Control-Only Test vs IFRS 9's Risk-and-Rewards Approach

Two genuinely different analytical starting points
US GAAP's ASC 860 uses a CONTROL-BASED test as its PRIMARY (and essentially ONLY) gatekeeping analysis for derecognition – there is no separate risks-and-rewards threshold that must be cleared first. IFRS 9 takes a genuinely DIFFERENT structural approach: it FIRST asks whether the transferor has transferred SUBSTANTIALLY ALL the RISKS AND REWARDS of ownership (e.g. credit risk, prepayment risk, interest rate risk) – if YES, derecognise; if the transferor has RETAINED substantially all risks and rewards, CONTINUE recognising the asset (secured borrowing-style outcome); ONLY in the INTERMEDIATE case – where risks and rewards are NEITHER substantially transferred NOR substantially retained – does IFRS 9 fall back to a SECONDARY CONTROL test (has the transferee obtained the practical ability to unilaterally sell the asset to an unrelated third party?) to determine whether partial derecognition is appropriate. This is a genuine STRUCTURAL divergence: identical transfer arrangements can, in principle, be analysed to DIFFERENT conclusions depending on which framework's STARTING analytical gate (control vs risks-and-rewards) is applied first, even though both frameworks ultimately care about the same underlying economic substance.
8

Sale Accounting Mechanics

Derecognition, with any retained interests recognised at fair value
Once all three sale conditions are met, the transferor DERECOGNISES the transferred financial assets, recognises any assets OBTAINED and liabilities INCURRED as part of the transfer (e.g. a SERVICING asset, if servicing is retained, or any RETAINED interest in the transferred assets) at FAIR VALUE, and recognises a GAIN or LOSS for the difference between the proceeds (plus any retained/obtained items) and the carrying amount of the assets transferred.
9

Secured Borrowing Accounting (Failed Sale)

Assets stay on the books, proceeds recorded as a liability
If ANY of the three sale conditions is NOT met, the transfer is accounted for as a SECURED BORROWING – the transferred assets REMAIN on the transferor's balance sheet (no derecognition), and the CASH PROCEEDS received are recorded as a LIABILITY (essentially collateralised debt), with interest expense recognised on that liability over time.
10

Servicing Assets and Liabilities

Recognised when servicing is retained and not proportionate to the benefits of ownership
When an entity SELLS financial assets but RETAINS the right/obligation to SERVICE them (e.g. collecting mortgage payments on behalf of the new owner), a SERVICING asset (if compensation exceeds an adequate arm's-length fee) or SERVICING liability (if compensation is less than adequate) is recognised – measured initially at FAIR VALUE. Entities may elect, on a CLASS-BY-CLASS basis, to subsequently measure a class of servicing assets/liabilities using either the FAIR VALUE method or the AMORTISATION method.
11

Repurchase Agreements (Repos)

Typically fail the effective control test, resulting in secured borrowing treatment
A typical REPURCHASE AGREEMENT (repo) – where the transferor sells securities while simultaneously agreeing to REPURCHASE substantially the same securities at a fixed price before maturity – generally FAILS the "absence of effective control" condition (Section 6), since the agreement both obligates repurchase AND typically involves substantially-the-same assets at a fixed price – resulting in SECURED BORROWING treatment rather than sale accounting, consistent with the economic substance of a repo as a form of collateralised financing.
12

Worked Example – Sale Accounting (All Three Conditions Met)

A genuine transfer of receivables
Example: Selling a pool of trade receivables
An entity sells a $10,000,000 pool of trade receivables (carrying amount $9,800,000, given expected credit losses) to an unrelated finance company for $9,900,000 cash, with no recourse, no repurchase obligation, and the buyer free to resell the receivables to anyone. Legal counsel confirms the transfer would survive the transferor's bankruptcy as a true sale.
All three conditions are met: (1) legal isolation confirmed by counsel; (2) the finance company can freely pledge/sell the receivables; (3) no repurchase obligation or unilateral reclaim right. The entity DERECOGNISES the $9,800,000 receivables and recognises a GAIN of $9,900,000 − $9,800,000 = $100,000.
13

Worked Example – Secured Borrowing (Effective Control Retained)

Failing condition 3
Example: A repurchase agreement
An entity "sells" $5,000,000 of government bonds to a counterparty for $4,900,000 cash, simultaneously agreeing to repurchase the SAME bonds in 30 days for $4,910,000.
Since the entity is BOTH obligated to repurchase the SAME bonds AND the price is fixed, condition 3 (absence of effective control) FAILS. The transaction is accounted for as a SECURED BORROWING: the bonds REMAIN on the entity's balance sheet, the $4,900,000 received is recorded as a LIABILITY, and the $10,000 difference ($4,910,000 − $4,900,000) is recognised as INTEREST EXPENSE over the 30-day term.
14

Worked Example – Servicing Asset Recognition

Retaining servicing on a mortgage sale
Example: Selling mortgages while retaining servicing rights
An entity sells a $20,000,000 mortgage pool (qualifying for sale accounting), retaining the right to service the loans for a fee of 0.30% annually – determined to be MORE than an adequate arm's-length servicing fee (0.25%) for this type of loan.
Since the retained servicing fee EXCEEDS an adequate arm's-length fee, the entity recognises a SERVICING ASSET at its FAIR VALUE at the sale date (representing the present value of the excess future servicing fees expected to be collected) – this servicing asset is included as part of the proceeds/retained interests in computing the overall gain/loss on the sale.

ASC 860 vs IFRS 9 – Comprehensive Comparison

Comparison – ASC 860 vs IFRS 9's derecognition provisions
FeatureUS GAAP (ASC 860)IFRS (IFRS 9)
Primary derecognition testCONTROL – three conditions, no separate risks/rewards gateRISKS AND REWARDS TRANSFER, assessed first
Secondary/fallback testNone – control is essentially the sole testControl test used ONLY in the intermediate case (risks/rewards neither substantially transferred nor retained)
Partial derecognition possibilityLess structurally prominent as a distinct outcome categoryExplicit intermediate outcome – partial derecognition based on continuing involvement
Repo treatmentGenerally secured borrowing (fails effective control test)Generally secured borrowing (fails risks/rewards transfer test) – same practical outcome via a different route
Despite the structural difference in ANALYTICAL SEQUENCE, both frameworks tend to reach the SAME practical conclusion for straightforward transactions (e.g. repos are secured borrowings under both) – the divergence becomes most consequential for COMPLEX or PARTIAL transfer arrangements where the analytical starting point genuinely can change the outcome.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Legal isolation
The requirement that transferred assets be presumptively beyond the reach of the transferor and its creditors, even in bankruptcy – the "true sale" analysis.
Effective control
Retained influence over transferred assets, such as through a repurchase agreement or unilateral reclaim right, that precludes sale accounting.
Servicing asset/liability
An asset or liability recognised when an entity retains the right/obligation to service transferred financial assets for compensation that is more or less than adequate.
