ASBJ Statement No. 12 – Accounting Standard for Quarterly Financial Reporting
Extended reference poster · Japan GAAP (J-GAAP) · 四半期財務諸表に関する会計基準 · LARGELY SUPERSEDED by ASBJ Statement No. 33/37 · JPY examples
LARGELY SUPERSEDED STATUS: Japan's mandatory quarterly financial statement regime, historically established by this Statement, has been LARGELY REPLACED as part of a broader 2024–2025 disclosure reform – moving toward SEMI-ANNUAL statutory financial statements (ASBJ Statement No. 33, then reworked into ASBJ Statement No. 37, Interim Financial Reporting) for the first and third quarters, integrated with stock exchange summary ("kessan tanshin") disclosure. This poster explains the historical quarterly regime and the direction of the reform; consult ASBJ's current materials for the precise, up-to-date scope.

Conceptual Framework Linkage

A once-mandatory regime, now folded into a lighter-touch semi-annual framework
ASBJ Statement No. 12 HISTORICALLY established Japan's MANDATORY quarterly financial reporting regime for listed companies – introduced following EARLY-2000s reforms to the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act (金融商品取引法) requiring listed companies to report FOUR times a year rather than just semi-annually or annually. This regime has since been LARGELY SUPERSEDED as part of a broader REGULATORY reform reducing the statutory reporting BURDEN, moving TOWARD semi-annual (interim) reporting instead.
2

Scope – the Historical Quarterly Regime

Listed companies filing quarterly reports
This Statement HISTORICALLY applied to LISTED companies required to file QUARTERLY reports (四半期報告書) under the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act, covering each of the first three quarters of the fiscal year (the fourth quarter being effectively covered by the ANNUAL report).
3

Why Quarterly Reporting Was Introduced

Enhanced investor transparency, following international disclosure trends
MANDATORY quarterly reporting was introduced to give INVESTORS more FREQUENT, TIMELY insight into a listed company's financial performance and position – REDUCING the information gap between annual (and previously semi-annual) reports, broadly FOLLOWING international disclosure trends toward more FREQUENT interim reporting seen in other major capital markets at the time.
4

Cumulative vs Single-Quarter Presentation

Two acceptable presentation approaches for the income statement
Companies could present quarterly results EITHER on a CUMULATIVE basis (year-to-date figures, e.g. the first six months for the second quarter) OR on a SINGLE-QUARTER basis (results for THAT quarter alone) – with the CUMULATIVE approach being the MORE COMMONLY adopted method in practice.
5

The 2024–2025 Reform – Moving Toward Semi-Annual Reporting

A deliberate reduction in the statutory reporting burden
As part of a BROADER disclosure reform, Japan MOVED AWAY from requiring FULL statutory quarterly financial statements for the FIRST and THIRD quarters, toward a LIGHTER-TOUCH regime centred on SEMI-ANNUAL (interim, second-quarter) statutory reporting instead – REFLECTED first in ASBJ Statement No. 33 (Semi-annual Financial Reporting), and SUBSEQUENTLY REWORKED into ASBJ Statement No. 37 (Interim Financial Reporting) – a genuinely SIGNIFICANT reduction in the STATUTORY reporting burden for listed companies, reflecting a POLICY judgement that quarterly stock-exchange summary disclosure (Section 6) already provides SUFFICIENT timely information WITHOUT requiring a FULL, separately-standardised statutory quarterly financial statement filing for every quarter.
6

Integration With Stock Exchange Summary Disclosure

"Kessan Tanshin" continuing to provide quarterly market disclosure
SEPARATELY from the STATUTORY financial reporting regime, Japanese stock exchanges (notably the Tokyo Stock Exchange) have LONG required listed companies to release a "KESSAN TANSHIN" (決算短信) – a SUMMARY earnings disclosure – on a QUARTERLY basis, under exchange LISTING RULES rather than the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act itself. The 2024–2025 reform's REDUCTION of the FULL statutory quarterly reporting burden was DESIGNED to work ALONGSIDE this CONTINUING exchange-level quarterly summary disclosure, avoiding DUPLICATIVE reporting obligations.
7

Worked Example – the Historical Quarterly Regime in Outline

Filing a first-quarter report under the old regime
Example: A listed company's Q1 filing before the reform
A listed Japanese company, under the OLD regime, filed a quarterly report for its first fiscal quarter, presenting condensed financial statements on a cumulative basis.
TODAY, the SAME company would generally NOT be required to file a FULL statutory quarterly report for that FIRST quarter – its PRIMARY statutory reporting obligation now centres on the SEMI-ANNUAL (second-quarter) interim report under ASBJ Statement No. 37, with QUARTERLY market transparency continuing to be served through its exchange-mandated "kessan tanshin" summary disclosure instead.
8

Current Treatment – ASBJ Statement No. 37

Interim Financial Reporting as the current primary framework
Today, LISTED companies apply ASBJ Statement No. 37, Interim Financial Reporting, for their SEMI-ANNUAL statutory reporting obligation – the CURRENT successor to this Statement's original quarterly regime, reflecting the genuinely REDUCED statutory reporting frequency now in force.

Relationship to IAS/IFRS

Comparison – J-GAAP's reformed regime vs IAS 34
IAS 34 (Interim Financial Reporting) does NOT itself MANDATE any particular reporting FREQUENCY – it applies WHENEVER an entity prepares an interim report, WHETHER quarterly, semi-annual, or otherwise, leaving the FREQUENCY decision to LOCAL regulatory requirements. Japan's SHIFT from mandatory quarterly to semi-annual statutory reporting is therefore a change in LOCAL REGULATORY POLICY, not a divergence from any SPECIFIC IFRS frequency requirement – IAS 34's PRINCIPLES (condensed statements, consistent accounting policies, materiality assessed at the interim level) remain broadly APPLICABLE regardless of which frequency a jurisdiction chooses.
