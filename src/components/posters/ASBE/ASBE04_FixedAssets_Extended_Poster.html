Conceptual Framework Linkage

Tangible assets used over more than one period
ASBE No. 4 governs the RECOGNITION, MEASUREMENT, and DISCLOSURE of FIXED ASSETS — TANGIBLE assets HELD for use in the PRODUCTION or supply of GOODS or SERVICES, for RENTAL to OTHERS, or for ADMINISTRATIVE purposes, EXPECTED to be used for MORE than ONE accounting PERIOD. The STANDARD broadly PARALLELS IAS 16 internationally, with ONE genuinely SIGNIFICANT measurement divergence (Section 4).
2

Scope and Recognition Criteria

Probable future economic benefits, reliably measurable cost
A FIXED ASSET is RECOGNISED where it is PROBABLE that FUTURE economic BENEFITS associated with the ASSET will FLOW to the ENTERPRISE, and the ASSET's cost can be MEASURED reliably — CONSISTENT with the GENERAL asset recognition PRINCIPLES applied THROUGHOUT the ASBE suite.
3

Initial Measurement — At Cost

Purchase price, taxes, and costs to bring the asset to working condition
A FIXED ASSET is INITIALLY measured at COST — the PURCHASE price, RELATED taxes, and OTHER costs DIRECTLY attributable to BRINGING the asset to its INTENDED working condition and LOCATION (e.g. TRANSPORT, installation, and TESTING costs).
4

THE KEY DIVERGENCE — No Revaluation Model Permitted

Cost model only, throughout an asset's life
UNLIKE IAS 16, which permits an ENTITY to ELECT the REVALUATION model (CARRYING an asset CLASS at FAIR VALUE, updated REGULARLY, WITH increases GENERALLY recognised in OTHER comprehensive income) as an ACCOUNTING policy CHOICE, ASBE No. 4 permits ONLY the COST model — fixed ASSETS are CARRIED at COST less ACCUMULATED depreciation and IMPAIRMENT, WITH NO REVALUATION option AVAILABLE. This is a GENUINELY significant, WELL-KNOWN measurement DIVERGENCE, CONSISTENT with the SAME general CAUTION around FAIR VALUE MEASUREMENT seen ELSEWHERE across the ASBE ASSET standards (Investment Property, BIOLOGICAL Assets — see the COMPANION posters).
5

Componentisation

Separate depreciation for components with materially different useful lives
WHERE a FIXED asset COMPRISES SEPARATE, SIGNIFICANT components with MATERIALLY DIFFERENT useful LIVES (e.g. an AIRCRAFT'S engines VERSUS its AIRFRAME), EACH component is DEPRECIATED SEPARATELY over its OWN useful LIFE — CONSISTENT with IAS 16's OWN componentisation REQUIREMENT internationally.
6

Depreciation Methods and Useful Life

A systematic allocation reflecting the pattern of consumption
DEPRECIATION is CALCULATED using a METHOD reflecting the PATTERN in which the ASSET'S future ECONOMIC benefits are EXPECTED to be CONSUMED — STRAIGHT-LINE, units-of-PRODUCTION, or an ACCELERATED method (e.g. DOUBLE-declining BALANCE) — over the ASSET'S estimated USEFUL life, DOWN to its ESTIMATED residual VALUE.
7

Subsequent Expenditure

Capitalised only if recognition criteria are met
SUBSEQUENT expenditure on a FIXED asset (e.g. a MAJOR overhaul or UPGRADE) is CAPITALISED ONLY where it MEETS the GENERAL recognition CRITERIA — PROBABLE future ECONOMIC benefits and RELIABLE cost MEASUREMENT — OTHERWISE, it is EXPENSED as INCURRED, CONSISTENT with ROUTINE repairs and MAINTENANCE costs.
8

Worked Example — Componentised Depreciation

Separate useful lives for significant components
Example: A manufacturing machine with a replaceable core component
A company purchases a machine for RMB 10,000,000, comprising a core processing unit costing RMB 4,000,000 with a 5-year useful life, and the remaining structure costing RMB 6,000,000 with a 15-year useful life.
The company depreciates the CORE unit separately: RMB 4,000,000 ÷ 5 = RMB 800,000 per year, and the REMAINING structure separately: RMB 6,000,000 ÷ 15 = RMB 400,000 per year — a TOTAL annual depreciation of RMB 1,200,000, REFLECTING each COMPONENT'S own distinct CONSUMPTION pattern.
9

Worked Example — Capitalising a Major Overhaul

Distinguishing capital expenditure from routine repairs
Example: A major equipment overhaul extending useful life
A company spends RMB 1,500,000 on a major overhaul of production equipment, extending its remaining useful life by 5 years and enhancing its output capacity.
Since the overhaul PROVIDES incremental FUTURE economic BENEFITS (an EXTENDED useful LIFE and ENHANCED capacity), the RMB 1,500,000 is CAPITALISED and DEPRECIATED over the REVISED remaining useful LIFE — RATHER than EXPENSED immediately as a ROUTINE repair would be.

Quick-Reference Glossary

Componentisation
Depreciating significant parts of a fixed asset separately when they have materially different useful lives from the asset as a whole.
Residual value
The estimated amount an enterprise would currently obtain from disposal of an asset, after deducting estimated disposal costs, at the end of its useful life.
